## E0393 | term | dissolution
forms: dissolution / Dissolution / dissolve
variants listed by readers (may include other names): dissolved / radical dissolution / solution / dissolute
- [ATWOOD] dissolution: Means of the triumphant reward; the spirit is carried through successive dissolutions to more perfect form; a radical dissolution of the spirit discovers the evil of original sin; dissolution by the water is the key of restoration. | “the process by which the spirit is carried on from each succeeding dissolution into a more perfect form of being” @I.3 n19
- [ATWOOD] dissolution: The stage after which the philosophic work begins; by it vapour, blackness and death are driven out; Atwood calls it an artificial dissolution of the vital bond. | “By an artificial dissolution of the vital bond, by means of Alchemy” @I.3 n64; “and this by dissolution” @I.3 ¶56
- [ATWOOD] Dissolution: The operation that brings the reward. Atwood describes it as dissolving what was fixed (the sensual compact) so that it can be renovated. | “by Dissolution thou shalt possess a triumphant reward” @II.3 ¶37; “that which was fixed, viz., the sensual compact, is dissolved, and that which is dissolved is renovated” @II.3 ¶37
- [ATWOOD] dissolution: The breaking-up of the body of the light and of the natural-born life, in which the three principles separate. Its cause appears to be vital heat artificially stirred in the blood. | “an eclipsation of the light takes place, and a dissolution of its body” @III.1 ¶53
- [ATWOOD] Dissolution: The core operation of the first work: dissolving the subject (in its own blood / own mercury) to remove superfluities; begins the whole work and must be total. | “The beginning of the whole work is a perfect solution” @IV.1 ¶4; “nothing short of dissolution can give it rest” @IV.1 ¶7
- [ATWOOD] dissolve: Term adopted from the old doctrine for the change in the vital relationship in trance; the Alchemists say the sensible medium ought to be dissolved; perfect solution is the beginning of the work. | “We adopt the term dissolve here in accordance with the old doctrine” @II.3 n2
