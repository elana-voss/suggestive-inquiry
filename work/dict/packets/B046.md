## E1382 | term | Prayer
forms: Prayer
variants listed by readers (may include other names): prayer / prayers / pray / prayed
- [ATWOOD] Prayer: Desire directed to its object, especially to God for wisdom; it collects the mind and, joined with labour ('cooperation'), brings revelation. Esoterically it has 'a far deeper sense' and works within the operations; Iamblicus distinguishes three classes leading to union and Divine Fire. | “every desire of the mind is in its kind a prayer and preparative for the acquirement of its object” @III.4 ¶22; “But the esoteric ground brings us acquainted with prayer in a far deeper sense” @III.4 ¶22; “prayer especially, which is its principle exercise, is profitable for great undertakings” @III.4 ¶22 | = great desire of knowing (“for if you burn with a great desire of knowing them, that is prayer” @III.4 ¶22); = Divine Key (“It likewise is the Divine Key which opens to men the penetralia of Wisdom” @III.4 ¶24)

## E1405 | term | projection
forms: projection / Projection
variants listed by readers (may include other names): projections / projected / Projeccion / project / projecting
- [ATWOOD] projection: What Arnold made with the transmuting powder; attested by reports of iron bars converted into gold. Not further defined. | “the wonderful projections he made with the transmuting powder” @I.1 ¶43
- [QUOTED: Nicholas Flamel] Projection: The act, after the magistery, of applying the Stone to a substance (here mercury), which Flamel says turned it into silver or, with the Red Stone, gold. | “Having attained this, I came now to the Projection; and the first time I made projection, was upon mercury” @I.1 ¶57
- [ATWOOD] projection: The operation of applying the elixir or powder to a metal to transmute it. | “In Dee’s diary we have the account of Kelly making projection with one small grain” @I.1 ¶66; “However, I made projection of a part” @I.1 ¶73
- [QUOTED: Van Helmont] projection: Casting a small amount of the powder on boiling quicksilver to transmute it into gold. | “I have made projections divers times” @I.1 ¶76
- [QUOTED: the adept] projection: Application of the medicine to imperfect metals, which must first be fermented with pure gold to bear its subtlety. | “there would ensue loss and damage in the projection” @I.3 n70
- [QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon] Projection: The final use or test of the product; those ignorant of imbibition found nothing in it; continually fermented medicine 'in Projeccion ren full fast'. | “And sure far it will be exalted at the last, And in Projeccion ren full fast:” @III.1 ¶65
- [ATWOOD] projection: Casting the Mercury on 'the imperfect metal of any life', which it dissolves, drawing its foundation into itself. | “projected on the imperfect metal of any life, dissolves, drawing away the foundation into itself” @III.3 ¶32
- [QUOTED: fraternity of the Rosy Cross] projection: Fifth and last gate; serves only for metals. | “The fifth and last gate is projection.” @III.3 ¶63
- [QUOTED: Eudoxus] Projection: Projection of the Medicine; deliberately not described, since success does not depend on it. | “the Projection of the Medicine” @IV.3 ¶16
- [ATWOOD] Projection: Casting the Stone or medicine onto metal or mercury to tinge or transmute it, done stepwise and by degrees onto purified metal. | “it is always best to make projection by degrees until the Stone yields no more tincture” @IV.3 ¶20 | = metalline perfectibility of the matter (“the final Projection, as the metalline perfectibility of the matter is sometimes called” @IV.3 ¶19)
