## E0298 | term | coction
forms: coction / decoction

- [ATWOOD] coction: A cooking/maturing operation; with superior skill it yields the elixir in vegetal life. Not defined in this chunk. | “through superior skill and coction in the vegetal life” @I.2 ¶46
- [QUOTED: Basil Valentine] coction: The boiling and maturing of the stone of Fire with the microcosm's corporeal fire. | “let no man wonder at this coction” @III.1 ¶37
- [ATWOOD] coction: The cooking or digesting that goes on during the severation. The waters 'decoct' the sensual dominant, and the dragons are 'decocted' in their own venom. | “a wonderful coction is described as going on” @III.1 ¶53
- [QUOTED: Synesius] coction: Gentle, gradual, long cooking; the single operation of which all the named operations are names. | “the coction of this water” @III.3 ¶60 | = dissolve, calcine, tinge, whiten, renew, bathe, wash, coagulate, imbibe, fix, grind, dry, distill (“are all one, and signify no more than to decoct nature until such time as she be perfected” @III.3 ¶60)
- [ATWOOD] decoction: The first part of the work: it dissolves the matter and discharges its impurities until the matter is close to annihilation and freed from every outward form, and so it reduces the matter. | “the former Hermes calls decoction, which dissolving the matter discharges also its impurities by a proper rule” @I.3 n7
- [QUOTED: Zachary] decoction: The cooking process, carried on linearly until complete. | “donec decoctio compleatur” @IV.1 ¶9
