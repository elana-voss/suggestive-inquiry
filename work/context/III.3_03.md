[III.3 ¶44] And these were a part of the lesson taught by the Memphian prophet to the young Aspirant to the
priesthood, even the most hidden mysteries of God’s creation. And how did he teach? By words
merely, or signs, or traditional authority? Or, if none of these can truly reach the understanding; shall
we say, more probably, by passing it inwards to the evolution of its proper mystery, thence to emanate
and recreate? When the initiated poet Ovid sat down to write his Fasti, he was inspired, as he declares,
by that same universal deity of the two-faced Janus.
