## Subject

1. **The human being is laboratory, vessel, subject and operator; the matter is his own life, drawn out of himself.** "Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art" (II.1 ¶30); "with thee it is found; by thee it is received" (II.1 ¶16). — *stated; ATWOOD, Morien endorsed.*

2. **Choose the noblest subject, which contains all the rest.** "the best and noblest ought to be chosen to operate with" (II.1 ¶9). — *stated; QUOTED: Boehme, Agrippa and Paracelsus' disciples.*

3. **More exactly: the vital life, fire, vital spirit, blood, circulation.** "a pure, naked, and unmingled fire of infinite capability" (II.1 ¶17); "the vitality of the greater world, which is included by respiration in the blood of all creatures" (II.2 ¶18). — *partly stated, partly interpreted (II.1 ¶15, ¶21).*

4. **In the Mysteries chapters the subject is the universal Mundane Spirit as first conscious in man: passive, convertible, intermediate, despised.** "a middle substance --- passive, undetermined, susceptible of conversion and all extremes" (II.2 ¶26); "it hath it in its sight, carries it in its hands, yet is ignorant thereof" (II.2 ¶18). — *stated; ATWOOD; that the art works on this spirit interpreted (II.2 ¶10, ¶14).*

5. **In its natural state it is degraded and defiled: the "vulgar Matter", the Black Saturn, the shapeless First Matter.** "as a subject fallen from its sphere and defiled" (II.2 ¶16); "It is the Black Saturn of the adepts, and that appearing corruption that precedes the mystical death and regeneration into new life" (II.3 ¶18); "The eye of man never saw her twice under one and the same shape" (II.3 ¶30). — *stated; ATWOOD, Vaughan; vulgar Matter = impure spirit interpreted.*

6. **Beneath lies a divine germ, a latent Reason, present but unawakened.** "a Principle of Universal Science latent in human life, real and efficacious though cognizable only under certain conditions which they specify" (II.1 ¶39); "the total Reason is in this life of ours hidden, as the fire in fuel that is not kindled, or as gold in the dark ore unseen" (II.4 ¶39). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

7. **All three principles lie together in the one subject; nothing need be destroyed to get them.** "lie hid together in one thing, well known" (II.1 ¶11); "the true alkaline original of life in this threefold essence separately contained" (II.3 ¶41). — *stated (Basil); one thing = man interpreted.*

8. **What is dissolved is the fixed bond to sense, not the body.** "that which was fixed, viz., the sensual compact, is dissolved, and that which is dissolved is renovated" (II.3 ¶37); "And all without destruction to the mortal body" (II.3 ¶37). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

9. **The matter costs nothing and is in all hands.** "the poor as well as the rich may have it in the same equal plenty" (II.1 ¶14). — *stated; QUOTED: Aliphili.*

10. **The vessel is withheld by all authorities; the Part implies it is within the person, later a cleansed receptacle of the new light.** "with exception of the vessel; which is a divine secret" (II.1 ¶8); "that living temple wherein alone the wise of all ages have been securely able to raise their rejected Corner Stone" (II.1 ¶8); "the already prepared medial receptacle of the newborn light" (II.4 ¶9). — *stated that it is concealed and essential; location interpreted.*

11. **The subject revives stronger from every dissolution.** "this mystical nature revives, fortified from every successive dissolution" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

12. **The descent reaches the first life, the lapsed soul (Proserpine), and a Power hidden in man; all knowledge is already within; the object is real experience, not trance.** "the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life" (II.3 ¶7); "This therefore is that Power which is hidden in man" (II.3 ¶55); "all things are in us psychically, that is to say, in the efficient Reason of our life" (II.4 ¶29, QUOTED: Proclus); "no common trance or day-dream, or any fanatical vision of celestials" (II.1 ¶46). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

## Agent

1. **The vital force, present and active, is what works.** "nothing vitally alternative is achieved, unless the vital force be present and in action" (II.1 ¶5). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

2. **The working fire is internal, named under many figures whose identity is never asserted.** "that internal fire and vessel of the adepts, so essential to the accomplishment of the Hermetic work" (II.1 ¶6); "no process can finally fail where the invisible Universal Mercury, or spiritual air of Antimony, is present" (II.1 ¶21). — *stated that it is essential and internal; ATWOOD, Boehme; identities interpreted.*

3. **The agent is within us and is the subject itself: the one thing acts on itself.** "in us is the Operator of all wonderful effects" (II.1 ¶26); "purifying and to be purified itself by itself, in turn agent and patient" (II.2 ¶26). — *stated; QUOTED: Agrippa; ATWOOD.*

4. **Intellect, the re-entering Light of Reason, is the agent of the descent: the adepts' Sulfur.** "the re-entering Light of Reason in the Mysteries, which is that Sulfur of adepts, causing all this manifold scenery in the disruption of life" (II.3 ¶30); "the inquisitive light once entering as a ferment combats meeting its proper pole, and conjointly kindling with it, absorbs, transmutes, and occultates the surrounding medium" (II.3 n40). — *stated (¶30); bough = intellect interpreted; n40 UNCLEAR.*

5. **Two fires, outer and inner, drawn together; the Prester is the Fire Spirit of Life. In II.4 the rational soul is pure fire infused into the vital spirit, quiet until God's will stirs it; joining this to the Prester is interpreted.** "the Prester and the Sun, the external and internal fire of life, the thing desiring and the thing desired" (II.3 ¶41); "This soul was of that most pure fire, infused by God into the vital spirit" (II.4 ¶39). — *stated; QUOTED: the adept, Sendivogius; ATWOOD.*

6. **Wisdom/Intellect is a real forming power; the soul in the blood governs and acts most powerfully out of the body.** "an affirmative operative hypostasis, informing, invigorating, and sustaining all things" (II.1 ¶44); "hath a far greater operation out of the body, because out of the body it absolutely reigns" (II.1 ¶10). — *stated; ATWOOD, Sendivogius.*

7. **The decisive agency is divine and theurgic: real media that work of themselves, and an illumination the soul cannot produce.** "the work of Piety leads it by the hand of God" (II.1 ¶51); "the Synthemata, i.e., the Theurgic aids and media themselves, perform by themselves their proper work" (II.4 ¶31); "Without the Pontic Medium, without Theurgic assistance we are unable to transcend the consciousness of this life" (II.4 ¶49); rites act "by the medium of the passive Ether" (II.4 ¶36). None is described. — *stated; QUOTED: Psellus, Iamblicus; ATWOOD.*

8. **Will is the chief purifier on the human side.** "Will is the greatest part of purgation" (II.2 ¶13). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

9. **A central attracting power moves the ascent.** "when the central magnet moves to the ascent" (II.4 ¶19). — *interpreted; referent open.*

10. **The true Efficient is within; the light of the true initiations was the Light of Life, which could be kindled.** "For there the true Efficient is to be found, which is not externally developed" (II.3 ¶46); "was the Light of Life which these could kindle and fortify" (II.4 ¶56). — *stated; ATWOOD ("these" open).*

## Operator

1. **Moral purity, piety, justice, chastity.** "to be good and just and of a profound reason, ready at hand to help mankind" (II.1 ¶14); "by sedulous obedience, religious services, and inviolable chastity" (II.4 ¶17). — *stated; QUOTED: Hermes, Apuleius.*

2. **Desire, will and consent first; a will held by a purely rational motive.** "without desire on our part, art will labor for us in vain" (II.2 ¶13); "a motive purely rational to withstand the temptations of its next including sphere" (II.3 ¶9). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

3. **A philosopher labouring rationally, knowing the principles in himself, concentrated and penetrating.** "None but a philosopher ever achieved the work, or for reasons that are imperative, ever will." (II.2 ¶29); "he who in himself knows no natural principles, is very remote" (II.1 ¶11); "an intellect penetrating into other spheres, rather than discursive in its own" (II.3 ¶5). — *stated; ATWOOD, Geber.*

4. **Preparation, ordeals, initiation, selection; capacity varies.** "they only, amongst the ancients who had fulfilled the previous rites and undergone all the required ordeals, were entrusted with the passport" (II.3 ¶5); "nor were the rites of Eleusis found to be equally efficacious for all" (II.4 ¶54). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

5. **Instruction and a guide; the art is entrusted.** "no art or skill availeth, unless one intrust another with somewhat" (II.1 ¶25); "only follow your guide who will offer himself to you and will meet you in the way" (II.3 ¶32). Guide unidentified. — *stated; QUOTED: Boehme, Brethren of the Rosy Cross.*

6. **Faith, love of truth, prayer, courage; never greed.** "through the love of truth by faith" (II.2 ¶13); "arm yourself with a resolute heroic courage" (II.3 ¶32); "nor will these studies be found to reward the sordid seeker after riches or gold only" (II.1 ¶30). — *stated.*

7. **Simplicity of mind, self-knowledge, will subdued to Wisdom's Law.** "known only to the wisely simple who have subdued their will to the Law of Wisdom" (II.3 ¶55). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

8. **Experimental, persevering labour, not speculation.** "to change their abstractions into extractions" (II.1 ¶26); "persevering labor and experience are required to ensure success" (II.3 ¶36). — *stated; Vaughan; ATWOOD.*

9. **At the end the operator ceases intellectual effort; his dispositions are only concauses.** "when we do not energize intellectually (all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)" (II.4 ¶31). — *stated; Iamblichus endorsed.*

10. **Possess the Art, its expressions, and the medium.** "he that knows the Art, and the expressions, and hath the medium, will be richer than any other" (II.2 ¶18). — *interpreted; QUOTED: the adept.*

11. **Secrecy.** "the Seal of God lieth before it, to conceal the true ground, unless a man knew for certain that it would not be misused" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; QUOTED: Boehme; also II.3 ¶7, II.4 ¶56 (ATWOOD).*

## Procedure and stages

Three kinds of sequence: (A) the cited colour-process in laboratory language (II.1 ¶21–25); (B) the inward philosophic sequence (II.1 ¶9, ¶26, ¶35–53); (C) the order of the Mysteries (II.2–II.4), which Atwood equates with the Hermetic work. The order below follows (C), with (A) and (B) placed where the text aligns them. Cross-chapter alignment is *interpreted* unless marked.

**General**

1. **Look within; self-investigation as actual practice.** "this advice was meant experimentally and ontologically" (II.1 ¶39); "If that were applied inwardly which now looks out, and every natural impediment removed, experience might then reveal to us the antecedent life" (II.2 ¶21). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

2. **Reduce nature to uncover the hidden principle, destroying only the modal life, removing superfluities.** "such a reducation of nature as shall discover this Latex without destroying her vehicle, but the modal life only" (II.1 ¶47); "only in the preparation removing superfluities" (II.2 ¶26). — *interpreted (II.1); stated (Aristhenes).*

3. **Take the subject where nature leaves it; the work is simple, the wisdom hard.** "taking her where nature leaves" (II.2 ¶27); "the Work is easy and simple, but the wisdom therein is great and the greatest mystery" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; ATWOOD, Boehme.*

4. **Dissolve, volatilize, fix, repeatedly; first rule, perfect solution; cleanse, then fix.** "to dissolve and recongeal the spirit, to make the fixed volatile and the volatile fixed" (II.3 n2); "The first rule of the work is a perfect solution" (II.2 ¶16), glossed "solve, that is comprehend, it on its own ground" (II.2 ¶15); "as dyers previously purify garments, and then fix in the colors" (II.2 ¶11). — *stated; also "Sove, Coagula, Fige" (II.1 ¶22; ours, interpreted).*

5. **Apply alchemy to human life by a method and medium of attraction.** "applying the practice of Alchemy to human life; suggesting also, as did the latter, the method and medium of attraction" (II.1 ¶9). — *interpreted; ATWOOD.*

6. **The operative particulars were deliberately disguised.** "what was their immediate efficient? Whence and whereon did they direct their fire? These things, with the laboratory, its vessels and various apparatus, they have disguised" (II.1 ¶47). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

7. **Named operations, unexplained.** "as of amalgamation, distillation, filtration, digestion, and lastly by sublimation to the Head of an appropriate Vessel" (II.2 ¶27). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

8. **Hermes' order; the fiery water's dissolution.** "except thou understandeth how to mortify and induce generation, to vivify the spirit, to cleanse and introduce light" (II.1 ¶23, QUOTED: Hermes, endorsed); the fiery water "reduces it into a fit black earth, like to black spittle" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Aliphili). — *stated.*

9. **Withdraw from the senses and gather to unity (Sequence B).** "the soul of man, being estranged from the corporeal senses, adheres to a divine nature" (II.1 ¶9, Agrippa); "she ascends to the highest watchtower of beings" (II.1 ¶37, Plato). — *stated; both endorsed.*

**Stage 1. The inner life opened (Lesser Mysteries; trance as entry).** "this was the only popular initiation open to all" (II.3 ¶3); "Previous to the purificative rites little change, therefore, was effected" (II.3 ¶4); entrancing the senses is "the key of the Hermetic vestibule" (II.3 ¶5). The mind is "laid asleep from its present life so utterly as to be brought into its divine nature" (II.1 ¶26). Lucidity and prevision belong here and were not valued (II.2 ¶8, interpreted). — *stated; ATWOOD, Agrippa endorsed; placing II.1 ¶26 here interpreted.*

**Stage 2. Purificative Rites: separating the Vital Spirit from the imprints of sense; reason restored to rule.** "to segregate the Vital Spirit away from those defilements and imaginative impressures which, by the birth into sense, had become implanted there" (II.2 ¶8); "the mental atmosphere made clear and passive before its objective light. Without this, they promise nothing; with it, all." (II.2 ¶22); rites "designed also to restore the monarchy of reason in the soul" (II.2 ¶20), "preparatory to undergoing the final initiations" (II.2 ¶20); trance alone leaves "a false imagination, which it is requisite to destroy" (II.2 ¶10). Alchemically: "all those preparations, solutions, calcinations, etc. before it becomes to be the Mercury of the Philosophers" (II.2 ¶16). Reason must act, not stay passive (II.2 ¶15). This is the Augean labour, "to turn the current of life into another channel, and purify the natural source" (II.2 ¶19). — *stated; ATWOOD; alchemical operations = this purification interpreted.*

**Stage 3. Descent: consciousness translated beneath the medium, through darkness, toward the Central Source; the black threshold.** "to translate the consciousness underneath this medium by obscuration, towards the Central Source" (II.3 ¶5); "when the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life" (II.3 ¶7). Sequence A's first stage: "in three months circulation, says he, by digestion, the powder becomes black, the opposition of attraction and repulsion ceases (in the vital spirit)" (II.1 ¶23); "sunk down black and motionless. And thus the head of Hermes’ crow is said to be in the beginning of this work" (II.1 ¶23); "the original evil is made manifest in the matter to be renewed" (II.1 ¶23). The mountain letter: night, guide, wind, earthquake, "a fire that will consume the earthly rubbish, and discover the treasure: but as yet you cannot see it" (II.3 ¶33), calm. — *stated (II.3 ¶5, ¶7; II.1 ¶23); alignment interpreted.*

**Stage 4. Holding course through the image-world; drying the moist vehicle.** "her final duty to separate the good and reject the evil therein by birth allied" (II.3 ¶12); "he apply not his mind to it, nor esteem the voice proceeding thence to be true" (II.3 ¶20); "strives to penetrate centrally without exploring the intermediate spheres), then she becomes a dry splendor from exhalation" (II.3 ¶27); "which if thou salt withdraw so often until the redness is made pure" (II.3 ¶27). Trials are intrinsic: "those temptations and trials which the consciousness must necessarily pass through, all the while regressive, before it reaches into the central illumination of truth" (II.2 ¶14). — *stated; Psellus, Porphyry, Hermes endorsed; "evaporative" = drying interpreted.*

**Stage 5. Rectifying the interior earth; finding the Seed; analysis to the foundation.** "our dark divulsed chaotic life from sense, which opened and rectified, dissolved and reunited, is changed from an earthly to a spiritual body, by rapport divine" (II.3 ¶29); "within the material extreme of this life, when it is purified, the Seed of Spirit is at last found" (II.3 ¶30); decompose natural life "stone by stone, setting all aside, with the dirt and rubbish, as he proceeds" (II.3 ¶41). — *stated; ATWOOD, the adept.*

**Stage 6. Philosophic death: dissolving the sensual compact; end of the Lesser Mysteries; expiration.** "And here the Lesser Mysteries ended; the soul as it were, on the borders of the Stygian lake in view of Tartarus" (II.3 ¶36); the soul "may dissolve her own conceptive vehicle, even the parental bond, and return consciously (the elementary principles remaining, nor yet suffered to depart) under the dominion of another law to life" (II.3 ¶37); "the first stage is nothing but errors and uncertainties; laborings, wandering, and darkness" (II.3 ¶37). II.4: "a liberation is effected through agony, as it were of death" (II.4 ¶19); the Reason "nor can be awakened to reminiscence without a resolution of the whole circulatory confine, when I arises identically reverse, perfect, and alone" (II.4 ¶39). — *stated; Porphyry, Stoboeus endorsed; ATWOOD. One step across chapters interpreted.*

**Stage 7. Whitening; reason enthroned; Elysian intellection.** "mild action of the principles in harmony have produced a brilliant whiteness in the matter" (II.1 ¶24). The mind's eye "purifying to the center, is converted and raised; and, as a Unit, now regards the circumference transitively" (II.4 ¶15); "the inferior powers will so venerate his leading motive that they will move only according to his movement" (II.4 ¶38); Elysium is "the nourishment and education of the understanding vehicle now standing in open presence before its Archetypal Light" (II.4 ¶16), "though there is a grade above" (II.4 ¶15). — *stated; Boehme; ATWOOD. Whitening = Elysium interpreted.*

**Stage 8. Conversion to the Generator; Introspection; kindling.** "the soul when purified should associate with its generator" (II.3 ¶43); "When the initiated person sees the Divine Light itself without any form or figure" (II.3 ¶46); "until light meeting light apprehends itself alone" (II.3 ¶50); "on a sudden a light, as it were a leaping fire, will be enkindled in the soul" (II.3 ¶50). II.4: "the eye of mind, all the while faithfully regarding, is drawn to contact suddenly, unable longer to sustain itself alone" (II.4 ¶19), "This is the method and arcane principle of Self- Knowledge, and the narrow way of regeneration into life" (II.4 ¶19). Compare II.1 ¶37 ("contact with its Antecedent Cause"). — *stated; Porphyry, Psellus, Plato endorsed; ATWOOD.*

**Stage 9. Reddening; ignition; the new world.** "which in three months more become a brilliant yellow, red, or purpling tincture" (II.1 ¶24); "the reign of sin is finished, and the king doth rule, and now he is invested wit the red garment" (II.1 ¶24). "Open the compound creature; look up the elements; divide the elements, and you shall find the quintessential nature" (II.3 ¶52); "He that maketh a furnace with our glass to it maketh a new world (58); --- a new hypostasis, and a new stone" (II.3 ¶52); the Demogorgon "at length becoming ignited" (II.3 ¶52). Furnace, glass, "triple proportion" and "generative heat" (II.3 ¶52) are unexplained. — *stated instructions; ignition as a stage interpreted.*

**Stage 10. Through the gods to the Unity; translation; the Paternal Port.** "having passed orderly through the whole progression of Intelligible Causes, by the Greeks called gods, were next promoted to a contemplation of their Highest Unity" (II.4 ¶39); "His contemplation, then, of the indwelling Unity was the final preparative to translation" (II.4 ¶40). Manner: "let the air and the universe be still (within us)" (II.4 ¶41); "Extend the empty mind of thy soul towards the Intelligible" (II.4 ¶47); "through the uncial silence imparted by faith" (II.4 ¶40). Then "He comes, says Plotinus, suddenly alone, bringing with him his own empyreal universe and total deity, in one" (II.4 ¶50). — *stated; Proclus, the Oracle, Plotinus endorsed; ATWOOD. "Translation" undefined.*

**Stage 11. Repetition and multiplication.** "if the process fails in any stage, an addition of the same will renew it" (II.1 ¶25); "increased tenfold in strength and quantity by each digestion of it with fresh i, wet with gas water, or oil of this antimony" (II.1 ¶25). "the grand perfection of their Art was to multiply the Prester and place him in the most supreme Ether" (II.3 ¶56). "increasing in divine virtue by each ascent, until the total life was irradiated" (II.4 ¶52). — *stated in each chapter; one multiplication across them interpreted.*

**Firmness.** Stated orders: Lesser Mysteries → Purificative Rites → final initiations (II.2 ¶20, hedged "as we take it"); telete → muesis → epopteia (II.4 ¶23); Hades vision before elysian light (II.4 ¶19, ¶29); Elysium below the Paternal union (II.4 ¶15, ¶41); Unity before translation (II.4 ¶40); black → white → red at three-month intervals (II.1 ¶23–24). Mapping A onto the Mysteries is interpreted (see Tensions 14).

## Signs

1. **Blackness and stillness at the start; the original evil appears with its corrective; a dark, grave-smelling, powerful entry.** "sunk down black and motionless" (II.1 ¶23); "on either side it is a signal of Art" (II.1 ¶23; II.3 ¶37); "giving forth a murky odor, like that of graves" (II.3 ¶18). — *stated.*

2. **Perplexity, dread, opposition at the threshold.** "a state of solicitude and painful amazement" (II.3 ¶29); "dread fills the sould about to relinquish her natal bond in life" (II.3 ¶37); "the material inflictors will then be roused to vengeance" (II.2 ¶13). — *stated; as signs interpreted.*

3. **Shifting shapes and crowded images mark Hades.** "as clouds driven by the wind are forced to this and that figure, but cannot possibly retain one constant form" (II.3 ¶30); "conversant with the whole phantasmagoric universe of his sphere" (II.3 ¶22). — *stated.*

4. **The vehicle mirrors the mind and measures purification.** "when it is in a wise and purified condition, is attenuated and clear seeing; when however the mind is sensually affected, then this vehicle is dulled and becomes terrene" (II.2 ¶15). — *stated; ATWOOD. Also II.4 ¶36 (Porphyry).*

5. **Test of a true vision: light without form; figured images are the mind's own.** "sees a mere apparition, as, for instance, of light in some form or figure" (II.3 ¶20); gods "by no means extend themselves as figured phantasms" (II.4 ¶29); "know that to be the mind’s maya (or image or delusion) as of light or darkness" (II.3 ¶48). — *stated; Psellus, Iamblichus, Indian record endorsed.*

6. **The Seed and the lapsed soul.** "a decompounded ens, extremely heavy, shining through the darkness like a fiery star, being full of eyes like pearls or aglets" (II.3 ¶30); "whose vapor is so subtle and transient that nothing but the glance of its proper intellect by faith can arrest it" (II.3 ¶11). — *stated, unexplained.*

7. **Colours black, white, yellow/red/purple; with the new light the colours return and circulation restarts.** (II.1 ¶23–24); "all the vegetable colours, before obliterated in darkness, return to neutralize their poison and restore the suspended circulation to a conscious equilibriate accord" (II.4 ¶9). — *stated; II.4 referent interpreted.*

8. **Calm, day-star, midnight Sun, a sudden flash while awake; the cloud lifts.** "you shall see the day-star arise, and the darkness will disappear" (II.3 ¶33); "At midnight I saw the Sun shining with a splendid light" (II.3 ¶45); "seen whilst vigilant you brave the deep" (II.3 ¶51); "the cloud is taken away from before his eyes" (II.1 ¶10). — *stated.*

9. **Languor on seeing the Divine Nature.** "affected in the same manner as fishes, when they are drawn upwards from the dark and turbid waters into the diaphanous clear air" (II.4 ¶19). — *stated; Iamblichus.*

10. **The Stone as balanced lines, a wheel, an armed magnet.** "an armed magnet included and circulating in a perpetual heaven" (II.1 ¶25); "as a luciferous wheel, with its radiant sections, all comprehending in their law" (II.3 ¶52). — *stated (UNCLEAR); interpreted (II.3).*

11. **Certainty by contact; inquiry ends; radiance spreads outward.** "it would then assert; and the assertion, as respects itself, would be true" (II.4 ¶49); "that mind which is become wise needs not to investigate any further" (II.1 ¶37); radiance "until it moved the dark circumference of sense itself" (II.4 ¶54). Lucidity and prevision are not signs of the higher work (II.2 ¶8, interpreted). — *stated; outward radiance as completion interpreted.*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Sense-dependence and imagination.** "nature, having her proper light, is by the shadowy body of sense, hidden from our eyes" (II.1 ¶10); "we are not accustomed to cogitate without imagination" (II.2 ¶10); "converted to externals, we desert our best life unconsciously" (II.4 ¶9). — *stated.*

2. **Fallen imbalance; anarchy of motives.** "the repulsive force so far predominating over the interior attraction, that the total circulatory life is expulsive" (II.1 ¶21); "the government of the natural life is oligarchical, almost an anarchy" (II.4 ¶38). — *stated; II.1 UNCLEAR.*

3. **Wrong method: literalism, outward search, modern chemistry, undirected mesmerism.** "modern art drives out, in fact, the very nature which the ancients prized" (II.1 ¶5); "the means they employed were from literal readings of receipts" (II.1 ¶6); "Our sleepwalkers are little better than dreamers" (II.2 ¶7). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

4. **Concealment and falsification of the texts.** "his revealment was instantly annulled by false or weakening commentaries, or as quickly as possible withdrawn" (II.1 ¶8). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

5. **Lack of desire; ignorance of ignorance; forgetfulness.** "which being ignorant that it is ignorant has no desire to emerge" (II.2 ¶13); "a threefold barrier of sense, ignorance, and disinclination" (II.4 ¶40); "oblivion, the mother of ignorance, stepped in" (II.4 ¶19). — *stated.*

6. **Entering unpurified: useless and dangerous; the Sphinx devours; evil imagination defiles the Spirit.** "with this same misunderstanding and infected Spirit, we enter in for the discovery and contemplation of ourselves, it will be useless" (II.2 ¶14); "if reason remains passive, this nature at length prevailing, will ravage and devastate and take possession of the whole mind" (II.2 ¶15); "defile by an evil imagination the very Spirit that ought to be refined" (II.1 ¶14, interpreted). — *stated; ATWOOD, Hermes.*

7. **Dangers of Hades: capture, sleep, phantoms, healing spirits, forgetting, the precipice.** "lest unprepared souls, presuming to enter, should be taken captive by deluding and fatal desires, and work irremediable evil there" (II.3 ¶7); "To fall asleep in Hades was indeed to be absorbed, without the encumbrance of body, in all its defilements" (II.3 ¶12); "material images, assuming the appearance even of things divine" (II.3 ¶22); healing spirits "deceive and detain the unwary" (II.3 ¶23); "Stoop not down, for a precipice lies below in the earth;" (II.3 ¶42). — *stated; Psellus, Proclus, Basil Valentine, the oracle; ATWOOD.*

8. **Clinging to nature; love of bodily beauty; inability to sustain contemplation; turning back.** "it attracts moisture when it continually endeavors to associate with nature" (II.3 ¶27); "his natural inability to sustain the arduous altitude of divine contemplation" (II.3 ¶13); "Hoc opus, hic labor est." (II.3 ¶8). — *stated.*

9. **Theory, enthusiasm, abstraction, unaided reason all fail.** "But not reason, nor enthusiasm, nor ardent desire, nor an intellectual conception, nor abstraction, as we are taught, conjoins theurgist with the One" (II.4 ¶50); "every attempt of the unassisted reason terminates negatively" (II.1 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

10. **Two opposite failures: content with visions, or wholly outward.** "satisfied in the dreams and deluding visions of an included imagination" (II.1 ¶39); "in whom the spirit of inquiry is wholly drawn to externals" (II.1 ¶39); "dismiss all visions which take place during the imperfect self-activity of the human mind as arbitrary and untrustworthy" (II.4 ¶29). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

11. **Nothing compound survives the Fire; having fire is not drawing it forth.** "Fire will not suffer anything that is compounded to come near to it" (II.4 ¶39); "many had the fire indeed, and were able even to perceive it, who were without the power to discover and draw it forth to manifestation" (II.4 ¶54). — *stated; Sendivogius; ATWOOD.*

12. **Errors on the way are obstacles, not refutations; without the method nothing can be tested.** "those delusive visions and errors which occur during the conscious transference to a more excellent condition of being" (II.3 ¶36); "being ignorant of the method, how should we presume to test the truth of this philosophy?" (II.1 ¶53). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

## Result

1. **The purified subject: Mercury of the Philosophers, the Stone, a new Form of Light, a spiritual body.** "the Mercury of the Philosophers --- pure, agile, intelligent, living --- as they say, in her own sphere, as a queen upon her throne" (II.2 ¶16); "establishing her in a new and concentric Form of Light" (II.2 ¶27); "changed from an earthly to a spiritual body, by rapport divine" (II.3 ¶29). — *stated; Mercury = purified spirit interpreted.*

2. **The internal Sun: the Stone ignited, the Prester set in the Ether.** "the true Astrum Solis gotten and conceived, the internal spiritual Sun which is the Perpetual Motion of the Wise" (II.3 ¶52); "that Saturnian Salt which, developed to intellect and made erect, subdues all nature to his will" (II.3 ¶52); the Prester "abides shining, not burning as below, or wrathful; but vital, calm, transmuting, recreating, and no longer a Consuming Fire" (II.3 ¶56). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

3. **The latent Reason awakened: Wisdom revealed, the rector restored, the Causal light manifest.** "the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom, which, when revealed and set alone, all the forms of things and hidden springs of nature become intuitively known" (II.1 ¶32); "to restore the true rector to his original rule" (II.1 ¶21); "This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum; their Secretu, Secretorum" (II.4 ¶39). — *stated.*

4. **Regeneration with the body preserved; freedom; life in open day.** "the willing life was made to pass out of its present oblivious fall, through regeneration, into the reminiscent consciousness of her Causal Source" (II.3 ¶37); "re-entering into and fortifying it with the elixir of an immortal life." (II.3 ¶39); "having vanquished an obscure and terrestrial life, he afterwards lived in open day" (II.4 ¶3). — *stated.*

5. **Union with the source in identity: the Whole, the Generator, the Paternal Port.** "a certain Art by which a Particular Spirit may be united to the Universal" (II.1 ¶26); "the soul knows herself as a Whole which before knew but a part only of her human nature" (II.3 ¶46); "to meet with him, to be united to Him, and to see Him Himself --- the Alone with the Alone" (II.4 ¶41); "nor as before, either in separation of subject and object; but absolutely, in Identity" (II.4 ¶42); "the rebirth and recreation of this principle in the Free Ether, prepared for it, is the end and plenitude of initiatory rites" (II.4 ¶25). — *stated; Vaughan, Proclus endorsed; ATWOOD.*

6. **Knowledge of all things and of God; Ontological Wisdom; the Epopteia.** "Whosoever, therefore, shall know himself, shall know all things in himself; but especially he shall know God" (II.1 ¶9); "became cognizant partakers in the Wisdom of true Being" (II.2 ¶5); "the Pantheistic revealment of the Universal Nature, her secret foundation, the soul’s essence, origin, hindrances, and proper end" (II.4 ¶21). — *stated.*

7. **Practical fruits; elixir and gold as by-products.** "the medicine of men and metals" (II.1 ¶25); "imparts virtue with understanding, and universal knowledge, health of body, and long length of days" (II.4 ¶19); gold and elixir "Both of which are vital products" (II.4 ¶58); "Cujus tunc Co-operator effectus potest Omnia." (II.4 ¶54; ours, interpreted). — *stated.*

## Principles

1. **One subject contains all; investigate that one.** "then this one needs only to be investigated for the discovery of all" (II.1 ¶9). — *stated.*

2. **Self-Knowledge is the root; the doubt is about means, not possibility.** "Self-Knowledge to be at the root of all Alchemical tradition" (II.1 ¶30); "the doubt rather remains also about the discovery of means, than the possibility of self-knowledge" (II.2 ¶23). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

3. **Man is a microcosm; his rank comes from Divine Reason.** "Thou hast in thy body the anatomy of the whole world" (II.1 ¶10); "the essence of everything is the summit of its nature" (II.1 ¶35). — *stated.*

4. **Three principles; harmony when balanced; only one Matter can be so harmonized.** "when they are in equilibrium, a circle is produced" (II.1 ¶19); "there is but One Matter truly qualifiable or capable of qualifying matter to be harmonized in this way" (II.1 ¶20); "equilibriate circulation in their Ether" (II.4 ¶29). — *stated; II.1 voice UNCLEAR.*

5. **The Art is Theurgy: rites really produce what they signify, by fixed canons; Wisdom came from a vital experiment.** "represents the rites themselves as really efficacious to procure it" (II.2 ¶4); "neither is it lawful to consider these canons as mutable" (II.2 ¶13); "Wisdom was the offspring of a vital experiment into nature" (II.1 ¶52). — *stated.*

6. **Art perfects only a middle thing; the subject takes the direction given it.** "neither from an utterly imperfect thing can art produce perfection" (II.2 ¶26); "the mind, once seated in this Spirit, should either follow or draw or be drawn by it" (II.2 ¶14). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

7. **Life is fire in moisture; unpurified moisture is Hades; imagination makes and desire animates the images.** "a thick fire imprisoned in a certain incombustible aerial moisture" (II.3 ¶29); "it is the imaginative spirit which is the maker of these images, as in dreams, only more intense" (II.3 ¶22); "desire, entering into those aerial forms, is said to vivify them" (II.3 ¶24). — *stated.*

8. **The light produces the dark phase; the seed lies in the purified lowest extreme.** "Our Stone comes with light and with light it is generated, and then it brings forth the clouds, and darkness which is the mother of all things" (II.3 ¶30, interpreted as this). — *Hermes; ATWOOD.*

9. **What the soul binds she can unbind; descent easy, ascent narrow; only the immortal essence, regenerated, passes.** "nature, indeed, binds the body to the soul, but the sol binds herself to the body" (II.3 ¶37); "nothing but the subtlety of an immortal essence, and that by regeneration, can pass into immortality" (II.4 ¶7). — *stated.*

10. **Fire descends, takes a body, reascends; it lies quiet until God's will stirs it; what multiplies is light.** "comes from above downwards, until having found a body, it reascends therewith in perpetual interchange" (II.3 ¶41); "Thou hast an example of this in the flint" (II.4 ¶39); "for what else but light should multiply?" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; the adept, Sendivogius; II.1 UNCLEAR.*

11. **Knowing is by contact and identity; like to like; only the experience proves.** "intuition is the evidence and end of every rational proof" (II.1 ¶33); "their regard was not extraneous but hypostatical, as of like to like" (II.4 ¶40); natural reason "cannot pass into the superstantial proof" (II.4 ¶49). — *stated.*

12. **The final method is passive: the divine moves itself; the soul is raised by love, hope and faith.** "things pertaining to the gods are moved by themselves, and do not receive from an inferior nature (i.e., to say, from the regardant subject) the principle of their energy" (II.4 ¶31); "the passive method of the Divine Intuition, and the three elevating causes of love, hope, and faith" (II.4 ¶40). — *stated; Iamblichus, Proclus endorsed.*

13. **We are drawn out of the life-fountain, not cut off; the principle within is near and knowable.** "we are not cut off from this fountain, but attracted out from it" (II.4 ¶19); "why should not this, thus proximate, be known?" (II.1 ¶26). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

14. **The ancients began where modern experiment ends, and reported what they saw.** "end in fact where the ancients began, purifying the Vital Spirit in its proper Light?" (II.2 ¶24); "Neither did they formerly invent, but what they knew and had seen, declared." (II.3 ¶55). — *stated; ATWOOD.*

## Tensions and open questions

1. **Laboratory or inner process.** Sequence A speaks of "powder", "three months", "gas water, or oil of this antimony", yet is glossed "(in the vital spirit)" (II.1 ¶23) and called "the process of working with the Vital Spirit" (II.1 ¶25). II.4 reports "reproducing the whole material principle to sense and visibility from the dissolution of the spirit in its proper kind without alloy" (II.4 ¶16) and calls elixir and gold "vital products" (II.4 ¶58). Basil's "true mineral, as it is taken from the mountains" (II.1 ¶25) may be ore or something in man. Reading A: one inner process with material by-products. Reading B: two parallel processes. Unsettled.

2. **One agent or many.** II.1's inner fire, vital force, Mercurial Spirit, light and vapor, fiery water, Invisible Mercury, Vaughan's Spirit, the Operator in us, Wisdom and divine illumination; II.2's self-acting subject; II.3's Light of Reason as Sulfur and the Prester; II.4's self-acting Synthemata. Their identity is never asserted.

3. **Active intellect versus passive method.** II.2 ¶15 and II.3 ¶5, ¶11 demand active rational energy; II.4 ¶31, ¶40 demand its cessation. Reading A: stages (active purification, then passive reception), supported by II.4 ¶31's "(all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)". Reading B: divergent authorities. Not stated.

4. **Where the vessel is.** Within man (II.1 ¶6, ¶8, implied), the purified soul or its vehicle (II.4 ¶18–19, ¶29), an unnamed container (II.4 ¶9), or "their water" (II.1 ¶8).

5. **The king / true rector.** The soul in the blood (II.1 ¶10), interior attraction or First Cause (¶21), the king in red (¶24), "the monarchy of reason" (II.2 ¶20), Reason as monarch (II.4 ¶38): never joined.

6. **Salt, mercury, sulfur against attraction, repulsion, circulation.** Juxtaposed without copula (II.1 ¶14); who speaks in ¶18–21 decides whether the physics is Atwood's.

7. **The means are never described.** "recreation and appeasing" (II.1 ¶26), "mystical exercises and preparations" (¶37), "material Rites" (¶51), "ineffable and sublime media" (II.2 ¶5), "auxiliaries" (II.2 ¶20), "the true ferment" (II.2 ¶26), "theurgic assistance" (II.3 ¶46), "Pontic Medium" (II.4 ¶49), "passive Ether" (II.4 ¶36). Deferred to "the Practice" (II.2 ¶24) and "the manifestation of the Philosophic Subject" (II.3 ¶56). Literal ceremonies with materials (II.1 ¶51) or the vital experiment itself: open.

8. **Self-work or operator on patient.** "only by the worthy man’s self-labor and investigation" (II.3 ¶32) against the mesmeric patient and agent of II.3 n2 and the human conductors (Hierophant ¶36, priest ¶45). Guide unidentified.

9. **Where the Lesser Mysteries end; rank of Elysium; Tartarus.** II.3 ¶14 against ¶36; II.4 ¶3 (Elysium as goal) against ¶15, ¶41 (a grade below); Tartarus "immortal abode" (II.3 ¶36) and Being of the dark Salt (¶41); ¶36 garbled.

10. **The Central Source** (II.3 ¶5): the dark Salt of Saturn whose "Being is Tartarus" (¶41), or the Generator/Causal/Identic Source (¶37, ¶43). II.3 ¶41–56 reads as one principle from dark Salt to ignited Sun (interpreted); whether the Demogorgon of ¶30, ¶41, ¶52 is one throughout is likewise interpreted.

11. **Pass through or bypass the intermediate spheres.** "quite necessary to pass" (II.3 ¶5) against "without exploring the intermediate spheres" (¶27); probably one instruction (¶22 "unmoved"), not stated.

12. **The magnet.** "central magnet" (II.4 ¶19), "Rational Magnet" (¶38), "our Loadstone" (¶39), "the point of our lodestone" (II.1 ¶10), "a perfect magnet" (II.1 ¶20), "armed magnet" (II.1 ¶25; II.3 ¶54). The ruling Reason, the First Cause, or a point of attraction in nature; recurrent but not unified.

13. **Prester and Sun.** II.3 ¶41 item by item makes the Prester the external fire; ¶54 makes it the centre of every living thing; ¶56 has it "burning as below". Perhaps a collective gloss only.

14. **Sequence A against the Mysteries.** Black = death-gate rests on "(in the vital spirit)" (II.1 ¶23), "the process of working with the Vital Spirit" (II.1 ¶25) and the reuse of "head of Hermes’ crow" (II.1 ¶23; II.3 ¶37); white = Elysium and red = ignition/Paternal union have no stated counterpart.

15. **Golden bough / Golden Branch.** Intellect's ray (II.3 ¶11) against II.4's Golden Branch "attracted from the first to its native soil, indifferent to every other lure, through death and darkness enters" (II.4 ¶7), either the immortal essence or the regenerating power. No stated link.
