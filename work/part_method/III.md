## Subject

1. **A real, living, invisible substance: the operator's own vital spirit.** "the Phantastic Spirit, which is the natural vehicle of our life" (III.1 ¶5); "the Thing itself, which is our Identity" (III.2 ¶7). *Stated* (III.2); Phantastic Spirit as subject *interpreted*; ATWOOD.
2. **One thing; faculties are its modes.** "as folly, and phantasy, and passion are modes of being of the One Thing, and Reason is another" (III.1 ¶21). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
3. **Double and receptive.** "the summit of the irrational mind relying on instinct, and the basis whereon o build the rational" (III.1 ¶6); "nothing in the Idea yet in Being all things" (III.2 ¶60). Double nature *stated*; receptive role *interpreted* (III.1); Passive Nature as pure and receptive *stated* (III.2); ATWOOD.

## Procedure and stages

No chapter gives one linear sequence. The arc below recurs in all four and is well supported: preparation; inward turn; ferment and loss; separation and dissolution in darkness; reunion under a divine act; repeated conjunctions and colours; fixation and multiplication. Placement of single operations is *interpreted*; the text never says the chapters' sequences are one process (Tensions 9).

**0. Preparation (III.4).** Study with prayer: "till he come to an entire understanding of the whole" (III.4 ¶20, the prince); "Pray, says Sendivogius, pray; but work." (III.4 ¶20). Then assistance, instruments, ministers, furnace, place; the Preparation omitting no faculty (III.4 ¶28); the Gross Work; then "the practice (that is of the ultimate Philosophic Work)" (III.4 ¶44). Three classes of prayer (III.4 ¶24), *interpreted* as stages. ATWOOD.

**1. Set the mind in motion; make the Spirit conceive; take the bough.** "the first step of philosophy is to set the mind a-going" (III.1 ¶8); "she must be made to conceive them" (III.1 ¶9); worked "whilst yet immanifest" and "joining self to self" (III.1 ¶8); Mesmerism only a first key (III.1 ¶8). "Ramus, non arbor" (III.1 ¶32); "Fire loves fire, say they, not to make gold, but to assimilate it" (III.1 ¶19). *Stated*; placement *interpreted*.

**2. Inward turn; pass through reason; reduce to simple being.** "retire into the depths of my essence" (III.2 ¶50, Plotinus); "all my bodily senses being entirely holden back" (III.3 ¶52); "Conceive well the Light in thy Mind, and Know It" (III.3 ¶54); "not slighting reason indeed, but passing through it" (III.2 ¶54); "deprivation of all passion, will, imagination, purpose, or reflective thought" (III.2 ¶64); "leaves behind him all the statues in the temple" (III.2 ¶66). *Stated*.

**3. The ferment enters; the first medial life is cut off; loss as motive.** "that perfect beginning of Wisdom, which is half of the perfect whole" (III.1 ¶23); the graft "re-enters to redeem its congenital life" (III.1 ¶24); the celestial medial life "is by necessity cut off, even in its prime" (III.1 ¶26); "Nothing is done radically to meliorate the Vital Spirit previous to this dissolution of the first medial life" (III.1 ¶28); "depriving it when in full vigor of its Understanding Light" (III.1 ¶28); "remains satisfied in the first fruits of his reason, loses his labour" (III.3 ¶22). *Stated*; ATWOOD.

**4. Separation, dissolution, putrefaction: the dark stage.** "This Separation is indeed the primary object of the Art" (III.1 ¶21); "the subtle from the gross, gently, with much sagacity" (III.1 ¶21, Hermes); "from the beginning in suffering, succedent dread, dissolution, and corruption, to the final resurrection of the pure Ether into Light" (III.1 ¶21). Steps gathered from all chapters (*interpreted* as one stage):
- Agent and patient both prepared, like natures: "the spiritual agent and the spiritual patient" (III.1 ¶41); "mortify two Argent vives together" (III.1 ¶43).
- The light meets the self-willed Identity and is eclipsed: "an eclipsation of the light takes place, and a dissolution of its body" (III.1 ¶53).
- The one life divides into three: "the ethereal hypostasis is divided against itself" (III.1 ¶53); III.2 ¶28.
- The body of Light becomes a vapour that condenses and "runs down continually, day and night, without ceasing" (III.1 ¶56).
- The opposed fires "prey upon each other incombustibly" (III.1 ¶56) and "are turned into a Fifth Essence" (III.1 ¶58).
- Force the matter to its extremity (Proteus): "torture and vex it in order to its annihilation" (III.3 ¶61); "it restores itself, if the force be continued" (III.3 ¶61); glossed "in the last exigence of life" (III.3 ¶61).
- Bind the freed vitality: "The philosophers chain up their matter with a strong chain or band" (III.2 ¶22); "Take, my son, the flying bird and drown it flying" (III.1 ¶53).
- Bind the sated Will: "Sunk in ebriety; fast bind the god." (III.1 ¶67); Intellect "prepares to cut him off" (III.1 ¶68).
- Near-annihilation "draws a new seed to begin a New Generation" (III.2 ¶28); "the soul retracts, rallying about her last deserted Unit" (III.2 ¶55, UNCLEAR).
Rules: "Nullo corrosivo adhibito" (III.2 ¶63, ours); "keepe hym in an easy fyre;" (III.1 ¶65).

**5. Reunion under the Fiat; the Water appears; the marriage.** "the returning faith and desire of the Passive Spirit attracts the Soul again into herself" (III.3 ¶10); then "it is first made subject to the artist" (III.3 ¶10). "the spirit and soul come together In the extreme or last heat" (III.1 ¶52); "the extraction of the soul out of the body, and the reduction of it afterwards upon the same body" (III.3 ¶60, Synesius); "the Thought solitary unites itself to the non-being, or simple Understanding of its ether" (III.3 ¶15); "death and contrition of the agent in the patient, and vice versa" (III.1 ¶49). *Stated*; ATWOOD.

**6. Two conjunctions; repeated deaths; solution–sublimation–fixation; three periods.** "In the first conjunction the Spirit predominates; in the second the Soul, i.e., its Light" (III.3 ¶30), the offspring "more perfect than either parent" (III.3 ¶30). "solution, sublimation, and fixation" (III.2 ¶64), "reiterated many times" (III.2 ¶64). "So each succeeding life must die, as transplanted in the next" (III.1 ¶30); "even to the fourth generation, or fifth" (III.1 ¶30); grafting "to a third, fourth, and fifth degree" (III.1 ¶9). Conversion: "the occult becoming manifest and the manifest occult" (III.3 ¶29). The threefold Law "revolutionized also in three period" (III.3 ¶72, hedged "it would seem"): Profundity, "the water that is below"; Altitude, "the ether that is above"; Latitude; meeting "in a Calx" (III.3 ¶72). *Stated* severally; coincidence of counts *interpreted*.

**7. Colours: black, white, all colours, red.** "Blackness passed he may grow white;" (III.1 ¶65); "Then is your elixir perfect as to the white work" (III.2 ¶5); "after the ashy colour, and the white, and the yellow, thou shalt behold the Stone" (III.3 ¶19); "To proceede with fire till all be Redd;" (III.1 ¶65). Greene Lyon regimen ("Six tymes thy Imbibtions make", III.1 ¶65) and Eirenaeus' day counts (III.3 ¶68) *stated*, undecoded.

**8. Fixation, ferment, multiplication, projection.** "To make by one fixt, one unfixed fixt" (III.3 ¶36); "without possibility of dissolution any more" (III.3 ¶35); "Which Ferment must be of such a thing, As was the workes begyning;" (III.1 ¶65); "continuall Firmentation" (III.1 ¶65); the five gates ending in "multiplication and augmentation" and "projection" (III.3 ¶63); "Unite the end to the beginning as the flame is united to the coal" (III.1 ¶50). *Stated*.

**Governing rules.** Gentle, long, sealed coction: "Decoct it gently by little and little" (III.3 ¶60); "Shut thy vessel well that it may not breathe out" (III.3 ¶60); "stand not long in the sun nor long in the shade" (III.4 ¶44). All operations one: "to dissolve, to calcine, to tinge, to whiten, to renew, to bathe, to wash, to coagulate, to imbibe, to decoct, to fix, to grind, to dry and to distill are all one" (III.3 ¶60). "Seek Three in One, and again seek One in Three, dissolve, congeal" (III.3 ¶9), with "attraction and repulsion and circulation" in "a perfect equilibriate accord" (III.3 ¶9). "Apply nomanual labor" (III.3 ¶9) against "Manual Operation" (III.1 ¶37) (Tensions 4). Backward along the wheel: "back into the Initial germ" (III.3 ¶79); "experimentally proving, it effects that inversion" (III.2 ¶67).

## Signs

1. **Certainty felt from the source.** "it feels itself move in conscious virtue from its own source" (III.1 ¶10). *Interpreted*; ATWOOD.
2. **The sudden solution, as if infused.** "in a moment, the solution will fall in; this is a revelation" (III.4 ¶22, Kirchringius); "a sudden Light" (III.1 ¶23, Plotinus; "sol" read as soul, *interpreted*). *Stated* (Kirchringius).
3. **Demonic forms, Protean colours, turmoil at the opening.** "in demoniacal forms is at first in vision made apparent" (III.2 ¶7); "from green to red and from red to black" (III.2 ¶20); "rolling like a tempestuous sea" (III.1 ¶24); the passions "without metaphor, in their imaged atmosphere appear terrific" (III.3 ¶71). *Stated*; visions or physical colours open (III.2 OQ 4).
4. **The Doves of Diana.** "your signal the Doves of Diana" (III.1 ¶14). *Stated*; Eirenaeus; unexplained.
5. **Flood, eclipse, suspended life, the Gate of Putrefaction, pain at the void.** "the two great lights are eclipsed, the air is darkened" (III.1 ¶53); "Now I have life, now I have none;" (III.1 ¶54); "continual terrification of the Spirit" (III.1 ¶58); "pained with the void of the retreating infinitude" (III.2 ¶55). *Stated*.
6. **Blackness, welcomed.** "Night, Saturn and the Antimony of the Wise will be present, Obscurity and the Head of the Crow" (III.3 ¶13); "The sable blacke is not disdained Which shows the spirit’s fixed" (III.3 ¶68); the white "magnet in the midst" (III.1 ¶65). *Stated*.
7. **Inner motion in contrition; tears of joy; the Water felt "in substance".** "where a peculiar motion is present" (III.3 ¶24); "motum in te, experis internum et pro gaudio, lachrymabis!" (III.3 ¶9, ours); "with power and effect in substance to bear it witness" (III.3 ¶3); "With thy hands thou shalt touch" (III.3 ¶9). *Stated*; physical or inward open (Tensions 1).
8. **Light visible; a Light about the summit, in the North.** "Lux manifeste et visibilis ad oculum" (III.3 ¶10); "a Light wonderfully about the summit" (III.3 ¶15). *Stated* gloss; ATWOOD.
9. **Whiteness; white milk.** "As all colours in their prismatic unison are white, just so is the Universal Nature" (III.2 ¶5); "most white milk" (III.1 ¶56, Sendivogius). *Stated*.
10. **All colours as reconciliation; toad, dragon, swan, Phoenix; green at quickening.** "Iris, God’s messenger, and the tail of the peacock" (III.3 ¶13), "token of reconciliation" (III.3 ¶13); swan, then "a red bird of a most deep color" (III.1 ¶50); "quickened first appears in colours green" (III.1 ¶69). *Stated* order; meaning *interpreted*.
11. **The universe mirrored in the Subject Waters.** "the pattern of the whole universe appears in those Subject Waters, as an image in a glass" (III.3 ¶39); III.2 ¶25; the physical "a secondary vestiment or witness" (III.3 n27). *Stated*.
12. **Body asleep, mind awake; no subject and object; felt, not known.** "the sleep of the body was the sober watchfulness of the mind" (III.3 ¶58); "the life understanding and the light understood are one" (III.2 ¶66); "may be felt indeed, not known" (III.2 ¶60). *Stated*.
13. **Two luminaries then one; the skin; vessels that speak.** "two luminaries, the one fixed, the other not" (III.3 ¶60); "a skin, Such as an Egg yelke lyeth in:" (III.1 ¶65); "like the planks of Argo, on occasion, are still oracular" (III.4 ¶32). *Stated* / *interpreted*.
14. **Once found, met everywhere.** "it will meet thee everywhere thereafter" (III.3 ¶78). *Stated*; Poemander.

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Starting errors.** "mistaken dreams for revelations, instinct for intellectual vision, and insensibility for the highest good" (III.1 ¶8); "throw our common lives to common lives" (III.1 ¶9); mesmerists lack "the important knowledge how to alter and amend the Thing" (III.1 ¶8); "some entranced sleepwalker" rejected (III.2 ¶62). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
2. **The worldly mind; the senses' dominance; love of body.** "the worldly mind is shut out from the imagination even, of occult truth" (III.2 ¶4); "the divine original is obscured" (III.3 ¶52); "the cause of death is the love of body" (III.3 ¶56). *Stated*.
3. **Avarice, selfish ends, misuse of a raised power.** "this first and last defect of Avarice" (III.4 ¶4); "having succeeded in inducing an exalted energy, have willfully denied the Light its true fulfillment" (III.4 ¶6); "falling into infinite snares" (III.1 ¶38); "this art will vanish from under thy hands" (III.4 ¶7). *Stated*; ATWOOD; endorsed. Wickedness bars approach to the material: the Arabian Magician must "steal a little boy" to supply it (III.4 ¶6, *interpreted*).
4. **Impure motive, pride, infidelity, instability.** "if the motive be not universal" (III.1 ¶32); "They weene theire wits sufficient this arte to finde;" (III.4 ¶14); "faithlessness, which, without rational foundation, denounces everything that is new" (III.4 ¶12); "a busy head nor a faithless heart, by impure hands" (III.4 ¶15). *Stated*.
5. **Imagination and striving.** "the phantasy, if suffered to prevail, will convert all to her own folly" (III.1 ¶21); "every imagination concerning it will be false" (III.2 ¶60); desire "diametrically opposed to the presence of the thing desired" (III.2 ¶57). *Stated*; Tensions 7.
6. **Abstraction without experiment; the letter without the spirit.** "stopped short nevertheless of objective realization" (III.2 ¶31); "the Organon began to work upon itself" (III.2 ¶42); "Stoop not down, for a precipice lies below in the earth" (III.2 ¶15); "the veil is upon their heart" (III.3 ¶59); "the inner sense is held aloof from the literal" (III.4 ¶16). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
7. **Not finding the Separator, key, door; failing to make the matter appear.** "To find the true Separator is described, in fact, as the greatest difficulty" (III.1 ¶32); "if you miss the door, all your course will be in error" (III.1 ¶37); "if he cannot cause it to appear" (III.3 ¶11). *Stated*.
8. **Working without exact knowledge; unprepared practice.** "thou wilt with my fire destroy thy five senses" (III.3 ¶64); "our Lyon ys Common Quicksilver, but truly they miss:" (III.1 ¶65); "lest folly or willfulness, precipitously passing into practice, should either perish or break the divine legislation" (III.4 ¶16). *Stated*.
9. **Too strong a fire, haste, violence, acting against nature.** "by force of too strong a fire" (III.1 ¶55); "burn not its flowers and its vivacity" (III.3 ¶60); "if thy heat exceeds the natural proportion" (III.4 ¶44); "against the right of nature, then it readily flies away" (III.2 ¶16); "Twixt overmoyst and too much rost [roft];" (III.1 ¶65); the light must keep "a fermental odor of the body" (III.1 ¶53). *Stated*.
10. **The dark stage itself.** "no good can come until it is overtaken and resolved" (III.1 ¶61); "the tempter Evil of the Son of Man made manifest" (III.1 ¶60); "violent, forcible restraint, contrariety, and subversion" (III.1 ¶63); "her household enemies within the stronghold of life" (III.3 ¶71); "the revenging Demon" (III.3 ¶57). *Stated*.
11. **Nature cannot free herself; chemistry cannot catch her.** "Nature is bound magically, nor is she able of herself to loosen the bond of coagulation" (III.2 ¶61); "she eludes their tests and closest vessels" (III.3 ¶65). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
12. **Falling back; the Light blinds the irrational.** "falling off from Intellect into the discursive energy of reason" (III.2 ¶50); "strikes the irrational intruder blind" (III.1 ¶70). *Stated* / *interpreted*.
13. **Bad conditions of the Gross Work.** "Wind will hurt in everie case:" (III.4 ¶41); impressions that "pollute the ether" (III.4 ¶42); ribaldry that "shall destroy part of their weks or all;" (III.4 ¶37); "All his worke falleth upon chance." (III.4 ¶39); without prayer God "stops the effect thereof" (III.4 ¶20). *Stated*; QUOTED.

## Result

1. **Illumination from within; self-knowledge; union.** "that Intellectual Ilumination which proceeds, when the conditions are duly offered, from the Divinity within" (III.1 ¶5); "that man may know himself; whence, what for, and whereto, he is allied" (III.1 ¶23); "a conjunction with the Demiurgic Intellect" (III.4 ¶25); "to move One with the First Mover, and Be His Will" (III.3 ¶78, posed as a question). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
2. **The hypostasis converted to light: Quintessence, Elixir, living gold.** "converted finally through faith into the identity of substant light within" (III.1 ¶11); "the living gold of the philosophers, transmuting and multiplicative" (III.1 ¶49). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
3. **The vital order reversed; Reason discovered with creative powers.** "the relationships of the vital elements are exchanged" (III.1 ¶50); "with creative attributes and powers" (III.3 ¶52). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
4. **A permanent spiritual body; Fifth Essence; indissoluble Tetractys.** "changed from a corporeal to a spiritual extreme" (III.1 ¶41); "a Fifth Essence, which neuter from all, yet partaking of all in perpetuity of union" (III.3 ¶32); "man cannot any more put asunder" (III.3 ¶35). *Stated*.
5. **Corner Stone; Divine Essentiality that qualifies the Adept.** "the Corner Stone or first Material foundation of the Hermetic Art" (III.1 ¶66); "this Essentiality it is which qualifies the true Adept; which sanctifies even as it qualifies" (III.3 ¶24). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
6. **The Freed Will; the Identical Spirit under its own Law.** "free in legal subordination only to its own perfect Law" (III.1 ¶68); "the operative Almighty Magnet freed from Tartarean bondage and obscurity" (III.3 ¶71). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
7. **Opposites reconciled; the First Matter, Diana, produced.** "circulating with her in equilibriate accord" (III.1 ¶63); "an emancipation of the Fontal Source; and this is Diana" (III.1 ¶70). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
8. **The Water, the Tincture, the seed that reproduces all.** "visible, tangible, and sensible to every sense, local and finite" (III.3 ¶73); "seminally equal to the whole of the parts whence it is derived" (III.3 ¶71); "the stage of all Forms" (III.2 ¶26). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
9. **Medicine and transmutation.** "a pure panacea from the god of Light" (III.1 ¶10); "an universal medicine for all diseases" (III.3 ¶63); "The fifth gate serves only for metals" (III.3 ¶63); "make gold of iron or copper" (III.2 ¶16, Boehme); yet "Reason alone can enter into it" (III.4 ¶11). *Stated* (QUOTED); non-material gold *interpreted*.
10. **The Archetypal Law seen; the whole work known; worlds agreeing.** "as in a glass, the great Archetypal Law of Light" (III.3 ¶39); "the beginning, the middle, and the end of the whole work" (III.3 ¶28, Roger Bacon); "the External and Internal Worlds were seen to blend together in confluent harmony" (III.3 ¶82). *Stated*.
11. **Reason an immortal Light, Mistress of Life.** "until reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light in the periphery of her fiery essence remains immortal, and is the Mistress of Life" (III.3 ¶80); "Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus" (III.3 ¶81, UNCLEAR). ATWOOD.
12. **Christian regeneration as end; the fables seen in act.** "with Christian regeneration for its most worthy end" (III.3 ¶61); "no longer fabulous but by art made natural" (III.1 ¶70, voice UNCLEAR). *Stated*.
13. **Success in degrees.** "every step is progressive where the Rule of Reason is pursued" (III.4 ¶11). *Stated*; ATWOOD.

## Principles

1. **Microcosm and macrocosm one; mind and matter consubstantial; each part holds the whole.** "for what the natural intelligent Spirit is in man, that Ether is in the universe" (III.1 ¶5); "consubstantial in their Cause" (III.2 ¶35); "each particular spark of fire contains the principle" (III.2 ¶45); III.1 ¶23 (*interpreted*). ATWOOD.
2. **Experience on its own ground; theory before practice; experiment effects what logic circles.** "without entering in upon the proper ground of their experience?" (III.1 ¶8); "the proper test of philosophy" (III.3 ¶61); "effects rationally investigated lead into their causes" (III.4 ¶45). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
3. **Imitate Nature, but help her; the sun is twice begotten.** "Naturam in primisimitabere in arte, magister." (III.1 ¶1, ours); "you must help her, that she may exceed her common course" (III.1 ¶13); "There is no sun but which is twice begot" (III.3 ¶36). *Stated*; endorsed.
4. **Like assimilates like; like known by like.** "Fire loves fire" (III.1 ¶19); "for the like is intelligible by the like" (III.3 ¶78); III.2 ¶53. *Stated*.
5. **No discovery without decomposition; death is the way of life; corruption before increase.** "nothing is discovered except the matte be decomposed" (III.1 ¶21); "Death is the way of Life" (III.2 ¶55); "corrupted before it is brought forth" (III.3 ¶23). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
6. **The governing rule: light out of darkness by solution then sublimation.** "by a preponderant affinity artificially endowed" (III.1 ¶53); III.2 ¶56. *Stated*; ATWOOD.
7. **Whichever mode prevails converts the inner life; no neutrality.** "this Reason gaining the ascendancy, would gather all up, as a ferment" (III.1 ¶21); "it must either be filled with a superior or inferior power" (III.4 ¶6). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
8. **The impure agent is necessary; the ferment is of the beginning; the end is in the beginning.** "it is nevertheless a necessary ingredient" (III.1 ¶61); "Which Ferment must be of such a thing, As was the workes begyning;" (III.1 ¶65); "the end is implied in the beginning" (III.4 ¶45). *Stated*.
9. **Final causes manifest; intention returns in kind; Law of Justice; hence secrecy.** "Final Causes are of all things most manifestly revealed" (III.4 ¶9); "the intention is received back according to its kind most exactly" (III.4 ¶9). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
10. **Reason needs divine illumination; grace alone finds; man conditions, God gives.** "Light is not given to reason except God impart it" (III.1 ¶23); "But only by grace, she is holy in her kind." (III.4 ¶10, Norton); "the Lesser is blessed by the Greater" (III.4 ¶20). *Stated*; endorsed.
11. **Knowledge and efficient arise together within: one in principle, two in practice.** "It is from within that the knowledge springs together with the true efficient" (III.1 ¶40). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
12. **Wisdom with Power; contemplation alternates with action.** "the Will may descend in safety to the abodes of Power" (III.1 ¶45); "the pair which are beneath should be closed" (III.1 ¶45, Synesius). *Stated*.
13. **First love and unity condition lasting union.** "Without this love the elements will never be married" (III.1 ¶50, voice UNCLEAR); III.4 ¶20 (Hermes). *Stated*.
14. **Three in one: agent, patient, offspring.** "born in it, one out of another: conjoin, die, and are mortified, one within the other" (III.3 ¶67). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
15. **The parent is bettered in the offspring; nothing gives what it lacks.** "the parent is bettered in the offspring, even to the fourth generation, or fifth" (III.1 ¶30); "there cannot come of any thing that virtue which it has not" (III.1 ¶43). *Stated*; ATWOOD.
16. **The receptive nature must be pure; Nature has none pure.** "no falsehood or commixture of images" (III.2 ¶60); "all is mixture and an adulterous manifestation" (III.2 ¶61); III.2 ¶64. *Stated*; ATWOOD.
17. **Self-knowledge requires losing all other knowledge; knowing God is self-annihilation; presence with oneself is presence with Being.** "Self-knowledge is impossible unless every other knowledge is deprived" (III.2 ¶56); "self-annihilation" (III.3 ¶78); "present with true Being, which is everywhere" (III.2 ¶59, Porphyry). *Stated*.
18. **The doctrine is veiled; read the spirit; names explain nothing.** "fables, symbols, similitudes, enigmas" (III.2 ¶40); "unhappy he, who mistakes the vestment for the Law itself" (III.3 ¶59); "ignotum per ingotius" (III.3 ¶51); "the same Royal Road" (III.4 ¶15). *Stated*.
19. **Every desire is a prayer; no sacred operation without prayer.** "every desire of the mind is in its kind a prayer" (III.4 ¶22); "No operation, however, in sacred concerns, can succeed without the intervention of prayer." (III.4 ¶24). *Stated*.
20. **Moral, physical and organic laws are one; instrument and place matter; superiors dissolve, inferiors coagulate.** "the whole structure sympathizing decays" (III.4 ¶8); "full concord of arte with hys instrument" (III.4 ¶39); "so superiors dissolve and inferiors coagulate" (III.4 ¶44). *Stated*.
21. **The fables record real experience; the dying and reviving god is the symbol.** "the physical secret of the regeneration" (III.3 ¶69); "so many records of the difficulties and dangers that the soul must endure" (III.3 ¶71). *Stated*; ATWOOD.

## Tensions and open questions

1. **Inward subject or substance in a vessel?** The Part's main open question. Inward: "our Identity" (III.2 ¶7); "in their gross body" (III.3 ¶24); the Light "in the contrition or distress of the sensible nature in the conscience" (III.3 ¶24); III.2 ¶64; "Apply nomanual labor" (III.3 ¶9). Outward: "the whole Hermetic laboratory --- furnace, fire, matter, and vessels" (III.2 ¶26); "manifest to sense even" (III.2 ¶46); "With thy hands thou shalt touch" (III.3 ¶9); "sensible to every sense, local and finite" (III.3 ¶73); Norton's glass, potter and ministers (III.4 ¶31–41). Atwood: "both in an internal and in an external sense" (III.2 n38); the physical "a secondary vestiment or witness" (III.3 n27). How they relate is never said.
2. **Within one person or between persons?** One: "joining self to self" (III.1 ¶8); "It is from within that the knowledge springs" (III.1 ¶40); III.4 ¶28. Two: "mesmerizing one another" (III.1 ¶8); III.1 ¶40–41; "a Subject to work with" (III.4 ¶29); "two are better than one" (III.4 ¶30); "the entranced Subject" and "the spiritual guide" (III.4 ¶42); "human aid to fortify" (III.2 ¶61). Trance-revelation by another is rejected (III.2 ¶62). Undecided (III.1 OQ 1; III.4 OQ 2).
3. **Mesmerism's place.** "a first key" (III.1 ¶8) versus "the neglected Door-keeper" (III.4 ¶13). Method or Atwood's way in: open.
4. **Manual or not.** "Apply nomanual labor" (III.3 ¶9; III.3 ¶24) against "Manual Operation" (III.1 ¶37), "with the Hand, as Adepts say" (III.1 ¶64), "manual skill" (III.4 ¶20), "Praxis manaulis caetera pandet" (III.4 ¶48). "Hand" not glossed (III.1 OQ 33).
5. **What is the true Separator / key / Fire?** Mind (III.1 ¶21); the Separator hardest to find (III.1 ¶32); Boehme's "artist of great subtlety in them" (III.1 ¶39); the "vertical separated Light", Pontanus' Fire, Basil's Antimony (III.1 ¶37); "Master Key" (III.4 ¶47); "the Door" (III.4 ¶44); "the most ancient cook of the planets" (III.2 ¶61). Never stated to be one (III.1 OQ 14, 18; III.2 OQ 11).
6. **Spontaneous or willed?** "spontaneously" (III.2 ¶8), "by itself regeneratively" (III.3 ¶50), "works not of itself" (III.2 ¶16) against "Springing directly from ourselves" (III.4 ¶9) and "the whole voluntary force of the central magnet" (III.2 ¶24, owner unfixed). Striving defeats itself (III.2 ¶57) while the will's struggle is "meritorious" (III.3 ¶71). Not reconciled.
7. **Imagination: agent or hindrance?** "the elevating power which the imagination possesses" (III.1 ¶6; III.1 ¶71) against "every imagination concerning it will be false" (III.2 ¶60; III.2 ¶64). Possibly different stages; not said.
8. **Roles of the named principles.** Isis acts (III.1 ¶23) and receives (III.3 ¶62). Mercury is dragon-subject (III.3 ¶64), acting Spirit (III.3 ¶30), prepared selfhood (III.3 ¶77), impure vapour (III.1 ¶24), fixed product (III.3 ¶32), perhaps rational soul (III.3 ¶81). Which of each pair (Argent vives, Sol/Luna, agent/patient, king/queen, lion/eagle) is which is unmapped (III.1 OQ 21; III.3 OQ 12). The triad is confirmed only for sulfur = soul (III.1 ¶53); III.3 ¶77 and Khunrath (III.3 ¶50) differ. Whether Salt is the Stone, a part, or the impure Instrument (III.1 ¶61) is open (III.1 OQ 25; III.3 OQ 10).
9. **One process or several?** The chapters' sequences (III.1: fermentation, separation, mutual death, Greene Lyon regimen, binding Saturn; III.2: separation–introspection–reunion, fermentation–division–new seed, Night of Matter, solution–sublimation–fixation; III.3: extremity–Fiat, conjunction under the cross, two conjunctions, five gates, three periods; III.4: Gross then Philosophic Work, three concentrations, three prayers) run parallel. Never said to be one; counts never aligned.
10. **Who dies?** "Achilles, too, must die and suffer" (III.1 ¶30); "the agent in the patient, and vice versa" (III.1 ¶49); the "first medial life" (III.1 ¶26); "flying bird" and "Arcadian slippery lad" undecoded (III.1 OQ 15–16); Poemander's dissolution of the body (III.3 ¶57) as death or regeneration (III.3 OQ 15).
11. **Literal gold and medicine?** "The fifth gate serves only for metals" (III.3 ¶63) and Boehme's Tincture (III.2 ¶16) beside "Reason alone can enter into it" (III.4 ¶11) and "All things may be reduced to gold according to this doctrine" (III.2 n38). Unresolved.
12. **Timing.** Day counts (III.3 ¶68) and "six weeks" (III.1 ¶65) beside "until it be the Divine pleasure" (III.3 ¶80) and "every day is a contracted year" (III.4 ¶44). Literal or not: unsaid (III.3 OQ 23; III.4 OQ 17).
13. **The vessel.** Gross body (III.3 ¶24); glass the Spirit "vitrifies" (III.4 ¶32); Norton's ware (III.4 ¶31–33); "a triune furnace spherically round" (III.3 ¶11); "Mesnura Ignis tui" (III.1 ¶40, read as Mensura, ours); Nature's own vessels (III.3 ¶65). Not reconciled (III.3 OQ 4; III.4 OQ 13).
14. **Voice boundaries deciding ownership.** III.1 ¶50's close (Atwood or Vaughan); III.2 ¶55, ¶66; Helvetius' catalogue (III.1 ¶70); Kirchringius' riddle passage (III.4 ¶22); "(that is of the ultimate Philosophic Work)" (III.4 ¶44); III.3 ¶81. No quotation marks.

Pierce's "Leave worke, take your beades and pray" (III.4 ¶21) against "Pray, says Sendivogius, pray; but work." (III.4 ¶20) (III.4 OQ 18).
