# Method synthesis — Part I: An Exoteric View of the Progress and Theory of Alchemy

Three layers. I.1 is history: its method material is mostly testimony from authorities Atwood quotes; her own statements concern motives, veiling and theory-before-practice, and she defers the subject and the "conditions of success" (I.1 ¶24, ¶39, ¶86). I.2 is her theory of the first matter, in her own voice. I.3 is the Golden Treatise: main text (¶) is Hermes, notes (n) are Atwood, who often adopts "our Scholiast" and other adepts. Labels keep the chapters' status; merging never upgrades an interpretation. "interp." = interpreted.

## Subject

1. **One thing only, the root of all natures, in every body.** "the whole matter I know to be one only thing" (I.3 ¶38); "every body hath passive principles in itself for this Light to work upon, and therefore needs not to borrow any from gold or silver" (I.2 ¶8). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Hermes, the adept.

2. **No sensible substance; the common names deceive.** "it is no one of the many things with which sense brings us acquainted" (I.2 ¶34); "Not with our gross elements, our mercuries, sulfurs, and our lifeless salts; but in a far different nature, with stranger arts" (I.1 ¶33); Ripley's trials: "profytt found I right none therein." (I.1 ¶65). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Ripley.

3. **Atwood's definition**, which she says does not teach how to work it: "the simple generated substance of life and light, immanifestly flowing throughout nature" (I.2 ¶34). — stated.

4. **A living spiritual water, "a stone and no stone", spirit, soul and body, holding its own active principle.** "this very permanent water is the stone, but whilst it is water it is no stone" (I.2 ¶29); "Our water is heavenly, not wetting the hands" (I.2 ¶33); "this water or mercury of the adepts has, within itself, its own good sulfur, or vital flame, thou mayest perfect all things out of mercury" (I.3 n5); "It dwells in his own body, as fire in trees and stones" (I.3 ¶54). — stated; QUOTED: Belus, Sendivogius, Hermes; ATWOOD.

5. **Universal and paradoxical.** "Not in heaven, earth, or water broad It lies, but everywhere!" (I.1 ¶23); "said by the alchemists to relate to the properties of their universal subject, as we shall hereafter endeavor to explain" (I.1 ¶24). — stated; endorsed.

6. **A middle or third substance, one in nature, from active and passive; its "elements" are properties.** "a middle substance" "in respect to its generation by active and passive relations of the Universal Spirit, whence it proceeds as a third, yet homogeneal from its radix" (I.2 ¶31); "the elements of the Mercury, properties of the universal spirit" (I.2 ¶26). — stated; ATWOOD.

7. **Never free on earth; everywhere mixed, defiled, despised.** "the ethereal spirit does not subsist of itself, separate or tangibly on earth; but, giving subsistence to other beings, is occultated even in their life, and defiled" (I.2 ¶20); "Our subject is presented to the eyes of the whole world, and it is not known!" (I.2 ¶33); "Our most precious stone cast forth upon the dunghill, being most dear is made altogether vile" (I.3 ¶32). Against: "it is found flowing upon earth" (I.2 ¶29, Lully; Tensions 4). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Synesius / the New Light, Hermes.

8. **The magi had it compact and tangible**, "not as a being of speculation merely, but of experimental science" (I.2 ¶16). — stated; ATWOOD.

9. **Where it is sought.** "In the caverns of the metals there is hidden the stone that is venerable, splendid in colour, a mind sublime, and an open sea" (I.3 ¶10), i.e. "the central motion of the mineral life, where the spirit is first coagulated" (I.3 n13); in the enquirer, "within themselves the substance of that Universal Nature in which they, with all beings in common, as it were, unconsciously live" (I.3 n8); Maria: "this science may be found in all bodies" (I.3 n48), easiest where it "most evidently contains the four philosophic elements" (I.3 n48). "It grows only in the caverns of the mountains." (I.2 ¶30) is unexplained. — stated; QUOTED: Hermes, Maria; ATWOOD. That Maria's answer also answers the concealed vessel (n48) is interp.

10. **Metals used because the metallic root shows first and risks least.** "the metalline radix first presents itself in the experimental process, and is most easily apportioned" (I.2 ¶49). — stated; ATWOOD.

11. **Always concealed; having it is not knowing its preparation.** "the Subject Matter and certain preliminaries, which are constantly concealed" (I.1 ¶64); Hermes never says "whence, when or how the Matter is to be taken" (I.3 n71); "I had now the prima material, the first principles, yet not their preparation, which is a thing most difficult above all things in the world" (I.1 ¶57). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Flamel.

12. **The divine water (I.1)**, "whence potable gold, and the stone of philosophers, takes its beginning" (I.1 ¶20): subject, agent or both unsaid. — stated; QUOTED: Hermolaus Barbarus.

13. **The hen's egg**, answering "in all respects, excepting the catholicity, to the compound simple of this art" (I.3 n26). — stated; ATWOOD.

## Agent

1. **The freed subject itself transmutes, by its own Light.** "the thing transmuting also, when set free and segregated in its proper essentiality" (I.2 ¶8); "It is Mercury alone which perfects in our work" (I.2 ¶11); "multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light" (I.2 ¶39). Inherent or supplied is unsettled (Tensions 2). — stated; reading interp.; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Aquinas.

2. **Light (sulfur) is the agent: ferment, Form, the one thing multiplied.** "It is the Light only that can be truly multiplied" (I.2 ¶8); "No, there must be an inward agent granted, Else would a thing unchanged still remain;" (I.2 ¶45); "this sulphur doth tinge and fix, and is the connection of the tinctures" (I.3 ¶21). — stated; QUOTED: the adept, Eiranaeus, Hermes.

3. **The philosophers' fire is not common fire; the matter has its own.** "their own special fire as magical, creative, vital; whereas the common element is without sagacity or discrimination" (I.3 n41); "Lapis noster, hic est ignis, ex igne creatus, et in ignem veritur, et anima ejus in igna moratur" (I.2 ¶44; ours: our stone is fire, from fire, turned into fire, its soul dwells in fire). Its "artificial excitation" is deferred (I.3 n14). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the adept; exclusion of kitchen fire interp. (I.2 ¶15, ¶24).

4. **The Ether, "always at hand, ready to break forth into action on predisposed subjects"** (I.2 ¶14). — stated; reported moderns.

5. **The universal spirit, ascending and descending, the only means of growth.** Bacon worked "ascending and descending experimentally" (I.1 ¶38) and found "the secret ligature of natures, and their magical dissolution" (I.1 ¶38); metals are exalted "out of the Universal Spirit or Mercury, by which also they may be exalted and multiplies, and by no other thing" (I.2 ¶49). — I.1 interp., I.2 stated; ATWOOD.

6. **One Spirit of life in many states: vulture, crow, "our Mercury".** "Whilst the Spirit of life appears active and devouring in the process, it has been called the vulture, and when it lies in a more obscured and passive condition, the crow" (I.3 n12); "this art beginning in our Mercury, likewise finishes in the same" (I.3 n55). — stated; ATWOOD.

7. **Two agents, outer and inner, kept to the end; the agent outranks the body.** "Include, therefore, and conserve in this sea, the fire, and the heavenly Flyer, to the latest moment of his exit" (I.3 ¶11); the agent "is repesentatively superior in operation, to compel the Protean Hypostasis of Nature to enter into his true Form" (I.3 n37). — stated; QUOTED: Hermes, Scholiast (n16); ATWOOD (n37); assignment unsaid.

8. **The Perscrutinator, the Rational Ferment**, deferred: "how to prepare it and use it in this work includes the whole art of perfection" (I.3 n32). — stated; Scholiast.

9. **The dissolving water, really fire.** "the viscous humidity made by the dissolution which radically dissolves all metals, and reduces them into their first ens, or water" (I.3 n12); "being conjoined in consciousness with the central Efficient, it becomes all-powerful, and the key of every magic art" (I.3 n36); the moisture "which doth dissolve gold without violence or noise" (I.2 ¶48). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Sendivogius.

10. **Personified lights.** Venus, "the central light of nature, which is occultated in her generations" (I.3 n49): "I liquefy them and wipe away their rust, and I extract their substance" (I.3 ¶39); Luna, the passive ethereal light (I.3 n7); the King, "the Rational Efficient" (I.3 n50). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Hermes.

11. **The ruling Spirit at nature's centre carries the operator.** "the way to the attainment of the magistery is by communion with the ruling Spirit of nature" (I.3 n19); "it will, as a vehicle, carry thee into the same root where all things are hidden" (I.3 n19); figuratively in I.1, "drawn about the fire of nature, as it were, into communication with her central source" (I.1 ¶39). — stated (I.3), interp. (I.1).

12. **Divine grace.** "through the grace of God it was, that I attained the perfect knowledge of all that I desired" (I.1 ¶57); "by the inspiration of the living God alone" (I.3 ¶3). — stated; QUOTED: Flamel, Hermes.

13. **The finished tincture acts by its inner fire.** "an astral fire which conquers all things and changes them into a nature like itself" (I.1 ¶76); "Azoth, viz., the Universal Mercury, which alone with its internal and external fire is sufficient for thee" (I.1 ¶81). — stated; QUOTED: Paracelsus, Khunrath.

## Operator

1. **Study, experience, divine blessing.** "three things said to be necessary for the attainment of the Hermetic science: viz., study, experience, and the divine benediction" (I.3 n1); the central experience "in the Universal Spirit, is not found without God" (I.3 n1). — stated; ATWOOD.

2. **Theory and a right beginning before practice.** "leave off experiments until the ground of wisdom is properly conceived" (I.1 ¶86); "Without theoretic knowledge and a right principle to begin with, many have wearied themselves in experimenting" (I.3 n2). But "Practice in the beginning is required, therefore, to interrogate and discern, from amongst so many shadowy representatives, the true light." (I.2 ¶42; Tensions 7). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Bacon.

3. **Experience won by labour; not fully teachable, yet a master or God for one point, and never alone.** "a reward of individual labor and perseverance only." (I.2 ¶34); "Nor can this secret be truly learned either from a master at all, but practice reveals it by the instinct of nature" (I.3 n36); "it can be revealed truly only by God, or by a master" (I.1 ¶36); "no man, for reasons hereafter explicable, can operate the Hermetic artifice alone" (I.3 n17). — stated; QUOTED: Scholiast, Artephius, the adepts (Tensions 8).

4. **Skilled hands, more-than-ordinary power, a fit artist.** "cannot be brought to knowledge without sagacious handling and human help" (I.2 ¶18); "is not the power of ordinary art, any more than of nature herself" (I.2 ¶24); "to find an artist able and fitted to perfect it" (I.2 ¶49). — stated; ATWOOD.

5. **Wisdom for its own sake, never gain.** "Wisdom must be sought for her own sake, neither for gold or silver or any intermediate benefit, lest these all should be denied together without the discovery of their source." (I.1 ¶17); failure comes "on account mainly of the indefiniteness and selfishness of their desires" (I.1 ¶64). — stated; ATWOOD.

6. **Moral fitness, holiness of life, fear of God as obedience to the Law of the work.** "the motives actuating them were of the purest possible kind; truthful, moral, always pious and intelligent" (I.1 ¶41); "the powers which sanctify of life and a well-grounded faith had gotten her" (I.1 ¶26); "to free himself from the vice of arrogance; and to be just and good, and of a profound reason, ready at hand to help mankind" (I.3 ¶25); "fear God, in whom is the strength of thy undertaking; and the bond of each separated element" (I.3 ¶15), "lest, being transgressed in any part, man should work evil instead of good through its means" (I.3 n19). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Hermes.

7. **Faith and free thought; a kindred light.** "Faith was the beacon light that led them on to conviction, by a free perspicuity of thought beyond things seen" (I.1 ¶39); the philosophers are seen "by a kindred light made visible, on their own ground" (I.1 ¶33); practice fails "without the true Light" (I.1 ¶86). — stated (¶39); the lights interp.

8. **Withdraw inward; enter the centre; reason, meditation, self-investigation.** "he who is rational shuts himself within from the threshold of ignorance; lest supinely he should be deceived" (I.3 ¶15), to "open to himself the door of a higher consciousness" (I.3 n19); "Thou must enter with thy whole spirit into the center of nature, and there behold how all things are begun, continued, and perfected" (I.3 n19); "let thy heart be fitted, as if thou wast thyself the author of that which I now teach" (I.3 ¶15); "without self-investigation" none perceive what is "spoken out of an experimental knowledge of Life" (I.3 n51). — stated; QUOTED: Hermes, Scholiast; ATWOOD.

9. **Learning, readiness; prove before teaching; secrecy and godly use.** "he that is not a great clerke, Is nice and lewd to medle with this werke;" (I.1 ¶52); "Prudence, patience, and penetration, the author owns, are required to understand him, and more than these for the discovery of his Great Art." (I.2 ¶50); written for "the predestined sons of wisdom, to guide them, already initiated" (I.3 n3); "provided one be not stiff- necked, and have a little experience" (I.1 ¶36); nothing "without the confident attestations of practice" (I.1 ¶62); "That to yourself ye shall keep yt secretly; And only yt use, as may be God’s pleasure," (I.1 ¶80); "that to no fatuous, ignorant, or inept person ye open this Stone" (I.3 ¶11). — stated; ATWOOD (n3); QUOTED: Norton, Tractatus Aureus, Artephius, Ashmole, Ripley, Hermes.

10. **After attainment: gratitude, vigilance, humility.** "watchful of every temptation, lest he should be drawn, even unwittingly, to abuse it" (I.3 n60); "sink away from every appetite of self love into the single adoration of the divine goodness" (I.3 n60). — stated; ATWOOD.

11. **A woman can do the work fully**: Perenelle "understands it as well as myself" (I.1 ¶57). — stated; QUOTED: Flamel.

## Procedure and stages

No chapter gives a single recipe; the order below is the one the texts support, with its firmness noted.

**Frame (firm; ATWOOD).** Two works: decoction "dissolving the matter discharges also its impurities by a proper rule; until, being at length on the verge of annihilation; i.e., freed from every exteriorly attracting form" (I.3 n7); then "the Second Work, which is only, in continuation of the First, to perfect the newly informed embryo and multiply its vivific light" (I.3 n7); "Decoction lessens the matter, but the tincture augments it" (I.3 ¶7). Before both, "the preliminary preparation of the matter being very generally termed the gross work" (I.3 n56). Cyclic: "repeated seven times before the final resurrection of the Quintessence into a permanent form of life" (I.3 n40); "Solve, Coagula, Fige." (I.2 ¶21). All operations are one sublimation, "though differently designated, are in their source the same" (I.3 n14).

**0. Find the preparation.** Flamel, three years "pondering the words of the philosophers, and then trying and proving the various operations which I thought they might mean by their words" (I.1 ¶57); then "literally following the directions in my book, I could not then miss the work if I would." (I.1 ¶57). — stated; QUOTED: Flamel; the preparation never given.

**1. Reduce (Solution), decompound, add nothing.** "the first work is to reduce the body into water, that is, into mercury, and this is called Solution, which is the foundation of the whole art" (I.2 ¶5); "nothing is done except that the matter be decompounded" (I.3 n4); "to which we add nothing nor take away anything, only in the preparation, removing superfluities" (I.2 ¶11); the Table's "separate the earth from the fire, the subtle from the gross, gently with much sagacity" (I.1 ¶9). — stated, firm; Table interp.

**2. Reverse nature's order.** "that which is fixed becoming volatile and the volatile nature fixed" (I.2 ¶20); "the order of natural procedure ought to be introverted for a true and perfect manifestation" (I.2 ¶27); the Table's "it ascends from earth to heaven, and again descends to earth: and receives the strength of the superiors and of the inferiors" (I.1 ¶9). — stated (I.2); Table interp.

**3. Drive to the verge of annihilation, catch the bare spirit, help it re-form.** "to reduce the matter, with her false forms, to the verge of annihilation, but to entrap the bare spirit and help her on from thence to operate her own intrinsical freed will" (I.2 ¶24); "it in some degree restores itself, if the force be continued" (I.2 ¶23). — stated; firm hinge of the first work.

**4. Seal the vessel, keep a continual fire, bind the volatile.** "let it be put into a furnace, which lute diligently, and observe that none of the spirit may escape" (I.3 ¶47); "decocted in the philosophic furnace called Athanor, with a continual fire" (I.3 n57); "the philosophers bind up their matter with a strong chain, that it may contend with the Fire" (I.3 ¶29), "by means of the affinity which is between the body and its spirit" (I.3 n37). I.1's laboratory "most complete with vessels, fuel, furnaces, and every material requisite, well adapted together and compact in one" (I.1 ¶33) stands beside this unglossed; the vessel is presupposed, never disclosed (I.3 n22, n48). — stated; position not fixed.

**5. Dissolution proper: vapour, blackness, death; death and revival.** "Take away therefore the vapour from the water, and the blackness from the oily tincture, and death from the faeces: and by dissolution thou shalt achieve a triumphant reward" (I.3 ¶23); "the evil of the original sin is said to be discovered by a radical dissolution of the spirit" (I.3 n31); "triturate it in most sharp vinegar till it becomes obscured" (I.3 ¶48), the superior life "obscured during the purification, and for the revealment of its true mineral radix" (I.3 n58); "it lives in the putrefaction" (I.3 ¶48). — stated; QUOTED: Hermes; ATWOOD. Blackness belongs to the dissolution; its place against I.2 ¶36's first whiteness is unsaid (Tensions 9).

**6. Purify by repetition until white: seven separations.** "The two contrary natures of light and darkness must contend together, as it were, in mortal strife, and the war must be waged unceasingly for the destruction of the foreign life until it succumbs" (I.3 n34); "Separate, says Eiraneaus, the light from the darkness seven times, and the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete" (I.3 n39); "seven times distilled, which after the eighth distillation is converted by force of the fire into ashes" (I.3 n6); the matter does it to itself: "it dissolves and coagulates itself ---whitens, rubifies, and makes itself to be both yellow and black" (I.2 ¶11). — stated; QUOTED: Scholiast, Eiraneaus, Aquinas; ATWOOD.

**7. First whiteness; three principles appear; spirit reduced to ash.** "the Matter only as it first appears, pure, as they say, and white, out of the philosophical contrition" (I.2 ¶36); then "the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury of adepts, in relation to each other of agent, patient, and offspring universal" (I.2 ¶36); "If you have brought your mercurial spirit to ashes or burnt it by its own fire, you have, continues our informant, an incomparable treasure" (I.3 n9). — stated; relation to I.3's post-black whitening unsaid (Tensions 9).

**8. Separate into essential relations, purify, rejoin in proportion, without killing the life.** "separate the matter into its essential relationships, and join them again together in harmonious proportion" (I.3 n23); "The elements of Mercury being separated, says Ripley, and again commixed by equal weight or proportion, make the elixir complete" (I.2 ¶25); "to dissolve without destroying the continental life" (I.3 n62). — stated.

**9. Conjunction: spirit, soul, body; the matter lives; reason turned back.** "the spirit thereafter penetrates the body, and the body fixes the spirit --- the soul being conjoined, tinges the whole of its proper color, whether white or red" (I.3 n21); "when we marry the crowned king to our red daughter, and in a gentle fire, not hurtful, she doth conceive a son" (I.3 ¶34); "The Matter lives, and that which may seem strange, Co-worketh with the Form t’attain the end" (I.2 ¶45); reason "returns, fastening, as Hermes says, upon the wheel of its proper life, as it were, introverting the natural channel and order of generation" (I.3 n50). — stated; QUOTED: Hermes, Eiranaeus; ATWOOD; n21 voice UNCLEAR (Scholiast or Atwood).

**10. Sow the seed in its prepared matrix (Second Work).** "planted in its proper ethereal vehicle, well prepared and fallow to bring forth its virtue in manifold increase" (I.3 n40); "our lune or silver, not common silver, is that which receives the seed of the gold, afterwards it is governed by our continual fire for seven months (philosophical), and sometimes ten" (I.3 n40); "the preparation, generation, birth, specification --- all proceeding, arte mirabili, on the hidden basis of its primal eduction" (I.2 ¶44). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the New Light author (n40); lune, milk of the earth, salt of nature unexplained (roles only).

**11. Ferment.** "put in them the ferment confecting the earth with the water until the Ixir ferment even as dough ferments" (I.3 ¶58); "The fermenting light by constant addition of the spirit, leavens more and more" (I.3 n45). — stated.

**12. White before Red; a second death; reddening.** "Till the white medicine be fully wrought;" (I.1 ¶60); "the fermentive light is indeed white before the multiplication of its internal form has rubified it, and the silvery spirit is made manifest before the solar ray" (I.3 n67); the light "must be induced toenter, to suffer again, and die in order to transmute the foul material into itself" (I.3 n44); "By a conjunction with its own permanent prepared spirit, the albified water is made red" (I.3 n54); "Burn then the Brazen Body with an exceeding great fire" (I.3 ¶42, link interp.). — order firm (white before red stated); QUOTED: Norton, Hermes; ATWOOD.

**13. Multiplication.** "again dissolved and coagulated by the water of its mercury, white or red, of which it was prepared, then the tinging virtue will be augmented each time by ten degrees" (I.3 n70); Norton's "Him to increase in multiplication" (I.1 ¶60) is unexplained. — stated (I.3).

**14. Projection.** First "fermented with the most pure gold" (I.3 n70); then "projected the fourth part of one grain, wrapped in paper, upon eight ounces of quicksilver boiling in a crucible" (I.1 ¶76); potencies to "1 upon 272,330" (I.1 ¶66); "they lost a great portion in trial before they found out the true height of the medicine" (I.1 ¶66). — stated as narrated; QUOTED: Van Helmont, Ashmole.

**The named ladders.** Atwood's (Keys 6); Arnold's "it is made citrine, then saltish, but without crystals; and no man may touch it with his tongue, for it is a deadly poison" (I.2 ¶29); the fermentation through every form to "a potential chaos and egg of philosophers; a precedential, nameless principle, always in mutation, becoming to be" (I.2 ¶37); the vulture's "I am the White of the Black, and the Red of the White, and the Citrine of the Red" (I.3 ¶9). Alignment with the steps unsaid (Tensions 9).

**Inner register.** "That which is within, viz., the causal light, must be drawn forth by art and fixed; and that which is without, viz., the sensual spirit of life must be made flexible and occultated" (I.3 n8); "whereby a door is marvelously opened into the most intimate recess of life" (I.3 n50). — stated; ATWOOD (Tensions 1).

**Unexplained instructions.** "to mortify two Argent vives together, and to venerate the Argent vive of Auripigment, and the oriental Argent vive of Magnesia" (I.3 ¶32); mingle the watery soul "with our Acetum" (I.3 ¶27); "our chalybs, or steel" (I.2 ¶33); "Return, then, O my son, the extinct coal to the water for thirty days" (I.3 ¶18); "boil them in that which comes from the horse's belly for seven, fourteen, or twenty-one days" (I.3 ¶47); "The power of it is Integral, if it be turned into earth." (I.1 ¶9); the lamp's elements "Digested With laborious art" (I.1 ¶19).

## Signs

1. **The colours must be fully passed; they are inner changes.** "it is not perfected unless the colours be thoroughly passed and accomplished" (I.3 ¶5); "the necessary phases which the philosophic material passes in order of color and qualitative virtue" (I.3 n47); colours mean "the qualities and changes which take place during the preparation, and multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light" (I.2 ¶39). — stated; the cry as an order interp.

2. **Blackness, the gate; corruption then improvement; the new light terrifies.** "O happy gate of blackness, cries the sage, which art the passage to this so glorious a change" (I.3 n42); "The blackness of the night is the putrefaction of the same, and the clearness of the day signifies its resurrection to a state of comparative purity" (I.3 n12); "Then it becomes the Dragon eating his own wings and destroying himself" (I.3 ¶47, interp.); "the corruption is made manifest in the matter to be renewed, and hence the melioration will appear and each side is a signal of Art" (I.3 ¶44); the new light "causes a terrification of the whole circumferential life" (I.3 n69). — stated.

3. **Whiteness; Luna; the Dragon withdraws.** "a white fume, in substance like to pure silver, resolving bodies into their original whiteness" (I.2 ¶29, Atrephius); "By the crowned king, Hermes signifies the first manifested resplendence of the vital tincture" (I.3 n22); "Here thou mayest rest and wait, and enjoy the glory of thy white elixir" (I.3 n22); "Luna after fifteen days is diminished; and in the third, she is augmented" (I.3 ¶7); "The Dragon, who watches the crevices, shuns the sunbeams" (I.3 ¶35). — stated; numbers unexplained.

4. **The decisive sign: the masculine tincture rises from the black united with the white.** "come forth out of the black darkness together in union with the white virginal spirit, he will then know that he hath the great Arcanum of the world" (I.3 n53); "throwing the glaucus veil aside, she shall appear entirely golden" (I.3 n53, interp.). — stated; QUOTED: "a more modern experimentalist", Democritus; ATWOOD.

5. **Red as flesh and blood; marks of the finished tincture; rapid colour change.** "a most pure and rubicund tincture of the color of flesh and blood" (I.3 n42); "It is of a ruby color of the greatest weight; and this is a true sign of the tincture of the philosophers" (I.1 ¶76); "liquid like resin, transparent like crystal, frangible like glass" (I.1 ¶76); Maria: "in less than an hour, the composition will pass from one color to another, till it comes to the perfect red or white" (I.3 n67). — stated; QUOTED: the adept, Paracelsus, Maria.

6. **The matter lives; scent.** "The Matter lives" (I.2 ¶45); Flamel knew it "by the scent and odor thereof." (I.1 ¶57). — both interp. as signs; which product Flamel means is unsettled.

7. **Projection; the tangible proof; gold takes the Light wholly.** "the quicksilver, with a small voice, presently stood in its flux, and was congealed like to yellow wax" (I.1 ¶76); "the transmutation of metallic bodies was a proof addressed to the senses" (I.1 ¶76); "the Formal Light is wholly swallowed up in it without residue, dissolving intimately, gently, and naturally, as they compare it to ice in warm water" (I.2 ¶48). — stated; QUOTED: Van Helmont; ATWOOD.

8. **False signs.** "Both red and white which were untrew." (I.1 ¶65); "Whites and Reds" (I.1 ¶84); profusion marks chance finders, "never by those who have themselves confected it." (I.1 ¶66). — interp. (¶65, ¶84), stated (¶66).

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Literal reading and literal practice.** "has already lost the clue to Ariadne" (I.1 ¶84); "surrounding many a literal soul with stills, coals, and furnaces, in hope by such lifeless instruments to sublime the Spirit of Nature" (I.1 ¶86); "men neither see nor do they believe there is any other fire but the common fire, nor any other sulfur or mercury --- thus they are deceived by their own opinions" (I.3 n41); "not of kitchen-stuff, those three pot-principles of water, oil, and earth" (I.3 n30); "those imposters who tell you of a sulphur tingens" (I.2 ¶8). — stated; QUOTED: Artephius (¶84), the adept (n41, I.2 ¶8), Vaughan (n30); ATWOOD.

2. **Fire-digesters destroy the spirit; its escape is "the common catastrophe".** "Such instruments do, in fact, expel the very nature which the ancients prized; leaving us without all recompense in the dead ashes of her consuming vesture" (I.2 ¶24); "if she is suffered to depart invisibly without pursuit or amendment, which is the common catastrophe, then she is caught up by other external compellants, and becoming defiled, is imprisoned by them" (I.2 ¶24); "lest the penetrative mineral vapor should expire and leave the dead body" (I.3 n57). — stated.

3. **Mistaking common air or light for the spirit.** "mistaking Olympus, embrace some cloud whilst the life-giving Juno is far away above all our idea and sight" (I.2 ¶18, interp.); ordinary phenomena are "shadowy and mingled" (I.2 ¶34). — ATWOOD.

4. **The spirit enclosed and impure; the Dragon; poison.** "her forms or sulfurs are perplexed, and the passive spirit of nature is included and impure" (I.2 ¶48); "The dragon is the self-willed spirit, which is externally derived into nature, by the fall into generation" (I.3 n31); "for the root of this science is a deadly poison" (I.3 n31); "A shadowy darkness passes always along with the philosophic body, moving in its own light until it is thoroughly purified from sensual defilements" (I.3 n21). — stated.

5. **Experiment without theory; a false idea of the matter; negligence.** "through negligence and a false opinion of the matter, the operation is perverted, as bad leaven on the dough" (I.3 ¶58); the moderns "could adduce no tangible proof of their doctrine." (I.2 ¶15). — stated.

6. **The false finish; projection without fermentation.** "the artist is rejoiced, and thinks perhaps his work is finished, and that he has the treasure of the world in hand; but it is not so" (I.3 n49); the light is "imperfect, alone, and transient, without the masculine tincture to fix it in manifestation" (I.3 n49); unfermented, "there would ensue loss and damage in the projection" (I.3 n70). — stated; adepts cited.

7. **Obscure, disordered and false books.** "introverted in their order, and dispersed with repetitions, to prevent the truth from becoming openly obvious" (I.1 ¶82); stages described "without note of order in the art" (I.2 ¶42); "an infinity of names, all more or less applicable, yet delusive" (I.2 ¶37); "I have termed this dissolved, and this joined, this nearest I have termed furthest off" (I.3 ¶46); "mutilated editions of the old masters, filled with inconsistencies, and the wicked inventions of designing fraud" (I.1 ¶53). — stated.

8. **Avarice, haste; power without conscience.** "With hastie credence to sume away their thrift;" (I.1 ¶52); Starkey "caught in the trap of my own covetousness" (I.1 ¶73); priests "having forsaken the law of conscience, attempted to wield without it the rod of magic power" (I.1 ¶30, interp.); "lest they should be made powerful in evil for the perpetuation of sinful things" (I.3 ¶11). — stated; QUOTED: Norton, Starkey, Hermes.

9. **Danger to the operator from the work.** Bacon led "into forbidden realms of self-sufficiency and unlawful peace of mind" (I.1 ¶38); Vaughan's "subtle light of life should in these instances have been swallowed up in the superior attraction of a greater flame" (I.1 ¶74); "a potent spiritual medicine, which would heighten the spiritual consequences of depravity and habitual excess" (I.1 ¶64). — interp.; ¶38 an unnamed report ("as it is suggested") relayed by Atwood; ¶64, ¶74 voice UNCLEAR.

10. **Exposure and persecution.** "sacred mysteries, too openly exposed to the multitude, became perverted and vilified by their abuse" (I.1 ¶11, interp.); "even the true adepts --- for the public knew not to distinguish --- suffered equal cruelty" (I.1 ¶66). — ATWOOD.

11. **Two errors about signs.** Holiness placed "in those material signs, apart from the spirit and only thing signified" (I.1 ¶26), or signs discarded, "mistaking these things for superstitions" (I.1 ¶26); without understanding of "that original virtue" they "do but mimic an efficacy" (I.1 ¶26). — stated; the virtue unidentified (interp.); ATWOOD.

## Result

1. **The Stone: purified, condensed Ether; perhaps Light essentialized.** "their stone is nothing more or less than the pure Ethereality of nature, separated by artificial means, purified and made concrete by constriction and scientific multiplication of its proper Light" (I.2 ¶44); "a fiery form of Light inspissate, and this is the Stone of Fire" (I.2 ¶44); "is Light essentialized in its proper substance, and exalted by fermentation into an immutable magnet, able to draw and convert the radical homogeneity of nature into its own assimilative accord?" (I.3 n70), "an ultimate promise only". — stated (¶44); n70 tentative.

2. **Stone of many colours, elixir of Light, gold without spots, Universal Ferment, the son.** "This, O son, is the concealed Stone of many colours; which is born in one colour" (I.3 ¶13); "that tincture of many dyes which, being dissolved renews itself, and dying survives itself" (I.3 n18); "the most precious gold without spots --- evenly tempered, which neither fire nor air, nor water, nor earth is able to corrupt" (I.3 ¶60); "For this son is himself the treasury, even himself bearing the Philosophic Matter" (I.3 ¶35). — stated; QUOTED: Hermes.

3. **It transmutes and multiplies without limit, perfecting each thing in its kind.** "which being transmuted, transmutes; and multiplying, multiplies its proper substance freely, in proportion to the virtue which it has acquired in the fermentation" (I.2 ¶48); "applied to any body, exalts and perfects it in its own kind" (I.2 ¶8); "one part thereof will tinge ten parts of an imperfect body, then one hundred, then a thousand, and so infinitely on" (I.3 n38); "overcoming every subtle and penetrating every solid thing." (I.1 ¶9). — stated.

4. **Gold better than mined; a universal medicine.** "much better indeed than common gold, more soft also, and more pliable." (I.1 ¶57); "a medicine the most potent and precious in the whole world" (I.2 ¶40); "cures all sorts of maladies whatever, and preserves any one in good health to the last term of his life" (I.3 n70); "said to prolong life, and cure disease and moral indulgence and physical defects" (I.3 n18). — stated as testimony; QUOTED: Flamel, the adept; ATWOOD.

5. **Results differ by kingdom; in man, a divine image.** "in him it assumes an Image that is Divine and more potent than all the rest: which is in this life yet an embryo" (I.2 ¶46), which "transcends the limits of this nether sphere, and passes into communion with the highest life" (I.2 ¶46); "the quintessential perfectibility of the three kingdoms in their homogeneal unity" (I.1 ¶6, interp.). — ATWOOD.

6. **Moral transformation and regeneration of the maker.** "takes away from him the root of all sin and evil; changing his evil into good" (I.1 ¶57); "the mystery of human regeneration, so zealously proclaimed, was also rationally understood" (I.1 ¶11); "the one Art and medium of vital perfectibility" (I.1 ¶74, interp.). — stated; QUOTED: Flamel; ATWOOD.

7. **Inner knowledge: the causal principle recalled; "divine things"; the King's seal.** "By an artificial dissolution of the vital bond, by means of Alchemy, the Causal principle of nature is said to be developed into reminiscence and to arise in the experience of the recreated life" (I.3 n64); reason enters "that Identity by which the powers of the Universal Nature are made manifest and intrinsically understood" (I.3 n8); "the sovereign philosophy and secret of all hidden things" (I.3 ¶56); "the great Law of Light or universal reason, which is finally impressed upon the regenerated vitality of nature" (I.3 n69); Agrippa's "final and convictive faith, which is as much above ordinary science as the vulgar credulity of mankind is below it" (I.1 ¶64). — stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Hermes.

8. **The higher aim is theosophy; proof of the unknown Cause; lesser benefits by the way.** "universal theosophy, rather than to rest its capabilities of quickening and perfection in the mineral kingdom" (I.1 ¶42); "that very unknown Cause it was the alleged object of the Hermetic experiment to prove." (I.2 ¶47); "many intermediate benefits by the way, alluring health, science and riches, of her mineral stores" (I.3 n70). — stated; ATWOOD.

## Principles

1. **One matter, worked by and through itself; nothing foreign.** "a certain pure matter, which, being discovered and brought by art to perfection, converts to itself proportionally all imperfect bodies that it touches" (I.2 ¶2); "fixed by the reciprocated action of its own proper Identity, as agent and patient, alternating to improve" (I.3 n21); "mortifying the same by its proper nature, which is also in it" (I.3 ¶26). — stated; QUOTED: Arnold, Scholiast, Hermes.

2. **Transmutation is of the subject, not of species; imperfections are accidental.** "Species non transmutantur, sed subjecta specierum optime et propriisime" (I.2 ¶5; ours: species are not transmuted, but their subjects); imperfections "may be expelled, and ought to be for the attainment of perfection" (I.2 ¶22). — stated; QUOTED: Rosarium.

3. **As above, so below; the work repeats creation.** "that which is above is as that which is below, and that which is below is as that which is above, for performing the miracles of the One Thing" (I.1 ¶9); "So the world was created." (I.1 ¶9); "And as it was in the great mystery, so it is in the lesser." (I.2 ¶25). — interp. (Table); stated (Paracelsus).

4. **Reverse nature's course; reduce to the origin; death before life; separation begins generation.** "nature operates her ordinary manifestation in direct contrariety to her perfect law" (I.2 ¶20); "the reduction of bodies to their original matter, by introversion of the generated life, is requisite to a true manifestation and permanence in any form" (I.2 ¶49); "The destroyers are the renovators" (I.3 ¶44); "as it dies by the taking away of the spirit, so it lives in the return" (I.3 ¶49); "separation was the principle and beginning of all generation." (I.2 ¶25). — stated; "introversion" undefined.

5. **Nature makes the matter; art unveils and helps her exceed herself.** "But nature alone engenders it; it is not possible to make it by art; for to create is proper to God alone" (I.2 ¶33); "Thus nature, by the help of art, is said to transcend herself" (I.2 ¶44); "if at any time nature be sweetly and wittily helped, then art may perfect that which nature could not" (I.2 ¶49). — stated; QUOTED: Synesius, Sendivogius; ATWOOD.

6. **Like from like; the ferment kindred; only Light multiplies; repetition improves.** "there is no ferment otherwise than from a kindred nature" (I.3 ¶58); "as leaven makes leaven, and every ferment begets its own exaltation" (I.3 n55); "the ethereal seed, on the other hand, tends always to improve its generation, fertilizing by the return of each successive growth, and enriching its maternal soil" (I.3 n40). — stated.

7. **One substance, many forms; the three principles are modes and stage marks; the threefold law.** "The three principles, the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury, are merely different as modes of being of the same thing" (I.2 ¶39); the names "do but indicate the stages of progress and development, as of a tree" (I.2 ¶39); "the threefold law of life; the ingress, egress, and alternatig action of which, under dominance of either of its principles, constitutes the whole phenomena of the Hermetic process" (I.3 n35). — stated; ATWOOD.

8. **Active and passive need a medium; sulfur seed, mercury earth; spirit held in body tinges and fixes; the operator's weights.** "Each needs the other; for the precepts demand a medium" (I.3 ¶23); "The purified sulfur, fixed and incombustible, is the generating seed of the universal nature, according to the adepts" (I.3 n29); "for, by this means they are made tinging and fixed" (I.3 ¶26); "if thou shalt know to add thy weights to the weights of nature, to double mercury and triple sulfur, it will quickly be terminated in good, then in better, until into best of all" (I.3 n5). — stated.

9. **The art is "association of the Spirit"; its aim Self-Knowledge.** "In a scientific association of the Spirit the Hermetic Art has been said summarily to consist" (I.3 n59); the vital light "re-entering from without inwards, when freed again, will it not then probably meet itself in a yet more profound experience?" (I.3 n46); "the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge, which alone can enable man to know and understand and possess divine things" (I.3 n46). — stated; ATWOOD, deferred.

10. **Reading rules.** "not corporally but spiritually and wisely" (I.3 n4); "All here is to be understood ethereally" (I.3 n49); "understand my writings, not so much from the outside of my words as from the possibility of nature" (I.1 ¶84, Sendivogius); "Constantly holding in mind the simplicity of the Substance, whence these images are all derived" (I.2 ¶42); "words do not convey true ideas unless their proper leader be previously entertained" (I.1 ¶6); observe nature "that from analogy we may be better able to imagine and judge of the proper method of experimenting" (I.3 n27), the farming analogy "almost without deviation, point out the method of its application" (I.3 n40). — stated; ATWOOD.

11. **Theory open, practice hidden; the veil purposeful.** "the roots of the Hermetic science are fairly stated; but the practice most carefully concealed" (I.1 ¶39); "a part of wisdom’s envelope, to guard her universal magistery from an incapable and dreaming world" (I.1 ¶33); "it being the custom of philosophers to use them; thou hast need of them" (I.1 ¶84, Sendivogius). — stated; ATWOOD.

12. **One hidden discovery underlies ancient philosophy, entered by experience.** "that occult discovery on which the systems of ancient philosophy appear to have been very uniformly based" (I.1 ¶12); Boehme "entered experimentally into the mystery" and revealed it "theosophically, but not intellectually." (I.1 ¶68, ¶70). — stated, hedged; ATWOOD.

13. **The beginning decides the end; one method in all three kingdoms.** "In delibi principio was myne inception: Therefore was frawde in fine the conclusion:" (I.1 ¶65, Ripley, interp.); the method "is described as the same in each of the three kingdoms of nature" (I.2 ¶49). — ATWOOD.

## Tensions and open questions

1. **On the operator or on a material?** I.3 n8, n19, n46, n50, n51, n64 frame the work inwardly ("within themselves", "Self-Knowledge", "reminiscence"); n57, n67, n70 and I.1 ¶76 are laboratory (Athanor, sealed vessel, crucible, gold ferment, Maria's hour). I.2 ¶49 uses metals because their radix "first presents itself in the experimental process" and is less "vital and consequential". Atwood admits both readings of the "seven bodies" and leans vital (I.3 n65); she followed "a certain experimental clue" she does not name (I.3 n71). I.1 ¶33's laboratory "compact in one" is either a single self-contained thing, not an external laboratory (favoured by ¶33's contrasts), or a laboratory of another kind. Unresolved.

2. **Light inherent or supplied?** I.2 ¶8 is defective. Inherent: "its proper Light" (I.2 ¶39), "its own included light" (¶16), "its own good sulfur" (I.3 n5). Supplied: "an inward agent granted" (I.2 ¶45), "supply the deficiency of Form to the purified body of the Spirit" (I.2 ¶49), "the external and internal agents" (I.3 n16). The "superior ferment or attraction" of I.2 ¶4 is unnamed.

3. **Mercury = Ether?** Never in one sentence (chain: I.2 ¶16, ¶37, ¶43, ¶44, ¶49). Likewise the divine water (I.1 ¶20): subject, agent, or both.

4. **Found on earth?** "it is found flowing upon earth" (I.2 ¶29, Lully) against I.2 ¶20, ¶30, ¶49: present only mixed and defiled, or the sources conflict. "Their earth", "caverns of the mountains", "minera", "chalybs", "our lune", "milk of the earth", "salt of nature" remain unexplained.

5. **One substance or two?** One matter, nothing added (I.2 ¶11, ¶43; I.3 ¶38). Yet: Mercury "without his sister (i.e., as agent and patient)" (I.2 ¶11, defective); "the union of masculine and feminine spirits" (I.2 ¶33); "the fire, and the heavenly Flyer" (I.3 ¶11); "two Argent vives" (I.3 ¶32); "these two fumes are the root of the Hermetic science" (I.3 n67); the seed sown in "our lune" (I.3 n40). The reconciling line is that the two are relations within one thing (I.2 ¶31, ¶42; I.3 n30); but "the son’s allusion is intimate to the art, and particular" (I.3 n29) withholds the operative sense.

6. **Force the spirit or free it?** I.2 ¶23 forces matter "if the force be continued"; ¶24 would "help her on from thence to operate her own intrinsical freed will", and its "most forcible excentric will" may describe ordinary nature or the method. I.3 has reason "artificially constrained" (n50) and matter bound "with a strong chain" (¶29), yet "by means of the affinity which is between the body and its spirit" (n37).

7. **Theory first or practice first?** I.1 ¶86 and I.3 n2 against "Practice in the beginning is required" (I.2 ¶42); both Atwood's. Whether early practice is the "gross work" (I.3 n56) is unsaid.

8. **Master or none; alone or not.** "revealed truly only by God, or by a master" (I.1 ¶36) and Flamel's "Jew stranger" (I.1 ¶57), against "Nor can this secret be truly learned either from a master at all" (I.3 n36). The "brother" of I.3 n17: human companion or a principle of the work.

9. **Order of colours and stages.** Four ladders do not align: I.2 ¶40; Arnold's clear → citrine → saltish (I.2 ¶29); Aquinas' white, red, yellow, black unordered (I.2 ¶11); the vulture's black → white → red → citrine (I.3 ¶9). I.2 ¶36 puts a first whiteness before the annihilate state; I.3 puts blackness first. "preparation, generation, birth, specification" (I.2 ¶44) and the "seven letters" (I.3 n47) are tied to neither. Two Elixirs (I.2 ¶46, ¶49) unreconciled.

10. **The vessel.** Concealed (I.3 n48), presupposed (n22). n48's answers (Maria's "found in all bodies"; the "innermost chamber") are the words n9 uses of the Argent vive. Whether vessel and matter are one is unsaid; compare "compact in one" (I.1 ¶33).

11. **The fire.** "moist fire" (I.3 ¶10) and "that which comes from the horse's belly" (I.3 ¶47): steady heat (n57) or a liquid medium. "Artificial excitation" deferred (I.3 n14). That "its proper fire" excludes external heat is interp. only.

12. **Compounded or decompounded.** "nothing is done except it be compounded" (I.3 ¶5) vs "nothing is done except that the matter be decompounded" (I.3 n4): variant or misprint. "one to two, and three to one" (I.3 ¶6) has two unreconciled glosses (n5, n6).

13. **Death by the elixir; moral change; Bacon's fault.** Literal overdose or "dissolution in the conflict of opposite principles" (I.1 ¶64, ¶74), and whether Atwood adopts it. Flamel's change from the work or from the contemplation that follows (I.1 ¶57). Bacon's "forbidden realms of self-sufficiency" (I.1 ¶38): operator's fault or a state the work produces.

14. **Names left undecoded.** Red Elixir, Red Stone, Red Powder, Powder of Projection, quintessence, elixir (I.1); Acetum, foliated sulphur, Cambar, the Citrine of Botri, the encaustic, "fiery water", "earth or sea", "radix", "rust", Buba/Jupiter, Alpha, Yda, Liber, Sol (I.3); Green Lion vs vitriol (I.1 ¶65); Blood of the Lion vs universal tincture (I.1 ¶81); "the operation of the Sun" and the earth/fire, subtle/gross pairing (I.1 ¶9); "temperate" vs "equal" composition (I.3 ¶61); "the work begins from the vegetable, next from the animal" (I.3 ¶62); the numbers (fifteen days, three, thirty, seven/fourteen/twenty-one days, seven or ten months), only "seven" glossed; Venus's "brother" and the King's "mother" (I.3 ¶39); "our Phoenix" and its "reviviscent spirit" (I.1 ¶37–38): the art, or something in the work; the three kingdoms vs "Three Parts of the philosophy" (I.1 ¶6, ¶9); "Whites and Reds" (I.1 ¶84): spurious products or misused stage talk.

15. **Status of Atwood's definitions.** I.3 n70 is a question followed by "an ultimate promise only"; I.2 ¶34's definition is said not to teach how to work the matter.

16. **Deferred within Part I.** The subject's properties (I.1 ¶24); "conditions of success" (I.1 ¶39); the fire's excitation (I.3 n14); the Rational Ferment (I.3 n32); the Intellectual Ground (I.3 n10); why no one works alone (I.3 n17); Self-Knowledge (I.3 n46); the inner work of n8; Mars and Venus "in the last extremity of life" (I.3 n49); "introversion" (I.2 ¶27); Hermes' veil "hardly expedient to look through at this stage" (I.3 n40).

17. **Textual cruxes.** I.2 ¶49: reduction "as by the ordinary process of unassisted nature" may lack a "not" (the next clause, "she must be helped", supports this). I.2 ¶49 "greater and more ignoble perfection": nobler, or as printed. I.3 n18 "with its course": path or body, possibly a misprint. I.2 ¶36 "waters of her extreme life": nature's or the Matter's. I.2 ¶48 "the philosophic Proteus": the fermented Vital Spirit or the matter in general. I.2 ¶25 "great" and "lesser" mystery: generation and the Art, or undefined.
