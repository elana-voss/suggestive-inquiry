# Method synthesis — Part IV: The Hermetic Practice

Sources: chapter method summaries IV.1 (Vital Purification / Gross Work), IV.2 (Philosophic or Subtle Work), IV.3 (Six Keys of Eudoxus, with Atwood's supplement and comment), IV.4 (Conclusion). Labels: *stated*/*interpreted*; voice ATWOOD, QUOTED (name), UNCLEAR. Refs as "IV.1 ¶4".

Shape of the Part: a manual "Gross" purification (IV.1); a hands-off "Subtle" work (IV.2); a laboratory sequence Atwood then says is not literally true (IV.3); a Conclusion placing the whole Art inside the human being (IV.4). Atwood's own thread: the thing worked on is the operator's life and understanding; the agent is Light (with Fire, Motive, Will and Mind as related names the text does not all equate); the work is repeated dissolution and re-fixing of that life until a stable Form of Light results; the end is union with the First Cause. The laboratory language is the same process in another register, and the link between the two is withheld.

## Subject

1. **One universal substance, nourishable in man; the Stone is its concentrated virtue.** "to rectify the Universal Element, and compress its invisible vapor into a tangible Form" (IV.1 ¶47); IV.4 ¶10, ¶2 above. — *stated; ATWOOD.*
2. **Concretely, the operator's own life, understanding and will; man is the laboratory.** "the component principles of life in the circulation" (IV.2 ¶18); "though this concerneth man, in the first place, yet secondly it concerneth the whole creation" (IV.3 ¶38); IV.4 ¶3 above. — *stated (IV.3, IV.4); interpreted for IV.1–2 (basis IV.1 ¶40, ¶47, ¶48; IV.2 ¶18–20); ATWOOD; the theosophist (endorsed).*
3. **As first found it is crude: a medium released by entrancing the senses, fantastic and unstable, loaded with foreign matter.** "the medium in its natural state is volatile, immanifest, phantastic, irrational, and important, compared with what it subsequently is become" (IV.1 ¶4); "such a mass of heterogeneous superfluities adhering to our subject, that nothing short of dissolution can give it rest" (IV.1 ¶7). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
4. **One matter, one vessel; hidden under countless names.** "one matter, one linear way throughout, one vessel uniform throughout, except removal" (IV.1 ¶9, Zachary); "among a million of pseudonyms and abstruse appellations" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated; QUOTED (endorsed); UNCLEAR (IV.2).*
5. **It carries its own agent and seed.** "the Mercury of the Wise carries in its centre its own Sulphur, which coagulates it" (IV.3 ¶12); "each has in himself, as Trismegistus says, the seed of his own regeneration" (IV.2 ¶18); man has "the whole fermentative virtue, motive, and principle of vital melioration and every requisite complete within himself" (IV.4 ¶3). — *stated; Eudoxus; the Adept; ATWOOD.*
6. **Three principles not truly united; two natures in every thing, to be reduced to one.** "though they appear pure and perfectly united together, there still wants much of their being so" (IV.3 ¶7); "That Two Natures be reduced to One, as they were at the beginning." (IV.3 ¶38). — *stated; Eudoxus; the theosophist (endorsed).*
7. **In the laboratory register, the Water drawn from the Stone is the foundation.** "Our Water is a living Spring which comes out of the Stone by a natural miracle of our philosophy." (IV.3 ¶8). — *stated; Eudoxus.*
8. **Deepest layer: the Root of Reason buried in sense-life (Wisdom, the saving Salt); the three kingdoms bound in man's identity.** "the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source, which lies profoundly buried in this life of sense" (IV.4 ¶20); "bound together in the threefold enchantment of his natural Identity" (IV.4 ¶4). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
9. **Nothing in the subject is discarded.** "he who pretends to separate anything from our subject knows nothing of philosophy" (IV.3 ¶7). — *stated; Pontanus via Eudoxus (endorsed).*

## Agent

1. **Light: Key, sole multiplier, the Stone's ferment, God.** "What else but Light can multiply, or does multiply, anyhow or anywhere" (IV.3 ¶30); "not common Light, but that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient, which is potentially included everywhere in either kingdom of nature, though laid asleep" (IV.3 ¶30). — *stated; ATWOOD (Light = God: Plotinus, IV.2 ¶41, endorsed).*
2. **The Secret Fire, never identified, dissolves without violence; compare, not identified with it, Flamel's even fire and the specific non-corrosive virtue of the Solution (IV.1 ¶39, Van helmont).** "this Secret Fire, which dissolves the Stone naturally and without violence" (IV.3 ¶5); "a bright shining fire like the Sun itself, whose heat must neither be excessive, but always of one and the same degree" (IV.1 ¶41, Flamel). — *stated (function); QUOTED (endorsed).*
3. **The interior Fire is the prepared subject, roused from outside; the worker is within the matter.** "this same Lunar Caustic is brought into act by an exterior excitation" (IV.1 ¶29); "The work is performed by an invisible Artist; for there is a secret incubation of the Spirit of God upon nature" (IV.2 ¶18); "The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself" (IV.3 ¶38). — *stated; ATWOOD; the Adept, the theosophist (endorsed).*
4. **In the Gross Work the Hand is the literal instrument.** "the Hand was the instrument employed, not only to impart the Spirit as a natural gift, but by a continual mechanic trituration, as it were, to dissolve and ultimately obliterate its innate defects" (IV.1 ¶5); "Nature cannot of herself enter into the dissolution, says the author of the Filum Ariadne, because she has no hands" (IV.1 ¶5). — *stated; ATWOOD with QUOTED authorities (Van Helmont, Lully, Vaughan). Mesmeric passes: interpreted (IV.1 ¶4).*
5. **Will, Motive, Mind; Motion (IV.1 ¶34); Power and Motive disciplining the "Heroic Fire" (IV.1 ¶33).** "no sooner does it attain to Self Knowledge, by proper inquiry within, than the Efficient proceeds out wards to image its motive in operation" (IV.1 ¶48); "It is the part of Mind alone to represent herself in this way by her own reflective energy, to embody the ethereal Image, and chisel it out in Light." (IV.2 ¶14). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
6. **Water/Spirit dissolves, revives, binds; Spirit and Water do all.** "The Spirit makes use of the water to purge and wash his body, and he will bring it at last to a celestial immortal constitution." (IV.2 ¶18); "the Spirit is the only tie which works this conjunction" (IV.3 ¶7). — *stated; the Adept, Eudoxus.*
7. **Several fires, none identified: three natural/unnatural/supernatural and a Fourth (IV.2 ¶11); a different "fire of putrefaction and generation" (IV.3 ¶21); a second Vulcan.** "Whatsoever the Vulcan in the Greater Orb leaves crude and perfects not, that in the Lesser World must be amended by a certain other Vulcan" (IV.1 ¶40). — *stated; the Adept, Sendivogius, Basil Valentine.*
8. **Divine Light and Wisdom finish the work by their own Rule; the Lord is the Hierophanta (IV.2 ¶16); a "catholic torch" kindles the Light of Nature.** "When once the Divine Light is loosened in life, everything is prospered as long as her Rule is observed" (IV.3 ¶39); "The catholic torch, miraculously kindled, kindles the Light of Universal Nature" (IV.4 ¶32), working "either externally or internally, morally or physically, or in all these ways, according to the application" (IV.4 ¶32). — *stated (¶39); interpreted (torch; Hierophanta applied); ATWOOD; Bishop of Alexandria.*

## Operator

1. **Study and instruction first; books alone do not give the practice; a guide or friend.** "nor would it be possible, we think, for any one to discover the Practice from their books alone" (IV.1 ¶3); "without the inspiration of Heaven, or without the help of a faithful friend, one remains undoubtedly in this labyrinth" (IV.3 ¶7). — *stated; ATWOOD; Eudoxus.*
2. **Knowledge of Nature in the three kingdoms, of the matter and of heat; the theory of the last stage, reached by one's own sight.** "a great understanding of the Laws of Nature, and of the qualities of Our Matter, as well as a perfect knowledge of chemistry and of the different degrees of heat" (IV.3 ¶16); "we ought to conduct ourselves rather by the polar star-light than by any footsteps" (IV.2 ¶4). — *stated; Eudoxus; ATWOOD.*
3. **Patience, toil, skill, vigilance, honesty about error; no haste.** "patience, toil, skill, unremitting attention, in the execution, and a free will to the discovery of error, without discordant slurring or disguise" (IV.1 ¶5); "And in the Gross Worke he is furthest behind, That dayly desireth the end thereof to find." (IV.1 ¶16). — *stated; ATWOOD; Norton.*
4. **Learn by doing and error; read books against one another; meditation and prayer.** "What was wrong taught me what was right, from day to day one book throwing light upon another, I was enabled to interpret them." (IV.1 ¶29); "pray daily, read good books, and meditate night and day on the operations of Nature" (IV.3 ¶16). — *stated; Khunrath, Eudoxus (endorsed).*
5. **Right intention from the start; the true Intention must be found; motive before machinery.** "The success of the experiment, as we have repeatedly shown, depends on the discovery of the true Intention." (IV.3 ¶54); "experiment is in need rather of a motive to dignify it than of practical machinery" (IV.4 ¶8). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
6. **Moral purification of the mind; purity without and within.** "all arrogant self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice, and variability of purpose, all but the one voluntary faith to rectify and perfect, be removed from the mind of him who is to enter into the radical dissolution of Life" (IV.1 ¶40); "In vain the ignorant, in vain the avaricious, the selfish, faithless, or frivolous, will in vain seek to percolate this Mystery." (IV.3 ¶54). — *stated; ATWOOD; also Scripture (IV.1 ¶39) and Norton (IV.1 ¶16), endorsed.*
7. **Self-denial and self-knowledge.** "the absolute Identification in theory is not conceived but by self-ablation and avoidance" (IV.2 ¶20); "It is by the searching and proving of His own Identity, not otherwise, that man can arrive at the assurance of this Wisdom" (IV.4 ¶27). — *stated; interpreted ("His" = man's); ATWOOD.*
8. **Grace, prayer and works; obedience to the Divine Will; the Blessing cannot be compelled.** "not by prayer and supplication alone, but by faithful and charitable works preparing the way before Him" (IV.1 ¶40); "man, searching in his own will alone, is incompetent to the Divine Discovery" (IV.4 ¶23). — *stated; ATWOOD; also Kelly (IV.1 ¶19), Eudoxus (IV.3 ¶16).*
9. **Faith directed by reason, with earnest desire; will and faith are what is lacking.** "faith is the very attracting loadstone which hope pursues, and desire and reason, and the whole willing armament of Mind" (IV.4 ¶19); "the will only is wanting to discover the way, and faith to resuscitate the Corner Stone" (IV.3 ¶37). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
10. **Rational inquiry into causes; seek only the First Cause; deliberate backward to the first means in one's power.** "till he discovers the first Medium which is immediately in his power, in the discovery of which inquiry terminates, and the work, beginning from thence, passes into accomplishment" (IV.1 ¶31); "let him search for the Root of Reason rationally, and hold by it, and conspire with It" (IV.4 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD; the philosopher (IV.1 ¶44, endorsed).*
11. **In the Gross Work: companions, assistants, cost.** "Fresh horses there are verily needed to this Celestial Ploughshare and laborious assistance for a toil that is incessant" (IV.1 ¶9); "Who would have trew worke may no labor spare, Neither yet his purse, though he make it full bare;" (IV.1 ¶16). — *stated; partners in a manual/mesmeric process interpreted (IV.1 ¶4, ¶11, ¶44); ATWOOD; Norton.*
12. **In the Subtle Work: hands off; keep the outward heat; the Artist prepares, cannot perform (IV.3 ¶38), must be inwardly alive.** "with the subject itself you have no more to do than the mother with child that is in her womb" (IV.2 ¶18); "which never can be done by one who is dead himself in his understanding and internal life" (IV.3 ¶38). — *stated; the Adept; the theosophist (endorsed).*
13. **At the end: conform oneself to the God received; invite by entreaty (IV.2 ¶39); then be silent; meditate with eyes closed (IV.3 ¶60).** "thou must prepare thyself till thou art conformable to Him whom thou wouldest entertain, and that in every way of similitude" (IV.2 ¶39); "quietude and a cessation of mental energy are becoming and necessary to the consummating knowledge of the First Cause" (IV.2 ¶33). — *stated; the philosopher; ATWOOD; UNCLEAR (IV.3 ¶60).*
14. **Charity, not gain (IV.3 ¶34, Helmont); humility; a lover of Wisdom for her own sake; a "congenial Sight" (IV.4 ¶32); few chosen (IV.1 ¶31), each alone.** "a true lover of Wisdom, who, for her sake, will devote everything else, studious, simple, ardent, and withal suscipient of appearing truth" (IV.4 ¶8); "each must labor singularly for himself." (IV.3 ¶54). — *stated; ATWOOD; Helmont (endorsed).*
15. **Discipline and inward fitness; humility.** "the reward only of peculiar and arduous discipline" (IV.4 ¶17); "he that hath the Key of David" (IV.3 ¶39, unexplained); "by humiliation under the exemplar cross of Christ" (IV.2 ¶17); friendship with Vulcan and "instituted the Fiery bath of Love" (IV.1 ¶40, Basil Valentine, unexplained). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED (Basil Valentine).*
16. **Astrological election (Norton).** "Whereof concord most kindly convenient Is a direct and Fiery ascendant" (IV.2 ¶34). — *stated; QUOTED: Norton; Atwood neither endorses nor rejects.*

## Procedure and stages

Three overlapping schemes: (A) Atwood's own (IV.1, IV.4); (B) the Subtle Work's three operations with a withheld link (IV.2); (C) Eudoxus' Six Keys plus Multiplication and Projection, declared not literal (IV.3). Order follows A; B and C placed where the text places them.

**Stage 0 — Prerequisites.** "a sufficient theory" (IV.1 ¶2); right intention (¶30); backward deliberation to the first means in one's power (¶31); study of the records (IV.3 ¶54). — *stated; ATWOOD.* Firm.

**Stage 1 — Entry: entrancing the senses (the vestibule).** "the tractive artifice now made publicly easy of entrancing the senses in their own medial light" (IV.1 ¶3); "by the hand or eye of another mesmerizing, or by a passive fixed gaze" (IV.1 ¶4); "a beginning only of the Hermetic art" (IV.1 ¶4); the Adepts "proceeded at once scientifically to purify" (IV.1 ¶4). Rank confirmed: Mesmerism "is a first step indeed, and this only before the entrance of that glorious temple of Divine Wisdom" (IV.3 ¶37); IV.4 ¶5 above. — *interpreted (that ¶4's "this" is ¶3's entrancing); otherwise stated; ATWOOD.* Firm as to rank.

**Stage 2 — Gross Work, first part: manual, linear purification by dissolution (First Operation / First Preparation / Khunrath's first act).**
- Dissolve the circulating body in its own blood; the beginning is a perfect solution. "depriving the ether of its wild affections and impressures by a dissolution of the circulating body in its own blood" (IV.1 ¶4); "The beginning of the whole work is a perfect solution" (IV.1 ¶4, Albertus); Arnold's parallel, "to dissolve the Stone in its own mercury" (IV.1 ¶7). — *interpreted (terms unexplained); ATWOOD; QUOTED (endorsed).*
- One way, Manual = Linear, one vessel, nothing mixed in. "called by the Adepts Manual, and their Linear way, which supersedes all other from beginning to end of the Dissolution" (IV.1 ¶5); "Beware of Commixtion that nothing miscape." (IV.1 ¶28). — *stated; ATWOOD; Norton.*
- Repeat without tiring; each repetition a death renewed; "pontically", hand to hand. "Grind, coct, says the wise author of the Rosarium, and reiterate your labor and be not weary" (IV.1 ¶7); "generated pontically, as it were by a reciprocal alternation, distributing its advanced virtue by hand to hand" (IV.1 ¶11). — *stated / interpreted ("pontically" undefined); ATWOOD; the Rosarium.*
- Slow, gentle, continuous; feed evenly; simmer, not boil (Norton, ¶12); bind the Matter, release it after its due time; drown the flying bird and draw off its corrupt redness repeatedly. "the trituration of the Philosophic Subject, which ought to be slow, gentle, and continuous" (IV.1 ¶13); "permit it not to fly suddenly, rather deliver it to Mercury, to be instructed by him gradually to accustom itself to flying; yea, bind it with a cord" (IV.1 ¶15, Basil); "Take the flying bird, says Hermes, and drown it flying" (IV.1 ¶15). — *stated (trituration); interpreted (rest); ATWOOD; QUOTED.*
- Literal labor on the matter as first found. "Neither would I have these things understood figuratively, continues he; I am not speaking here indeed of the commencement of the Supernatural Work, but of things as we first find them" (IV.1 ¶6). — *stated; Eirenaeus (endorsed).*
- Khunrath's first act, by hands and Fire: loosen Adrop; lure out and pierce the Green Lion; the mud earth and black Wine appear; the soul–body bond relaxes; the body dies, blackens, goes to Ashes. "the bond by which the soul is tied to the body and united together with it into one substance is relaxed and dissolved" (IV.1 ¶36). — *stated; Khunrath.*
- Inward counterpart: reflexive analysis by alternation; mind first, then hand. "That which analyzes even must be analyzed; that, returning analytically, it may resolve the separable Selfhood and reiterate the same by alternation until it arrives at the inseparable Unit of Truth." (IV.1 ¶20); "the hand is found best able to express and impart what the mind has well premeditated" (IV.1 ¶34). — *stated; parallel interpreted; ATWOOD.*
Firm that a manual, repeated dissolution comes first; the internal order merges authors the text does not align.

**Stage 3 — Gross Work, second part: Solution of the Philosophic Salt (Second Work), by Nature alone.** "By the Second Work we are to understand, therefore, the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond)" (IV.1 ¶36); "since hands and intellect are here alike incapable, and the truth of this discovery was never yet put to paper" (IV.1 ¶39); only God reveals it (IV.1 ¶39, Lully). Washed body, spirit and soul in one vessel, furnace and fire "rot of themselves and without manual cooperation, by the sole labors of nature, are dissolved, conjoined, and reunited" (IV.1 ¶36), then coagulate, color, calcine and fix; "In the Second, however, Nature alone operates, and without any imposition of hands, by the sole assistance of a well-regulated external fire" (IV.1 ¶11). By a specific virtue, not corrosives (¶39); wrong ways exist (the common tedious process; Ripley's alternate heat and cold); Lully did it in nine days with the secret "from God himself" (¶36). — *stated; ATWOOD; Lully, Khunrath, Eirenaeus, Van helmont; UNCLEAR (¶36 wrong ways).* Firm that a hands-off stage follows; whether still Gross or already Philosophic is open (Tension 1).

**Stage 3a — Circulation, ferments, vital heat (IV.1 ¶41–48).** Hold the subject in two elements at once (Vaughan, ¶41); "extracting water from the earth and returning it again, so long and so often until the earth is completely putrefied" (¶41, Morien); two works done apart, then reconciled (¶42, Eirenaeus). Atwood's reading: "by action and reaction raising successively actives by passives, and, vice versa, passives by actives, until the spiritual ability is complete." (¶41); "purified by successive interchanging of ferments, fretted, dissolved, and rectified into a consummate and immortal Form of Light" (¶46); the operations are acts of will, "By applying the proper voluntary corrosive they teach to obliterate its defilements; by gentleness to mollify its Durity; by beneficence to sweeten its acerbity; by justice to moderate its intensity" (¶47); reason penetrates the understanding till "lucid throughout, and to be no longer what it was before" (¶48). Eirenaeus' recipe (Fiery Dragon and Lodestone, "purge thrice with Salt and with Fire", ¶43) and Democritus' firing of the fixed Brass till Paphia is "entirely Golden" (¶43) picture "Which Vulcanic action, to destroy life and to maintain it" (¶43). — *stated (Atwood's readings); interpreted (the recipes); ATWOOD; QUOTED.*

**Stage 4 — Threshold: the Philosophic Work begins only when all is pure without and within.** "it is not until the right Motive is discovered, and until the mundification of the Spirit is completed in both kinds, and all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity, that the Philosophic Work has been said truly to being" (IV.1 ¶48); "The philosopher’s worke doe not begin Till all things be pure without and within" (IV.1 ¶49). Duration: three years for the Gross Work "were a blessed chance" (¶16); the periods refer chiefly to the Second Operation and are "often to be understood metaphorically with respect to the discovery of the philosophic Salt" (¶17). — *stated; ATWOOD; Norton.* Firm.

**Stage 5 — Philosophic or Subtle Work: three operations, degrees of the Spirit from conception to manifestation.**
- The "fixed principles": "you must make the conversion of the Elements, the separation and the reunion of the Three Principles" (IV.2 ¶6), a white then a citrine Mercury, "nourish it with its own blood, to the end that it may be converted into the fixed Sulphur, which is the Stone of Philosophers" (¶6). — *stated; The Triumph (endorsed by ATWOOD).*
- The secret Fire disposes Nature to give up her last life; a Divine Law revives her "by a revolutionary course"; the Quintessence is congealed "into an incombustible fixed unguent"; "by the entire revolution of its body, to awaken the occult Light to Life" (all ¶6). — *stated; UNCLEAR voice.*
- Three fires lit quickly, a Fourth supervening; three vessels of decreasing opacity, the third "containing truly Him who is to be born" (¶11). — *stated; the Adept; UNCLEAR.*
- Water stage, then Fire/Light stage: the forerunner baptizes "prior to the Divine Light" (¶12); "Whom to seeke it availeth right nought, Till the white medicine be fully wrought." (¶13, Norton); one vessel and working "Till all quick things be made dead" (¶13), then vessel and method change; Water baptism, then "the Holy Ghost and with Fire" (¶15). — *elements stated; ordering interpreted; the Adepts, Norton, St Matthew.*
- The Master's sequence: dissolve with the Water; with the watery soul take a sulphurous Form and mix with "our Acetum"; pour fire on the Foliated Sulphur; "the boundary of hearts (i.e., the Final Cause) does enter in above it, and is washed in the same, and the mortal matter thereof is extracted" (¶14); the Son-King is "transformed in his Tincture" (¶14). — *stated; the Master.*
- Atwood's register: do not let the Duad generate directly; "by carrying the circulation upward through a Third principle for reprobation, it is rectified, overcome in its proper volition, and dying (if the divine rule be thenceforth followed), is raised again by reversion" (¶20); "reduce the Ternary by the Quaternary, through the rejection of the Binary, to the simplicity of the Monad" (¶18, the Theosophist); it "ascends from earth to heaven, and again descends to earth" (¶44, Smaragdine Tablet). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED (endorsed).*
- Freed Will and Understanding attract like steel and magnet; agent and patient "must be united upon the throne of friendship and love" (¶30); the Spirit rises by rotary circulation "from its beginning in voluntary indigence" (¶32) till its light "is brought forth to sight" (¶32); "the Stone of the Wise is completed in three successive circulations" (¶33); "by a Triplicate growth of Light in the Understanding, becoming consciously allied, It emanates a Fourth Form" (¶18). — *stated; that the threefold passages are one process is interpreted; ATWOOD; the Adept; steel/loadstone simile voice UNCLEAR.*
- "Thou shalt separate the Earth from the fire, the subtle from the gross, gently, with much sagacity." (¶44); "The power of it is integral, if it be turned into Earth." (¶44). — *stated; Smaragdine Tablet (endorsed).*
- Bloomfield: join "The Fixt, the Variable, and the Fugitive --- Till they together taste death and live." (¶36); the Dragon slays the other two; sixty days of blackness; Phoebus with strange colours. Vaughan: two sublimations, "a double nativity, visible and invisible" (¶35). — *stated; QUOTED.*
- A step withheld: "our willful reservation of an important link in the application of these principles to practice" (¶45). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
Firm as pattern (three operations, then a Fourth); internal sequence merges unaligned authors.

**Stage 5, laboratory register — Eudoxus' Six Keys; Multiplication and Projection (IV.3 ¶2–26).**
- Three analogous works; in each "the Wise Artist ought to dissolve the body with the spirit; he must cut off the Raven's head, whiten the Black, and vivify the White" (¶3). Keys 1–3 in the First Work, the Fourth "the entrance to the Second Work" (¶12), the Fifth the Third Work (¶14); ¶9 complicates this (Tension 7).
- First Key: free the Sulphur from its "dark prisons" (¶3), extract the seed, join spirit with body, separate the black fume; ends "at this shining whiteness" (¶3). Second Key: sublimation by the Secret Fire, converting the elements, dissolving the Stone into Water after "a proportioned digestion and putrefaction" (¶5). Third Key (the longest, most concealed): distil off the Water, keep the black earth, "wash it exactly with its spirit, to extract out of it the Fixed Salt" (¶7); "moisten oftentimes the earth with its water" (¶7); dissolve the body entirely, wash the residue till the blackness goes, animate the water with the Fiery Essence (¶8); whiten the faeces, extract the white sulphur; "the affusion of the water on the body, reiterated many times, impregnates, fattens, and fertilizes our Mercury, and makes it fit to be fixed" (¶9); make the two Mercuries, kept apart, and the King's bath with the blood of the Innocents, giving the animated Mercury (¶10). Fourth Key: "The terrification of the Spirit is the only operation of this work." (¶12), by patient boiling and repeated moistening and drying. Fifth Key: "the Fermentation of the Stone with the perfect body, to make thereof the medicine of the Third order" (¶14), in bread-making proportions. Sixth Key: multiplication by "opening and shutting, dissolving and coagulating, imbibing and drying" (¶16); Projection omitted "because the success you expect depends not thereon" (¶16).
- Atwood's supplement: Multiplication (one part Perfect Matter to three or four of the First Work's Mercury, sealed, gentle fire, "pass through rapidly in seven days", ¶18, faster each round); "a twofold fermentation --- a spiritual and a bodily" (¶21); Sendivogius' process through the colours to the iron test (¶21); Projection "by degrees until the Stone yields no more tincture" (¶20). Then: "even supposing the whole of the assertions to be true (and which literally taken they are not)" (¶26). — *stated; Eudoxus, E. Philalethes, Lucerna Salis, Eirenaeus, Vaughan, Sendivogius; ATWOOD.*

**Stage 6 — Fermentation as union; vital rectification; the final step.**
- Fermentation, the last operation, cements the Stone's parts and relates it to the world; it is "the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God" (IV.3 ¶27). The Stone is fermented with its own metal (Light), "the work of three days" (¶29); the Spirit "so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined, until the central force, wholly drawn out, acts forcibly at the circumference" (¶29). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶27, ¶30); voice of the ¶29 "three days" sentence UNCLEAR (Khunrath n9 or Atwood); centre/circumference reading interpreted.*
- Vital rectification: "when the soul gives herself to Divinity, and wholly delivers herself to the guidance of a better nature, divine institutions, through purification, taking the lead, the Divine Light will immediately shine on her" (¶30, Julian); the light fire "by its shining through the wrath, must subdue and keep it under" (¶38); "Earth must be turned in, and Heaven out." (¶38); "Line upon line, Precept upon precept, (purifying and perfecting) here a little and there a little" (¶39). — *stated; that Julian's sequence is what Atwood means by "the vital rectification" is interpreted; "Earth turned in" interpreted; Julian, the theosophist (endorsed); ATWOOD.*
- Discipline and ordeal, then liberation; "Once delivered from the exterior bondages of sense and heterogeneous desire, from the passions and false affections of this transitory life, the final step is declared comparatively easy" (¶60); "transcending by the energy of faith, from the separable selfhood, the Identity passes into universal accord" (¶60); "To go forth and to return" (¶60). — *stated; ATWOOD; UNCLEAR (Kabalah).*
- IV.4's compressed statement: from the free Ether through elaboration to effect (¶2); enter "experimentally within" (¶4); "bear the life within him through dissolution into a new birth" (¶12); excavate the Salt and exalt it by circulating media (¶20); the faculties restore "their King" (¶20); "returning again, however, into the Universal and abandoning the selfhood" (¶27); "He who knows the first entrance, and how to render the fixed tincture of life volatile, and to return it, being free" (¶32) is admitted to the whole, since "through all the interior recesses, the method is allied" (¶32). — *stated (¶2, ¶12, ¶27); interpreted (¶20, ¶32); ATWOOD.*
Fairly firm that the work ends in union with God, called Fermentation (IV.3 ¶27, ¶62; IV.4 ¶32); that IV.2's illumination (¶41–43) is the same final stage is interpreted (Tension 5); the "three days" and "three circulations" are not related to the Keys.

## Signs

1. **Simmering, not boiling; cleansing takes longer than expected.** "To know the simperings of our Stone, Till it may no more simper do, nor cease," (IV.1 ¶12). — *interpreted; Norton.*
2. **Blackness, the desired sign; the black Wine "blacker than black" (IV.1 ¶36); the body to ashes; about sixty days (IV.2 ¶36).** "this blackness so desired by all the philosophers" (IV.3 ¶8). — *stated; Eudoxus, Khunrath, Bloomfield ("mourning sable" as blackness interpreted).*
3. **Putrefaction; absent blackness or coagulation proves earlier failure.** "to lose its Form, and for a season to remain dead" (IV.1 ¶41); "if they appear not in their time, they will never appear" (IV.3 ¶12). — *stated; Morien, Eudoxus.*
4. **Whiteness, then colours, then gold.** "at this shining whiteness, that the work of the First Key is ended" (IV.3 ¶3); "until colors appear like a peacock’s tail" (IV.3 ¶21), then green, then fiery ashes and red water (¶21); "Then shall Phoebus appear first out, With strange colours in all the Firmament" (IV.2 ¶36); the principles "diversifiedly colored" (IV.1 ¶36); Paphia "entirely Golden" (IV.1 ¶43). — *stated; Eudoxus, Sendivogius, Bloomfield, Khunrath; Democritus' Paphia interpreted.*
5. **Thickening water; viscous "ponticity"; spirit and body ascending together.** "When I saw that the water by degrees did become thicker and harder I did rejoice" (IV.3 ¶9); "ascend insensibly together, leaving but a little faeces in the bottom of their vessel" (IV.3 ¶7). — *stated; Hermes via Eudoxus.*
6. **Green Duenech and Vitriol of Venus appear once Adrop is loosened** (IV.1 ¶36). — *stated; Khunrath.*
7. **Darkness and waters withdraw; the dead Son lives; the King from the Fire.** "darkness and the waters flee away. The Dragon shuns the sunbeams which dart through the crevices, our dead Son lives" (IV.2 ¶14). — *interpreted as sign; the Master.*
8. **Hidden light made visible; sudden illumination.** "until its substance, being replenished, it is brought forth to sight" (IV.2 ¶32); "we have seen Him, says Plotinus, when the Soul receives a sudden Light" (IV.2 ¶41); then (Latin, our translation) fire, wind, power, knowledge, a sound mind (¶41). — *stated; ATWOOD; Plotinus, the philosopher.*
9. **Crystalline transparency as readiness; a forerunning apparition; the Sign of Omnipotence.** "all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity" (IV.1 ¶48); "who has foreshown all things in his apparition to the wise" (IV.2 ¶12); "the Central Pole extravasated and bearing the notable Sign of Omnipotence" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated (condition); interpreted (sign); ATWOOD; the Adepts.*
10. **Each stage opens new prospects; Fire tries the work.** "it will solve itself at every stage, opening into new prospects within the veil of life" (IV.1 ¶44); "the Fire shall try every man’s work of what sort it is" (IV.1 ¶46); "Some have met the Light sooner, some later" (IV.1 ¶17). — *stated (¶44, ¶17); interpreted (¶46 applied to the work); ATWOOD; the apostle.*
11. **In multiplication colours run faster; in projection instant penetration; the medicine not used up.** "every regiment of the colors will in that brief space be passed through" (IV.3 ¶18); "That never shall thy stock with use be spent," (IV.3 ¶25). — *stated; Open Entrance, Eirenaeus.*
12. **Signs in the person: powers and temptations revealed to conscience (IV.4 ¶9); desire made prolific in the "magic region" (IV.4 ¶10); the whole person acting by rule and with power (IV.3 ¶39); the Spirit drawing its own kind; Nature opening her resources (IV.4 ¶32); gifts as tokens (IV.3 ¶46).** "every desire is, in the magic region, made prolific, embodying itself, by the ethereal conception, as a principle to enact its voluntary accord" (IV.4 ¶10); draws "by a supernatural magnetism, the Homogeneal Nature everywhere into itself" (IV.3 ¶29). — *stated (¶10, ¶29, ¶46); interpreted (as signs); ATWOOD; the disciple.*
13. **End-state.** "is confirmed in operation, visibility, and luminous increase, when rising in rational supremacy over sense and finite reflection, the Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause" (IV.3 ¶62). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
14. **Minor signs.** Distillation like vinegar, phlegm first (IV.3 ¶8, Eudoxus); humidity increasing under philosophic calcination (IV.3 ¶16, Eudoxus); the purging made easy "if Saturn have chanced to regard his beauty in the glass of Mars" (IV.1 ¶43, Eirenaeus, unexplained); "signal marks" beyond the sea of sense (IV.4 ¶32, ATWOOD). — *stated / interpreted as signs.*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Stopping at first phenomena; drugs instead of the mesmeric entry** ("by influxion of darkness, drowns the oxygenating spirit, prostrates and confounds the mental powers", IV.1 ¶4). — *stated / interpreted; ATWOOD.*
2. **Haste and overshoot; over-excitation splitting the Chaos.** "Excess of one half quarter of an hour May destroy all" (IV.1 ¶12); "lest the centers should be stirred up before the circumferences are ready to conceive them" (IV.1 ¶13); the Chaos split and "the two Principles stand up one against the other, before the intended mastery is secure" (IV.1 ¶15). — *stated; Norton; ATWOOD; Basil (Icarus, ¶15).*
3. **Incomplete or interrupted dissolution; discarding the heavy part.** "before the total solution of her enigma, the Sphinx should retrieve her dominion unawares and frustrate the work begun" (IV.1 ¶7); "persuade themselves that they must only take the subtile and cast away the heavy" (IV.3 ¶7); without full dissolution, no blackness and no coagulation (IV.3 ¶12). — *interpreted / stated; ATWOOD; Eudoxus.*
4. **Violent fire, corrosives, common calcination (IV.3 ¶16); mixing the two Mercuries (IV.3 ¶10); premature fixing.** "if any permanent confection is made or suffered to take place beforehand, the immature offspring does not abide" (IV.1 ¶48). — *stated; Eudoxus; ATWOOD.*
5. **Nature's intermediate fruits are lures.** "Nature halts many times before this final rest, at each stage offering the fruits of her conceptive imagination to allure" (IV.1 ¶46). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
6. **The Brazen Wall; Herculean labors after the first barriers; the Gross Work foul and full of faults (IV.1 ¶25–28).** "that Brazen Wall celebrated by Antiquity, which surrounds our Heaven and must be scaled, and passed through" (IV.1 ¶4); "greater obstacles await him, and Herculean labors, who dares, approaching to the Nether confines, to make choice of Light" (IV.1 ¶25). — *interpreted (referent of "this"); ATWOOD; Norton.*
7. **Lack of the theory (IV.1 ¶7), of the Secret Fire, of the ultimate theory.** "faltered in defect of the ultimate theory whereon to proceed" (IV.2 ¶4); "spend fifteen years entirely in the work, in study and in meditation, without being able to extract out of the Stone the precious juice" (IV.3 ¶16). — *stated; Urbigeranus; ATWOOD; Eudoxus.*
8. **The willful confusion of the records; literal readings (IV.4 ¶29); reading alone.** "The willful confusion of the Hermetic doctrine has doubtless checked many aspirants" (IV.2 ¶4); "when one imagines that they speak of one work, they often treat of another" (IV.3 ¶3). — *stated; ATWOOD; Eudoxus.*
9. **The Duad generating directly; aiming at any end but the First Cause.** "if in the Duad the divine Idea were suffered immediately to bring forth, an imperfect offspring would result" (IV.2 ¶20); "will be the fruit of the Fall only" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
10. **Faults in the operator; gain and ambition withdraw the gift; the operator's sin passes into the work.** "after that the study of ambition and gain were practiced, charity grew cold, mercy was extinguished, art perished, and the Giver of Lights withdrew his Gifts" (IV.3 ¶34); "lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring" (IV.3 ¶56); pride, avarice, ambition, ignorance refusing "the subaltern station which the Divine Law assigns to them" (IV.4 ¶23). — *stated; Helmont (endorsed); ATWOOD.*
11. **Seeking gold; private ends; self-idolatry; pride in success (IV.3 ¶57).** "rest in a mere metalline good, like Midas" (IV.3 ¶48); "who abandons the Divine purpose for the accomplishment of private ends" (IV.3 ¶56); "with every temptation of self-idolatry laid open to view" (IV.4 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD; Deuteronomy (by analogy).*
12. **Externals, sense, modern science; the body and a scattered mind.** "every advance we make in externals leads us further off from the First Source" (IV.4 ¶18); "the earthly tabernacle weigheth down that mind that museth upon many things." (IV.2 ¶39). — *stated; ATWOOD; Solomon.*
13. **Incredulity (wisely provided); believing the work impious.** "Incredulity is the strongest barrier of possibilities over the world, no doubt wisely provided to prevent a too rapid movement of mind into practice, before it is well prepared" (IV.4 ¶19). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
14. **The highest knowledge cannot be held in reflective personality; becoming "dividual".** "we awaken, by reflection, into that created personality wherein it is impossible any longer to know Him" (IV.2 ¶33, Schelling); "in becoming dividual, he ceases to be the Universe" (IV.4 ¶27). — *stated; Schelling (IV.2 ¶33); ATWOOD ("dividual" as impediment interpreted).*
15. **Hasty trial; the withheld link; the device passed by mouth only; an "ominous" association of Vulcan with Minerva at the Altar (IV.1 ¶34, unexplained).** "we would deter all from hasty trial" (IV.2 ¶45); "communicating almost by word of mouth only the practical device" (IV.4 ¶7). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
16. **Further hindrances.** Fires quenching each other: "lest one should put out the other, or that this should stifle that" (IV.2 ¶11, the Adept); contrary astrology: "Unless that your nativity pretend infection" (IV.2 ¶34, Norton); "the natural periods are protracted by faulty conditions from the commencement" (IV.1 ¶17); ordinary chemistry: "Chemistry and all our experimental physics drive it forcibly without the means of Identification" (IV.1 ¶47); "it is wholly unsuited to minds commonly so styled practical" (IV.4 ¶8); foreign admixture: "without heterogeneous hindrance or alloy" (IV.3 ¶30); "the mutative and erroneous motion of the spirit" (IV.3 ¶30, Julian); impure receiving metals in projection (IV.3 ¶20, ¶24). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED as named.*

## Result

1. **Of the Gross Work: the cleansed "double" Argent vive (IV.1 ¶10); the Ashes; a renewed World (IV.1 ¶36); the Chaos holding all Arcana in potential; never repeated (IV.1 ¶28).** "they are the diadem of thy body; in them lies our pigmy, conquering and subduing giants." (IV.1 ¶36); "our Chaos, in which all the Arcana are contained; not in act as yet, but in virtue" (IV.1 ¶43). — *stated / interpreted; the Adept, Khunrath, Eirenaeus, Norton.*
2. **The Stone: a petrification of the Spirit; an immortal Form of Light; a crown of light worn as a new body.** "a consummate and immortal Form of Light" (IV.1 ¶46); "wherein it can rule over the elementary world and pass through it, overcoming evil and falsehood, and ignorance and death" (IV.4 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
3. **Laboratory results, qualified as not literal: a nobler new substance, the animated Mercury, the fertile earth, a leaven multiplying to infinity, fixed Sulphur and Universal Medicine, the shining fiery liquid and the Arabian Elixir (IV.3 ¶7–28).** "the universal medium of restoration and preservation" (¶28). Atwood: the object "was not gold however, or silver, but the substance of these things" (¶26), itself "swallowed up in the vision of a yet more alluring prospect of the Light" (¶26). — *stated; Eudoxus, Vaughan, Khunrath; qualified by ATWOOD.*
4. **Understanding made lucid; speculation carried into life; the self overthrown; consciousness in Light.** "that which before lay in speculation only is carried out in Life" (IV.1 ¶48); "by an actual subversion of the selfhood, they made their Sublimate sublime" (IV.1 ¶47); "center meeting center, the consciousness transcends in revolutionary Light" (IV.1 ¶33). — *stated / interpreted; ATWOOD.*
5. **Regeneration: the Good; a supernatural offspring (Horus); the Son-King tinctured through death; the Fourth Form; the true metempsychosis; Seven, the rest of God.** "perceiving itself indeed to be the Final Object of its own First Cause, is good" (IV.2 ¶8); "Our Son the King takes his Tincture from the fire and death even" (IV.2 ¶14); "attain to that supremacy of Reason which re-enters to its First Cause" (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated; ATWOOD; the Master, the Adept.*
6. **Illumination, the sum of the Mystery.** "But when illuminated, it has That which is sought for; and the Thought and understanding are in the experience One." (IV.2 ¶41); "That was the sum of the Hermetic Mystery, and the ultimate object of the Alchemical Art to accomplish" (IV.2 ¶43). — *stated; UNCLEAR (¶41); ATWOOD (scope open).*
7. **Union with God; the circle closed; co-operation with the First Cause.** "as man, united with God, becomes divinely empowered, and therefore can do what he wills" (IV.3 ¶27); "The line returns to form a circle into its’ beginning, and they join not in time, for their union is Eternity." (IV.3 ¶62); "he co-operates with the First Cause" (IV.4 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
8. **Vital rectification; the power to heal and pass on the gift; matter redeemed; the seeker transmuted.** "above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification" (IV.3 ¶30); "to redeem matter from the curse, and to raise it from the dead" (IV.3 ¶38); "transmute their worshipper into that Harmony and Beauty" (IV.3 ¶50). — *stated; ATWOOD; the theosophist (endorsed).*
9. **Wisdom above all knowledge; to find and effect the Divine Nature; knowing the First Truth experimentally; having the All (IV.4 ¶27); Reason immortalized, the faculties renewed under "the triumphant Monarchy of Light" (IV.4 ¶20); the Catholic Effect of Life, where inner and outer worlds blend (IV.4 ¶11); riches as Wisdom's dowry (IV.4 ¶10).** "that man should be able not only to find the Divine Nature, but to effect It" (IV.4 ¶10); "to know the First Truth of things really and experimentally, and to revolve the Causal Light in permanence of intellection" (IV.4 ¶22). — *stated; ATWOOD (riches' literalness open).*
10. **Further results.** "From preparation arises knowledge, even such as opens all the fundamentals of Alchemy and Medicine" (IV.1 ¶2, the Monk Basil); "the equilibriate felicity of Being within" (IV.1 ¶4); purified bodies and spirits "never quit one another when they are united together" (IV.3 ¶10, Hermes via Eudoxus); the soul changed "wholly into the Essence of God" (IV.2 ¶33, Trismegistus); a new birth "superior to nature and beyond the reach of elementary discord to destroy" (IV.4 ¶12); "is already admitted to the temple of Divine Science, and joins in with the whole conclave" (IV.4 ¶32, interpreted). — *stated unless marked; ATWOOD; QUOTED as named.*

## Principles

1. **Learned by seeing and doing (IV.1 ¶2); theory from practice; manual operation the ballast (IV.1 ¶2); experience the only evidence.** "The working theory, as we long ago suggested, can be obtained through the practice only" (IV.1 ¶18); "the evidence is self-sufficient, and without itself cannot, however truly imaginable, be known" (IV.4 ¶12). — *stated; the Monk Basil (endorsed); ATWOOD.*
2. **One method and one matter throughout; all is dissolution and coagulation; what generation binds dissolution unlooses.** "through all the interior recesses, the method is allied" (IV.4 ¶32); "That which generation binds dissolution only can unloose" (IV.4 ¶17). — *stated / interpreted; ATWOOD.*
3. **Fixed and volatile act on each other; actives and passives alternate; intermediates used, then discarded.** "volatilized and fixed reciprocally into a perfect Fixity" (IV.3 ¶9, Geber); "though intermediates apply themselves naturally for the generation, they are rejected in the accomplishment" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; Geber via Eudoxus (endorsed); ATWOOD.*
4. **Make haste slowly; the end depends on the beginning; everything on the Motive.** "Gutta cavat lapidem non vi sed saepe cadendo." (IV.1 ¶14); "The end depends from the beginning" (IV.1 ¶46); "according to the virtue of the fermenting principle is the result obtained." (IV.1 ¶44). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
5. **Nature's laws are the rules; the Artist only lends a hand (IV.3 ¶9); nothing discarded; power integral only when turned into earth.** "The laws of Nature are the rules you ought to follow in the practice of our magistery." (IV.3 ¶14); "power of it is not integral until it be turned into earth" (IV.3 ¶7). — *stated; Eudoxus, Hermes.*
6. **The two keys: matter and fire; fire becomes body in its kin (IV.3 ¶16); Fire and Azoth suffice.** "if you know the Stone which is the only matter of Our Stone, and if you have the Understanding of Our Fire, which is both secret and natural, you have the Keys of the Art" (IV.3 ¶16); "Ignis et Azoth tibi sufficient" (IV.2 ¶13). — *stated; Eudoxus; Hermes via Norton.*
7. **Only Light multiplies; the Stone exhibits the law of Light; multiplication by reiteration.** "their Stone exhibits, alike in its origin, development and artificial preparation throughout, the Universal Law of Light in Nature" (IV.3 ¶27); "augmented each time by ten degrees in perfection" (IV.3 ¶24). — *stated; ATWOOD; Lucerna Salis.*
8. **Transmutation acts on the Universal Subject, not species.** "relating not to Species but to their Universal Subject, whose concentrated virtue the Stone likewise itself is" (IV.4 ¶2). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
9. **One parallel process in soul and nature; the Work threefold as Nature (IV.2 ¶3); the magistery a type of the Creed; the Mysteries continued in Christianity (IV.2 ¶17); as above, so below (IV.2 ¶44).** "by a parallel process in each" (IV.3 ¶38); "in every deed a type, and promise, and foundation of our Christian Creed" (IV.3 ¶27). — *stated; the theosophist, Smaragdine Tablet (endorsed); ATWOOD.*
10. **Theory and practice correspond; universals in the mind as ground; Wisdom transcends reason without contradicting it; true science is Causal Discovery.** "neither is Nature reduced to the Monadic simplicity of her Element but by rejection of her Binary conception" (IV.2 ¶20); "neither can anything short of the Causal Discovery fulfill the Idea of Truth" (IV.4 ¶13). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
11. **Grace and Divine Will: not forced, not transmitted without it; the Art a remedy, salvation by the Divine Will; immortality only in union and surrender of self-will.** "cannot of himself (or in alliance, unless he dare a deadly sin), compel the Divine Blessing without its free accord" (IV.1 ¶40); "the Divine Will operates its fulfillment alone" (IV.4 ¶25); "For immortality is only to be consummated in union, and in the abandonment of the dual selfhood and its volition to the Divine Will." (IV.3 ¶58). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
12. **Only a vital power reaches inbred evil; knowledge without inner participation is empirical; Alchemy "the only true glass of the mind" (IV.4 ¶12).** "no power that is not vital can touch the evil that is inbred, or even discover it truly without introspective proof" (IV.4 ¶17); "nor shall we ever learn, without Identic co-operation, how Nature works" (IV.4 ¶12). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
13. **Health is equilibrium from inner unity.** "health is a certain symmetry or equilibrium of nature, which it is the pre-eminent power of the Telestic art to impart, ordering all things by the unity within" (IV.3 ¶30). — *stated; Julian.*
14. **Veiled texts read as veiled; the language is the Art; deliberate concealment (IV.3 ¶54).** "They, we repeat, who can understand the language of the philosophers, will understand their Art" (IV.4 ¶32); "we have opened the way only, which if any one will consent to travel in, we assure him of success, but not otherwise" (IV.4 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD.*
15. **The Salt's solution is the Gordian Knot; its untier "named, openly".** "We have already named him, and openly; but this Light shining everywhere in Darkness, how hardly should it be comprehensible without Itself?" (IV.1 ¶37). — *interpreted (who); ATWOOD.*
16. **Further principles.** The Art is the offspring "of involved thought" (IV.1 ¶18); Intellect brings itself into effect "by begetting a supernatural offspring" (IV.2 ¶7); "The ethereal humidity nourishes the Solar Light" (IV.1 ¶29, Nemean Lion, interpreted); "the Art is said to be a remedy of imperfect souls" (IV.4 ¶25, reported). — *stated unless marked; ATWOOD.*

## Tensions and open questions

1. **Alignment of the stage-divisions.** IV.1: Eirenaeus' First Operation vs Second (Nature alone) and "Supernatural Work"; Vaughan's First Preparation vs "Second part of the Gross Work"; Khunrath's first act vs Second Operation; "the Philosophic Work" beginning only after mundification (¶48). IV.2: three operations of the Philosophic Work. IV.3: three works over Six Keys. (A) The Gross Work has two parts, manual then hands-off Solution of the Salt, and the Philosophic Work with its own three operations follows (IV.1 ¶36, ¶39, ¶48). (B) The hands-off "Second Operation" is already the Supernatural/Philosophic Work (IV.1 ¶6, ¶48; IV.2 ¶18 "not manually"). IV.1 ¶17 favours A. Nothing aligns Eudoxus' three works with either.
2. **Literal handwork vs inward work.** IV.1 insists on the literal Hand and a mesmeric entry; IV.2 says "Proceed then patiently but not manually." (¶18) and Mind alone builds; IV.3 gives a full regimen then says "literally taken they are not" (¶26); IV.4 makes man the laboratory and the torch work "morally or physically". (A) A literal manual/mesmeric process between persons for the Gross Work, the later work inward (IV.1 ¶4, ¶9, ¶11, ¶17, ¶44; IV.3 ¶37). (B) "Hand" stands throughout for the soul's instrument of putting thought into act (IV.1 ¶5, ¶34; IV.2 ¶34). Atwood withholds "an important link in the application of these principles to practice" (IV.2 ¶45).
3. **What the Salt is.** "the voluntary bond" (IV.1 ¶36); Root of Reason = Wisdom (IV.4 ¶20); Fixed Salt = Blood of the Stone from the black earth (IV.3 ¶7); a menstruum that calcines gold (IV.2 ¶30). (a) One Salt, the bond of will/selfhood, its solution the removal of self-will (IV.1 ¶40, ¶31; IV.3 ¶58). (b) Khunrath's soul–body bond (IV.1 ¶36). (c) Distinct Salts in distinct registers. No cross-chapter identity asserted.
4. **Mind vs Light as "the Key".** IV.1 ¶48 against IV.4 ¶32. Either Mind made lucid is the Light (IV.1 ¶48; IV.2 ¶41), or two Keys for two stages. Not stated.
5. **The sum of the Mystery: illumination alone or the whole sequence?** IV.2 ¶43 follows the illumination passage but also names "analysis and pure synthesis of vital agencies"; IV.3 ¶62 calls the closed circle "the Hermetic method and its end".
6. **Universal Nature or the operator's own nature?** IV.1 ¶36 "of our very selves"; IV.2 ¶6 Nature's "sacrifice of her last life"; IV.4 ¶6 Nature "operating voluntarily about her own annihilation" vs ¶12 "bear the life within him through dissolution into a new birth". Subtle Nature (IV.4 ¶12) and Universal Subject (¶10) not equated. The theosophist's "man, in the first place" (IV.3 ¶38) is the only ordering given.
7. **Eudoxus' Keys and Works.** ¶5 Second Key in the First Work; ¶12 Fourth Key entrance to the Second; ¶14 Fifth in the Third; but ¶9, inside the Third Key, has "the end of the second Work" and "in the first work purify the Serpent". The Third Key straddles the Works, or "Work" is loose; ¶3 warns of exactly this.
8. **Are the fires one?** Secret Fire (IV.2 ¶6; IV.3 ¶5), "Our Fire" (IV.3 ¶7, ¶16, ¶21), Red of Magic Fire (¶10), fire of putrefaction distinct from the first (¶21), three fires and a Fourth (IV.2 ¶11), Lunar Vulcan (IV.1 ¶29), "a certain other Vulcan" (IV.1 ¶40), Vaughan's vital heat (IV.1 ¶41), the life-fire of man (IV.4 ¶4). None identified with another. Atwood's cross-chapter clue: the fire is in man's life (IV.4 ¶4) and the prepared subject is itself the interior Fire (IV.1 ¶29).
9. **The recurring Fourth and three.** Fourth cause (IV.2 ¶7–8), Fourth over the fires (¶11), "the Fourth in that Burning Fiery Furnace" (¶12), Fourth Form (¶18), "the Fourth, as the artist" (¶32), Fourth Monarchy (IV.3 ¶49), Quaternary (IV.2 ¶18); three circulations (IV.2 ¶33), three days (IV.3 ¶29), Maria's three hours (IV.2 ¶31), three works (IV.3 ¶3). A three-then-fourth pattern is plain; identities not stated.
10. **Who is "named, openly" (IV.1 ¶37)?** The Light; Cheiron or Vulcan; God who alone reveals; someone named earlier. IV.4 ¶32 supports the first but is not linked.
11. **The pairs.** Salt/Sulphur/Mercury to spirit/soul/body (IV.3 ¶7, order open); Vaughan's two elements (IV.1 ¶41), the two works (¶42), actives/passives (¶41), wrath fire / light fire (IV.3 ¶38), the Binary (IV.2 ¶20), "both kinds" (IV.1 ¶48). The text always works with a pair to be reconciled or rejected and never aligns the names.
12. **"Patients" and "agents" (IV.1 ¶17; IV.3 ¶56; IV.2 ¶30).** Persons, matter and operator, or a generic pairing. Bears on Tension 2.
13. **Literal or figurative riches, metals, medicine.** IV.4 ¶10 "dowry", "intermediate benefits"; IV.3 ¶26 promises not literal, the object "the substance of these things"; IV.3 ¶24 "(let not the allusion here be misapprehended)"; IV.3 ¶48 results "in plain physical type and experience". Physical results are asserted and the literal reading denied in the same chapter, without saying what the physical type is.
14. **Mesmerism's rank and continuation.** IV.1 ¶3–4 gives mesmeric entrancing as the public form of the entry; IV.3 ¶37 and IV.4 ¶5 confine it to a first step. Whether the ancients' "more scientific Handicraft" (IV.3 ¶37) continues the same manual art (IV.1 ¶5) is not said.
15. **Astrological election** (IV.2 ¶34): quoted at length, neither endorsed nor rejected.
16. **Textual and voice doubts at the hinges.** Probable misprints: "important" (IV.1 ¶4), "including" (IV.1 ¶5), "fives" (IV.2 ¶32), "out Mercury" (IV.3 ¶20), "holt" (IV.4 ¶25); "ether" used for both anaesthetic and medium (IV.1 ¶4). Voice: who states the IV.2 ¶6 requirements; whether "(i.e., the Final Cause)" (IV.2 ¶14) and the steel/loadstone gloss (IV.2 ¶30) are Atwood's; the extent of the theosophist's words (IV.3 ¶38); whether the fermentation sentence (IV.3 ¶29) and the eyes-closed meditation (IV.3 ¶60) are Atwood's; whose nature is annihilated (IV.4 ¶6); what the "wards", the "catholic torch" and "the fixed tincture of life" are (IV.4 ¶32).
17. **Further local open points.** "A certain other Vulcan" (IV.1 ¶40): the operator's inner fire/motion (¶34, ¶40 gloss) or the art's fire (colours "which proceed from the Fire"). Which spirit is "this Argent vive" (IV.1 ¶10): the tinging Second Spirit or the Preparing Spirit. The circulating body "in its own blood" (IV.1 ¶4) and the Stone "in its own mercury" (IV.1 ¶7): one operation or two. Osiris fable (IV.2 ¶6–7): mapped to stages or general meaning only. Ternary/Quaternary (IV.2 ¶18): body, soul, spirit vs divine Ternarius and four elements. Bloomfield's Phoebus (IV.2 ¶36): a stage in the matter or the God of Light (¶37–41). "this Nature wholly Divine" (IV.3 ¶5): the Secret Fire or the Water. "the Stone" vs "Our Stone" (IV.3 ¶16): raw matter and product, or one Stone. The King (IV.4 ¶20): Reason or its Source. "root of Light" (IV.4 ¶15) and "Root of Reason" (¶20): if one, the Salt line joins the Light line; not stated.
