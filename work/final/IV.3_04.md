# IV.3_04

*Atwood sums up the last operation, Fermentation, which she makes the same as the Kabalah's union of man with God, and through Khunrath lists the fermented Stone's universal powers, reading its ferment, 'its own metal', as Light. She then moves from the Stone as outward medicine to the vital rectification of man himself (Julian), to the divinely gifted and charitable physician (Hippocrates, Helmont), to the loss of the Healing Art through ambition and gain, and to the healing of the mind by Wisdom.*

## Argument

- Summing up: in the last operation the Stone's union is cemented, its parts relating to the external world; this consummation of its transmutative virtue is Fermentation, the Kabalah's union of man, reduced to the Monad, with God. `IV.3 ¶27` — ATWOOD
- Pious Adepts (Khunrath, Boehme, Freher, Grasseus, early Adepts) make their magistery a type of the Gospel and Creed, without confounding metalline perfection (primary evolution of life only) with Divine association: the Stone exhibits the Universal Law of Light in Nature. `IV.3 ¶27` — ATWOOD
- As man united with God does what he wills, the fermented Stone transforms itself into what it wills and coequals itself to all. `IV.3 ¶27` — ATWOOD
- Khunrath: the Stone, universal medium of restoration, expels disease of mind and body by equilibriate virtue in every kingdom; its Azoth reduces bodies to First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form; it makes gold potable, burns perpetually in a lamp, expels evil spirits (the author of confusion could not endure symmetry), grants memory and joy; sublunary spirits obey it. `IV.3 ¶28, IV.3 n8` — QUOTED: Khunrath
- The vivific Ens shares the origin of what it assimilates, the Universal Spirit, so often fermented, rectified and recalcined that its central force acts at the circumference, magnetically drawing Homogeneal Nature in; hence 'azoth'. Khunrath: who rightly conceives this has begun aright, which is half the whole. `IV.3 ¶29` — ATWOOD
- The Stone is fermented with its metal: silver for the White, gold for the Red; the work of three days. `IV.3 ¶29, IV.3 n9` — UNCLEAR
- Its 'own metal' is Light, the Aurific Seed, not common light but that asleep in all nature; only Light multiplies, once freed from heterogeneous hindrance. Its chief benefit to man is the vital rectification. `IV.3 ¶30` — ATWOOD
- Julian, cited in illustration: the purified soul receives Divine Light and strengthens her connate spirit, which then preserves the body; disease comes from erroneous motion of the spirit; health is equilibrium, imparted by the Telestic art. The oracles agree. `IV.3 ¶30, IV.3 ¶31, IV.3 n10` — QUOTED: Julian
- What a man receives he can impart. Petris to Artaxerxes: Hippocrates, of divine descent, cured by a divine nature, taught himself the art, and earned divine honors. `IV.3 ¶32, IV.3 ¶33, IV.3 ¶34, IV.3 n11, IV.3 n12` — QUOTED: Petris
- Helmont, after Solomon: God's chosen physician is led by compassion and charity, without hope of gain, and is blessed. Arrogance, sloth, ambition and gain split surgeon from physician, extinguished charity and the art, made the Giver of Lights withdraw His Gifts; Galenist doctrine left both inefficient. `IV.3 ¶34, IV.3 n12, IV.3 n13` — QUOTED: Helmont
- Atwood laments the loss: Van Helmont's 10,000 yearly cures without diminishing his medicine are now credited only by the few who, applying a fragment of the virtue benevolently, conceive the illimitable increase of vitalization; these will vindicate the Light. `IV.3 ¶35` — ATWOOD
- Minds ail too: Greek temples disciplined the understanding toward Wisdom; Aesculapius, a demigod only, is Mesmerism's only just pride. She closes citing the prophet (n14, n15): honor the physician; Wisdom is with every man by his gift, her secret with the Faithful. `IV.3 ¶36, IV.3 n14, IV.3 n15` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **stage** — “which union or consummation of its transmutative virtue is called Fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Fermentation is the last operation. In it the Stone's parts are finally united, agree with one another and are brought into relation with the external world, which completes its power to transmute.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶27 defines Fermentation as the union finally cemented 'In the last operation'.
  - Also: “In the last operation the union of the Philosophic Stone is said to be finally cemented, in its component parts agreeing and having relation to the external world” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **principle** — “that which in the Kabalah is denominated the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God, that in metaphysico-chemistry is called Fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Fermentation in the chemical language is the same mystery the Kabalah calls the union with God of a man who has been reduced to the simplicity of the Monad.
  - Basis: Explicit identity in IV.3 ¶27.
- **principle** — “their Stone exhibits, alike in its origin, development and artificial preparation throughout, the Universal Law of Light in Nature” `IV.3 ¶27` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Every stage of the Stone's preparation follows the same universal law of Light that runs through nature, which is why the work corresponds to the Gospel and the Creed. Metallic perfection belongs only to life's first level and is not the same as final union with God.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶27: the Adepts do not 'confound the metalline perfection' with 'the ultimate Divine association', yet the magistery 'corresponds' to the Christian Creed.
  - Also: “the metalline perfection, which belongs to the primary evolution of life only” `IV.3 ¶27`
  - Also: “their magistery not only corresponds, but is in every deed a type, and promise, and foundation of our Christian Creed” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **result** — “transform itself into what it wills, and works for the diverse natures of all things, coequals itself to all” `IV.3 ¶27` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once fermented, the Stone can adapt itself to any nature and act on it, as a man united with God can do what he wills because he wills what God wills.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶27 draws the comparison directly: 'as man, united with God ... so does the Philosopher’s Stone in the greater world'.
  - Also: “as man, united with God, becomes divinely empowered, and therefore can do what he wills” `IV.3 ¶27`
  - Also: “Fermented in its parts by reason of the fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **result** — “It is the universal medium of restoration and preservation” `IV.3 ¶28` (QUOTED: Khunrath (reported with apparent approval))
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone is a universal medicine. Its balancing (equilibriate) power removes disease of mind and body in every kingdom of nature, according to what each can receive.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28 (Khunrath) lists its effects in each kingdom.
  - Also: “by its own equilibriate virtue expels suffering and every disease whether of mind or body” `IV.3 ¶28`
  - Also: “in either Kingdom of nature rectifying, according to the capability of each” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **result** — “Metals it is said to benefit by transference of their radical moisture, depriving its terrestreity and foreign oxides” `IV.3 ¶28` (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - **Plain (stated):** In metals the Stone works through their radical moisture and removes their earthiness and foreign oxides; in vegetables it increases their flowering spirit; in animals it acts according to their nature. This chunk does not define radical moisture.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28.
  - Also: “vegetables, by an increase of their efflorescent spirit” `IV.3 ¶28`
  - Also: “crystals it advances to gems, and many pearls artificially it concretes into one” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **procedure** — “Azoth of our Stone, continues he, reduces bodies to their First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form” `IV.3 ¶28` (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone's active principle works in two steps: it dissolves a body back to its First Matter, then gives it new life with the Universal Form.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28 states the function; IV.3 ¶29 explains the name azoth.
  - Also: “Metals, also, as I myself have seen, crystals, gems, as well as gold, it makes fluid and potable” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **result** — “its enduring water, lighted by art, burns perpetually, for ever” `IV.3 ¶28` (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Fermented with the specific essence of simples and lit in a suitable lamp, the Stone's water is said to burn forever. This chunk does not explain the image.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28 gives only the literal claim, no gloss.
  - Also: “fermented with the specific essence of simples, and methodically applied in a fitting lamp” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **result** — “The author of confusion could not endure symmetry.” `IV.3 ¶28` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Stone drives out evil spirits because it is a universal power of symmetry, which malignant powers cannot bear.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28: 'There are particular potencies in nature which malignant powers give way to --- why not to the universal?'; its 'equilibriate virtue' (¶28); health as 'a certain symmetry or equilibrium of nature' (¶30).
  - Also: “It expels and drives away evil spirits from the possessed” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **result** — “it draws down sublime memory and prudence” `IV.3 ¶28` (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone also acts on the mind: it strengthens healing ability, memory, prudence, joy and courage, and all sublunary spirits obey it.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶28.
  - Also: “It wonderfully excites perpetual joy, and an honest boldness and fortitude of mind exhilarating all life” `IV.3 ¶28`
  - Also: “the sublunary spirits, for every and all of these, by an inherent necessity, obey this Stone” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **agent** — “this powerful vivific Ens is of the same origin as that which it assimilates --- viz., the Universal Spirit” `IV.3 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The life-giving essence of the Stone can assimilate other things because it comes from the same source as they do, the Universal Spirit.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶29. Whether 'viz.' names the shared origin or the Ens itself is open (see ambiguities).
- **procedure** — “so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined, until the central force, wholly drawn out, acts forcibly at the circumference” `IV.3 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Universal Spirit is put through repeated cycles of fermentation, rectification and recalcination until its inner power is wholly drawn out and acts outwardly.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶29 names the repeated operations and their end; reading 'central force ... at the circumference' as inner power acting outwardly is the narrowest reading; the chunk does not gloss the image further.
- **sign** — “is able to draw, by a supernatural magnetism, the Homogeneal Nature everywhere into itself, according to the Rational Form inbred” `IV.3 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once perfected, the Spirit attracts and absorbs whatever is of its own kind, following the rational form innate in it. This attraction is why it is called azoth, 'which adheres to bodies'.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶29: 'And hence we may conceive why the Hermetic philosophers have given it the name of azoth'.
  - Also: “the Hermetic philosophers have given it the name of azoth, which adheres to bodies” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **principle** — “He who has begun aright, has done half of the whole” `IV.3 ¶29` (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - **Plain (stated):** Rightly understanding the azoth (the Universal Spirit drawn out until it attracts its like) is half of the whole work.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶29: 'which if thou dost rightly conceive, says Khunrath, thou art he of whom it may be truly said'.
- **procedure** — “The Stone is fermented with its metal existing in the highest purity, with pure silver for the White, and with Red for fine Gold” `IV.3 ¶29` (UNCLEAR (n9 cites Khunrath, Amph. Sap. Etern.; endorsed by ATWOOD in IV.3 ¶30))
  - **Plain (interpreted):** In the final operation the Stone is fermented with its own kind in its purest form: silver for the White, gold for the Red. Atwood reads 'its own metal' as Light (IV.3 ¶30). This chunk does not explain the 'three days' or the two colours.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶29 states the instruction; IV.3 ¶30: 'The Stone is here said accurately to be fermented with its own metal: for is not Light the true Aurific Seed'. No gloss on three days, White or Red.
  - Also: “And this is the work of three days” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **agent** — “is not Light the true Aurific Seed” `IV.3 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone's 'own metal', the gold-making seed, is Light: not common light, but that which 'in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient' and lies asleep, potentially, everywhere in nature.
  - Basis: Atwood's own gloss in IV.3 ¶30.
  - Also: “The Stone is here said accurately to be fermented with its own metal” `IV.3 ¶30`
  - Also: “not common Light, but that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient, which is potentially included everywhere in either kingdom of nature, though laid asleep” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **principle** — “What else but Light can multiply, or does multiply, anyhow or anywhere” `IV.3 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Only Light can transmute or multiply by assimilation; it does so in the mineral kingdom too.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30, rhetorical questions with an evident answer.
  - Also: “Or what else should possibly transmute or multiply by its own assimilative virtue?” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **impediment** — “when set free to attract its proper fuel indeficiently, without heterogeneous hindrance or alloy” `IV.3 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Light multiplies only when freed from foreign admixture so it can draw its proper nourishment without interruption; foreign matter is the obstacle.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30.
- **result** — “Nor in the outward ministration of the medicament only, but above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification” `IV.3 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** For man, Light's greatest and most lasting benefit is not as an outward medicine but in 'the vital rectification', which the text illustrates (Julian) as the purified soul strengthening her connate spirit and so preserving the body.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30 names 'the vital rectification' without defining it; the reading rests on the Julian passage Atwood introduces immediately after with 'Thus Julian ... for example'. Whether 'man' is the operator or man in general is open (see ambiguities).
- **procedure** — “when the soul gives herself to Divinity, and wholly delivers herself to the guidance of a better nature, divine institutions, through purification, taking the lead, the Divine Light will immediately shine on her” `IV.3 ¶30` (QUOTED: Julian (offered by ATWOOD as illustration of vital rectification))
  - **Plain (stated):** The inner sequence: the soul surrenders to the divine, purification coming first, and Divine Light shines on her. Thus deified, she strengthens her connate spirit, and that spirit, once in control, keeps the whole body healthy.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30. Atwood introduces it with 'Thus Julian ... for example' to illustrate vital rectification.
  - Also: “she transfuses a certain vigorous strength into her connate spirit, which when included, and as it were possessing dominion, becomes, through this spirit the cause of safety to the whole body” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **impediment** — “diseases, or at least the greater part, happen from the mutative and erroneous motion of the spirit” `IV.3 ¶30` (QUOTED: Julian)
  - **Plain (stated):** Most illness comes from disordered, unstable movement of the vital spirit.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30.
- **principle** — “health is a certain symmetry or equilibrium of nature, which it is the pre-eminent power of the Telestic art to impart, ordering all things by the unity within” `IV.3 ¶30` (QUOTED: Julian)
  - **Plain (stated):** Health is balance. The Telestic art gives that balance by ordering everything from an inner unity. This matches the Stone's 'equilibriate virtue' in Khunrath (¶28).
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30; compare IV.3 ¶28 'equilibriate virtue'.
- **procedure** — “through purifying ceremonies bodies become worthy of receiving divine assistance and health” `IV.3 ¶30` (QUOTED: the oracles)
  - **Plain (stated):** Purification makes the body fit to receive divine help, and so preserves it.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶30; Latin verse IV.3 ¶31.
  - Also: “the mortal vestment of bitter matter will be by these means be preserved” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **operator** — “And that which a man has received, will he not be able to impart again, and bestow of this exuberant felicity for the benefaction of others?” `IV.3 ¶32` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Someone who has received the vital renewal can pass it on to heal others.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶32, following the vital rectification of IV.3 ¶30.
- **operator** — “he cured extraordinary maladies because he was endowed with a divine nature” `IV.3 ¶32` (QUOTED: Petris)
  - **Plain (stated):** Hippocrates is the model healer: his power came from a divine nature, by descent and by using that nature to teach himself the whole art.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶32.
  - Also: “himself did also teach himself --- having made use of a divine nature --- the whole art” `IV.3 ¶32`
- **operator** — “But a physician chosen by God, continues Helmont, His own signs shall follow, and wonders for the schools” `IV.3 ¶34` (QUOTED: Helmont (praised by ATWOOD as 'one of its brightest ornaments', IV.3 ¶35))
  - **Plain (stated):** The true healer is chosen by God and recognized by results. He must act from compassion, truth, charity and mercy, without thought of gain; God then blesses his work, enlightens him with his Spirit, and diseases flee from him.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶34 lists these requirements and promises.
  - Also: “compassion shall be his leader” `IV.3 ¶34`
  - Also: “the hope of gain shall not be in his thoughts” `IV.3 ¶34`
  - Also: “the Lord loveth those that work mercy; and therefore he will enlighten them with his Spirit, the Comforter” `IV.3 ¶34`
- **impediment** — “after that the study of ambition and gain were practiced, charity grew cold, mercy was extinguished, art perished, and the Giver of Lights withdrew his Gifts” `IV.3 ¶34` (QUOTED: Helmont)
  - **Plain (stated):** The healing art is lost through ambition, desire for gain, arrogance and sloth: when charity fails, the divine gift is withdrawn. Discursive, Galenic theorizing (measuring diseases by degrees of heat and cold) took its place.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶34; Atwood endorses it as the 'decline, and fall of the Healing Art' in IV.3 ¶35.
  - Also: “arrogance and sloth, which long since extinguished Charity” `IV.3 ¶34`
  - Also: “being allured with the facility of the art of Galen, promised to measure all diseases by the geometrical demonstrations of degrees of heat and cold” `IV.3 ¶34`
- **sign** — “without any diminution of his medicine” `IV.3 ¶35` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Van Helmont's medicine was not used up by healing some 10,000 people a year. The few who apply even a fragment of this virtue benevolently can grasp that vital power can be increased by art without limit.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶35.
  - Also: “with a fragment of the same virtue, have, by a benevolent application, learnt to conceive something of the illimitable powers and artificial increase of vitalization” `IV.3 ¶35`
- **impediment** — “suffer it again to fall into disuse through negligence, or by obloquy be tempted to an uncharitable ambuscade” `IV.3 ¶35` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Now that the Light is dawning again, the dangers are neglect, and being provoked by public abuse into acting without charity.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶35 'These will vindicate the Light, if any, who observing its first dawn, shall suffer it...'; the syntax is obscure (see ambiguities).
- **principle** — “all ministering to the defects of mind, educating and disciplining the understanding for the conception of Wisdom and a better life within” `IV.3 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The mind needs healing as well as the body; the Greek temples served to discipline the understanding toward Wisdom.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶36.
  - Also: “Are not our minds, too, ailing, and is there no balm ready?” `IV.3 ¶36`
- **operator** — “She is with every man, replies the prophet, according to his gift; but Her secret is with the Faithful” `IV.3 ¶36` (QUOTED: the prophet (n15 'Idem., 1', after n14 'Eccles. 8'); endorsed by ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Everyone has some share of Wisdom according to his gift, but her secret is held only by the faithful.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶36, the chunk's closing words.

## Terms

- **Fermentation** (also: fermentation, fermented) — The last operation, in which the Stone's union is finally cemented, its parts agreeing and relating to the external world: the consummation of its transmutative virtue. It is the metaphysico-chemical name for the Kabalah's union of man with God. The Stone is fermented with its own metal (silver for the White, gold for the Red), which Atwood reads as Light. (ATWOOD)
  - = the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God (Kabalah): “that which in the Kabalah is denominated the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God, that in metaphysico-chemistry is called Fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27`
  - “which union or consummation of its transmutative virtue is called Fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **Philosophic Stone** (also: Philosopher’s Stone, Stone, our Stone) — The product of the work. Once fermented it transforms itself into what it wills and coequals itself to all. It exhibits the Universal Law of Light in Nature, is a universal medicine, and is fermented with its own metal, Light. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Philosopher’s Stone in the greater world” `IV.3 ¶27`
  - “It is the universal medium of restoration and preservation” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **Monad** — The simplicity to which man is reduced in the union with God that the Kabalah describes. (ATWOOD)
  - “the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **metaphysico-chemistry** — Atwood's name for the Hermetic science, whose term 'Fermentation' matches the Kabalah's union with God. (ATWOOD)
  - “that in metaphysico-chemistry is called Fermentation” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **magistery** — The Adepts' work, which they say corresponds to the Christian Creed and is a type, promise and foundation of it. (ATWOOD)
  - “their magistery not only corresponds, but is in every deed a type, and promise, and foundation of our Christian Creed” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **metalline perfection** — The perfection reached in metals. It belongs only to the primary evolution of life and is not to be confused with the ultimate Divine association. (ATWOOD)
  - “the metalline perfection, which belongs to the primary evolution of life only” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **Universal Law of Light in Nature** — The law the Stone exhibits throughout its origin, development and artificial preparation. (ATWOOD)
  - “their Stone exhibits, alike in its origin, development and artificial preparation throughout, the Universal Law of Light in Nature” `IV.3 ¶27`
- **equilibriate virtue** — The Stone's balancing power, by which it expels suffering and disease of mind and body. Compare Julian's health as 'symmetry or equilibrium of nature' (¶30). (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “by its own equilibriate virtue expels suffering and every disease whether of mind or body” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **radical moisture** — Something in metals through whose transference the Stone benefits them, removing their terrestreity. Not further defined. (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “Metals it is said to benefit by transference of their radical moisture” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **terrestreity** — The earthiness of which the Stone deprives metals, together with foreign oxides. (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “depriving its terrestreity and foreign oxides” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **efflorescent spirit** — Something in vegetables that the Stone increases. Not further defined. (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “vegetables, by an increase of their efflorescent spirit” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **Azoth** (also: azoth) — The Stone's active principle. It reduces bodies to First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form. The Hermetic philosophers give the vivific Ens this name because it adheres to bodies, drawing its like into itself by supernatural magnetism. (ATWOOD)
  - = vivific Ens: “the Hermetic philosophers have given it the name of azoth, which adheres to bodies” `IV.3 ¶29`
  - “Azoth of our Stone, continues he, reduces bodies to their First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **First Matter** — The state to which the Azoth reduces bodies before reanimating them. Not further defined here. (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “reduces bodies to their First Matter” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **Universal Form** — That with which the Azoth reanimates bodies reduced to First Matter. (QUOTED: Khunrath)
  - “reanimates them with the Universal Form” `IV.3 ¶28`
- **vivific Ens** — The life-giving essence of the Stone. It has the same origin as what it assimilates, the Universal Spirit, and draws the Homogeneal Nature into itself. (ATWOOD)
  - = azoth: “the Hermetic philosophers have given it the name of azoth, which adheres to bodies” `IV.3 ¶29`
  - “this powerful vivific Ens is of the same origin as that which it assimilates --- viz., the Universal Spirit” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **Universal Spirit** — The common origin of the vivific Ens and of what it assimilates (or the Ens itself; see ambiguities). It is repeatedly fermented, rectified and recalcined until its central force acts at the circumference. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Universal Spirit, which has been so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **central force** — The inner force of the Universal Spirit, which repeated operations draw wholly out until it acts forcibly at the circumference. (ATWOOD)
  - “until the central force, wholly drawn out, acts forcibly at the circumference” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **supernatural magnetism** — The attraction by which the perfected Spirit draws the Homogeneal Nature into itself. (ATWOOD)
  - “by a supernatural magnetism” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **Homogeneal Nature** — Nature of the same kind, which the perfected Spirit draws into itself everywhere. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Homogeneal Nature everywhere into itself” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **Rational Form** — The inbred form according to which the Spirit draws the Homogeneal Nature to itself. (ATWOOD)
  - “according to the Rational Form inbred” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **the White / the Red** (also: White, Red) — The two forms of the Stone, fermented respectively with pure silver and with fine gold. Not further explained. (UNCLEAR)
  - “with pure silver for the White, and with Red for fine Gold” `IV.3 ¶29`
- **Light** (also: Divine Light) — Not common light but that which becomes efficient in the first rotary wheel of life, latent ('laid asleep') in every kingdom of nature. It is the Stone's 'own metal' and the only thing that multiplies by assimilation. In Julian, the Divine Light shines on the purified soul; in ¶35 the 'Light' is what the few will vindicate. (ATWOOD)
  - = Aurific Seed: “is not Light the true Aurific Seed” `IV.3 ¶30`
  - = the Stone's 'own metal' (its ferment): “The Stone is here said accurately to be fermented with its own metal: for is not Light the true Aurific Seed” `IV.3 ¶30`
  - “not common Light, but that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **first rotary wheel of life** — The stage of life in which the true Light, as distinct from common light, becomes efficient. Not further explained. (ATWOOD)
  - “that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **multiply** — To increase or transmute by assimilative virtue; a property of Light alone. (ATWOOD)
  - “Or what else should possibly transmute or multiply by its own assimilative virtue?” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **vital rectification** — Light's greatest and most permanent benefit to man, as distinct from its outward use as a medicine; not defined, but illustrated by Julian's account of the purified soul strengthening her connate spirit. (ATWOOD)
  - “but above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **connate spirit** — The soul's own spirit ('her connate spirit'). The deified soul transfuses vigorous strength into it, and it then becomes the cause of safety to the whole body. (QUOTED: Julian)
  - “she transfuses a certain vigorous strength into her connate spirit” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **Telestic art** — The art whose pre-eminent power is to impart health, understood as symmetry or equilibrium, by ordering all things by the unity within. (QUOTED: Julian)
  - “health is a certain symmetry or equilibrium of nature, which it is the pre-eminent power of the Telestic art to impart, ordering all things by the unity within” `IV.3 ¶30`
- **Healing Art** — Medicine as once perfectly attained and divinely gifted; lost through ambition, gain and Galenic theorizing. (ATWOOD)
  - “So the origin, decline, and fall of the Healing Art has been pictured by one of its brightest ornaments” `IV.3 ¶35`
- **vitalization** — Vital power, capable of illimitable artificial increase, as those who apply a fragment of the same virtue benevolently can conceive. (ATWOOD)
  - “the illimitable powers and artificial increase of vitalization” `IV.3 ¶35`
- **Wisdom** — What the Greek temples prepared the understanding to conceive. She is with every man according to his gift, but her secret is with the Faithful. (ATWOOD)
  - “educating and disciplining the understanding for the conception of Wisdom and a better life within” `IV.3 ¶36`
  - “She is with every man, replies the prophet, according to his gift; but Her secret is with the Faithful” `IV.3 ¶36`

## Ambiguities

- “The author of confusion could not endure symmetry.” `IV.3 ¶28`
  - Reading: Atwood's own interjection inside the Khunrath paraphrase
  - Reading: Part of Khunrath's statement
  - Basis: The rhetorical asides ('wherefore not?', 'why not to the universal?') break the run of 'continues he' reporting, but there are no quotation marks either way.
- “this powerful vivific Ens is of the same origin as that which it assimilates --- viz., the Universal Spirit” `IV.3 ¶29`
  - Reading: The common origin shared by the Ens and what it assimilates is the Universal Spirit
  - Reading: The Ens itself is the Universal Spirit, repeatedly fermented etc.
  - Basis: 'viz.' could attach to 'origin' or to the Ens. The following clause ('which has been so often fermented ...') describes something worked upon, which favours the second, but the syntax does not settle it.
- “The Stone is fermented with its metal existing in the highest purity, with pure silver for the White, and with Red for fine Gold. And this is the work of three days (9).” `IV.3 ¶29`
  - Reading: Khunrath's instruction (n9 cites Amph. Sap. Etern.)
  - Reading: Atwood's own summary of the tradition
  - Basis: ¶30 'The Stone is here said accurately' suggests a cited statement, and n9 points to Khunrath; ¶29 gives no explicit attribution for this sentence.
- “how much greater benefits will it not bestow on man who nourished it?” `IV.3 ¶30`
  - Reading: The operator who nourished the Light within himself in the work
  - Reading: Man in general, as the being in whom this Light is nourished (same origin as what it assimilates, ¶29)
  - Reading: Loose phrasing or misprint for the man who nourishes or uses it
  - Basis: ¶30 then speaks of 'vital rectification' and the soul's own spirit (Julian); ¶32 'that which a man has received' suggests an individual recipient; ¶29 gives the shared origin. ¶30 itself does not explain.
- “Ruth remained buried in the grave of science” `IV.3 ¶34`
  - Reading: Misprint for 'Truth', buried and replaced by brawling taken for doctrine
  - Reading: Literally 'Ruth'
  - Basis: The next clause, 'instead thereof a confused kind of brawling arose, being discursive, which was accounted for doctrine', fits 'Truth'. The chunk has other evident misprints ('hnis', 'Bt').
- “These will vindicate the Light, if any, who observing its first dawn, shall suffer it again to fall into disuse through negligence, or by obloquy be tempted to an uncharitable ambuscade.” `IV.3 ¶35`
  - Reading: The few who understand will defend the Light against anyone who lets it lapse
  - Reading: The few will hold to account (avenge the Light upon) those who, having seen it, abandon or betray it
  - Basis: 'vindicate' and 'if any' can be construed either way; the preceding sentence identifies 'These' as the few with a fragment of the virtue.
- “Aesculapius was a demigod only amongst them, who is of modern Mesmerism the only just pride.” `IV.3 ¶36`
  - Reading: Praise: Mesmerism rightly takes Aesculapius as its pride
  - Reading: Limitation: Mesmerism has only the bodily healer, a mere demigod, below the gods whose temples healed the mind
  - Basis: The paragraph contrasts defects of body with those of mind ('have we no other defects than those of body') and stresses 'demigod only' and 'the only just pride'.

## Threads

- `IV.3 ¶27` 'To sum up then, lest we should never come to an end of this fugitive pursuit' and 'In the last operation': a summing-up of the series of operations treated before this chunk, ending with the last, Fermentation.
- `IV.3 ¶29` 'which has been so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined' may refer back to earlier treatment of these repeated operations on the Universal Spirit.
