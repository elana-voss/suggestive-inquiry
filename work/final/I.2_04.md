# I.2_04

*Atwood concludes Chapter II's theory: the Stone is the purified Ether of nature perfected by its own Light acting as ferment, working by the same method in all three kingdoms (most easily shown in metals), which nature cannot do unaided; she then introduces the Golden Treatise of Hermes as an epitome of the Art.*

## Argument

- The philosophers agree: the stone is nature's pure Ethereality, separated by art, purified and concentrated by multiplying its own Light, through preparation, generation, birth, specification. `I.2 ¶44` — ATWOOD
- It is perfected first and most easily in minerals; the adept calls the seed of gold fiery thickened Light, the Stone of Fire. Helped by art, nature transcends herself; Light is the ferment perfecting the Ether. `I.2 ¶44, I.2 n43` — ATWOOD
- Verse: an inward agent, the Form matter lacked, is needed. This Form is Light, source of central heat; with matter it begets a seed that stamps its character on the matter, which then lives and co-works with the Form. `I.2 ¶45, I.2 n44` — QUOTED: Eiranaeus
- Formal Light multiplied in its Ether yields gold in minerals; with more skill and coction, the elixir of the wise in plants; rarely in animals, most in man. The material is one, forms diverse: in man a Divine Image, embryonic now, which through new birth enters communion with the highest life. `I.2 ¶46` — ATWOOD
- Science exploits light, electricity, magnetism but cannot find their source. That unknown Cause was what the Hermetic experiment claimed to prove; she proposes recovering the ancient method. `I.2 ¶47, I.2 n45` — ATWOOD
- Truth is hidden on earth because nature's forms or sulfurs are confused and her passive spirit enclosed and impure. Hermetic dissolution yields the right recipient, separates pure from impure, and makes agent and patient one (Emerald Table). `I.2 ¶48` — ATWOOD
- Hence transmutation of species is not absurd: fermentation (juice to wine, milk to cheese) and caterpillars becoming moths change one thing into other natures without addition. So the Vital Spirit is raised by its own leaven through many stages, then reacts on its original elements. `I.2 ¶48` — ATWOOD
- Unlike species bound by natural law, the philosophic Proteus forms itself freely about its own self-multiplying Light; transmuted, it transmutes, in proportion to virtue gained in fermentation. Transmuting means drawing crude life into its own light. `I.2 ¶48` — ATWOOD
- Gold is nearest the common Spirit: other metals lose weight through impurity, but gold absorbs the Formal Light wholly, like ice in warm water. Sendivogius: seek the hidden thing yielding a moisture that gently dissolves gold; it is gold's source and like a mother to it. `I.2 ¶48, I.2 n46` — ATWOOD
- The method of giving Form to the purified Spirit is the same in all three kingdoms, only preparation differing. Metals are used most because the metalline radix appears first, is easily apportioned, and risks less; Geber: less perfect metals may be perfected more freely. `I.2 ¶49, I.2 n47` — ATWOOD
- Metals come from seeds of the Universal Spirit or Mercury, the only means of their increase; they do not grow for lack of heat, their spirit overcome by gross elements, unless purged of terrestreity and their tincture freed. Sendivogius: art perfects gold by fire alone, but the body must first be dissolved and opened. Another: pure mineral spirit raises like things to the Elixir. `I.2 ¶49, I.2 n48, I.2 n49` — ATWOOD
- She admits the theory inverts common notions. Recap: one Homogeneal Subject is the sole principle of transmutation; bodies must be reduced to original matter by introversion of the generated life; unaided nature cannot exceed herself. Needed: the true matter and a fit artist. `I.2 ¶49` — ATWOOD
- She introduces the Tractatus Aureus of Hermes as an ancient epitome of the Art, requiring prudence, patience, penetration; such books are for diligent students of nature, not the vulgar or casual. Reasons for concealment come later. `I.2 ¶50, I.2 ¶51, I.2 n50, I.2 n51` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **subject** — “their stone is nothing more or less than the pure Ethereality of nature, separated by artificial means, purified and made concrete by constriction and scientific multiplication of its proper Light” `I.2 ¶44` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone is the subtle ethereal substance of nature, isolated by art, purified, and condensed by multiplying the light proper to it.
  - Basis: Atwood states this directly as the inference from the philosophers' evidence.
- **stage** — “the preparation, generation, birth, specification --- all proceeding, arte mirabili, on the hidden basis of its primal eduction” `I.2 ¶44` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The work runs through four named phases (preparation, generation, birth, specification), all resting on a first drawing-out of the ethereal substance; this chunk does not explain each phase.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶44 lists the phases in apposition to the separation and multiplication of the Ether; 'primal eduction' read narrowly as the initial separation 'by artificial means' in the same sentence.
- **agent** — “Light is the true fermental principle which perfects the Ether in its proper kind” `I.2 ¶44` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Light acts as the ferment: it is what perfects the purified Ether, the matter of the work.
  - Basis: Atwood states it; the verse in ¶45 ('This Form is Light') and ¶46 ('the Formal Light, by multiplication in its Ethe') confirm Light as the forming agent.
  - Also: “This Form is Light, the source of central heat, Which clothed with mater doth a seed beget.” `I.2 ¶45`
  - Also: “No, there must be an inward agent granted, Else would a thing unchanged still remain;” `I.2 ¶45`
- **result** — “the seed of gold, says the adept, is a fiery form of Light inspissate, and this is the Stone of Fire” `I.2 ¶44` (QUOTED: the adept)
  - **Plain (stated):** The seed of gold is light thickened into a fiery form; this is what is called the Stone of Fire.
  - Basis: The text identifies seed of gold = fiery inspissated Light = Stone of Fire; the Latin that follows (I.2 ¶44) speaks of the stone as fire.
  - Also: “Lapis noster, hic est ignis, ex igne creatus, et in ignem veritur, et anima ejus in igna moratur” `I.2 ¶44`
- **principle** — “Thus nature, by the help of art, is said to transcend herself” `I.2 ¶44` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Nature alone cannot produce the Stone; art must assist her so she exceeds her ordinary limits.
  - Basis: Restated plainly in I.2 ¶49.
  - Also: “It is vain, therefore, to seek for that in nature which is an effect beyond her strength; she must be helped, that she may exceed herself, or all will be useless.” `I.2 ¶49`
- **procedure** — “On it it stamps its character, which done, The Matter lives, and that which may seem strange, Co-worketh with the Form t’attain the end” `I.2 ¶45` (QUOTED: Eiranaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once Light (the Form) joined to matter produces a seed, the seed impresses its character on the matter, which then becomes alive and cooperates with the Form toward the seed's intended end.
  - Basis: The verse states the sequence explicitly (I.2 ¶45).
- **result** — “in him it assumes an Image that is Divine and more potent than all the rest: which is in this life yet an embryo” `I.2 ¶46` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The same work yields gold in minerals, the elixir of the wise in plants, and in man a divine form, embryonic in this life, which through a new birth in universal intelligence passes into communion with the highest life.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶46 states the gradation across kingdoms and the outcome in man.
  - Also: “when unfolded through a new birth in universal intelligence, transcends the limits of this nether sphere, and passes into communion with the highest life” `I.2 ¶46`
  - Also: “through superior skill and coction in the vegetal life, the elixir of the wise” `I.2 ¶46`
- **principle** — “though the material is one throughout, forms are diverse” `I.2 ¶46` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** One single matter underlies all kingdoms; only the forms it takes differ.
  - Basis: Direct statement; echoed in ¶49 'Homogeneal Subject' and ¶48 'the One Thing'.
- **result** — “These phenomena are effects then of a Cause unknown, and that very unknown Cause it was the alleged object of the Hermetic experiment to prove.” `I.2 ¶47` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Hermetic experiment aimed to demonstrate the hidden cause behind light, electricity and magnetism, which modern science observes but cannot identify.
  - Basis: Direct statement in I.2 ¶47.
- **impediment** — “her forms or sulfurs are perplexed, and the passive spirit of nature is included and impure” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** In ordinary nature the forms are entangled and the receptive spirit is enclosed and contaminated, so truth cannot show itself; dissolution is needed to remedy this.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶48: the next sentence opposes this condition with the Hermetic dissolution which separates pure from impure and yields 'the right recipient'.
- **procedure** — “by the Hermetic dissolution the right recipient is said to be obtained, the pure is separated from the impure, the subtle from the gross, and the agent and the patient are one identity” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Dissolution frees and purifies the receptive spirit so it becomes a proper receiver; then the active and passive principles are found to be one and the same thing.
  - Basis: Direct statement, supported by the Emerald Table citation in the same paragraph.
  - Also: “That which is below is as that which is above” `I.2 ¶48`
- **procedure** — “the Vital Spirit said to be, by the art of Alchemy, promoted from one form of being into another by its won prepared must or leaven” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Like juice turned to wine by its own ferment, the Vital Spirit is raised into a higher form by a ferment prepared from itself, without adding anything foreign; it passes many stages from imperfect to perfect, then acts back on the elements it came from.
  - Basis: The fermentation analogy is drawn explicitly in I.2 ¶48 ('without requiring the addition of anything different').
  - Also: “it reacts convertively on the elements of its original extraction; having previously passed on, through many stages, from imperfection to perfection” `I.2 ¶48`
- **result** — “which being transmuted, transmutes; and multiplying, multiplies its proper substance freely, in proportion to the virtue which it has acquired in the fermentation” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once transformed, the Spirit transforms other things and multiplies itself; its power depends on how much virtue it gained in the fermentation.
  - Basis: Direct statement in I.2 ¶48.
- **principle** — “transmutes, i.e., attracts the same away from other forms into intimate coalescence with its own assimilative light” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Transmutation means the fermented Spirit (the Stone) draws the crude life of nature, from which it came, out of its existing forms and assimilates it into its own light.
  - Basis: Atwood glosses 'transmutes' with 'i.e.' in I.2 ¶48.
- **principle** — “the Formal Light is wholly swallowed up in it without residue, dissolving intimately, gently, and naturally, as they compare it to ice in warm water” `I.2 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Gold is the substance most akin to the Spirit: having no impurity, it absorbs the Light completely, the two differing only in state and being originally one.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶48 explains the simile: 'the commingling natures differ in state only and were originally one'.
  - Also: “in all other metals there is some impurity, and therefore a certain weight is lost in transmuting from them” `I.2 ¶48`
- **subject** — “you must seek for that hidden thing, out of which is made, after a wonderful manner, such a moisture or humidity which doth dissolve gold without violence or noise” `I.2 ¶48` (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - **Plain (stated):** The matter to seek is a hidden thing from which a moisture is made that dissolves gold gently; this is the same thing from which nature produces gold, and it is 'like a mother' to gold.
  - Basis: Sendivogius in I.2 ¶48 identifies it: 'if you find this out you have that thing out of which gold is produced by nature'. The hidden thing itself is not named in this chunk.
  - Also: “it is even like a mother to it” `I.2 ¶48`
- **procedure** — “the method of working to this discovery, and to supply the deficiency of Form to the purified body of the Spirit, is described as the same in each of the three kingdoms of nature” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operation (giving Form to the purified Spirit) is one method for mineral, vegetable and animal; only the preparatory stage varies with the material.
  - Basis: Direct statement in I.2 ¶49, continuing 'the preparation only being diversified'.
- **subject** — “the metalline radix first presents itself in the experimental process, and is most easily apportioned” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Art is most often shown on metals because the metallic root appears first in the experiment, is easiest to apportion, and involves less vital responsibility; not because the power is limited there or because adepts desired gold above every other good.
  - Basis: Direct statement in I.2 ¶49; Geber's quotation in the same paragraph supports metals being 'more freely' perfected.
  - Also: “because the responsibility involved is less vital and consequential, it has been more freely exhibited and worked at large” `I.2 ¶49`
- **agent** — “from metalline seeds out of the Universal Spirit or Mercury, by which also they may be exalted and multiplies, and by no other thing” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Metals are produced from metalline seeds out of the Universal Spirit, called Mercury, which is the only means of exalting and multiplying them; without this spirit there is no growth, transmutation or increase, and by it all natures are generated in their kinds.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶49: 'without this spirit growth is impossible, or transmutation or increase'.
- **impediment** — “the Spirit being overcome in the gross, preponderating elements of their hard composition, so that they cannot fructify” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Metals contain the prolific spirit but cannot grow because of lack of heat: the spirit is smothered by their gross earthy matter. They must first be purged of this earthiness and their tincture released.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶49 gives the reason ('a deficiency of heat') and the remedy ('unless they be first purged from their terrestreity and their tincture set free in the subtle Origin of all life'); Sendivogius's simile of transplanted trees and 'crud earth' in the same paragraph.
  - Also: “unless they be first purged from their terrestreity and their tincture set free in the subtle Origin of all life” `I.2 ¶49`
  - Also: “Vulgar gold Sendivogius compares to a herb without seed, which when it is ripe bears seed” `I.2 ¶49`
- **procedure** — “it is necessary that the body be loosened and dissolved, and the pores thereof opened, whereof nature may work” `I.2 ¶49` (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - **Plain (stated):** Art perfects gold only through fire or heat, but since no spirit appears in a solid metal, the body must first be dissolved and opened so nature can act within it.
  - Basis: Sendivogius in I.2 ¶49: 'by no other medium than fire or heat; but seeing this cannot be done, since in a congealed metallic body there appear no spirits'.
  - Also: “if at any time nature be sweetly and wittily helped, then art may perfect that which nature could not” `I.2 ¶49`
- **result** — “it will work such an alteration in things of like nature with themselves, that they shall equalize the Philosophical Elixir” `I.2 ¶49` (QUOTED: another (n49: Nuysement))
  - **Plain (stated):** The purified mineral spirit does more than reproduce its kind: it raises similar things to the level of the Elixir.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶49, 'when the mineral spirit is pure'.
- **principle** — “the reduction of bodies to their original matter, by introversion of the generated life, is requisite to a true manifestation and permanence in any form” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Bodies must be brought back to their original matter, by turning their life inward, before anything can be truly manifested or made lasting.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶49 states the requirement; 'introversion' read narrowly as an inward turning of the life, alongside ¶48's dissolution back to the 'One Thing' and ¶49's purging to 'the subtle Origin of all life'. The chunk does not define introversion.
- **subject** — “the existence of a Homogeneal Subject in nature, showing that the same was the material basis of their philosophy, and the only principle of transmutation, life, increase, and perfection” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** There is one uniform substance in nature which is the material basis of the Art and the only source of transmutation and growth.
  - Basis: Atwood's recap in I.2 ¶49.
  - Also: “the Mercury of the philosophers is not found of itself on earth, nor can be detained or perfected without this occult and needful Art assisting her” `I.2 ¶49`
- **operator** — “to know what the true matter is, where and how it may be taken, and to find an artist able and fitted to perfect it” `I.2 ¶49` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Two prerequisites: knowledge of the true matter and how to obtain it, and a qualified operator; without the matter attempt nothing, and without the artist the matter is useless.
  - Basis: Direct statement in I.2 ¶49.
- **operator** — “Prudence, patience, and penetration, the author owns, are required to understand him, and more than these for the discovery of his Great Art.” `I.2 ¶50` (QUOTED: the author (of the Tractatus Aureus); Atwood endorses)
  - **Plain (stated):** Understanding the Hermetic text needs prudence, patience and insight; the Art itself needs more. Diligent study of nature is the first step; casual reading does not make a philosopher.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶50, including 'this is the first step towards the recovery of truth, to be diligent in the investigation'.
  - Also: “our books are made for those who have been or intend to become conversant about the search of nature” `I.2 ¶50`
  - Also: “For this is the first step towards the recovery of truth, to be diligent in the investigation” `I.2 ¶50`

## Terms

- **Stone** (also: stone, Lapis noster) — The product of the Art: the purified, concentrated Ether of nature, perfected by its own Light; in its fermented, crystalline perfection called the Stone; it transmutes by assimilating crude life into its own light. (ATWOOD)
  - = pure Ethereality of nature: “their stone is nothing more or less than the pure Ethereality of nature” `I.2 ¶44`
  - = fermented Spirit: “this fermented Spirit or Stone, (as in the crystalline perfectness of its essence it has been called,)” `I.2 ¶48`
  - = Stone of Fire / seed of gold: “the seed of gold, says the adept, is a fiery form of Light inspissate, and this is the Stone of Fire” `I.2 ¶44`
  - “Lapis noster, hic est ignis, ex igne creatus” `I.2 ¶44`
- **Light** (also: Formal Light, light) — The forming, fermenting agent that perfects the Ether; the Form the matter lacks; source of central heat; self-multiplying. (ATWOOD)
  - = Form: “This Form is Light, the source of central heat” `I.2 ¶45`
  - = fermental principle: “Light is the true fermental principle which perfects the Ether in its proper kind” `I.2 ¶44`
  - “the universal magnet of its own infinite self-multiplicative Light” `I.2 ¶48`
- **Ether** (also: Ethereality, Ethe) — The subtle material of nature which, separated and purified, is the matter of the Stone, and in which the Formal Light multiplies. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Formal Light, by multiplication in its Ethe, is said to produce gold” `I.2 ¶46`
- **Form** (also: forms) — The inward agent that matter lacks and that, joined to matter, begets a seed; also the diverse specific shapes the one material assumes. (ATWOOD)
  - = Light: “This Form is Light” `I.2 ¶45`
  - = sulfurs: “her forms or sulfurs are perplexed” `I.2 ¶48`
  - “This agent is the form that matter wanted” `I.2 ¶45`
  - “though the material is one throughout, forms are diverse” `I.2 ¶46`
- **seed** (also: seed of gold, metalline seeds) — What Form (Light) clothed with matter begets; it impresses its character on matter. Metals arise from metalline seeds out of the Universal Spirit; vulgar gold lacks seed until art helps it. (ATWOOD)
  - = Stone of Fire: “the seed of gold, says the adept, is a fiery form of Light inspissate, and this is the Stone of Fire” `I.2 ¶44`
  - “Which clothed with mater doth a seed beget.” `I.2 ¶45`
- **ferment** (also: fermentation, fermental) — A thing's own agent of change that converts it into a different nature without anything foreign added; in the Art, the Spirit's own prepared leaven, by which it is raised and gains virtue. (ATWOOD)
  - “by its won prepared must or leaven” `I.2 ¶48`
  - “in proportion to the virtue which it has acquired in the fermentation” `I.2 ¶48`
- **Vital Spirit** — The spirit that alchemy promotes from one form of being into another by its own leaven, passing through many stages to perfection. (ATWOOD)
  - “Just so is the Vital Spirit said to be, by the art of Alchemy, promoted from one form of being into another” `I.2 ¶48`
- **Universal Spirit** (also: Spirit) — The common source of metals and all things, from which metalline seeds come; the only means of growth, transmutation and increase. (ATWOOD)
  - = Mercury: “from metalline seeds out of the Universal Spirit or Mercury” `I.2 ¶49`
  - “without this spirit growth is impossible, or transmutation or increase” `I.2 ¶49`
- **Mercury of the philosophers** (also: Mercury) — Not found of itself on earth; cannot be held or perfected without the Art. (ATWOOD)
  - = Universal Spirit: “the Universal Spirit or Mercury” `I.2 ¶49`
  - “the Mercury of the philosophers is not found of itself on earth” `I.2 ¶49`
- **Hermetic dissolution** — The operation that obtains the right recipient, separates pure from impure and subtle from gross, and makes agent and patient one. (ATWOOD)
  - “by the Hermetic dissolution the right recipient is said to be obtained” `I.2 ¶48`
- **agent and patient** — The active and receptive principles, which after Hermetic dissolution are one identity. (ATWOOD)
  - “the agent and the patient are one identity” `I.2 ¶48`
- **passive spirit of nature** — Nature's receptive spirit, in ordinary state enclosed and impure; made a right recipient by dissolution. (ATWOOD)
  - “the passive spirit of nature is included and impure” `I.2 ¶48`
- **transmutation** (also: transmutes, transmuted, transmuting) — Attraction of crude life away from other forms into coalescence with the Stone's own light. (ATWOOD)
  - “transmutes, i.e., attracts the same away from other forms into intimate coalescence with its own assimilative light” `I.2 ¶48`
- **philosophic Proteus** — Something in the Art whose will, unlike natural species, is free to form itself about its own self-multiplicative Light; context ties it to the fermented Vital Spirit, but the chunk does not define it. (ATWOOD)
  - “the will of the philosophic Proteus is free to be drawn without hindrance” `I.2 ¶48`
- **Homogeneal Subject** — The one uniform substance in nature that is the material basis of Hermetic philosophy and sole principle of transmutation, life, increase and perfection. (ATWOOD)
  - “the existence of a Homogeneal Subject in nature” `I.2 ¶49`
- **terrestreity** — The gross earthy part of metals that overcomes their spirit and must be purged away. (ATWOOD)
  - “unless they be first purged from their terrestreity” `I.2 ¶49`
- **tincture** — Something in metals that must be set free in the subtle Origin of all life before they can fructify; not further defined here. (ATWOOD)
  - “their tincture set free in the subtle Origin of all life” `I.2 ¶49`
- **Philosophical Elixir** — Product of the work, whose divine virtues wise men admire; things altered by the pure mineral spirit come to equal it. (¶46 separately names 'the elixir of the wise' as the result of superior skill and coction in vegetal life; the chunk does not assert the two are identical.) (ATWOOD)
  - “the Philosophical Elixir, whose divine virtues wise men so much admire” `I.2 ¶49`
- **metalline radix** — The metallic root, which appears first in the experimental process and is most easily apportioned. (ATWOOD)
  - “the metalline radix first presents itself in the experimental process” `I.2 ¶49`
- **coction** — A cooking/maturing operation; with superior skill it yields the elixir in vegetal life. Not defined in this chunk. (ATWOOD)
  - “through superior skill and coction in the vegetal life” `I.2 ¶46`
- **original matter** — That to which bodies must be reduced, by introversion of the generated life, for true manifestation and permanence. (ATWOOD)
  - “the reduction of bodies to their original matter, by introversion of the generated life” `I.2 ¶49`
- **One Thing** — In the Emerald Table, that from which all the rest proceed by adaptation; the unitary basis of production. (QUOTED: Emerald Table)
  - “the miracles of the One Thing whence all the rest proceed by adaptation” `I.2 ¶48`

## Ambiguities

- “the will of the philosophic Proteus is free to be drawn without hindrance to form itself about the universal magnet of its own infinite self-multiplicative Light” `I.2 ¶48`
  - Reading: Proteus is the fermented Vital Spirit itself, free (unlike natural species) to take any form around its own Light.
  - Reading: Proteus names the shape-changing matter of the Art in general, with 'will' implying a volitional element not otherwise explained.
  - Basis: Reading 1: the sentence contrasts with 'imprisoned nature' in species and continues 'which being transmuted, transmutes', describing the Spirit of the preceding sentence. Reading 2: the word 'will' and the unglossed figure 'Proteus' are not tied explicitly to any named subject.
- “She is moreover specified elsewhere, and does not consequently, as a true passive, reflect without truly to itself.” `I.2 ¶48`
  - Reading: Nature's passive spirit is already bound into specific forms, so it cannot act as a pure receiver that reflects truly.
  - Reading: Truth is located elsewhere than in earthly nature and so is not mirrored there.
  - Basis: Antecedent of 'She' may be 'Truth' (start of sentence pair) or 'the passive spirit of nature'; the following sentence offers dissolution and 'the right recipient' as the remedy, favoring reading 1.
- “is requisite to a true manifestation and permanence in any form, as by the ordinary process of unassisted nature, as is evident” `I.2 ¶49`
  - Reading: Reduction is required, as in ordinary nature's own processes.
  - Reading: Reduction is required and cannot happen by unassisted nature (text possibly garbled, e.g. 'not by').
  - Basis: The following clause says nature 'never withstands or alters ... her mode of being' and 'must be helped', supporting reading 2; the literal wording supports reading 1.
- “are by God endued with greater and more ignoble perfection” `I.2 ¶49`
  - Reading: Weaker-composed (living) things receive a greater and nobler perfection.
  - Reading: Text as printed: a 'more ignoble' perfection of proportionate mixture.
  - Basis: Atwood's gloss '(i.e., where life predominates over corporeal consistency)' and Geber's ranking of animals above metals suggest reading 1; the printed word supports reading 2.
