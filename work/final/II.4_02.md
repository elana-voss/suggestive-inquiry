# II.4_02

*Atwood ends Apuleius' episode of the goddess's oracle and reads it, with Agrippa, Virgil's Aeneas, Proclus, Plato's Phaedrus, Plotinus and Iamblichus, as the final initiation: the purified soul turns inward, passes through a death-like liberation into the 'Paternal' source, and beholds the true gods as simple light, which she sharply distinguishes from self-made phantasms seen 'in Hades'.*

## Argument

- The goddess's oracle finished, the philosopher-narrator wakes in fear, joy and sweat, sprinkles himself with marine dew, and the sun rises and puts darkness to flight. `II.4 ¶18, II.4 n10` — QUOTED: Apuleius
- Atwood: the dragon shuns the sun's beams, the dead son lives, and the purified new vessel is consecrated in Light at Eleusis: not an artificial lamp for display or emblems, but a self-kindling 'gas-lamp' that holds, sustains and measures, burning in equilibrium about the fuel of all life. `II.4 ¶18` — ATWOOD
- Apuleius' further praise of the goddess (she rolls the heavens, illuminates the sun, treads on Tartarus; stars, hours and elements obey her) is admissible of the Universal Nature. `II.4 ¶18, II.4 ¶19, II.4 n11` — ATWOOD
- We are drawn out from, not cut off from, this life-giving fountain; foreign impressions, passions and evil generations defile our passive purity, hiding the inner light of which present life is a vestige. Revealed in a purified soul, it gives virtue, knowledge, health, long life and felicity, emits harmonious light, and finally draws the mind's eye into contact. This is the method of Self-Knowledge, the narrow way of regeneration. `II.4 ¶19` — ATWOOD
- Iamblichus: the initiated, seeing the Divine Nature, are like fishes drawn into clear air, languid and deprived of their co-nascent spirit. `II.4 ¶19, II.4 n12` — QUOTED: Iamblichus
- The vision in Hades belongs to that co-nascent spirit and is borne without much disturbing ordinary life; but when the 'central magnet' ascends, an expiration occurs: liberation through death-like agony, oscillating circulation, and the soul with its vehicle passes into elysian light. This is Lucius' rosy crown, shedding the beast's hide for the Divine Form of humanity. `II.4 ¶19` — ATWOOD
- Agrippa: leave flesh, human traditions and schools; enter into yourself, where all knowledge is compact; the first parent's sin brought oblivion; cast out Lethe and pass from light to light. Atwood glosses: senses, reason, faith, glorification of all. `II.4 ¶19, II.4 n13` — QUOTED: Agrippa
- Verses on Helius descending to Erebus, then a Virgilian passage, lead to Aeneas meeting his father in Elysium, where the whole Epopteia opens: the Universal Nature and the soul's essence, origin, hindrances and end. `II.4 ¶20, II.4 ¶21, II.4 ¶22, II.4 n14` — ATWOOD
- Per the Alexandrian Platonists, as 'Socrates explains in Phaedrus' (n15 cites Proclus): initiation to the Paternal abode opens all divine paths; telete precedes muesis, muesis epopteia, through perfective, connective and collective gods; many steps, all ending at the Paternal port. `II.4 ¶23, II.4 n15` — QUOTED: Socrates in Phaedrus (n15 cites Proclus)
- The paternal is the first source of life and the last initiation; its rebirth in the Free Ether is the end of initiatory rites. `II.4 ¶25` — ATWOOD
- Ascelpius Trallianus (via Taylor): Wisdom derives from clearness, clearness from light. Atwood: Minerva-Phosphor bears the Demiurgic Fire; all gods are this Fire manifest through Intellect, each an aspect (Minerva to Jupiter, back to Phanes). Empedocles: the god's members lay concealed in harmony until the ether moved. `II.4 ¶26, II.4 ¶27, II.4 ¶28, II.4 n16` — ATWOOD
- Plotinus: the gods are beautiful through Intellect; Truth nourishes them. Plato's Phaedrus: souls, pure and freed from the body, saw simple, stable visions in Pure Light with the gods. `II.4 ¶29, II.4 n17, II.4 n18` — QUOTED: Plotinus; Plato
- Against Warburton's 'illuminated image', Atwood says the initiated were joined to their leaders' deity. Wholeness comes from equilibriate circulation in the soul's Ether. True gods show as simple light, not figured phantasms like the mind's self-made Hades visions (Iamblichus, Proclus). `II.4 ¶29, II.4 n19, II.4 n20, II.4 n21` — ATWOOD
- Turning to herself, the soul finds herself, then understanding, then in the Adytum of Life the gods; all things are in us psychically; best is to still one's powers and unite with the ineffable. `II.4 ¶29, II.4 n22` — QUOTED: Proclus
- The ancients rejected visions from imperfect mental self-activity, separating enthusiasm from fanaticism and imagination from the light of the true gods. `II.4 ¶29` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **sign** — “The dragon shuns the sun’s beams which look through the crevices, and the dead son lives” `II.4 ¶18` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A turn from darkness to light at the moment of awakening: the dragon shuns the light and the dead son revives. This chunk does not explain the dragon or the son.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶18: placed directly after 'the sun arose, and put to flight the darkness of black night' in the Lucius narrative; no gloss given.
- **subject** — “the new vessel, purified and holy, is brought into the Eleusinian temple, to be consecrated in Light” `II.4 ¶18` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A purified container is consecrated in Light; Atwood insists it is not an artificial lamp or teaching device but something that holds, sustains and measures, and kindles a perpetual flame within itself, burning in balance about the fuel of life.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶18 negation ('Not ... a crystal night-lamp or magic lanthorn') and positive description; what the vessel is is not named (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “a far more pellucid gas-lamp, an infallible gasometer, able to hold and sustain and measure simultaneously, even within itself to kindle a perpetual flame” `II.4 ¶18`
  - Also: “shining in equilibriate constancy about the sufficient fuel of all life” `II.4 ¶18`
- **principle** — “we are not cut off from this fountain, but attracted out from it” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Human beings remain connected to the Universal Nature that perpetually supplies life; we exist by its influence.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19: 'which supplies all things with life perpetually, so that we are what we are by its influence'.
- **impediment** — “receiving the impressure of foreign forms, passions, accidents, and evil generations, the passive purity is defiled and obscured” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** External impressions, passions and accidents defile the soul's receptive purity, so it becomes unconscious of its inner light.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 continues: 'and unconscious of that inner light which lives in reality'.
  - Also: “unconscious of that inner light which lives in reality” `II.4 ¶19`
- **result** — “whose spontaneous revelation in a purified soul imparts virtue with understanding, and universal knowledge, health of body, and long length of days” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** When the inner light reveals itself in a purified soul it gives virtue, understanding, universal knowledge, bodily health, long life, riches and felicity.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 lists the effects directly; the precondition is a 'purified soul'.
- **sign** — “It also emits light accompanied with harmony of intellection” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Successive signs of the revelation: light with harmonious understanding, then a very rarified radiant form; the steadily attending mind is suddenly drawn into contact with it and cannot remain separate.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 describes the sequence explicitly ('also emits ... and finally exhibits').
  - Also: “the eye of mind, all the while faithfully regarding, is drawn to contact suddenly, unable longer to sustain itself alone” `II.4 ¶19`
- **principle** — “This is the method and arcane principle of Self- Knowledge” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Atwood names this process (purification, inner revelation, contact) as the method of Self-Knowledge and the way of regeneration.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19: 'and the narrow way of regeneration into life'.
  - Also: “the narrow way of regeneration into life” `II.4 ¶19`
- **sign** — “the initiated, when surveying it, are affected in the same manner as fishes, when they are drawn upwards from the dark and turbid waters into the diaphanous clear air” `II.4 ¶19` (QUOTED: Iamblichus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Seeing the Divine Nature makes the initiated languid and deprives them of the use of their co-nascent spirit.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19: 'becoming languid as soon as they perceive it'; Iamblichus via n12.
  - Also: “becoming languid as soon as they perceive it” `II.4 ¶19`
- **stage** — “For to this spirit the vision in Hades is allied which is born be through without much disturbance of the common life” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The lower vision 'in Hades' belongs to the co-nascent spirit and can be undergone without much disrupting ordinary life, unlike the later ascent.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 contrasts it with what happens 'when the central magnet moves to the ascent'. Wording 'born be through' appears corrupt; 'undergone' assumes 'borne through' (see ambiguities).
- **stage** — “when the central magnet moves to the ascent, this expiration is described as taking place; a liberation is effected through agony, as it were of death” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** In the higher stage the 'central magnet' moves to ascent and an 'expiration' takes place: liberation through a death-like agony, the circulation oscillates, and the soul joined with its vehicle passes free of bodily hindrance into the elysian light. 'Central magnet' is not explained here.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 describes the sequence directly; the identity of the 'central magnet' is unstated (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “the circulation oscillates, and the soul, coalescing with its vehicle, transcends free from corporeal hindrance into the elysian light” `II.4 ¶19`
- **result** — “That was the rosy crown of which the Hierophant was to assist Apuleius’ Lucius to partake” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Lucius' rosy crown = the liberation into elysian light just described; putting off the hide of 'that worst of beasts' = re-entering the Divine Form of humanity.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19: 'That was' refers back to the liberation into elysian light; 'when he was enabled to put off the hide of that worst of beasts'.
  - Also: “put off the hide of that worst of beasts” `II.4 ¶19`
- **procedure** — “entering not either into the schools of other philosophers, but into yourself, ye shall know all things, for the knowledge of all things is compact in you” `II.4 ¶19` (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - **Plain (stated):** Knowledge is sought by entering into oneself, not from human traditions or schools, because the soul contains all forms and knowledges.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 (Agrippa, n13); Atwood adopts it, glossing his 'light to light'.
  - Also: “he created the soul as a reasonable tree full of forms and knowledges” `II.4 ¶19`
  - Also: “be you loosed from the darkness of the flesh and blood” `II.4 ¶19`
- **impediment** — “oblivion, the mother of ignorance, stepped in” `II.4 ¶19` (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - **Plain (stated):** Forgetfulness (through the first parent's sin) is the obstacle; one must remove the veil of understanding, reject Lethe, and wait for the True Light.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19 (Agrippa): 'Set you then now aside ... the veil of your understanding'; 'wait for the True Light'.
  - Also: “Cast out the drink of Lethe, you who have made yourselves drunken with forgetfulness” `II.4 ¶19`
- **stage** — “ye shall pass from the light to light” `II.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Ascent runs from sense-light to reason, from reason through its highest faith to the glorification of all.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶19: Atwood glosses it as 'from the light of the senses to the illumination of reason, and from reason through its topmost faith into the substantive glorification of all'.
  - Also: “from the light of the senses to the illumination of reason, and from reason through its topmost faith into the substantive glorification of all” `II.4 ¶19`
- **result** — “has the whole Epopteia opened to him” `II.4 ¶21` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The final revelation shows the Universal Nature, its hidden foundation, and the soul's essence, origin, hindrances and end.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶21 defines it: 'the Pantheistic revealment of the Universal Nature, her secret foundation, the soul’s essence, origin, hindrances, and proper end'.
  - Also: “the Pantheistic revealment of the Universal Nature, her secret foundation, the soul’s essence, origin, hindrances, and proper end” `II.4 ¶21`
- **stage** — “For telete precedes muesis, and muesis, epopteia” `II.4 ¶23` (QUOTED: Socrates in Phaedrus (n15 cites Proclus))
  - **Plain (stated):** Three ordered grades: being initiated by the perfective gods; viewing with closed eye (the pure soul) through the connective gods; becoming fixed spectators of the intelligible watch-tower through the collective gods.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶23 glosses each verb (teleioumetha, muoumetha, epopteuomen).
  - Also: “But we view with closed eye, i.e., with the pure soul itself (muoumetha) entire and stable appearances” `II.4 ¶23`
  - Also: “we become fixed in, and spectators of (epopteuomen) the intelligible watch-tower” `II.4 ¶23`
- **result** — “there are indeed many steps of ascent, but all of them extend to the Paternal port and the Paternal Initiation” `II.4 ¶23` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** All stages lead to the Paternal principle: the first source of life and the last initiation. Its rebirth in the Free Ether is the goal of the rites.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶25: 'the paternal is the first source of life, and the last into which the conscience is initiated'.
  - Also: “the rebirth and recreation of this principle in the Free Ether, prepared for it, is the end and plenitude of initiatory rites” `II.4 ¶25`
  - Also: “This initiation to the Paternal abode” `II.4 ¶23`
- **principle** — “what is Wisdom? We reply, that it is a certain clearness, as being that which renders all things conspicuous” `II.4 ¶26` (QUOTED: Ascelpius Trallianus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Wisdom is clearness, and clearness comes from light: it brings hidden things out of ignorance into knowledge.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶26 (Ascelpius Trallianus via Taylor).
  - Also: “From whence was this word clearness denominated? We reply, from light.” `II.4 ¶26`
- **agent** — “what are all the gods but manifestations of this same Fire germinating through the projecting energy of Intellect distinct in Light?” `II.4 ¶26` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The gods are aspects of one Demiurgic Fire showing itself through Intellect: Minerva = its lucid understanding, Apollo = golden ideality, Venus = beauty and attraction, Mars = concentrated force, Diana = compact purity, Mercury = varied thought and imagination, Jupiter = fabricative virtue.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶26 gives each identification directly.
  - Also: “Minerva is sometimes called Phosphor, as being the bearer and measure of the Demiurgic Fire” `II.4 ¶26`
- **stage** — “by the fanning of the celestial ether set in motion, Then all the members of the god appeared” `II.4 ¶28` (QUOTED: Empedocles)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** What lay concealed in dense harmony becomes manifest when the ether is set in motion.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶27–¶28 (Empedocles, n16): 'in dense harmony established lay Concealed' until ether moved; Atwood places it after the gods-as-Fire passage. The chunk does not apply it to the work explicitly.
  - Also: “But in dense harmony established lay Concealed” `II.4 ¶27`
- **operator** — “being ourselves pure and liberated from this surrounding vestment, which we denominate body, and to which we are bound like an oyster to its shell” `II.4 ¶29` (QUOTED: Plato)
  - **Plain (stated):** The true visions in Pure Light are seen when the soul is pure and freed from the body.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29 (Plato, Phaedrus, n18).
- **result** — “the initiated were conjoined with the total deity and intellectual perfection of their leaders, and were replenished with the divine essentiality” `II.4 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Phaedrus visions were not a priest's illuminated image but real union with the divine leaders.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29: Atwood rejects Warburton's 'a mere illuminated image, which the priest had purified'.
- **principle** — “the being entire is derived to souls from equilibriate circulation in their Ether; which contains, and is connective of all the Divine genera” `II.4 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The soul's wholeness comes from balanced circulation in its own Ether, which holds and connects all the divine kinds.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29.
- **sign** — “reveal themselves each in his particular form or essence of light, but by no means extend themselves as figured phantasms, such as the mind before beheld in Hades from its own self-shadowing creative fancy” `II.4 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Test of a true vision: the gods appear as simple light or idea, not as figured images; figured images belong to the earlier Hades stage and come from the mind's own imagination.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29, supported by Iamblichus (n20) 'emits illuminations, true representations of himself' and Atwood's closing contrast of imaginative vision with the light of the true gods.
  - Also: “does Divinity either transform himself into phantasms nor extend these from himself to other things, but emits illuminations, true representations of himself” `II.4 ¶29`
  - Also: “they exhibit to Theurgists a Fire which is itself, to itself, visible” `II.4 ¶29`
- **impediment** — “if various phantasms are hurled forth before the presence of them; nor if they, being uniform, should in their appearance be multiform” `II.4 ¶29` (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Varied phantasms may appear before the gods' presence; they come from nature and the demiurgic intellect producing images, not from the gods themselves.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29 (Proclus, n21): 'For nature and the demiurgic intellect extend corporeal formed images of things corporeal, sensible images of things intelligible'.
- **procedure** — “when she converts herself to herself, she evolves her own Essence, and the vivific reasons which she contains” `II.4 ¶29` (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Turning inward in stages: the soul first perceives herself, then finds understanding and the Reason of created beings, then in her innermost 'Adytum of Life' perceives the gods with closed eye. Looking outward shows only shadows.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29 (attributed via n22 'Idem' to Proclus) gives the sequence directly.
  - Also: “she proceeds into her interior recesses and into the Adytum of Life, as the great theologist declares, she perceives, with the eye closed, as it were, the genus of the gods” `II.4 ¶29`
  - Also: “all things are in us psychically, that is to say, in the efficient Reason of our life” `II.4 ¶29`
- **procedure** — “having our individual powers at rest, to revolve harmoniously round it, to excite all the multitude of the soul to this union” `II.4 ¶29` (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Best use of one's energy: still individual faculties, circle the divine in harmony, gather the soul's many parts into union, and set aside everything below the One.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29 (n22).
  - Also: “to become seated and conjoined to that which is ineffable and beyond all things” `II.4 ¶29`
- **principle** — “dismiss all visions which take place during the imperfect self-activity of the human mind as arbitrary and untrustworthy” `II.4 ¶29` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Visions produced while the mind is still acting on its own are untrustworthy; the ancients firmly separated enthusiasm from fanaticism and imaginative vision from the light of the true gods.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶29 closing.
  - Also: “between enthusiasm and fanaticism, between the shadowy world of imaginative vision and the light of the true gods” `II.4 ¶29`

## Terms

- **Universal Nature** — The life-supplying source to which Apuleius' praise of Isis applies; we are drawn out of it and exist by its influence; revealed in the Epopteia. (ATWOOD)
  - “All which is readily admissible of the Universal Nature” `II.4 ¶19`
- **Causal fountain** (also: Causal Fountain) — Source of all things; riches come from it; the true gods are its emanative splendour. (ATWOOD)
  - “riches as from the Causal fountain of all things” `II.4 ¶19`
  - “as an emanative splendour from the Causal Fountain, make manifest in energy its Intellectual Law” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Self-Knowledge** — Name Atwood gives to the method of inner revelation in a purified soul; the narrow way of regeneration. (ATWOOD)
  - “the narrow way of regeneration into life” `II.4 ¶19`
- **co-nascent spirit** — A spirit born with us, whose use the initiated lose on seeing the Divine Nature; the vision in Hades is allied to it. (ATWOOD)
  - “For to this spirit the vision in Hades is allied” `II.4 ¶19`
- **vision in Hades** (also: Hades) — Lower visionary stage tied to the co-nascent spirit, endured without much disturbing ordinary life; its figured images come from the mind's own fancy. (ATWOOD)
  - “such as the mind before beheld in Hades from its own self-shadowing creative fancy” `II.4 ¶29`
- **vehicle** — That with which the soul coalesces as it passes into elysian light; not further defined here. (ATWOOD)
  - “the soul, coalescing with its vehicle, transcends free from corporeal hindrance into the elysian light” `II.4 ¶19`
- **elysian light** (also: Elysian fields) — The state into which the liberated soul passes; the Elysian fields are where Aeneas meets his father and receives the Epopteia. (ATWOOD)
  - “going forth to meet his father in the Elysian fields” `II.4 ¶21`
- **Epopteia** (also: epopteia, epopteuomen) — Final grade of initiation, after telete and muesis: being fixed as spectators of the intelligible; its content is the revelation of the Universal Nature and the soul. (ATWOOD)
  - “For telete precedes muesis, and muesis, epopteia” `II.4 ¶23`
- **telete** (also: teleioumetha) — First grade: being initiated in ascending by the perfective gods. (QUOTED: Socrates in Phaedrus (n15 cites Proclus))
  - “we are initiated (teleioumetha) in ascending by the perfective gods” `II.4 ¶23`
- **muesis** (also: muoumetha) — Second grade: viewing with closed eye, i.e. with the pure soul itself, entire and stable appearances through the connective gods. (QUOTED: Socrates in Phaedrus (n15 cites Proclus))
  - “But we view with closed eye, i.e., with the pure soul itself (muoumetha) entire and stable appearances” `II.4 ¶23`
- **Paternal** (also: paternal, Paternal abode, Paternal port, Paternal Initiation) — The first source of life and the last initiation; goal of all steps of ascent; its rebirth in the Free Ether ends the rites. (ATWOOD)
  - “the paternal is the first source of life, and the last into which the conscience is initiated” `II.4 ¶25`
- **Ether** (also: Free Ether, celestial ether, Ethereal Hypostasis) — Medium in which the paternal principle is reborn; its movement makes the concealed god manifest; balanced circulation in the soul's Ether gives wholeness and connects the divine kinds. (ATWOOD)
  - “the being entire is derived to souls from equilibriate circulation in their Ether; which contains, and is connective of all the Divine genera” `II.4 ¶29`
  - “filling the Ethereal Hypostasis with acme and power” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Wisdom** — Clearness derived from light, which brings concealed things into knowledge. (QUOTED: Ascelpius Trallianus)
  - = clearness: “what is Wisdom? We reply, that it is a certain clearness, as being that which renders all things conspicuous” `II.4 ¶26`
- **Demiurgic Fire** (also: Fire) — The fire that the gods manifest through Intellect in Light; Minerva as Phosphor bears and measures it. Theurgists are shown a self-visible Fire. (ATWOOD)
  - “Minerva is sometimes called Phosphor, as being the bearer and measure of the Demiurgic Fire” `II.4 ¶26`
  - “they exhibit to Theurgists a Fire which is itself, to itself, visible” `II.4 ¶29`
- **gods** (also: true gods) — Manifestations of the Demiurgic Fire through Intellect, each an aspect; made visible and nourished by Intellect, an intelligible union and Truth; they appear as simple light, not phantasms. (ATWOOD)
  - = manifestations of the Demiurgic Fire: “what are all the gods but manifestations of this same Fire germinating through the projecting energy of Intellect distinct in Light?” `II.4 ¶26`
  - = unities of all being: “the genus of the gods, which are the unities of all being” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Intellect** (also: intellect) — That through which the gods are beautiful and visible by its Light. (QUOTED: Plotinus)
  - “What else, however, is it but Intellect through which they are such?” `II.4 ¶29`
- **phantasms** (also: figured phantasms) — Figured images, produced by the mind's own fancy or by nature and the demiurgic intellect; not the gods' true self-manifestation. (ATWOOD)
  - “if various phantasms are hurled forth before the presence of them” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Adytum of Life** — The soul's innermost recess, where she perceives with closed eye the genus of the gods. (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - “into her interior recesses and into the Adytum of Life” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Theurgists** — Those to whom the gods show a Fire visible to itself. (QUOTED: Iamblichus)
  - “they exhibit to Theurgists a Fire which is itself, to itself, visible” `II.4 ¶29`
- **enthusiasm** — Distinguished by the ancients from fanaticism, in parallel with the line between imaginative vision and the light of the true gods; not otherwise defined here. (ATWOOD)
  - “between enthusiasm and fanaticism” `II.4 ¶29`
- **Lethe** — Drink of forgetfulness, to be cast out. (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - “Cast out the drink of Lethe” `II.4 ¶19`

## Sources cited

- **Apuleius**, *Metam., Book 11* — “Thou rollest the heavens round the steady poles, dost illuminate the sun, govern the world, and tread on the dark realms of Tartarus.” `II.4 ¶18, II.4 ¶19, II.4 n10, II.4 n11` — Supports: Lucius' awakening after the Isis oracle and praise of the goddess, which Atwood applies to the Universal Nature; Lucius' rosy crown and shedding the beast's hide illustrate the soul's liberation.
- **Taylor**, *translations cited by page; 'Taylor’s notes to his Pausanias'* `II.4 n10, II.4 n12, II.4 ¶26, II.4 n18, II.4 n19, II.4 n20` — Reference only (cited by page in n10, n12, n18, n20; his Pausanias notes supply the Ascelpius extract; n19 points to his note on the Phaedrus passage, no marker in the text).
- **Iamblichus**, *on the Mysteries* — “By no means therefore, says Iamblichus, does Divinity either transform himself into phantasms” `II.4 ¶19, II.4 ¶29, II.4 n12, II.4 n20` — Supports: effect of the divine vision on the initiated (fishes image); the gods emit true illuminations, not phantasms; truth with the god as light with the sun.
- **Agrippa**, *Vanity of the Sciences* — “entering not either into the schools of other philosophers, but into yourself, ye shall know all things” `II.4 ¶19, II.4 n13` — Supports: knowledge by entering into oneself; oblivion as impediment; passage from light to light.
- **the Apostle** — “and from glory to glory” `II.4 ¶19` — Supports Agrippa's 'light to light'.
- **Aeneid**, *Aeneid, Lib. 6: 724* — “Spiritus intus alit” `II.4 ¶21, II.4 ¶22, II.4 n14` — Illustrates: Aeneas meeting his father in Elysium receives the Epopteia of Universal Nature; spirit and mind pervading the whole.
- **unattributed verse (Helius)** — “To Erebus he went, and the sad realms of night” `II.4 ¶19, II.4 ¶20` — Illustrates descent to the realm of night and the ancestors, leading to Aeneas.

## Ambiguities

- “the new vessel, purified and holy, is brought into the Eleusinian temple, to be consecrated in Light” `II.4 ¶18`
  - Reading: The purified soul or its vehicle, consecrated in the inner light.
  - Reading: The subject or container of the work generally, described as self-kindling and self-measuring.
  - Basis: (1) II.4 ¶19 'spontaneous revelation in a purified soul' and 'the soul, coalescing with its vehicle'; ¶29 'equilibriate circulation in their Ether' echoes 'equilibriate constancy' in ¶18. (2) ¶18 itself names only a 'vessel' that holds, sustains, measures and kindles a flame.
- “when the central magnet moves to the ascent” `II.4 ¶19`
  - Reading: A central attracting principle in the soul that draws it upward.
  - Reading: Unspecified; the chunk does not define it.
  - Basis: ¶19 earlier speaks of the 'attractive subtilty of the Divine Nature' and the mind's eye being 'drawn to contact'; no explicit gloss.
- “which is born be through without much disturbance of the common life” `II.4 ¶19`
  - Reading: 'borne through': the Hades vision is endured without much disturbing ordinary life.
  - Reading: 'born': the vision arises without much disturbance.
  - Basis: Text appears corrupt; the contrast with the agony of the ascent favours either reading equally.
- “as the great theologist declares” `II.4 ¶29`
  - Reading: Proclus, to whom n22 ('Idem') attributes the passage.
  - Reading: An authority cited by Proclus within the passage.
  - Basis: n22 'Idem., Chap. 3' follows n21 'On the Theology'; the phrase 'the great theologist' itself is unnamed.
