# I.3_03

*Section Third of Hermes' text (main text): the matter is bound so that spirits inhabit the washed bodies, the dead elements revive, darkness follows the generated light, the king and red daughter are married in a gentle fire, and the son takes his tincture from the fire and is clothed in red. Atwood's footnotes gloss it through the Scholiast and adepts: binding by affinity of body and spirit, the dissolution and multiplication of the medicine, the despised catholic nature, the agricultural analogy of the seed of gold, the secret philosophic fire, putrefaction as the gate of every new form, and a closing pointer to the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge.*

## Argument

- Philosophers bind their matter with a strong chain to contend with the Fire; spirits dwell in the washed bodies, vivify them, and stay inseparably. `I.3 ¶29` — QUOTED: Hermes
- As Democritus' 'manacles and fetters': the constriction works by affinity of body and spirit, from one fountain; the agent, vivifying and holding the particles, compels the 'Protean Hypostasis of Nature' into its true Form. `I.3 n37` — ATWOOD
- Dead elements revive; composed bodies tinge, working permanent wonders. `I.3 ¶30` — QUOTED: Hermes
- Scholiast: bodies house spirits that purify them and wake dormant life, which acts only once bodies dissolve into their radical source; then it tinges imperfect bodies permanently. The medicine tinges 10, 100, 1000 parts, gaining virtue with each dissolution. `I.3 n38` — QUOTED: Scholiast (endorsed)
- The permanent watery Form, creatrix of regal elements, obtains the tincture by moderate regimen and rests. `I.3 ¶31` — QUOTED: Hermes
- This is the earlier watery form matured, Trevisan's fountain, drawing in its agent's rubified light. Eiraneaus: separating light from darkness seven times completes the philosophic mercury; then rest, then a purifying supernatural son. `I.3 n39` — ATWOOD
- The precious stone on the dunghill is made vile; mortify two Argent vives; venerate those of Auripigment and Magnesia. `I.3 ¶32` — QUOTED: Hermes
- The catholic nature, precious when exalted, is defiled, found in putrefactions, despised; Hermes and Moses veil it. Light or sulfur is the seed of gold, which like grain grows from its middle principle and must be planted in its ethereal vehicle. `I.3 n40` — ATWOOD
- Agriculture almost points out the method. New Light: our lune receives gold's seed; continual fire seven or ten months; milk of the earth, salt of nature; an infant of the second generation purifies its matrix. The light dies many times yet enriches its soil; seven repetitions precede the Quintessence's resurrection. `I.3 n40` — ATWOOD
- Nature contains and separates natures in a middle principle; the Stone, generated with light, brings forth darkness, mother of all things. `I.3 ¶33` — QUOTED: Hermes
- King and red daughter marry in gentle fire; their son lives by our fire. Fire on foliated sulphur; the boundary of hearts is washed; tincture red as flesh; death and darkness flee. `I.3 ¶34` — QUOTED: Hermes
- Quintessences reunite for fructification; as Isis fed Horus with fire, nurses are nursed. Men know only common fire; the philosophers' fire is magical, creative, vital, secret. `I.3 n41` — ATWOOD
- The 'gate of blackness': knowing it is knowing all, as putrefaction precedes every new form. The fire vivifies and purges its vehicle, leaving a flesh-red tincture, coagulated blood, the 'boundary of hearts'. `I.3 n42` — ATWOOD
- The Dragon shuns sunbeams; the son revives from the fire; virgin's milk whitens; the son, treasury of the Philosophic Matter, ends death's reign, clothed in red. `I.3 ¶35` — QUOTED: Hermes
- The Dragon hides as internal light manifests; Solve, Coagula, Fige; the son is quintessential nature personified. `I.3 n43` — ATWOOD
- The manifest light must enter another life and die again to transmute foul matter; the next chapter repeats the work in other images. `I.3 n44` — ATWOOD
- Fermenting light leavens by constant addition of spirit. `I.3 n45` — ATWOOD
- The hidden vital light, drawn forth and re-entering inwards, may meet itself deeper: the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge, sole way to divine things; deferred. `I.3 n46` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **procedure** — “the philosophers bind up their matter with a strong chain, that it may contend with the Fire” `I.3 ¶29` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The matter is held fast, so it can endure the fire; this constriction is made not by chance but by the affinity between the body and its own spirit.
  - Basis: I.3 n37 glosses: 'this constriction ... is not made by chance, but by means of the affinity which is between the body and its spirit'.
  - Also: “makes use of manacles and fetters, laying hold on her in the extremest degree” `I.3 n37`
- **agent** — “the agent, because it vivifies and holds the particles of the matter together, is repesentatively superior in operation, to compel the Protean Hypostasis of Nature to enter into his true Form” `I.3 n37` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Body and spirit share one source, but of the two the agent is superior in operation: it gives life, holds the matter's particles together, and compels the 'Protean Hypostasis of Nature' into its true form.
  - Basis: I.3 n37: 'they both proceed form one fountain, though, of the two, the agent ... is repesentatively superior in operation'.
- **subject** — “the spirits in the washed bodies desire to dwell therein and rejoice in them” `I.3 ¶29` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The bodies are houses for their spirits; when the spirits are received, the bodies' earthy substance is thinned and purified and their dormant life and fire are awakened.
  - Basis: I.3 n38: 'The bodies of the metals ... are the domiciles of their spirits, which when they are received by the bodies, their terrestrial substance is by degrees made thin, extended, and purified'.
  - Also: “when these spirits are united to them, they vivify them and inhabit them” `I.3 ¶29`
- **procedure** — “unless the bodies (i.e., the sensible and vegetable media of life) be first dissolved and turned into their radical source” `I.3 n38` (QUOTED: Scholiast)
  - **Plain (stated):** The life in the matter is asleep and cannot act until its 'bodies' (explained as the sensible and vegetable media of life) are dissolved back into their root source.
  - Basis: I.3 n38: 'the life which dwells in the metals is laid, as it were, asleep (in sense), nor can it exert its powers ... unless the bodies ... be first dissolved'.
- **result** — “the dead elements are revived, the composed bodies tinge and are altered, and operate wonderful works which are permanent” `I.3 ¶30` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once dissolved and revived, the bodies, by their own internal light, pass their tinging power to imperfect bodies and change them into a fixed, permanent substance.
  - Basis: I.3 n38: 'by the abundance of their internal light, they communicate their tinging property to other imperfect bodies, transmuting them into a fixed and permanent substantive'.
- **result** — “one part thereof will tinge ten parts of an imperfect body, then one hundred, then a thousand, and so infinitely on” `I.3 n38` (QUOTED: Scholiast)
  - **Plain (stated):** The medicine multiplies: each repeated dissolution increases its strength; without further dissolution it stays at its simple level.
  - Basis: I.3 n38: 'by how much the oftener the medicine is dissolved, by so much more it increases in virtue'.
  - Also: “Here there is a celestial and divine fountain set open, which no man is able to draw dry” `I.3 n38`
- **subject** — “O, permanent watery Form, creatrix of the regal elements!” `I.3 ¶31` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The fixed watery form of the philosophic matter, a more mature state of the watery form praised earlier; it 'frames the royal elements' by drawing into itself the reddened light of its own internal agent throughout its whole substance.
  - Basis: I.3 n39: 'The fixed watery Form of the philosophic matter ... is the same as was before celebrated only more mature'; 'It is the Framer of the royal elements ... i.e., it draws to itself the rubified light of its internal agent'.
  - Also: “this is the fountain which Berhard Trevisan mentions” `I.3 n39`
- **stage** — “the light from the darkness seven times, and the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete” `I.3 n39` (QUOTED: Eiraneaus)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Light is to be separated from darkness seven times; this completes the philosophic mercury, followed by a rest ('Sabbath') and, within a year-long cycle, the birth of a 'son' who purifies his brethren. This chunk does not say concretely what the light and darkness are.
  - Basis: I.3 n39 (Eiraneaus) gives the sequence; the meaning of the 'son' as the purifying product is supported by 'to purify his brethren from their original sin' (n39) and the son's role in ¶34-35.
  - Also: “thou mayest expect the generation of the supernatural son of Sun” `I.3 n39`
- **subject** — “Our most precious stone cast forth upon the dunghill, being most dear is made altogether vile” `I.3 ¶32` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The subject is a universal ('catholic') nature that is precious when exalted but in ordinary life is defiled, found in decay and the lowest forms of life, despised and unrecognized.
  - Basis: I.3 n40: 'The same catholic nature, which in its preternatural exaltation appears so very precious ... is in the common world defiled; abiding everywhere in putrefactions and the vilest forms of life'.
- **procedure** — “it behooves us to mortify two Argent vives together” `I.3 ¶32` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Two 'Argent vives' are to be put to death together; also named are the Argent vive of Auripigment and the oriental Argent vive of Magnesia, to be venerated. This chunk does not explain which they are.
  - Basis: Unexplained in this chunk; 'mortify' read as 'put to death' in line with the repeated death-and-corruption stages in n40, n42, n44.
  - Also: “to venerate the Argent vive of Auripigment, and the oriental Argent vive of Magnesia” `I.3 ¶32`
- **agent** — “light or sulfur, as they call it, is the true form or seed of gold, and the concentering virtue of their philosophic stone” `I.3 n40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** What the adepts call 'sulfur' is light; it is the seed of gold and the concentrating power of the stone.
  - Basis: I.3 n40, directly.
- **principle** — “needs only to be planted in its proper ethereal vehicle, well prepared and fallow to bring forth its virtue in manifold increase” `I.3 n40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** As grain grows from its own specific form when sown in earth, the seed of gold (light) must be set in a properly prepared ethereal medium to multiply; the farming analogy is said almost to point out the method.
  - Basis: I.3 n40: grain's 'eduction is in its middle principle, that is to say, in the specificative form'; 'the analogy ... point out the method of its application'.
  - Also: “the Alchemical art has been continually compared to agriculture” `I.3 n40`
- **procedure** — “our lune or silver, not common silver, is that which receives the seed of the gold” `I.3 n40` (QUOTED: author of the New Light)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A receiving vessel or matrix ('our lune', not common silver) takes the seed of gold; it is then kept under continual fire for seven, sometimes ten, philosophical months until the water consumes three parts and leaves one, then nourished ('milk of the earth') and preserved from rot ('salt of nature'). These further names are not explained in this chunk.
  - Basis: I.3 n40 (New Light): roles stated (lune receives seed; fire governs; milk nourishes; salt preserves); 'matrix' from 'put again into its own matrix' (n40).
  - Also: “governed by our continual fire for seven months (philosophical), and sometimes ten, until our water consume three and leave one” `I.3 n40`
- **stage** — “it must many times, therefore, suffer itself to be eclipsed, and die and corrupt” `I.3 n40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The work is cyclic: the metallic light dies and decays many times; each new generation, its seed returned to its matrix, purifies and enriches the ground rather than exhausting it; repeated seven times before the Quintessence rises into a permanent form of life.
  - Basis: I.3 n40: 'the ethereal seed ... tends always to improve its generation, fertilizing by the return of each successive growth'; 'repeated seven times before the final resurrection of the Quintessence'.
  - Also: “the infant of the second generation is produced” `I.3 n40`
- **stage** — “The Stone comes with light, and with light it is generated, and then it generates and brings forth the black clouds or darkness, which is the mother of all things” `I.3 ¶33` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** After the light is generated, a dark phase follows; this darkness is generative, not mere loss.
  - Basis: ¶33 order (light, then black clouds as 'mother of all things'); I.3 n42: 'putrefaction precedes the generation of every new form'.
- **sign** — “O happy gate of blackness, cries the sage, which art the passage to this so glorious a change” `I.3 n42` (QUOTED: the sage (endorsed by Atwood))
  - **Plain (stated):** Blackness, i.e. putrefaction, is the necessary passage to the transformation; knowing this is called the whole secret.
  - Basis: I.3 n42: 'to know this is to know all'; 'For putrefaction precedes the generation of every new form into existence'.
- **procedure** — “when we marry the crowned king to our red daughter, and in a gentle fire, not hurtful, she doth conceive a son” `I.3 ¶34` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The newly born quintessences are reunited so that they bear fruit; what had nourished is now itself nourished, by a gentle heat like the heavenly fire, not ordinary boiling.
  - Basis: I.3 n41: 'The new-born Quintessences are here shown to be reunited for fructification'; 'not in the vulgar way of decocting, but conformable to the heavenly fire'.
  - Also: “as the fable relates to Isis, that she brought forth Horus, even feeding him with fire” `I.3 n41`
- **agent** — “Our fire is a most subtle fire, inhabiting in himself an infernal secret fire” `I.3 n41` (QUOTED: the adept)
  - **Plain (stated):** The fire of the work is not ordinary fire: it is called magical, creative and vital, discerning, very subtle and volatile.
  - Basis: I.3 n41: 'The philosophers uniformly distinguish their own special fire as magical, creative, vital; whereas the common element is without sagacity or discrimination'.
  - Also: “he lives by our fire” `I.3 ¶34`
- **impediment** — “men neither see nor do they believe there is any other fire but the common fire, nor any other sulfur or mercury” `I.3 n41` (QUOTED: the adept)
  - **Plain (stated):** The chief error: taking the fire, sulfur and mercury of the texts as the ordinary substances.
  - Basis: I.3 n41: 'thus they are deceived by their own opinions'.
- **operator** — “It is the business of the philosophic fire not only to vivify, but also to depurate and segregate the heterogeneity of its vehiculum” `I.3 n42` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The fire both gives life and purifies, separating out what is foreign in its carrier; then a pure red tincture of flesh-and-blood colour appears in the residue.
  - Basis: I.3 n42: 'which being done there appears at length in the faeces, a most pure and rubicund tincture'.
- **procedure** — “when thou shalt send forth fire upon the foliated sulphur, the boundary of hearts doth enter in above it, let it be washed from the same, and the refined matter thereof be extracted” `I.3 ¶34` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Fire is applied to the 'foliated sulphur'; what enters is washed and its refined part extracted. 'Foliated sulphur' is not explained in this chunk; 'boundary of hearts' see next entry.
  - Basis: ¶34 sequence only; washing/extraction parallels the fire's work of depurating and segregating (n42).
- **result** — “the boundary of hearts” `I.3 ¶34` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The tincture itself, described as coagulated blood: the thing sought, which satisfies when attained.
  - Basis: I.3 n42: 'our tincture is of blood coagulated, which blood is the boundary or satisfaction of hearts, as Hermes alludes, the object sought for'.
  - Also: “his tincture by help of the fire remains red, as flesh” `I.3 ¶34`
- **sign** — “The Dragon, who watches the crevices, shuns the sunbeams” `I.3 ¶35` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The 'Dragon' withdraws and is hidden as the internal light rises into manifestation.
  - Basis: I.3 n43: 'this Dragon ... which becomes so occultated in the rising of the internal light to manifestation'. Its nature is said to have been discussed earlier (not in this chunk).
- **sign** — “the virgin's milk is whitened” `I.3 ¶35` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A whitening that accompanies the son's coming forth from the fire. Not explained in this chunk.
  - Basis: ¶35 context only.
- **result** — “this son is himself the treasury, even himself bearing the Philosophic Matter” `I.3 ¶35` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (stated):** The 'son' is the whole quintessential nature gathered into one, as if personified, carrying the light of life to spread it universally.
  - Basis: I.3 n43: 'is he not in truth the whole quintessential nature concentrated, as it were personified, bearing in hand the golden light of life'.
  - Also: “our son, king-born, takes his tincture from the fire” `I.3 ¶34`
- **stage** — “he is invested with the red garment, and the purple is put on” `I.3 ¶35` (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Final reddening: death's reign ends and the son is clothed in red; in the footnote's terms the internal light is now manifest to sense.
  - Basis: ¶34 tincture 'red, as flesh'; ¶35 'the reign of death is finished'; I.3 n44 'The internal light, once made manifest to sense'.
  - Also: “death even and darkness, and the waters flee away” `I.3 ¶34`
- **procedure** — “Solve, Coagula, Fige” `I.3 n43` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Dissolve, coagulate, fix: dissolve the fixed, make the dissolved volatile, fix the volatile.
  - Basis: Based on our translation of the Latin verse in I.3 n43; the book gives no translation.
- **stage** — “ready for the perscrutination of another life into which it must be induced toenter, to suffer again, and die in order to transmute the foul material into itself” `I.3 n44` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once the internal light is manifest, the cycle begins again: it is led into another life, suffers and dies, so as to convert gross material into its own nature. The next chapter describes the same work in different images.
  - Basis: I.3 n44, directly.
- **principle** — “The fermenting light by constant addition of the spirit, leavens more and more” `I.3 n45` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The light acts as a ferment and grows by continued additions of spirit, tending toward its final purpose.
  - Basis: I.3 n45, directly.
- **principle** — “inquire into the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge, which alone can enable man to know and understand and possess divine things” `I.3 n46` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The vital light hidden at nature's centre, once drawn out and turned back inward, may 'meet itself' in a deeper experience; Atwood names this the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge and the only route to divine knowledge, but postpones discussing it.
  - Basis: I.3 n46: 're-entering from without inwards, when freed again, will it not then probably meet itself in a yet more profound experience? This is the problem proposed for the truly intelligent'.

## Terms

- **Argent vive** (also: Argent vives) — Something of which there are two, to be mortified together; two kinds are named (of Auripigment; oriental, of Magnesia). Not explained in this chunk. (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - “it behooves us to mortify two Argent vives together” `I.3 ¶32`
- **watery Form** (also: permanent watery Form, fixed watery Form) — The fixed watery form of the philosophic matter, a more mature state of one praised earlier; creatrix/framer of the royal elements, drawing to itself the rubified light of its internal agent. (ATWOOD)
  - = the fountain which Berhard Trevisan mentions: “this is the fountain which Berhard Trevisan mentions” `I.3 n39`
  - “The fixed watery Form of the philosophic matter” `I.3 n39`
- **tincture** (also: tinge, tinging property) — The tinging power that transmutes imperfect bodies; ends red as flesh; described as coagulated blood. (ATWOOD)
  - = boundary of hearts: “our tincture is of blood coagulated, which blood is the boundary or satisfaction of hearts” `I.3 n42`
  - “a most pure and rubicund tincture of the color of flesh and blood” `I.3 n42`
- **medicine** — The product into which the bodies are reduced; tinges 10, 100, 1000 parts and gains virtue with each dissolution. (QUOTED: Scholiast)
  - “the oftener the medicine is dissolved, by so much more it increases in virtue” `I.3 n38`
- **bodies** (also: washed bodies, composed bodies) — Houses of their spirits; glossed as the sensible and vegetable media of life, which must be dissolved into their radical source. (QUOTED: Scholiast)
  - = the sensible and vegetable media of life: “the bodies (i.e., the sensible and vegetable media of life)” `I.3 n38`
  - “The bodies of the metals, explains our Scholiast, are the domiciles of their spirits” `I.3 n38`
- **agent** (also: internal agent) — The spirit, which vivifies and holds the particles of the matter together; superior to the body in operation. (ATWOOD)
  - “the agent, because it vivifies and holds the particles of the matter together” `I.3 n37`
- **sulfur** (also: sulphur, foliated sulphur) — Named by the adepts for light, the seed of gold. 'Foliated sulphur' (¶34) is not explained. (ATWOOD)
  - = light: “light or sulfur, as they call it, is the true form or seed of gold” `I.3 n40`
- **catholic nature** — The universal nature that is the subject: precious when exalted, defiled and despised in the common world. (ATWOOD)
  - “The same catholic nature, which in its preternatural exaltation appears so very precious” `I.3 n40`
- **middle principle** — In the grain analogy, the specificative form by which a thing is generated as its own kind; also the principle in which Nature contains and separates natures (¶33). (ATWOOD)
  - = specificative form: “in its middle principle, that is to say, in the specificative form” `I.3 n40`
  - “which containest and separatest natures in a middle principle” `I.3 ¶33`
- **ethereal vehicle** — The prepared medium in which the seed of gold is planted to bring forth its virtue in manifold increase. (ATWOOD)
  - “planted in its proper ethereal vehicle, well prepared and fallow” `I.3 n40`
- **lune** — 'Our lune or silver, not common silver': what receives the seed of gold. (QUOTED: author of the New Light)
  - = silver (not common silver): “our lune or silver, not common silver” `I.3 n40`
- **Quintessence** (also: Quintessences, quintessential nature) — What is resurrected into a permanent form of life after seven repetitions; the new-born quintessences are reunited for fructification; the son is the quintessential nature concentrated. (ATWOOD)
  - “the final resurrection of the Quintessence into a permanent form of life” `I.3 n40`
- **philosophic mercury** — Completed by separating light from darkness seven times. (QUOTED: Eiraneaus)
  - “the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete” `I.3 n39`
- **our fire** (also: philosophic fire) — The philosophers' own fire: magical, creative, vital; most subtle, inhabiting in himself an infernal secret fire, extremely volatile; vivifies, depurates and segregates; distinct from common fire. The son is nourished with a gentle heat conformable to the heavenly fire. (ATWOOD)
  - = the miracle of the world: “Some call it the miracle of the world” `I.3 n41`
  - “magical, creative, vital” `I.3 n41`
- **putrefaction** — Precedes the generation of every new form; the 'gate of blackness'. (ATWOOD)
  - “putrefaction precedes the generation of every new form into existence” `I.3 n42`
- **Dragon** — Watches the crevices and shuns the sunbeams; is occultated as the internal light rises to manifestation. (ATWOOD)
  - “which becomes so occultated in the rising of the internal light to manifestation” `I.3 n43`
- **son** — Conceived of king and red daughter; takes his tincture from the fire; the treasury bearing the Philosophic Matter; the whole quintessential nature concentrated and personified. (QUOTED: Hermes)
  - “this son is himself the treasury, even himself bearing the Philosophic Matter” `I.3 ¶35`
- **internal light** — The light within the matter, which becomes manifest as the Dragon is occultated, and once manifest to sense must enter another life and die again (n43, n44). This chunk also speaks of 'metallic light', 'vital light' and 'fermenting light' in related roles without asserting they are the same. (ATWOOD)
  - “The vital light, as we have before explained, is centrally hidden in nature until it is drawn forth” `I.3 n46`
- **Philosophic Matter** (also: philosophic matter) — Borne by the son; has a fixed watery form. (QUOTED: Hermes)
- **Self-Knowledge** — The Hermetic method which alone enables man to know, understand and possess divine things. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge” `I.3 n46`

## Ambiguities

- “which containest and separatest natures in a middle principle” `I.3 ¶33`
  - Reading: The same as the 'middle principle' of n40: the specificative form by which a thing is generated as its kind.
  - Reading: A distinct, unexplained sense in Hermes' address to Nature.
  - Basis: n40 defines 'middle principle' only within the grain analogy; n40 is keyed to ¶32, not ¶33.
- “our dead son will lives king comes forth from the fire” `I.3 ¶35`
  - Reading: The dead son lives and comes forth as king from the fire.
  - Reading: Text corrupt; the son's revival is clear, the syntax is not.
  - Basis: Garbled wording in ¶35; revival supported by 'The Son, already vivified' (¶35).
- “the generation of the supernatural son of Sun” `I.3 n39`
  - Reading: 'Son of the Sun' (son of gold/light).
  - Reading: A misprint for 'son' or 'Sun' alone.
  - Basis: Wording in n39 only; not explained.
- “conceive a son, conjoined and superior, in it, and he lives by our fire (41)” `I.3 ¶34`
  - Reading: Note 41 applies to both ¶33 and ¶34.
  - Reading: One marker is a misnumbering; n41's content (reunion for fructification, feeding with fire) fits ¶34.
  - Basis: Marker (41) appears in both ¶33 and ¶34; n45 and n46 have no marker in this chunk's text.
