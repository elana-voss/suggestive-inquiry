# III.3_06

*Atwood sets the Zohar's account of Thought unfolding into Spirit/Intelligence and voice, and Poemander's call to enlarge the self until it understands God, against our self-enclosed, profane thought; she then describes life's return from individual Identity to its First Source, where the hidden Logos is born into Light, and urges faithful work on the impalpable beginning until reason becomes an immortal Light and the outer and inner worlds agree.*

## Argument

- Zohar: Thought, Principle of all, first shut up in itself, becomes Spirit, named Intelligence; thence a voice, the reunion of the celestial choirs, articulate from the Mind. `III.3 ¶78, III.3 n50` — QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar
- Atwood calls this magnificent but impervious: who can imagine being the Understanding of that Light, One with the First Mover? `III.3 ¶78` — ATWOOD
- Living profanely, without self-knowledge, we cannot think the Divine Idea; God's creative Thought inverts ours; knowing Him in It is self-annihilation. `III.3 ¶78` — ATWOOD
- Poemander: like knows like; enlarge thyself beyond body and time, become Eternity, and understand God. The soul shut in the body cannot. Knowing, willing, hoping is the divine way proper to the Good; it then meets one everywhere. `III.3 ¶78, III.3 n51` — QUOTED: Poemander
- The exemplary Logos is hidden, slain in the fiat of our Identity, in two reverse poles; life must return from individuality to the Initial germ, its First Source. `III.3 ¶79` — ATWOOD
- Hailing Reflection and Divine Experience: Thought emanates Light, Life feels itself to Be; this Divine I Am the Kabalah calls the Substant Unity. `III.3 ¶79` — ATWOOD
- Visibles spring from the invisible: recreation is a birth into Light (Sybil in Boissard). `III.3 ¶79` — ATWOOD
- So work the impalpable faithfully (dissolve, coagulate, resolve, refine, regulate) until reason becomes immortal Light, Mistress of Life. `III.3 ¶80` — ATWOOD
- Latin: this is our most noble Mercury; nothing nobler created except the rational soul. `III.3 ¶81` — UNCLEAR
- External and Internal Worlds then blend, confirming each other, under the Universal Law. `III.3 ¶82` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **principle** — “Thought is the Principle of all that is; but it is as first Unknown and shut up in itself.” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar)
  - **Plain (stated):** Everything originates in Thought, at first hidden in itself; it unfolds into Spirit (called Intelligence) and then issues as an articulate voice from the Mind.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78 gives the sequence itself; Atwood repeats the opening clause as her theme ('Magnificent, yet impervious assertion').
  - Also: “Arrived at this estate, it takes the name of Intelligence, and is no longer as before it was shut up in itself.” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “there proceeds a voice which is the reunion of the celestial choirs” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “a voice which rolls forth in distinct utterance articulate, for It comes from the Mind” `III.3 ¶78`
- **result** — “To Be the Understanding of that Light, of which all Nature is the Efflux, to move One with the First Mover, and Be His Will” `III.3 ¶78` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The highest aim Atwood names: to be one with the source Light of nature and with the First Mover's will. She asks whether any imagination can even glimpse it.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78: posed as a question ('What imagination strong or hardy enough to glance into the full faith?') right after the Zohar assertion; read as the aim because ¶79-82 describe return to the First Source and reason becoming Light.
- **impediment** — “We cannot, profanely as we live without the knowledge of ourselves, attain to the Divine Idea” `III.3 ¶78` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Living in the ordinary way, without self-knowledge, one cannot reach the Divine Idea, nor entertain or think it by one's own activity.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78, stated directly.
  - Also: “either to entertain or think It self-actively is impossible” `III.3 ¶78`
- **principle** — “the Thought which is of God creative is the inversion of our thought; and to know Him in It is self-annihilation in the life which is eternal” `III.3 ¶78` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** God's creative Thought runs opposite to ordinary human thinking; knowing God in it means annihilation of the self, within eternal life.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78, stated directly.
- **principle** — “judge of this by thyself, assimilating, for the like is intelligible by the like” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A thing is known by becoming like it: the soul judges the incorporeal from itself and understands God by assimilating itself to Him.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78: the principle is stated; its application to knowing God rests on what follows directly, 'Increase thyself ... and thou shalt understand God'.
- **procedure** — “Increase thyself into an immeasurable greatness, leaping beyond every body, and transcending time, become Eternity, and thou shalt understand God.” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (stated):** Enlarge oneself past body and time, taking every creature, element, place, time and condition of life into oneself; this is the condition for understanding God.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78 spells out the enlargement and ends 'or else thou canst not yet understand God'. Atwood introduces it with 'Yet', answering her stated impossibility.
  - Also: “Yet if thou wilt even break the Whole, instructs Poemander, and see those things that are without the world, thou mayest.” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “become higher than all height, lower than all depth, comprehend in thyself the qualities of all the creatures; of the fire, the water, the dry, and the moist” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “conceive likewise that thou canst at once be everywhere, in the sea and in the earth” `III.3 ¶78`
- **operator** — “If thou art able to believe in thyself, that nothing is impossible, but perceivest thyself to be immortal” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator must believe nothing is impossible to him and see himself as immortal and able to understand all things.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78, continuing 'and that thou canst understand all things, every art, every science'.
- **operator** — “But to be able to know, and to will, and to hope, is the straight way and the divine way proper to the Good” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (stated):** The right disposition combines knowing, willing and hoping.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78, stated directly.
- **impediment** — “But if thou has shut up thy soul in the body, to abuse it” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (stated):** A soul confined to the body that professes its own ignorance, weakness and fear cannot approach God; not knowing God is the greatest evil.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78: followed by 'what hast thou to do with God?' and 'It is the greatest evil not to know God'.
  - Also: “I understand nothing, I can do nothing, I am afraid of the sea, I cannot climb up into heaven, I know not who I am” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “It is the greatest evil not to know God” `III.3 ¶78`
- **sign** — “it will meet thee everywhere thereafter and everywhere be seen of thee, plain and easy, even when thou dost no longer expect or look for it” `III.3 ¶78` (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once on this way (the divine way proper to the Good), it meets one everywhere and at all times, unsought, because everything is God's Image.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶78: 'It will meet the waking, sleeping ... For there is nothing which is not the Image of God'.
  - Also: “It will meet the waking, sleeping, sailing, traveling by night and by day, when thou speakest and when thou keepest silence.” `III.3 ¶78`
  - Also: “For there is nothing which is not the Image of God” `III.3 ¶78`
- **subject** — “the exemplary Logos is hidden, --- slain from the foundation in the exterminating fiat of our Identity” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The exemplary Logos lies concealed, as if dead, in us; what conceals it is the act that makes us separate individual selves. The chunk does not define 'Logos'.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79 ties the hiding to 'our Identity', and the return it prescribes runs 'from the manifest created individuality, back into the Initial germ'. How the 'two poles' fit is unclear (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “the occultation of this does not take place therefore, but in two poles or principles diametrically reverse” `III.3 ¶79`
- **procedure** — “We must pass the eternal wheel of the vicissitudes of things, from the manifest created individuality, back into the Initial germ” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The work runs backward: life is led from its created individual form, through all experience, back to its seed-origin and First Source.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79 states direction and end, with the tide image.
  - Also: “until life, as an ocean tide flowing to its extreme boundary, returns to refund its treasury again its First Source” `III.3 ¶79`
- **agent** — “Most mighty and surpassing magic of Reflection.” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Reflection and Divine Experience are hailed as the powers at work in this return. The chunk defines neither.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79: both exclamations follow directly on life's return to its First Source; 'Experience is present with Power' later in ¶79 ties Experience to the outcome. Meaning of 'Reflection' unsettled (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “And thou august Mother of all things, Divine Experience.” `III.3 ¶79`
- **stage** — “Thought emanating Light, as by Intelligence excruciated, Life springs forth with motion, feeling itself to Be.” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A sequence: Thought gives off Light; through Intelligence, 'excruciated' (not explained here), Life arises, moving and conscious of its own being.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79; parallels the Zohar's Thought, then Spirit/Intelligence, then voice in III.3 ¶78.
- **principle** — “in the Divine I Am, is by the Kabalah signified the Substant Unity of all that is” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Life's self-affirmation, 'I Am', signifies (per the Kabalah, which Atwood endorses) the one underlying substance of all things: fountain of nature and her law, source of miracles and of all natural and supernatural increase.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79, stated directly in apposition.
  - Also: “the fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law, the source of so many miracles and magical accordances” `III.3 ¶79`
  - Also: “where Wisdom is poured forth like Water and Glory faileth not before Him for ever” `III.3 ¶79`
- **sign** — “where Experience is present with Power, and Effect in substance to bear them witness” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** At that source experience comes joined with real power, and substantial effects attest to it.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79: the clause qualifies the Substant Unity as source of 'every natural and supernatural increase'; 'bear them witness' marks the effects as proof.
- **result** — “the recreation was seen to be a stupendous metaphysical birth out of the Infinite into Light” `III.3 ¶79` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The re-creation is a birth of the visible out of the invisible, something out of a prior nothingness, into Light.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶79: 'visibles here are said verily to spring out of that which is invisible'. The Sybil's Latin line (our translation) says the invisible Word becomes palpable and grows like a root.
  - Also: “For visibles here are said verily to spring out of that which is invisible, as from the precedent nothingness Something is produced” `III.3 ¶79`
  - Also: “Verbum invisible fiet palpabile et germinabit ut Radix.” `III.3 ¶79`
- **subject** — “That which is impalpable and imperfectly conceived at first” `III.3 ¶80` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The material of the work starts out intangible and only dimly grasped.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶80; 'therefore' links it to the invisible-to-visible birth of III.3 ¶79.
- **operator** — “work faithfully, as the philosopher tells us, until it be the Divine pleasure to make it appear” `III.3 ¶80` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator must persevere faithfully; when the matter becomes manifest depends on God, not the worker.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶80, stated directly.
- **procedure** — “to dissolve, coagulate, resolve, refine, and regulate” `III.3 ¶80` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The named operations are performed on the impalpable matter until reason itself becomes Light. This chunk does not explain the operations.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶80: their object is 'That which is impalpable' and their end 'until reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light'; reading them as worked on reason rests on that end clause.
- **result** — “reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light in the periphery of her fiery essence remains immortal, and is the Mistress of Life” `III.3 ¶80` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The aim is a transformed reason: luminous at the edge of its fiery essence, immortal, and governing life.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶80, the stated end of the operations.
- **subject** — “Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus” `III.3 ¶81` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** 'Our most noble Mercury' is named directly after reason becomes immortal Light, and the same line ranks the rational soul as God's noblest creation. Whether the two are one is unclear (see ambiguities).
  - Basis: III.3 ¶81 follows ¶80 directly and 'Hic' (this) points back; translation is ours. No speaker is given.
- **result** — “the External and Internal Worlds were seen to blend together in confluent harmony, proving and establishing each other” `III.3 ¶82` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** In the completed state outer and inner reality coincide and confirm each other; reason has nothing left to doubt, the senses nothing left to desire.
  - Basis: III.3 ¶82, stated directly.
  - Also: “leaving reason nothing more to doubt of, or the senses to desire, but a fulfillment under the Universal Law” `III.3 ¶82`

## Terms

- **Thought** — The Principle of all that is; at first unknown and shut up in itself, it develops into Spirit/Intelligence and emanates Light. God's creative Thought is the inversion of human thought. (QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar; ATWOOD)
  - “Thought is the Principle of all that is; but it is as first Unknown and shut up in itself.” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “the Thought which is of God creative is the inversion of our thought” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “Thought emanating Light” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Spirit** — The degree Thought reaches when it develops forth; at that estate it is called Intelligence, is no longer shut up in itself, and develops in the mystery surrounding it. (QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar)
  - = Intelligence: “Arrived at this estate, it takes the name of Intelligence” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “When the Thought begins to develop itself forth, it arrives at that degree when it becomes Spirit.” `III.3 ¶78`
- **Intelligence** — The name Thought takes on becoming Spirit. In ¶79 Life springs forth 'by Intelligence excruciated'. (QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar; ATWOOD)
  - = Spirit: “Arrived at this estate, it takes the name of Intelligence” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “as by Intelligence excruciated” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Mind** — That from which the articulate voice proceeding from Spirit comes. (QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar)
  - “a voice which rolls forth in distinct utterance articulate, for It comes from the Mind” `III.3 ¶78`
- **Light** — That of which all Nature is the Efflux; Thought emanates it; the recreation is a birth into it; reason is to become a bright Light. (ATWOOD)
  - “that Light, of which all Nature is the Efflux” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “Thought emanating Light” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “a stupendous metaphysical birth out of the Infinite into Light” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light” `III.3 ¶80`
- **First Mover** — The one with whom Atwood asks whether anyone can 'move One' and be His Will; at once the Antecedent and Final Cause of all. (ATWOOD)
  - = Antecedent and Final Cause of all: “the First Mover, and Be His Will, who is at once the Antecedent and Final Cause of all” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “to move One with the First Mover, and Be His Will, who is at once the Antecedent and Final Cause of all” `III.3 ¶78`
- **Divine Idea** — What cannot be attained, entertained or thought self-actively by those living profanely without self-knowledge. (ATWOOD)
  - “attain to the Divine Idea; either to entertain or think It self-actively is impossible” `III.3 ¶78`
- **incorporeal** — That which contains all things; most capacious, swift and powerful. (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - “Consider that which contains all things” `III.3 ¶78`
  - “it is most capacious, most swift, and most strong” `III.3 ¶78`
- **Image of God** — What everything is; hence the divine way proper to the Good meets one everywhere. (QUOTED: Poemander)
  - “For there is nothing which is not the Image of God” `III.3 ¶78`
- **exemplary Logos** — Hidden and 'slain from the foundation' in the fiat of our Identity; its occultation is in two diametrically reverse poles or principles. Not otherwise defined. (ATWOOD)
  - “the exemplary Logos is hidden, --- slain from the foundation in the exterminating fiat of our Identity” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Identity** — Used specially for our individual selfhood, whose 'exterminating fiat' hides the exemplary Logos. (ATWOOD)
  - “the exterminating fiat of our Identity” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Initial germ** — The origin to which life is led back from created individuality; its arrival is described as a return to its First Source. (ATWOOD)
  - “from the manifest created individuality, back into the Initial germ” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “returns to refund its treasury again its First Source” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Reflection** — Hailed as a 'most mighty and surpassing magic' right after the account of life's return to its source; not defined. (ATWOOD)
  - “Most mighty and surpassing magic of Reflection.” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Divine Experience** — Addressed as the august Mother of all things; at the Substant Unity 'Experience is present with Power'. (ATWOOD)
  - = Mother of all things: “And thou august Mother of all things, Divine Experience.” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “where Experience is present with Power, and Effect in substance to bear them witness” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Divine I Am** — The affirmation in which Life feels itself to Be; by it the Kabalah signifies the Substant Unity of all that is. (ATWOOD)
  - = Substant Unity: “in the Divine I Am, is by the Kabalah signified the Substant Unity of all that is” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “Life springs forth with motion, feeling itself to Be.” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Substant Unity** — The unity of all that is; fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law; source of miracles, magical accordances, and every natural and supernatural increase. (ATWOOD)
  - = fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law: “the Substant Unity of all that is; the fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law” `III.3 ¶79`
  - “the source of so many miracles and magical accordances, as of every natural and supernatural increase” `III.3 ¶79`
- **Exemplary Law** — Universal nature's law, of which the Substant Unity is the fountain. (ATWOOD)
  - “the fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law” `III.3 ¶79`
- **recreation** — A metaphysical birth out of the Infinite into Light, in which the visible springs from the invisible. (ATWOOD)
  - “the recreation was seen to be a stupendous metaphysical birth out of the Infinite into Light” `III.3 ¶79`
- **reason** — Used specially for what the work brings to completion: it becomes a bright Light at the periphery of her fiery essence, immortal, Mistress of Life; at the end it has nothing left to doubt. (ATWOOD)
  - “reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light in the periphery of her fiery essence remains immortal, and is the Mistress of Life” `III.3 ¶80`
  - “leaving reason nothing more to doubt of” `III.3 ¶82`
- **Mercurius noster** — 'Our most noble Mercury', said of what precedes (¶80); ranked against the rational soul in the same line; relation unclear (see ambiguities). (UNCLEAR)
  - “Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus” `III.3 ¶81`
- **animam rationalem** — The rational soul; per our translation, the line ranks it against 'our Mercury' as God's noblest creation under heaven (see ambiguities). (UNCLEAR)
  - “Deus nunquam creavit rem nobiliorem sub coelo praeter animam rationalem” `III.3 ¶81`
- **External and Internal Worlds** — Outer and inner reality, seen at completion to blend in confluent harmony and confirm each other. (ATWOOD)
  - “the External and Internal Worlds were seen to blend together in confluent harmony” `III.3 ¶82`
- **Universal Law** — The law under which fulfilment is reached once outer and inner worlds agree. (ATWOOD)
  - “a fulfillment under the Universal Law” `III.3 ¶82`

## Ambiguities

- “the occultation of this does not take place therefore, but in two poles or principles diametrically reverse” `III.3 ¶79`
  - Reading: The two poles are God's creative Thought and our thought, its inversion.
  - Reading: The two poles are the manifest individuality and the Initial germ between which life travels.
  - Reading: The hidden Logos is concealed by being divided into two opposed principles within the created individual.
  - Basis: Reading 1: III.3 ¶78 'the Thought which is of God creative is the inversion of our thought'. Reading 2: III.3 ¶79 'from the manifest created individuality, back into the Initial germ'. Reading 3: III.3 ¶79 places the hiding in 'our Identity'. The text does not name the poles.
- “Most mighty and surpassing magic of Reflection.” `III.3 ¶79`
  - Reading: Reflection as mental self-reflection, the mind turning back on itself.
  - Reading: Reflection as the reflux or turning back of life to its source, just described.
  - Basis: Reading 1: III.3 ¶78 stresses self-knowledge and judging 'by thyself'. Reading 2: the exclamation follows directly on life returning 'as an ocean tide' to its First Source (III.3 ¶79). Not defined.
- “Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus, et Deus nunquam creavit rem nobiliorem sub coelo praeter animam rationalem.” `III.3 ¶81`
  - Reading: 'Hic' points back to reason made immortal Light (III.3 ¶80), so 'our Mercury' is the rational soul brought to perfection.
  - Reading: Mercury is ranked as the noblest thing except/besides the rational soul, so the two are distinct.
  - Basis: Reading 1: placement directly after III.3 ¶80, whose subject is reason. Reading 2: the literal grammar of 'praeter' in our translation. The book gives no translation or gloss.

## Translations

- `III.3 ¶79` “Verbum invisible fiet palpabile et germinabit ut Radix.” → The invisible Word shall become palpable and shall germinate as a Root. *(ours)*
- `III.3 ¶81` “Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus, et Deus nunquam creavit rem nobiliorem sub coelo praeter animam rationalem.” → This is our most noble Mercury, and God never created a nobler thing under heaven except (besides) the rational soul. *(ours)*
