# II.4_03

*Atwood argues that theurgic union is effected by divine media acting of themselves, not by intellectual effort, and that the purified soul, its passions ruled by an established Reason, is led through the gods to a passive, faith-borne contemplation of the One. Along the way she names the hidden Reason, once arisen, the alchemists' Salt, Mercury and Secret of Secrets.*

## Argument

- Verses praise the divine mover and the Creator's laws. `II.4 ¶30` — QUOTED: unnamed verse; 'the mathematician'
- Theurgists unite with the Divine through divine Media, not mental abstraction, or theorists would share the union. Ineffable works and symbols impart it. With preparation complete, the Synthemata act by themselves; the soul's dispositions and purity are mere concauses, and divine things take no energy from the 'regardant subject'. `II.4 ¶31, II.4 n23` — QUOTED: Iamblichus (endorsed by ATWOOD)
- Chaldaic Oracle ('operative language'): fire spread into air; the Paternal Fountain; Presters of Intellectual Fire serving the Father; armour of Light fortifying soul and mind. `II.4 ¶31, II.4 ¶32, II.4 ¶33, II.4 ¶34, II.4 ¶35, II.4 n24` — QUOTED: Chaldaic Oracle
- Through the passive Ether, theurgic rites unfold the soul's new life, awaken intellect into reminiscence and, like a midwife, bring forth the total nature in Light. Wisdom is like a mother who educates her son, then bids him use his understanding. `II.4 ¶36` — ATWOOD
- Porphyry: with discipline complete, the inferior powers gather about Reason and their motions dissolve near it. `II.4 ¶36, II.4 n25` — QUOTED: Porphyry (endorsed)
- ¶37 duplicates ¶36 almost verbatim. `II.4 ¶37` — UNCLEAR
- Natural life is an oligarchy, almost anarchy: each motive usurps the throne and the highest faculties serve covetousness, pride and the like. By contrast we faintly glimpse the force moving about the Rational Magnet. Under an established monarch the inferior powers move only with him. `II.4 ¶38` — ATWOOD
- Sendivogius: Fire, purest element, is quiet like a chariot that moves only when drawn. It holds the reasons of life and understanding, the rational soul, which makes man a microcosm sealed by God. Fire destroys everything compounded, so no mortal sees God naturally. Like fire in flint it stirs only at God's will. `II.4 ¶39, II.4 n26` — QUOTED: Sendivogius (endorsed)
- The total Reason lies hidden in this life like fire in unkindled fuel or gold in ore ('our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone', 'microcosmos et Adam', in which we are as dead). It wakes only through a resolution of the whole circulatory confine. 'This' is the Sal Sepientum, Mercurius Philosophorum, Secret of Secrets; Morieni calls the magistery God's secret given to the prophets. `II.4 ¶39, II.4 n27` — ATWOOD
- Perfected Initiates pass through the Intelligible Causes ('gods') to contemplating their Highest Unity. Having overcome irrational inclination, the soul holds reason whole and desires only her own essence in the Absolute. `II.4 ¶39` — ATWOOD
- Orphic fragment: a secret for the Initiated; Musaeus must keep purity and contemplate the One, whom no mortal eyes have seen. `II.4 ¶40, II.4 n28` — QUOTED: Orphic Fragment
- Contemplating the indwelling Unity was the last preparation for translation. Initiates did behold Him: their regenerate souls were not mortal, their regard hypostatical, like to like. The compound creature cannot survive the Fiery Centre, and partial unions already strained the ethereal vehicle. Sense, ignorance and disinclination bar the uninitiated. `II.4 ¶40` — ATWOOD
- Initiates do not grasp Deity self-actively. Plato teaches passive Divine Intuition raised by love, hope and faith; muesis comes through faith-given silence, above every Gnostic energy. `II.4 ¶40, II.4 n29` — ATWOOD, drawing on Plato and QUOTED: Proclus
- The translation rite buries the body except the head, signifying that all but intellectual life sinks into oblivion while the charioteer's head rises to Demiurgic Wisdom. `II.4 ¶40` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **agent** — “it is indeed by divine Media, and not a mere conception of the mind or metaphysical abstraction, either, that Theurgists are conjoined to the Divine nature” `II.4 ¶31` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Union with the Divine comes about through real divine media acting in the soul. Thinking or abstract theory does not produce it.
  - Also: “the power of inexplicable symbols which are known only to the gods themselves, impart Theurgic union” `II.4 ¶31`
- **impediment** — “what would hinder those who philosophize theoretically from participating of this union? Which they do not” `II.4 ¶31` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Theory alone does not achieve the union; people who only philosophize theoretically do not attain it.
- **agent** — “the Synthemata, i.e., the Theurgic aids and media themselves, perform by themselves their proper work” `II.4 ¶31` (QUOTED: Iamblichus (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** The Synthemata are the theurgic aids and media. Once preparation is complete, they do the work themselves without the operator's intellectual activity.
  - Also: “the things which properly excite the Divine will being the Divine Synthemata themselves” `II.4 ¶31`
- **operator** — “when we do not energize intellectually (all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)” `II.4 ¶31` (QUOTED: Iamblichus (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator's part is to fulfil the preparatory conditions and then stop intellectual activity. The soul's good dispositions and purity count only as contributing causes ('concauses').
  - Also: “it is requisite to consider these and all the best dispositions of the soul, and also the purity pertaining, as certain concauses” `II.4 ¶31`
- **principle** — “things pertaining to the gods are moved by themselves, and do not receive from an inferior nature (i.e., to say, from the regardant subject) the principle of their energy” `II.4 ¶31` (QUOTED: Iamblichus, with gloss probably by ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Divine activity moves itself. It does not draw its source of action from the person who observes or undergoes it.
  - Also: “the ineffable power of the gods itself, knows by itself its own images” `II.4 ¶31`
- **procedure** — “Extending the like fire sparkling into the spacious air” `II.4 ¶32` (QUOTED: Chaldaic Oracle)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Atwood calls these Chaldaic lines 'operative language', so she ties them to the work. This chunk does not explain the individual images.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶31 introduces the Oracle as speaking 'in its own operative language'. The images themselves are not glossed.
  - Also: “Having put on the completely armed vigour of resounding Light, with triple strength, fortifying the soul and the mind.” `II.4 ¶34`
  - Also: “And not to walk dispersedly on the empyreal channels, but stiffly” `II.4 ¶32`
- **agent** — “by the medium of the passive Ether” `II.4 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Theurgic rites act through the Ether as a passive medium to develop the soul's newly conceived life. This chunk does not say further what the Ether is.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶36 names the passive Ether as the medium of the rites. II.4 ¶40 mentions 'the ethereal vehicle' as what bears the unions, which suggests the Ether is the soul's own vehicle, though the text does not say so outright.
  - Also: “unfold the embryo vigour of her newly conceived life” `II.4 ¶36`
- **result** — “awakening intellect into reminiscence and filling it with the conscious reasons of things manifest and occult” `II.4 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The rites awaken the intellect so that it recollects, and fill it with conscious knowledge of the causes of both visible and hidden things. Like a midwife, they bring the whole nature to birth in Light.
  - Also: “as it were by an obstetric hand and action, bringing forth the total nature and ornamenting it with Light” `II.4 ¶36`
- **procedure** — “Wisdom here enacts the apart of a discreet mother, who having educated her son and furnished him with understanding, bids him use it” `II.4 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Wisdom first instructs and equips the soul, then sets it to exercise virtue and theoretic discipline until it is finally converted and complete.
  - Also: “exercising him in every virtue and theoretic discipline for the final conversion and accomplishment of his soul” `II.4 ¶36`
- **sign** — “the motions themselves will be dissolved through their proximity to the reasoning power” `II.4 ¶36` (QUOTED: Porphyry (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** When discipline is complete, the lower powers line up around Reason, are ashamed of acting on their own, and their motions dissolve in Reason's presence.
  - Also: “the whole inferior powers will range in harmonious concord about their proper rule” `II.4 ¶36`
- **impediment** — “the government of the natural life is oligarchical, almost an anarchy” `II.4 ¶38` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** In ordinary life no single principle rules the soul. Each passing motive or passion (covetousness, ambition, envy, pride) seizes control in turn, and the higher faculties end up serving lower ends.
  - Also: “each motive rising, as it were, becomes a usurper of a vacant throne” `II.4 ¶38`
- **agent** — “the Almighty force that moves about the Rational Magnet” `II.4 ¶38` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A ruling Reason which, once established as 'monarch', draws all the inferior powers to move only with it. The tyranny of the passions gives a faint picture of this force by contrast.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶38 sets 'Rational Magnet' beside the monarch whose 'leading motive' the inferior powers follow. II.4 ¶36 (Porphyry) has the inferior powers venerate 'this Reason'. See ambiguities for the divine reading.
  - Also: “the inferior powers will so venerate his leading motive that they will move only according to his movement” `II.4 ¶38`
- **agent** — “the Presters of Intellectual Fire follow in radiant order the will of their First Cause” `II.4 ¶38` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Powers of intellectual fire that act in ordered obedience to the First Cause. Atwood offers them as a picture of the well-ordered soul. The chunk does not define them further.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶38 places this beside the monarch image. II.4 ¶33: 'to these Intellectual Presters of Intellectual Fire all things are subservient by the persuasive will of the Father'.
  - Also: “to these Intellectual Presters of Intellectual Fire all things are subservient by the persuasive will of the Father” `II.4 ¶33`
- **agent** — “In it are the reasons of life and understanding, which are distributed in the first infusion of man’s life, and these are called the rational soul” `II.4 ¶39` (QUOTED: Sendivogius (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** The purest Fire holds the principles of life and understanding. In man this fire is the rational soul, infused into the vital spirit, and it is what makes him like God and a microcosm.
  - Also: “This soul was of that most pure fire, infused by God into the vital spirit” `II.4 ¶39`
- **principle** — “Thou hast an example of this in the flint, in which there is fire, and yet is not perceived, neither doth appear until it is stirred up by motion” `II.4 ¶39` (QUOTED: Sendivogius (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** The Fire is present in all things but is utterly quiet and imperceptible. It moves only when it is stirred, and only the will of the Most High stirs it.
  - Also: “This nature is of all the most quiet and like unto a chariot, when it is drawn, it runs; when it is not drawn, it stands still.” `II.4 ¶39`
  - Also: “is not moved unless it be stirred up by the proper will of the Most High” `II.4 ¶39`
- **impediment** — “Fire will not suffer anything that is compounded to come near to it: but is the death and separation of everything that is compounded” `II.4 ¶39` (QUOTED: Sendivogius; restated by ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Whatever is impure or compounded cannot approach the divine Fire, which destroys and separates it. So no one reaches God naturally.
  - Also: “how much less, therefore, can the compound creature, approaching to the Fiery Centre, live?” `II.4 ¶40`
  - Also: “no mortal man can see God, or come to Him naturally” `II.4 ¶39`
- **operator** — “they are manifest to philosophers into whom the incomprehensible Deity has inspired his own Wisdom” `II.4 ¶39` (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - **Plain (stated):** Knowledge of these hidden things comes only to philosophers whom God has inspired with Wisdom.
- **subject** — “our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone, celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam” `II.4 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The total Reason lies hidden in our present life, in which we are 'as dead', like fire in unkindled fuel or gold in dark ore. Whether 'our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone ... microcosmos et Adam' names the hidden Reason or the life/ore that hides it is unclear (see ambiguities).
  - Basis: II.4 ¶39: the names follow the fuel/ore similes, and 'in which we are all now as dead' fits the container rather than the Reason.
  - Also: “the total Reason is in this life of ours hidden, as the fire in fuel that is not kindled, or as gold in the dark ore unseen” `II.4 ¶39`
- **procedure** — “nor can be awakened to reminiscence without a resolution of the whole circulatory confine” `II.4 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The hidden Reason wakes only when the whole enclosing circulation (the bounded life that holds it) is dissolved. It then rises reversed, perfect and alone. This chunk does not explain 'circulatory confine' further.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶39: the Reason is hidden 'in this life of ours' and we are in it 'as dead'. A 'resolution' of what confines it is the condition for waking. II.4 ¶40 asks similarly for the whole life except the intellectual to be 'buried in profound oblivion'.
  - Also: “when I arises identically reverse, perfect, and alone” `II.4 ¶39`
- **result** — “This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum; their Secretu, Secretorum” `II.4 ¶39` (ATWOOD; QUOTED: Morieni)
  - **Plain (stated):** What arises perfect (the awakened hidden Reason) is what the alchemists call the Salt of the Wise, the Mercury of the Philosophers and their Secret of Secrets. Through Morieni, Atwood adds that this magistery is God's own secret, entrusted to the prophets.
  - Also: “hoc magisterium nihil aliud est, nisi Arcanum et secretum secretorum Dei altissimi et magni” `II.4 ¶39`
- **stage** — “having passed orderly through the whole progression of Intelligible Causes, by the Greeks called gods, were next promoted to a contemplation of their Highest Unity” `II.4 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** There is an ordered ascent. The soul, perfected in rites and virtue, passes through all the intermediate causes (the 'gods') and then rises to contemplate the highest Unity.
- **stage** — “having vanquished every irrational and gravitating inclination, the soul, holding the circle of reason complete” `II.4 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** When every irrational, downward-pulling tendency has been overcome, the soul holds reason entire and lacks only her own essence. That essence, her final cause in the Absolute, becomes her one desire.
  - Also: “possessing all, except her own identic essence, desires this now alone” `II.4 ¶39`
- **operator** — “persevere in purity if mind and heart” `II.4 ¶40` (QUOTED: Orphic Fragment)
  - **Plain (stated):** The candidate has to keep purity of mind and heart, set prejudice aside, and contemplate the One.
  - Also: “suffer not therefore former prejudice to debar thee” `II.4 ¶40`
- **stage** — “His contemplation, then, of the indwelling Unity was the final preparative to translation” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Contemplating the Unity within is the last preparatory step before 'translation'. The chunk does not define 'translation'; the burial rite in the same paragraph is its mandate.
- **principle** — “their regard was not extraneous but hypostatical, as of like to like” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Initiates could behold God because their regenerate souls were fortified by assimilation and nearness. Their regard was 'hypostatical', as like to like, not from outside.
  - Also: “the initiated were nowhere considered as mortal men, in respect of their souls, which were regenerate” `II.4 ¶40`
- **impediment** — “previous unions, which were but partial and instantaneous, as it were, tried the ethereal vehicle to its utmost susceptibility” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Even the earlier, partial and momentary unions strained the soul's ethereal vehicle to its limit, so full approach to the divine centre is beyond any compound nature.
- **impediment** — “a threefold barrier of sense, ignorance, and disinclination” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Three things keep the uninitiated from the truth: being bound to the senses, ignorance, and unwillingness. The impure may not touch the pure.
- **principle** — “Plato beautifully unfolds the passive method of the Divine Intuition, and the three elevating causes of love, hope, and faith” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The soul does not seize Deity by its own activity, which would invert Creator and creature. It receives the divine intuition passively and is raised by love, hope and faith.
- **agent** — “muesis, in short, is neither through intelligence nor judgment, but through the uncial silence imparted by faith” `II.4 ¶40` (QUOTED: Proclus (n29), endorsed)
  - **Plain (stated):** Muesis (not defined in this chunk) comes through a silence that faith imparts, not through intelligence or judgment. This faith ranks above every 'Gnostic energy' and establishes souls in the ineffable Unknown.
  - Also: “which is then better than every Gnostic energy (when it surpasses this)” `II.4 ¶40`
- **procedure** — “the whole body should be buried, except the hear” `II.4 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** In the theurgic rite of translation the body is buried up to the head. This signifies that all life except the intellectual must sink into deep oblivion while the intellect alone is raised to the Demiurgic Wisdom.
  - Also: “sublimely signifying that the total life, with exception of that which is intellectual, should be buried in profound oblivion” `II.4 ¶40`

## Terms

- **Synthemata** (also: Divine Synthemata) — The theurgic aids and media that act of themselves to bring about divine union and that properly excite the Divine will. (QUOTED: Iamblichus)
  - = Theurgic aids and media: “the Synthemata, i.e., the Theurgic aids and media themselves” `II.4 ¶31`
  - “the things which properly excite the Divine will being the Divine Synthemata themselves” `II.4 ¶31`
- **divine Media** — The real means by which Theurgists are joined to the Divine nature, as opposed to mental conception. (ATWOOD)
  - “it is indeed by divine Media, and not a mere conception of the mind or metaphysical abstraction” `II.4 ¶31`
- **Theurgic union** (also: Theurgists, Theurgic rites, theurgic mandates) — The conjunction of the soul with the Divine nature, imparted by ineffable works and symbols rather than by intellectual perception. (QUOTED: Iamblichus)
  - “the perfect efficacy of ineffable works” `II.4 ¶31`
- **concauses** — Contributing causes: the best dispositions of the soul and its purity, which accompany the work without being its principal cause. (QUOTED: Iamblichus)
  - “all the best dispositions of the soul, and also the purity pertaining, as certain concauses” `II.4 ¶31`
- **regardant subject** — The inferior nature, i.e. the person engaged in the rite, from whom divine things do not take their principle of energy. (UNCLEAR)
  - = inferior nature: “from an inferior nature (i.e., to say, from the regardant subject)” `II.4 ¶31`
- **Ether** (also: passive Ether, ethereal vehicle) — The passive medium through which theurgic rites unfold the soul's new life. 'Ethereal vehicle' names what the divine unions strained. (ATWOOD)
  - “by the medium of the passive Ether” `II.4 ¶36`
  - “tried the ethereal vehicle to its utmost susceptibility” `II.4 ¶40`
- **reminiscence** — The awakened recollection of the intellect, which the rites produce and which the hidden Reason cannot reach without the resolution of its confine. (ATWOOD)
  - “awakening intellect into reminiscence” `II.4 ¶36`
  - “nor can be awakened to reminiscence without a resolution of the whole circulatory confine” `II.4 ¶39`
- **Reason** (also: total Reason, reasoning power, circle of reason) — The rightful ruling principle of the soul. When it is established, the inferior powers settle around it. In this life it is hidden and latent. (ATWOOD)
  - = Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum: “This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum” `II.4 ¶39`
  - “will so venerate this Reason” `II.4 ¶36`
- **natural life** — The ordinary, unregenerate state of the soul, in which no permanent leader rules and passing motives usurp control. (ATWOOD)
  - “the government of the natural life is oligarchical, almost an anarchy” `II.4 ¶38`
- **Rational Magnet** — Something the Almighty force moves about; Atwood sets it beside the image of an established monarch whom the inferior powers follow. Not defined further. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Almighty force that moves about the Rational Magnet” `II.4 ¶38`
- **Presters of Intellectual Fire** (also: Intellectual Presters of Intellectual Fire) — Powers of intellectual fire to which all things are subservient by the Father's will and which follow their First Cause in radiant order. (QUOTED: Chaldaic Oracle; ATWOOD)
  - “to these Intellectual Presters of Intellectual Fire all things are subservient by the persuasive will of the Father” `II.4 ¶33`
  - “the Presters of Intellectual Fire follow in radiant order the will of their First Cause” `II.4 ¶38`
- **Fire** (also: Fiery Centre) — The purest element, quiet, present indiscernibly in all things, and moved only by God's will. It holds the reasons of life and understanding, it bears God's seal and majesty, and it is the death and separation of everything compounded. (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - “Fire, says the adept philosopher, is the purest and most worthy of all the elements” `II.4 ¶39`
  - “It is also in all things indiscernibly.” `II.4 ¶39`
- **rational soul** — The reasons of life and understanding contained in fire and infused into man. By it alone man differs from other creatures and is like God. (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - = the reasons of life and understanding (in Fire): “In it are the reasons of life and understanding, which are distributed in the first infusion of man’s life, and these are called the rational soul” `II.4 ¶39`
  - “This soul was of that most pure fire, infused by God into the vital spirit” `II.4 ¶39`
- **microcosm** (also: microcosmos) — Man as a 'particular world', so made because of the fiery rational soul infused into him. Atwood uses 'celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam' alongside the hidden Reason (referent unclear; see ambiguities). (QUOTED: Sendivogius; ATWOOD)
  - “man, after the creation of all things, was created into a particular world or microcosm” `II.4 ¶39`
  - “celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam” `II.4 ¶39`
- **compounded** (also: compound creature) — Whatever is mixed or impure. It cannot approach the divine Fire, which destroys and separates it. (QUOTED: Sendivogius; ATWOOD)
  - “nothing that is filthy or compounded, or blemished may come near Him” `II.4 ¶39`
- **circulatory confine** — What encloses the hidden Reason. It must undergo 'resolution' before the Reason can awaken. Not further defined. (ATWOOD)
  - “without a resolution of the whole circulatory confine” `II.4 ¶39`
- **Sal Sepientum** — Salt of the Wise (our rendering): a name Atwood gives to what arises when the hidden Reason is awakened. (ATWOOD)
  - = Mercurius Philosophorum: “This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum” `II.4 ¶39`
- **Mercurius Philosophorum** — Mercury of the Philosophers (our rendering), named together with the Sal Sepientum for what arises when the hidden Reason is awakened. (ATWOOD)
  - = Sal Sepientum: “This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum” `II.4 ¶39`
- **Secretu, Secretorum** (also: secretum secretorum) — Secret of Secrets (our rendering): the philosophers' name for the same thing. In Morieni it is the magistery itself, God's secret entrusted to the prophets. (ATWOOD; QUOTED: Morieni)
  - = magisterium: “hoc magisterium nihil aliud est, nisi Arcanum et secretum secretorum Dei altissimi et magni” `II.4 ¶39`
- **Intelligible Causes** — The ordered progression through which the perfected souls of the Initiated pass before contemplating the Highest Unity. (ATWOOD)
  - = gods: “the whole progression of Intelligible Causes, by the Greeks called gods” `II.4 ¶39`
- **telestic** — Used of the accomplishments in which the souls of the Initiated are made perfect; not defined in this chunk. (ATWOOD)
  - “being made perfect in every telestic accomplishment and virtue” `II.4 ¶39`
- **translation** — The final passage for which contemplation of the indwelling Unity is the last preparation. Its rite involves burying the body except the head. (ATWOOD)
  - “the final preparative to translation” `II.4 ¶40`
  - “the theurgic mandates for this translation” `II.4 ¶40`
- **hypostatical** — Used of the Initiates' regard of God: 'not extraneous', 'as of like to like'. Not defined further. (ATWOOD)
  - “their regard was not extraneous but hypostatical, as of like to like” `II.4 ¶40`
- **Divine Intuition** — The passive reception of the divine, raised by love, hope and faith, as opposed to self-active comprehension of Deity. (ATWOOD)
  - “the passive method of the Divine Intuition” `II.4 ¶40`
- **muesis** — Not defined in this chunk; said to come neither through intelligence nor judgment but through silence imparted by faith, and caused by faith. (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - “muesis, in short, is neither through intelligence nor judgment, but through the uncial silence imparted by faith” `II.4 ¶40`
- **Gnostic energy** — An energy of the soul that faith surpasses; set beside 'intelligence' and 'judgment'. Not defined further. (QUOTED: Proclus)
  - “which is then better than every Gnostic energy” `II.4 ¶40`
- **Wisdom** (also: Demiurgic Wisdom) — Acts like a discreet mother who educates the soul and sets it to exercise. God inspires it into philosophers, and the elevated intellect is filled with it. (ATWOOD; QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - “philosophers into whom the incomprehensible Deity has inspired his own Wisdom” `II.4 ¶39`
  - “where he is filled with the Demiurgic Wisdom and an empyreal life” `II.4 ¶40`
- **Paternal Fountain** — In the Oracle, the source from which intellectual notions crop the Flower of Life. Not further explained. (QUOTED: Chaldaic Oracle)
  - “Intellectual notions from the Paternal Fountain cropping the Flower of Life” `II.4 ¶32`

## Sources cited

- **Iamblichus**, *on the Myst., Chap 11* — “the perfect efficacy of ineffable works” `II.4 ¶31, II.4 n23` — Supports: union comes from divine media and Synthemata, not intellectual perception. Endorsed.

## Ambiguities

- “when I arises identically reverse, perfect, and alone” `II.4 ¶39`
  - Reading: 'I' is a misprint for 'it', meaning the hidden total Reason.
  - Reading: 'I' is meant literally: the true self arises.
  - Basis: The grammar ('I arises') and the surrounding subject ('the total Reason', 'which') favour 'it'. The theme of the soul's 'own identic essence' later in II.4 ¶39 could support the literal 'I'.
- “the uncial silence imparted by faith” `II.4 ¶40`
  - Reading: A misprint for 'unical' (unifying or one-making) silence.
  - Reading: Read as printed, 'uncial', with an unclear sense.
  - Basis: The context stresses establishing souls 'in the ineffable Unknown' and union with the One (II.4 ¶40), which suits a unifying silence. The text itself prints 'uncial'.
- “And where else is truth to be hoped for, asks the philosopher, except in this place?” `II.4 ¶40`
  - Reading: 'The philosopher' is Plato, named just before.
  - Reading: 'The philosopher' is Proclus, cited at n29 for the passage.
  - Basis: Plato is named in the preceding clause. Footnote n29 at the end of the passage cites Proclus.
- “the Almighty force that moves about the Rational Magnet” `II.4 ¶38`
  - Reading: The Rational Magnet is the established ruling Reason in the soul, around which the powers move.
  - Reading: The Rational Magnet is the divine First Cause, with the soul's order as a faint image of it.
  - Basis: II.4 ¶38: the monarch's 'leading motive' is followed by the inferior powers, and the ¶39 'our Loadstone' points to Reason within. The same sentence also speaks of the Presters following 'their First Cause', which points to the divine.
- “our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone, celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam, in which we are all now as dead” `II.4 ¶39`
  - Reading: These names refer to the hidden total Reason itself.
  - Reading: These names refer to the life/ore in which the Reason is hidden.
  - Basis: They follow the similes of the hidden Reason (fuel, ore); 'in which we are all now as dead' suits a container, while the next clause 'nor can be awakened' returns to the Reason.
