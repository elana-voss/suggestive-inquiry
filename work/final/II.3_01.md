# II.3_01

*Atwood moves from the Lesser Mysteries (likened to mesmeric trance, which only dissolves the sensible medium) to the concealed deeper initiation: a descent of consciousness beneath that medium towards the Central Source, through a dark, corrupt and dangerous region, which she reads through Virgil's sixth Aeneid (Sybil, golden bough, Avernus) and identifies with the Black Saturn of the adepts; passage requires a firm, purely rational will.*

## Argument

- The Lesser Mysteries revealed only the internal life: the one popular initiation, open to all, contemplation without rule. `II.3 ¶3` — ATWOOD
- Before purification little changed: a passing experience for most, desire for better in a few. Mesmerism, working in the same matter, likewise opens another life; the Free Spirit may reveal hidden things. But trance marvels did not satisfy the ancients, who sought the Thing itself and its Causes, experimenting within. `II.3 ¶4` — ATWOOD
- Theurgy's object: concentrate vitality, turn the spiritual eye, purify and analyze the total essence, draw forth the true Efficient and know it in co-identity. The Spirit's freedom from matter does not guarantee truth; concentrative energy and penetrating intellect are needed. `II.3 ¶5` — ATWOOD
- Entrancing the senses (dissolving the sensible medium) is the known key of the Hermetic vestibule; the next resolution, translating consciousness beneath that medium by obscuration towards the Central Source, is concealed, given only to those past the ordeals. Its entrance is dark, delusive, more corrupt, yet must be passed to reach Elysian light. `II.3 ¶5` — ATWOOD
- The Alchemists say the sensible medium ought to be dissolved: selfhood by its proper light, animal into vegetable, cerebral into ganglionic. Rosarium and Albertus: solution begins the work; dissolve and recongeal. `II.3 n2` — ATWOOD
- Hades/Lethe is not the body but vital submersion of consciousness in the penetralia of its first life. Descent is easy, re-ascent arduous; the way is secret lest unprepared souls be captured by deluding desires. Hence the Sybil's warning. `II.3 ¶7, II.3 ¶8` — ATWOOD
- After purification, the will must conceive a purely rational motive to resist the next sphere's temptations and rise through darkness and dissolution to Wisdom. Hence the golden bough. `II.3 ¶9, II.3 ¶10` — ATWOOD
- The bough (sacred equipage per Warburton; linked to the caduceus by Apuleius) is read ontologically as a ray of living light, Lully's Brancha Spiritualis: intellect penetrating the chloric ether into Proserpine, our lapsed soul, whose subtle vapor only intellect's glance by faith can arrest. `II.3 ¶11` — ATWOOD
- Only zeal, piety and prevailing reason fit the mind to separate the good from the evil allied by birth. Plato: opinion firm as adamant in Hades; who cannot define the Good sleeps profoundly there. Such sleep, absorption in defilements, is the direst evil. `II.3 ¶12` — ATWOOD
- Only Theseus and Pirithous were detained (Proclus): love of corporeal beauty; inability for divine contemplation. Aeneid VI alludes to the Mysteries; Atwood follows it as Intellect's inquiry after its Paternal Source. `II.3 ¶13` — ATWOOD
- The few found fit receive more arcane mysteries: consciousness, clear and rational, passes out of the Aquaster into the Fire World; the dark descent begins. `II.3 ¶14, II.3 ¶16` — ATWOOD
- The cave signifies thickening nether air verging on the chaos of matter: heavy, grave-smelling. It is the adepts' Black Saturn, corruption before mystical death and regeneration (lapis niger; Sendivogius' Urinus Saturni); the Ether is their mineral tree, the species lying asleep in sense, doubly locked. `II.3 ¶17, II.3 ¶18` — ATWOOD
- Reception in Hades: earth bellowing, dogs howling at the Goddess's approach; Claudian's parallel at Eleusis with triple Hecate. `II.3 ¶19` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **subject** — “the Thing itself, the subject of so many marvels” `II.3 ¶4` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The underlying cause or being behind trance phenomena, which the ancients investigated directly rather than resting in the phenomena.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶4: they passed the first phenomena (insensibility, cures, exaltation) to look for Causes, experimenting within.
  - Also: “they passed the first phenomena to look for Causes, experimenting within” `II.3 ¶4`
- **principle** — “Mesmerism, which of all modern arts is most pertinent to this philosophy as working in the same matter” `II.3 ¶4` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Mesmeric trance operates on the same material as the Hermetic art and corresponds to the first, Lesser Mysteries stage, but not beyond it.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶4 sets it beside the Lesser Mysteries (passing experience, entrance to another life); ¶5 says entrancing the senses is the key already in hand while the next resolution is concealed.
- **result** — “draw forth the true Efficient and to know it in co-identity” `II.3 ¶5` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The aim of Theurgy: to extract the true active cause within one's own essence and know it by becoming identical with it.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶5 names this, with concentrating vitality and purifying and analyzing the total essence, as their object and the Art of Theurgy; 'co-identity' read as knowing by identity.
  - Also: “to concentrate the whole vitality, to turn the spiritual eye, to purify and analyze the total essence” `II.3 ¶5`
- **operator** — “a concentrative energy is needed, and an intellect penetrating into other spheres, rather than discursive in its own” `II.3 ¶5` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Mere release of the spirit from the body does not ensure true insight; the operator needs concentrated energy and an intellect that penetrates rather than reasons discursively.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶5 directly.
  - Also: “it is not, as they say, that the Spirit is free from material bondage, or able to range the universe of her own sphere, that guarantees the truth of her revealments” `II.3 ¶5`
- **procedure** — “the key of the Hermetic vestibule may be said to be already in our hands, which are able to dissolve the sensible medium and convert it to the experience of another life” `II.3 ¶5` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The first step, entrancing the senses so that ordinary sensory consciousness is dissolved and opens onto another life, is already known (as in mesmerism).
  - Basis: II.3 ¶5: 'There are many ways known and practiced of entrancing the senses'; n2 explains the term dissolve.
  - Also: “the Alchemists, with one accord, say it ought to be dissolved” `II.3 n2`
- **procedure** — “the dark dominion of the selfhood by the magnetic friction of its proper light” `II.3 n2` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Dissolution of the sensible medium is likened to acid dissolving alkali: the selfhood's dominion dissolved by its own light through magnetic friction; animal life passed into vegetable, cerebral into ganglionic; the dense body of sense made fluid.
  - Basis: II.3 n2 offers these as what dissolution of the sensible medium in trance should be supposed to be.
  - Also: “the sensible or animal into the vegetable, the cerebral into the ganglionic life” `II.3 n2`
  - Also: “that the compact earthy body of sense may be rarified and flow as a passive watery spirit” `II.3 n2`
- **procedure** — “to dissolve and recongeal the spirit, to make the fixed volatile and the volatile fixed” `II.3 n2` (QUOTED: Albertus Magnus (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** The whole teaching is repeated dissolution and recoagulation of the spirit until the nature is perfected in both Solar and Lunar form; perfect solution is the beginning.
  - Basis: II.3 n2, Albertus Magnus as quoted; Atwood cites it in support of dissolution.
  - Also: “The beginning of the work, says Albertus Magnus, is a perfect solution” `II.3 n2`
- **stage** — “the next solution, or resolution rather, was operated which was to translate the consciousness underneath this medium by obscuration, towards the Central Source” `II.3 ¶5` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The second, secret operation: after the senses are dissolved, consciousness is carried below the sensible medium, through darkening, towards its central origin.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶5 directly; reserved for those who completed the previous rites and ordeals.
  - Also: “the concession of more arcane mysteries succeeded” `II.3 ¶14`
- **stage** — “a state of vital submersion in the Mysteries, when the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life” `II.3 ¶7` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Hades or Lethe means not the body or bodily life but a condition in which consciousness is deliberately immersed in the innermost recesses of its own original life.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶7: 'neither do we conceive that by Hades, or that profound Lethe, the ancients understood a corporeal nature... the whole allusion is to' this state.
  - Also: “the infernal gates lie open to mortal men on earth” `II.3 ¶7`
- **sign** — “described as dark, delusive, and dangerous, and more corrupted far than the foregoing” `II.3 ¶5` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** On entering the deeper state there is a sense of power and a life that is dark, deceptive, dangerous and more corrupt than the previous state; it must be passed through to reach the light.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶5 directly.
  - Also: “It discovers a fearful mystery in the opening sensation of power” `II.3 ¶5`
- **impediment** — “lest unprepared souls, presuming to enter, should be taken captive by deluding and fatal desires, and work irremediable evil there” `II.3 ¶7` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The descent is easy but the return is hard; unprepared souls are captured by deceptive desires there and do lasting harm, which is why the way was kept secret.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶7; the Sybil's lines in ¶8 (descent easy, return the labor) are cited for it.
  - Also: “Sed revocare gradum superasque evadere ad auras, Hoc opus, hic labor est.” `II.3 ¶8`
- **operator** — “the will should conceive within itself a motive purely rational to withstand the temptations of its next including sphere” `II.3 ¶9` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** After the sensual inclinations are purified, the will must hold a purely rational motive firm enough to resist the temptations of the deeper sphere, so it can climb through darkness and dissolution to Wisdom.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶9 directly; reinforced by ¶12 (zeal, piety, prevailing reason; Plato's adamant opinion).
  - Also: “exceeding zeal and piety of intention, such as is ascribed to Aeneas in search of his father, and a prevailing reason” `II.3 ¶12`
  - Also: “right opinion as firm as adamant” `II.3 ¶12`
- **agent** — “a ray of living light, golden and flexible, the true Brancha Spiritualis of Raymond Lully” `II.3 ¶11` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The golden bough, needed before the descent, is read as a living ray of light, the 'Brancha Spiritualis', which Atwood ties to the subtle intellect.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶11: 'we therefore understand' the bough ontologically thus, followed by Lully's 'Intellectus naturam habens subtilem...' (translation ours) and 'insinuating by rational penetration alone'.
  - Also: “Intellectus naturam habens subtilem ad intellignedum res intellibiles” `II.3 ¶11`
- **procedure** — “insinuating by rational penetration alone through the murky circumference of the chloric ether into its own congenial life, which is Proserpine” `II.3 ¶11` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The intellect works its way by reason alone through a dark enveloping ether into the life akin to it, which is our fallen soul hidden in its dark ground.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶11: Proserpine is glossed as 'that lapsed soul of ours, seated in her dark hypostasis unknown'.
  - Also: “that lapsed soul of ours, seated in her dark hypostasis unknown” `II.3 ¶11`
- **sign** — “whose vapor is so subtle and transient that nothing but the glance of its proper intellect by faith can arrest it” `II.3 ¶11` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The lapsed soul shows itself as an extremely subtle, fleeting vapor that can be held only by the soul's own intellect glancing at it in faith.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶11 directly.
- **sign** — “those doves that lead the way too, are they not known to our Alchemists and those chosen seats?” `II.3 ¶11` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Doves that lead the way are hinted to be a known alchemical image; this chunk does not explain them.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶11 rhetorical question only; no gloss given.
- **procedure** — “her final duty to separate the good and reject the evil therein by birth allied” `II.3 ¶12` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once established in her own essence, the mind's task is to separate out the good and reject the evil that has been joined to it from birth.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶12 directly.
- **principle** — “where Desires are Images and Will their Act” `II.3 ¶12` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** In that inner state desires take form as images and the will acts directly; hence the need for a firm rational motive.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶12 context: the mind must know what to aspire to, 'dismissing every other consideration'; ¶7 warns of capture by deluding desires.
- **impediment** — “To fall asleep in Hades was indeed to be absorbed, without the encumbrance of body, in all its defilements” `II.3 ¶12` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Failure in the deeper state is to lose vigilance and be swallowed up in its impurities; one who cannot grasp the Good by reason falls into this profound sleep.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶12 directly, after the Plato (Republic VII) passage on sleep in Hades.
  - Also: “or, as Virgil has it, to be a king in hell” `II.3 ¶12`
- **impediment** — “the one because he was too much a lover of corporeal beauty, the other through his natural inability to sustain the arduous altitude of divine contemplation” `II.3 ¶13` (QUOTED: Proclus (endorsed))
  - **Plain (stated):** Two causes of being trapped in the descent: attachment to bodily beauty, and lack of capacity for sustained divine contemplation (Theseus and Pirithous).
  - Basis: II.3 ¶13, Proclus as cited, called 'admirably' explained (endorsed).
- **result** — “an inquiry of Intellect after its Paternal Source” `II.3 ¶13` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The goal of the descent is the intellect's reaching its originating source, which is also called Wisdom in its light and the Elysian light.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶13 (allegory of Aeneas seeking his father); ¶9 'Wisdom in her light abode'; ¶5 'Elysian light'; ¶2 motto: the purified soul associates with its Generator.
  - Also: “the discovery of Wisdom in her light abode” `II.3 ¶9`
  - Also: “It s necessary that the soul, when purified, should associate with its Generator” `II.3 ¶2`
- **stage** — “As the consciousness passing the middle region, clear and rational from out the Aquaster, enters the Fire World” `II.3 ¶16` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** A transition: consciousness, keeping clarity and reason, passes from a middle region (the Aquaster) into a Fire World, coinciding with the dark descent. This chunk does not define Aquaster or Fire World.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶16 only; placement after the ordeal rites (¶14).
- **stage** — “It is the Black Saturn of the adepts, and that appearing corruption that precedes the mystical death and regeneration into new life” `II.3 ¶18` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The dark, heavy, grave-smelling region at the entry to Hades is the alchemists' blackness stage: apparent corruption that comes before mystical death and rebirth.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶18: the cave imagery points at 'the thickening darkness of the nether air verging to the chaos of matter'; 'It is the Black Saturn of the adepts'.
  - Also: “the thickening darkness of the nether air verging to the chaos of matter and flowing out from the perpetual motion of the first life” `II.3 ¶18`
  - Also: “lapis niger, vilis, foetens, et dicitur origo mundi et oritur sicut germinantia” `II.3 ¶18`
- **subject** — “Sendivogius calls it Urinus Saturni, with which he waters his lunar and solar plants” `II.3 ¶18` (QUOTED: Sendivogius)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The same dark matter is used to nourish the Lunar and Solar growths of the work; a parallel image has mists from 'my sea' bearing blessed waters that make herbs and flowers grow. This chunk does not explain further.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶18: 'as describing the same ens' introduces these names; watering image stated, its operational sense not.
  - Also: “Ex mari meo oriuntur nebulae, quae ferunt aquas benedictas et ipsae irrigant terras et educant herbas et flores” `II.3 ¶18`
- **subject** — “the true species was laid asleep in sense, and doubly locked up, as it were, within both corporeal and spiritual confines” `II.3 ¶18` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The real matter of the work (which the Alchemists call the Ether, their mineral tree) lies dormant within the senses, enclosed by both body and spirit, so its naming needed little concealment.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶18: 'the Alchemists also call the Ether their mineral tree; for they were not so careful to hide this in general, seeing...'; reading 'true species' as the matter of the work is inference.
  - Also: “the Alchemists also call the Ether their mineral tree” `II.3 ¶18`
- **sign** — “Sub pedibs mugire solum, et juga coepta moveri Sylvarum, visaeque canes ululare per umbram, Adventante Dea” `II.3 ¶19` (QUOTED: Virgil; Claudian)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Phenomena at reception into Hades: the ground roars, the woods move, dogs seem to howl at the approach of the Goddess; Claudian's parallel adds trembling shrines, light, roaring earth and the rising of triple Hecate. This chunk does not explain them.
  - Basis: II.3 ¶18 end: 'The reception of Aeneas in Hades is next described'; ¶19 verses (translation ours).
  - Also: “Ecce procul ternas Hecate variata figures Exortur” `II.3 ¶19`

## Terms

- **Lesser Mysteries** — The first, popular initiation open to all, revealing only the internal life; a field of natural contemplation without rule. (ATWOOD)
  - “this was the only popular initiation open to all” `II.3 ¶3`
- **purificative rites** (also: first purificative rites) — Rites after which the inclination is freed from the dominion of sense; prerequisite for the deeper mysteries. (ATWOOD)
  - “the inclination being already freed from the dominion and all the superficial progeny of sense” `II.3 ¶9`
- **Free Spirit** (also: Spirit) — The spirit released in trance, which can range its sphere, reveal hidden things and philosophize, but whose freedom does not guarantee truth. (ATWOOD)
  - “the intrinsic intelligence and power of the Free Spirit which can expatiate into the whole circumference of its sphere and reveal hidden things” `II.3 ¶4`
- **Theurgy** (also: Art of Theurgy) — The art whose object is to concentrate vitality, turn the spiritual eye, purify and analyze the total essence, and draw forth and know the true Efficient. (ATWOOD)
  - “this was their object and the Art of Theurgy” `II.3 ¶5`
- **Efficient** — The true active cause within the total essence, to be drawn forth and known in co-identity. (ATWOOD)
  - “draw forth the true Efficient and to know it in co-identity” `II.3 ¶5`
- **sensible medium** — The medium of sense-consciousness that trance dissolves; consciousness is later translated beneath it. (ATWOOD)
  - “dissolve the sensible medium and convert it to the experience of another life” `II.3 ¶5`
- **dissolve** (also: solution, dissolved, dissolution) — Term adopted from the old doctrine for the change in the vital relationship in trance; the Alchemists say the sensible medium ought to be dissolved; perfect solution is the beginning of the work. (ATWOOD)
  - “We adopt the term dissolve here in accordance with the old doctrine” `II.3 n2`
- **Central Source** — The goal towards which consciousness is translated beneath the sensible medium by obscuration. (ATWOOD)
  - “to translate the consciousness underneath this medium by obscuration, towards the Central Source” `II.3 ¶5`
- **Hades** — Not the corporeal nature or fleshly existence, but a state of vital submersion in the Mysteries, consciousness drawn about the penetralia of its first life. (ATWOOD)
  - = Lethe: “by Hades, or that profound Lethe” `II.3 ¶7`
  - “a state of vital submersion in the Mysteries, when the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life” `II.3 ¶7`
- **golden bough** (also: myrtle branch, branch) — Required propitiation for the descent; literally part of the sacred equipage; read ontologically as a ray of living light, the intellect. (ATWOOD)
  - = Brancha Spiritualis: “a ray of living light, golden and flexible, the true Brancha Spiritualis of Raymond Lully” `II.3 ¶11`
  - “connected with the Mercurial caduceus” `II.3 ¶11`
- **Proserpine** — The intellect's congenial life: our lapsed soul hidden in her dark hypostasis. (ATWOOD)
  - = lapsed soul: “its own congenial life, which is Proserpine, and that lapsed soul of ours, seated in her dark hypostasis unknown” `II.3 ¶11`
- **chloric ether** — A murky enveloping medium through whose circumference the intellect penetrates to the lapsed soul; not further explained. (ATWOOD)
  - “the murky circumference of the chloric ether” `II.3 ¶11`
- **Aquaster** — Linked to the middle region out of which consciousness passes, clear and rational, into the Fire World; not defined here. (ATWOOD)
  - “clear and rational from out the Aquaster, enters the Fire World” `II.3 ¶16`
- **Fire World** — The region consciousness enters after the middle region, at the dark descent; not defined here. (ATWOOD)
  - “enters the Fire World” `II.3 ¶16`
- **Aornus** (also: Aornum) — Name of the cave-place of descent; read as the heavy, inelastic nether air verging on the chaos of matter. (ATWOOD)
  - “destitute of elasticity, Aornus, heavy like that of an enclosed cave, and vast” `II.3 ¶18`
- **Black Saturn** — The adepts' name for the dark nether region and the apparent corruption preceding mystical death and regeneration. (ATWOOD)
  - = lapis niger: “as describing the same ens, they call it lapis niger, vilis, foetens” `II.3 ¶18`
  - = Urinus Saturni: “Sendivogius calls it Urinus Saturni” `II.3 ¶18`
  - “It is the Black Saturn of the adepts, and that appearing corruption that precedes the mystical death and regeneration into new life” `II.3 ¶18`
- **Ether** — Called by the Alchemists their mineral tree, with allusion to the dark matter of the descent. (ATWOOD)
  - = mineral tree: “the Alchemists also call the Ether their mineral tree” `II.3 ¶18`
- **first life** — The original life whose penetralia consciousness is drawn about in Hades, and from whose perpetual motion the nether darkness flows. (ATWOOD)
  - “flowing out from the perpetual motion of the first life” `II.3 ¶18`
- **selfhood** — Whose dark dominion is dissolved by the magnetic friction of its proper light. (ATWOOD)
  - “the dark dominion of the selfhood” `II.3 n2`
- **Elysian light** — The light reached after passing through the dark region; the object of inquiry. (ATWOOD)
  - “before inquiry can hope to meet its object in the Elysian light” `II.3 ¶5`
- **Paternal Source** — That which Intellect seeks in the allegory of the descent. (ATWOOD)
  - “an inquiry of Intellect after its Paternal Source” `II.3 ¶13`

## Ambiguities

- “where Desires are Images and Will their Act” `II.3 ¶12`
  - Reading: In the inner sphere desires appear as images and the will directly enacts them (a description of that state).
  - Reading: A precept: the mind should treat desires as mere images and let the will alone act.
  - Basis: ¶12 gives no gloss; ¶7 (deluding desires) supports the first; 'dismissing every other consideration' in ¶12 fits either.
- “the temptations of its next including sphere” `II.3 ¶9`
  - Reading: The next deeper sphere (Hades / the dark region) that encloses the purified will.
  - Reading: The sphere of sense immediately surrounding the will.
  - Basis: ¶9 says the inclination is already freed from sense, favoring the first; 'including' is not explained.
- “or anything in fact with which the whole allusion is to a state of vital submersion” `II.3 ¶7`
  - Reading: Text appears to have dropped words; the denial covers corporeal nature generally, and the positive claim is the state of vital submersion.
  - Reading: The clause may qualify which things are denied as the meaning of Hades.
  - Basis: The sentence is grammatically incomplete as printed in ¶7.
- “clear and rational from out the Aquaster, enters the Fire World” `II.3 ¶16`
  - Reading: Aquaster is the middle region itself, left behind.
  - Reading: Aquaster is something within the middle region from which consciousness emerges clear.
  - Basis: ¶16 syntax only; neither term is defined in this chunk.
