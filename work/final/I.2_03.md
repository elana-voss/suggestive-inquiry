# I.2_03

*Atwood gathers authorities on the First Matter, concludes it is a single, indescribable ethereal substance of life and light known only by experience, and explains that its many names and the Salt, Sulfur and Mercury are modes and stages of this one Proteus-like subject during the work, so that alchemical writings must be read accordingly.*

## Argument

- Basil Valentine: the First Matter is like no manifested particular; description fails without true experience. Rupecissa agrees. `I.2 ¶31` — QUOTED: Basil Valentine; Rupecissa
- Ripley: not common water or earth but a middle substance of celestial and terrestrial extremes. Atwood: a first matter is called middle or third because generated by active and passive relations of the Universal Spirit, yet homogeneal from its radix; Lully calls it tertium; Basil's verse: one thing, two and three, bind volatile and fixed. `I.2 ¶31, I.2 ¶32, I.2 n33` — ATWOOD
- Vaughan: union of masculine and feminine spirits; a formless animated silver-like mass, neither power nor act, a weak virgin substance. `I.2 ¶33, I.2 n34` — QUOTED: Vaughan
- Sendivogius: our water is heavenly, not wetting hands. Atwood: tears, rain, dew, milk, wine, oil denote the fermental principle of the spirit and its distilled quintessence. `I.2 ¶33, I.2 n35` — ATWOOD
- Synesius/New Light: the Matter is a clear Light, bond of elements; the quintessence is our celestial soul drawn from its minera by our magistery. Nature alone makes it; art only unveils it. This Latex is the vinegar making gold spirit; unknown though before all eyes; its epithets are those of Hermes' Bird; it is our sea water congealed in gold and silver, extracted by our chalybs. `I.2 ¶33, I.2 n36` — QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light
- Atwood: defining it as the simple substance of life and light flowing unmanifest through nature does not teach how to work it; only experience, earned by labor, fixes the idea. `I.2 ¶34` — ATWOOD
- It is no sensible thing: not the elements (though containing them in principle), metals, salts or any animal, vegetable or mineral, despite adepts' deceptive titles; it is the Laelia Aelia latent in all. `I.2 ¶34` — ATWOOD
- Ordinary light phenomena resemble those the adepts describe, but are shadowy and mingled beside the Philosophic Subject. As colors part from and return to white light, the one Alchemical Pan is multitudinous in offspring; a verse hails Proteus. `I.2 ¶34, I.2 ¶35` — ATWOOD
- The Mercurial quintessence produces all effects within itself. The Matter first appears pure and white from the philosophical contrition; after the artist brings all to an annihilate condition, nature reacting shows Salt, Sulfur, Mercury as agent, patient, offspring. Pan contains Proteus (Democritus, Orphic oracles). `I.2 ¶36` — ATWOOD
- Proteus, Mercury or quintessence is hidden under infinite, delusive names; in artificial fermentation it becomes everything in turn, then a potential chaos and egg of philosophers, yet the Identity stays unchanged. `I.2 ¶37, I.2 ¶38, I.2 n37` — ATWOOD
- Adepts exploit this mutability; their elements, colors, operations mean changes during preparation and multiplying the Mercury by its Light. The three principles are modes of one thing; names mark stages, like parts of a tree. `I.2 ¶39` — ATWOOD
- Stage names: vile and cheap; sublimed, dragon/green lion; putrefying, toad; calcined, magnesia, lead of the wise; dissolving, acetum; whitened oil, milk, dew, quintessence; perfected, phoenix, salamander, Red Stone. Bloomfield's verse adds more names. `I.2 ¶40, I.2 ¶41, I.2 n38` — ATWOOD
- Writers describe stages without order, to obscure; early practice is needed. Holding to the Substance's simplicity, Geber's argent vive and sulfur, Sendivogius' twofold matter and the 'third thing' mean relations of the Ethereal being. `I.2 ¶42, I.2 n39, I.2 n40` — ATWOOD
- So the Water of Hermes' separation into four, Thales and Moses is the ethereal body of life and light, a tortured water corporified by art. The Arabian: nothing added or extracted; only superfluities removed. `I.2 ¶43, I.2 n41, I.2 n42` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **subject** — “comparable to no manifested particular whatever” `I.2 ¶31` (QUOTED: Basil Valentine)
  - **Plain (stated):** The First Matter resembles no particular visible thing; it cannot be described adequately, only known by real experience.
  - Basis: Same sentence: 'all description fails in respect of it, without the light of true experience'.
  - Also: “without the light of true experience” `I.2 ¶31`
- **subject** — “a middle substance” `I.2 ¶31` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Matter is called middle or third because it is generated from the active and passive relations of the Universal Spirit, coming forth as a third thing while remaining of one nature with its root.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶31: Atwood explains 'this is done in respect to its generation by active and passive relations of the Universal Spirit, whence it proceeds as a third, yet homogeneal from its radix'.
  - Also: “Aquosa substantia sicca reperta” `I.2 ¶31`
  - Also: “Lully also calls it tertium, and compounded in this sense” `I.2 ¶31`
  - Also: “partaking of extremes celestial and terrestrial” `I.2 ¶31`
- **procedure** — “Volatile et fixum simul colliga” `I.2 ¶32` (QUOTED: Basil Valentine)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Bind the volatile and the fixed together (our translation); the verse says the whole thing proceeds from one and is one, though two and three. The chunk does not explain the operation further.
  - Basis: Rests on our translation of the Latin verse I.2 ¶32; context ¶31 on the Matter as one yet third.
  - Also: “Procedit ex uno et est res una” `I.2 ¶32`
- **subject** — “the First Matter is indeed the union of masculine and feminine spirits” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - **Plain (stated):** Vaughan's definition: the First Matter is a union of a masculine and a feminine spirit, an unformed, half-potential living substance.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶33 states it directly, adding 'neither mere power nor perfect action'.
  - Also: “a weak virgin substance, a certain soft prolific Venus, the very love and seed of nature, the mixture and moisture of heaven and earth” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Also: “a world without form, a divine animated mass of complexion like silver, neither mere power nor perfect action” `I.2 ¶33`
- **subject** — “by such familiar analogies as tears, rain, dew, milk, wine, and oil, the fermental principle of the spirit and her distilled quintessence are very ordinarily denoted” `I.2 ¶33` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Liquid images (tears, rain, dew, milk, wine, oil) in the writers stand for the fermenting principle of the spirit and its distilled quintessence, not literal liquids.
  - Basis: Atwood's direct gloss in I.2 ¶33, following Sendivogius' 'heavenly' water that does not wet the hands.
  - Also: “Our water is heavenly, not wetting the hands, not of the vulgar, but almost rain water” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Also: “Our water, that wets not the hands; without which nothing grows or is generated in the whole world!” `I.2 ¶33`
- **subject** — “the quintessence is no other than our viscous celestial and glorious soul drawn from its minera by our magistery” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - **Plain (stated):** Said 'in plain terms': the quintessence is a celestial soul, drawn from its minera by the work (magistery); this chunk does not gloss 'minera'.
  - Basis: Introduced in I.2 ¶33 by 'in plain terms'; also 'a clear Light, which fills with true virtue every mind that has once perceived it'.
  - Also: “a clear Light, which fills with true virtue every mind that has once perceived it” `I.2 ¶33`
- **principle** — “But nature alone engenders it; it is not possible to make it by art; for to create is proper to God alone” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - **Plain (stated):** The art does not create the Matter; nature produces it. The philosopher's role is only to make the hidden thing appear by removing its veil.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶33 continues: what is granted to the philosopher is 'to make things that are not perceived, but which lie in the shadow to appear, and to take from them their veil'.
  - Also: “to make things that are not perceived, but which lie in the shadow to appear, and to take from them their veil, is granted to an intelligent philosopher by God, through nature” `I.2 ¶33`
- **agent** — “this Latex is the sharp vinegar which makes gold a pure spirit, seeing she is even that blessed water which engenders al things” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - **Plain (stated):** The same Matter, as a liquid, is called the sharp vinegar; acting on gold it renders it pure spirit. It is identified with the universal generating water.
  - Basis: Identity asserted in the sentence itself (I.2 ¶33); how gold is treated is not explained in this chunk.
- **subject** — “Our subject is presented to the eyes of the whole world, and it is not known!” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - **Plain (stated):** The subject is everywhere and in plain sight but unrecognized and held cheap.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶33; I.2 ¶40 gives the reason it is called cheap: 'as the Spirit is in nature, said to be everywhere'.
  - Also: “It is of small account, yet no body can be without it” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Also: “In the common estate, as the Spirit is in nature, said to be everywhere, it is called a thing vile and cheap” `I.2 ¶40`
- **procedure** — “nothing else than our sea water, which is congealed in gold and silver, and extracted by the help of our chalybs, or steel, by the art of philosophers” `I.2 ¶33` (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The precious thing is 'our sea water', said to be fixed in gold and silver and drawn out by means of an agent called chalybs or steel. This chunk does not explain what the chalybs is.
  - Basis: Wording of I.2 ¶33 only; 'sea' here is the 'sea of the world' (same ¶), so not common sea water.
- **subject** — “the simple generated substance of life and light, immanifestly flowing throughout nature” `I.2 ¶34` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Atwood's own tentative definition: an unseen, simple substance of life and light pervading nature, necessary to every existing thing.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶34, Atwood speaking ('if we presume to add here'); echoed in ¶43 'the ethereal body of life and light'.
  - Also: “that without which nothing that exists is able to be” `I.2 ¶34`
  - Also: “the alone Laelia Aelia latent in and about all” `I.2 ¶34`
- **operator** — “needing actual experience to fix its idea” `I.2 ¶34` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Words cannot give a true notion of the Matter; only direct experience can, and it comes to the patient worker as reward of personal labor and perseverance.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶34; reinforced ¶42: truth 'can hardly be well conceived, without an insight into the experimental ground'.
  - Also: “This the art promises to a patient and true philosopher, but as a reward of individual labor and perseverance only.” `I.2 ¶34`
  - Also: “which the Alchemists alone teach experimentally to expound” `I.2 ¶34`
- **subject** — “it is no one of the many things with which sense brings us acquainted” `I.2 ¶34` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Matter is not any sensible substance: not water, earth, air, fire, gold, silver, mercury, antimony, alkali, gas or vitriol, nor any animal, vegetable or mineral particular; these names in the books are deceptive.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶34 lists the exclusions explicitly.
  - Also: “neither water, nor earth, nor air, nor fire, though it contains in principle the nature of all these” `I.2 ¶34`
  - Also: “though these titles are found interspersed abundantly with others, equally deceptive, in the pages of the adepts” `I.2 ¶34`
- **principle** — “so is it said to be with the Alchemical Pan, who, being but one himself, is in his offspring multitudinous” `I.2 ¶34` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** One substance shows itself in many forms and colors and returns to unity, as white light splits into colors through a prism and recombines.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶34 gives the prism analogy explicitly; ¶36 'Pan contains Proteus'.
  - Also: “For Pan contains Proteus” `I.2 ¶36`
- **principle** — “so is the Mercurial quintessence said to produce all various effects, but within itself consummately without external reference, or elementary confusion” `I.2 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Like light and heat acting on bodies, the quintessence hardens, softens, destroys and nourishes, but these changes happen within itself, not by mixture with outside things.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶36, analogy stated in the same sentence.
- **stage** — “the Matter only as it first appears, pure, as they say, and white, out of the philosophical contrition” `I.2 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The descriptions so far concern the Matter's first appearance, pure and white, after a breaking-down called the philosophical contrition. The chunk does not explain the contrition.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶36.
- **stage** — “when the wise artist has brought all into this annihilate condition, and pressed out the waters of her extreme life” `I.2 ¶36` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** After the artist reduces everything to this annihilated state, nature reacts and the one Matter displays three principles, Salt, Sulfur and Mercury, as agent, patient and offspring.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶36: 'nature re-acting, as it is said, exhibits from out her unity three great magnetic principles of being'.
  - Also: “three great magnetic principles of being --- the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury of adepts, in relation to each other of agent, patient, and offspring universal” `I.2 ¶36`
- **principle** — “The three principles, the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury, are merely different as modes of being of the same thing” `I.2 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Salt, Sulfur and Mercury, and the twofold or 'third' matter of other authors, are not separate substances but relations (action and passion) within the one ethereal being; the many names mark its stages of development, as parts of one tree.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶39 and Atwood's explicit reading rule in ¶42.
  - Also: “the many names arising out of the action and passion of these, do but indicate the stages of progress and development, as of a tree” `I.2 ¶39`
  - Also: “but of the magnetic relation, action, and passion of the Ethereal being in itself” `I.2 ¶42`
  - Also: “the thing which perfects in minerals, is the substance of argent vive and sulfur, proportionally commixt in the bowels of clean inspissate earth” `I.2 ¶42`
- **subject** — “This Proteus, then, or Mercury, or quintessence of philosophers, is warily concealed by them under an infinity of names, all more or less applicable, yet delusive” `I.2 ¶37` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Proteus, Mercury and quintessence are one subject; because it is universal every name fits it somewhat, so the names mislead.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶37: 'nothing can be said amiss of a Universal Subjet'; verse ¶41 'few or none at all doe name it in its kinde'.
  - Also: “But few or none at all doe name it in its kinde, It is a privy quintessence; keepe it well in minde” `I.2 ¶41`
  - Also: “And these are the Epithetes of Hermes, his Bird, which is never at rest.” `I.2 ¶33`
- **procedure** — “In its artificial fermentation and progress towards perfection, the changes it undergoes are manifold” `I.2 ¶37` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** During the worked fermentation the subject passes through every form in turn, then becomes a formless potential chaos ('egg of philosophers'), yet as one Identity remains unchanged throughout.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶37.
  - Also: “but a potential chaos and egg of philosophers; a precedential, nameless principle, always in mutation, becoming to be” `I.2 ¶37`
  - Also: “Proceeding homogeneal through each omniform variety, and returning into herself manifestly the life and all phenomena which she as constantly supplies, the great Identity is as herself unchanged” `I.2 ¶37`
- **sign** — “it is to the qualities and changes which take place during the preparation, and multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light, they allude” `I.2 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** When adepts speak of elements, colors and operations, they mean changes occurring in the subject during its preparation and the multiplying of the Mercury by its own Light, not chemical elements or surface phenomena.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶39.
  - Also: “not to any superficial phenomena or those elements which the moderns have so triumphantly decomposed” `I.2 ¶39`
- **result** — “in its perfectly prepared form, a medicine the most potent and precious in the whole world” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The same thing that is cheap and everywhere in its common state becomes, when fully prepared, the most potent medicine; intermediate stages share in either extreme.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40.
  - Also: “the intermediate stages partake of the predominance of either extreme” `I.2 ¶40`
- **stage** — “being sublimed at first, it is called a serpent, dragon, or green lion, on account of its strength and crude vitality” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Serpent, dragon, green lion name the first sublimed stage, so called for its raw strength and vitality.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40.
- **stage** — “which putrefying, becomes a stronger poison, and their venomous toad” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The toad names the putrefying stage that follows sublimation.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40; verse ¶41 'Some call it a Toade for his great vehemence'.
  - Also: “Some call it a Toade for his great vehemence” `I.2 ¶41`
- **stage** — “which afterwards appearing calcined by its proper fire, is called magnesia and lead of the wise” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Magnesia and lead of the wise name the stage when the matter is calcined by its own fire.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40.
- **stage** — “which again dissolving, becomes their vitriolic solvent and most sharp acetum” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The vitriolic solvent or sharp vinegar (acetum) names the next, dissolving stage.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40; compare ¶33 'the sharp vinegar which makes gold a pure spirit'.
  - Also: “this Latex is the sharp vinegar which makes gold a pure spirit” `I.2 ¶33`
- **stage** — “this afterwards is changed into an oil, which, whitening, is called milk, dew, quintessence, and by may other names” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Milk, dew and quintessence name the stage when the matter becomes an oil and whitens.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40.
- **result** — “until raised to the final perfection, it is henceforth a phoenix, salamander, their royal essence and Red Stone” `I.2 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Phoenix, salamander, royal essence and Red Stone name the final perfected state.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶40.
- **impediment** — “now describing in the natural state, then in its purified condition, or otherwise in any one of the intermediate stages through which it passes, without note of order in the art” `I.2 ¶42` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Writers describe the subject partially, at random stages, in metaphor, deliberately obscuring; so the texts contradict themselves until the starting ground is understood, and early practice is needed to tell the true light among the images.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶42; also ¶39 'to baffle the blind searcher'.
  - Also: “Practice in the beginning is required, therefore, to interrogate and discern, from amongst so many shadowy representatives, the true light.” `I.2 ¶42`
  - Also: “Adepts have taken advantage of the mutable nature of their subject, to baffle the blind searcher” `I.2 ¶39`
- **principle** — “Constantly holding in mind the simplicity of the Substance, whence these images are all derived” `I.2 ¶42` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Reading rule: all the animal and bird images derive from one simple Substance; keeping this in mind lets the reader pass through the symbolism safely.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶42.
  - Also: “this Hermetic labyrinth of birds and wild beasts” `I.2 ¶42`
- **subject** — “a certain tortured water, having suffered alteration by art and becomes corporified” `I.2 ¶43` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The 'Water' of Hermes (and of Thales and Moses) is not ordinary water but the ethereal body of life and light, altered by art and made bodily.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶43: 'it is the ethereal body of life and light which they profess to have discovered'.
  - Also: “it is not the common elementated water to which he alludes” `I.2 ¶43`
- **procedure** — “the separation of the ancient philosophers is made upon Water, dividing into four substances” `I.2 ¶43` (QUOTED: Hermes; the Arabian)
  - **Plain (stated):** A separation is performed on this ethereal Water, dividing it into four substances; nothing different is added or extracted, only superfluities removed. The four substances are not identified here.
  - Basis: I.2 ¶43, Hermes and the Arabian; Atwood glosses the Water as ethereal in the same ¶.
  - Also: “to which we add nothing different or extract, only in the separation removing superfluities” `I.2 ¶43`

## Terms

- **First Matter** (also: fist matter, Matter, true Matter of the Alchemists) — The one indescribable subject of the art: a middle/third substance generated from the Universal Spirit's active and passive relations; the simple substance of life and light flowing unmanifest through nature; not any sensible thing. (ATWOOD)
  - = union of masculine and feminine spirits: “the First Matter is indeed the union of masculine and feminine spirits” `I.2 ¶33`
  - “the simple generated substance of life and light, immanifestly flowing throughout nature” `I.2 ¶34`
  - “Thus obscure, after all, is the true Matter of the Alchemists” `I.2 ¶34`
- **Universal Spirit** (also: Spirit) — Source whose active and passive relations generate the First Matter; in its common estate said to be everywhere in nature. (ATWOOD)
  - “its generation by active and passive relations of the Universal Spirit” `I.2 ¶31`
  - “as the Spirit is in nature, said to be everywhere” `I.2 ¶40`
- **quintessence** (also: Mercurial quintessence, privy quintessence, quintessence of philosophers) — The subject as celestial soul drawn out by the work; produces all effects within itself; also a name of the whitened oil stage. (ATWOOD)
  - = soul: “the quintessence is no other than our viscous celestial and glorious soul drawn from its minera by our magistery” `I.2 ¶33`
  - = Proteus; Mercury: “This Proteus, then, or Mercury, or quintessence of philosophers” `I.2 ¶37`
  - “which, whitening, is called milk, dew, quintessence” `I.2 ¶40`
- **Proteus** — The ever-changing omniform aspect of the one subject; contained in Pan. (ATWOOD)
  - = Mercury; quintessence: “This Proteus, then, or Mercury, or quintessence of philosophers” `I.2 ¶37`
  - “For Pan contains Proteus” `I.2 ¶36`
  - “Etherial, watery, earthy general soul, Immortal Fire! Even all the world is thine And pars of thee, O Proteus, power divine;” `I.2 ¶35`
- **Pan** (also: Alchemical Pan) — The subject as one being whose offspring are multitudinous; contains Proteus and evolves all forms from his central will. (ATWOOD)
  - “so is it said to be with the Alchemical Pan, who, being but one himself, is in his offspring multitudinous” `I.2 ¶34`
- **Mercury** (also: mercury) — Name of the subject (Proteus/quintessence) and one of the three principles; 'multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light' is part of the preparation. Common mercury is excluded as the Matter. (ATWOOD)
  - = Proteus; quintessence: “This Proteus, then, or Mercury, or quintessence of philosophers” `I.2 ¶37`
  - “multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light” `I.2 ¶39`
- **Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury** (also: three principles, three great magnetic principles of being) — Three principles nature exhibits from the Matter's unity after the annihilate condition, related as agent, patient and offspring; modes of being of one thing. (ATWOOD)
  - “The three principles, the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury, are merely different as modes of being of the same thing” `I.2 ¶39`
- **philosophical contrition** — Process out of which the Matter first appears pure and white; not further explained here. (ATWOOD)
  - “pure, as they say, and white, out of the philosophical contrition” `I.2 ¶36`
- **annihilate condition** — State into which the artist brings all, pressing out 'the waters of her extreme life', after which nature reacts. (ATWOOD)
  - “when the wise artist has brought all into this annihilate condition” `I.2 ¶36`
- **magistery** — The work by which the soul/quintessence is drawn from its minera. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - “drawn from its minera by our magistery” `I.2 ¶33`
- **minera** — That from which the quintessence is drawn; not glossed in this chunk. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - “drawn from its minera by our magistery” `I.2 ¶33`
- **Latex** — The Matter as liquid. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - = sharp vinegar; blessed water: “this Latex is the sharp vinegar which makes gold a pure spirit, seeing she is even that blessed water which engenders al things” `I.2 ¶33`
- **chalybs** — Means by which 'our sea water' is extracted; otherwise unexplained. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - = steel: “our chalybs, or steel” `I.2 ¶33`
- **sea water** (also: sea of the world, our sea) — The precious thing, congealed in gold and silver; abiding 'in the sea of the world'. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - = a ting more precious than the whole world: “a ting more precious than the whole world; which I plainly tell thee is nothing else than our sea water” `I.2 ¶33`
- **caput mortuum** — One of the epithets of the Matter. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - = dead head; foeces of our sea: “O our caput mortuum, or dead head, or foeces of our sea!” `I.2 ¶33`
- **Hermes, his Bird** — The listed epithets of the Matter (heaven, water, mercury, salt nitre, sulfur, caput mortuum etc.) are said to be its epithets; never at rest. (QUOTED: Synesius; the New Light)
  - “And these are the Epithetes of Hermes, his Bird, which is never at rest.” `I.2 ¶33`
- **Laelia Aelia** — Name for the Matter as latent in and about all; celebrated by 'the Enigma'. Not further explained. (ATWOOD)
  - “the alone Laelia Aelia latent in and about all, which the Enigma celebrates as comprehending all” `I.2 ¶34`
- **Philosophic Subject** (also: Universal Subjet, Our subject) — The Matter as subject of the work, with virtue and perfective properties beyond ordinary light phenomena; universal, so every epithet fits it. (ATWOOD)
  - “the alleged virtue and perfective properties of the Philosophic Subject” `I.2 ¶34`
  - “inasmuch as nothing can be said amiss of a Universal Subjet” `I.2 ¶37`
- **egg of philosophers** — The subject when it is 'nothing', a formless potential state amid its mutations. (ATWOOD)
  - = potential chaos: “but a potential chaos and egg of philosophers” `I.2 ¶37`
- **fermentation** (also: fermental principle) — The artificial process of the subject's progress to perfection; the 'fermental principle of the spirit' is denoted by liquid analogies. (ATWOOD)
  - “In its artificial fermentation and progress towards perfection” `I.2 ¶37`
  - “the fermental principle of the spirit and her distilled quintessence” `I.2 ¶33`
- **green lion** — Name, with serpent and dragon, of the matter when first sublimed, for its strength and crude vitality. (ATWOOD)
  - “it is called a serpent, dragon, or green lion, on account of its strength and crude vitality” `I.2 ¶40`
- **toad** (also: Toade) — Name of the putrefying stage, a stronger poison. (ATWOOD)
  - “which putrefying, becomes a stronger poison, and their venomous toad” `I.2 ¶40`
- **magnesia** — Name of the matter calcined by its proper fire. (ATWOOD)
  - = lead of the wise: “is called magnesia and lead of the wise” `I.2 ¶40`
- **acetum** — Name of the dissolving stage. (ATWOOD)
  - = vitriolic solvent: “becomes their vitriolic solvent and most sharp acetum” `I.2 ¶40`
- **Red Stone** — The final perfection of the matter. (ATWOOD)
  - = phoenix; salamander; royal essence: “it is henceforth a phoenix, salamander, their royal essence and Red Stone” `I.2 ¶40`

## Ambiguities

- “the fermental principle of the spirit and her distilled quintessence” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Reading: 'her' refers to the spirit (the spirit's distilled quintessence)
  - Reading: 'her' refers to nature or the Matter
  - Basis: Nearest antecedent is 'the spirit'; elsewhere Atwood uses feminine pronouns for the Matter/nature (¶36 'her extreme life', ¶37 'herself').
- “pressed out the waters of her extreme life” `I.2 ¶36`
  - Reading: 'her' = nature, mentioned next ('nature re-acting')
  - Reading: 'her' = the Matter brought into the annihilate condition
  - Basis: ¶36 grammar allows either; ¶37 uses 'she/herself' for the subject.
- “the alone Laelia Aelia latent in and about all, which the Enigma celebrates as comprehending all” `I.2 ¶34`
  - Reading: A proper name for the Matter drawn from 'the Enigma'
  - Reading: An allusion whose sense the chunk does not give
  - Basis: The chunk names neither the Enigma's author nor the meaning of 'Laelia Aelia'.
- “extracted by the help of our chalybs, or steel” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Reading: An agent/means in the work called steel
  - Reading: A further epithet of the subject itself
  - Basis: Only this mention; the chunk does not explain it.
- “multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light” `I.2 ¶39`
  - Reading: The Mercury is increased by its own inherent light
  - Reading: 'Light' refers to a distinct agent proper to the Mercury
  - Basis: The Matter is 'a clear Light' (¶33) and 'substance of life and light' (¶34), favoring the first, but the chunk does not specify.
- “It is, says this esteemed author, speaking of the same Matter, a clear Light” `I.2 ¶33`
  - Reading: The whole passage to the end of ¶33 is Synesius
  - Reading: The passage blends Synesius and Sendivogius' New Light
  - Basis: Introduced as 'from the ancient book of Synesius, and the New Light'; n36 cites both.
- “Hear me, O Mercury, and Son of Maia; the bright expositor of things!” `I.2 ¶36`
  - Reading: From the Orphic oracles
  - Reading: From another unnamed source
  - Basis: Preceded by 'as the Orphic oracles declares; also of Mercury, with like allusion' with no footnote.

## Translations

- `I.2 ¶31` “Aquosa substantia sicca reperta” → A watery substance found dry. *(ours)*
- `I.2 ¶32` “Corpus anima spiritus in duobus existit, Ex quibus tota res procedit: Procedit ex uno et est res una, Volatile et fixum simul colliga, Sunt duo et tria et saltem unum Si non intelliges, nihil obtines” → Body, soul, spirit exist in two, from which the whole thing proceeds: it proceeds from one and is one thing; bind the volatile and the fixed together; they are two and three and yet one. If you do not understand, you obtain nothing. *(ours)*
- `I.2 ¶38` “Et, quanto illa magis formas se vertet in omnes, Tanto, nate, magis tenacia vincla” → And the more she turns herself into all forms, the more, son, [hold fast] the tenacious bonds. (The second line as printed lacks its verb.) *(ours)*
- `I.2 ¶41` “Some limus derti from his body evacuate, Some the Eagle flying fro’ the northwith violence,” → Some [call it] slime of earth (?) evacuated from its body; some the Eagle flying from the north with violence. *(ours)*

## Threads

- `I.2 ¶36` 'as we have seen before from Democritus' — earlier citation that Pan contains Proteus.
- `I.2 ¶33` 'We conclude these verbal instructions' — closes the series of authorities on the First Matter begun before this chunk.
- `I.2 ¶34` 'We may content ourselves thus early' with negative definition — implies fuller treatment later; the Alchemists 'alone teach experimentally to expound' it.
- `I.2 ¶36` 'Hitherto we have had account of the Matter only as it first appears, pure ... and white' — the preceding descriptions concern its first appearance.
- `I.2 ¶42` Lully, Ripley, Basil calling it a 'third thing' refers back to ¶31.
- `I.2 ¶43` 'from the foregoing we may also judge' — applies the preceding reading rule to the Water of Hermes.
