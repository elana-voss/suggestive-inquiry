# II.1_03

*Completes the cited account of the colour stages and multiplication of the Stone, then turns, through Vaughan, Agrippa and Manilius, to Atwood's conclusion that man is the true laboratory and subject of the Hermetic art, with Self-Knowledge at its root, while conceding that the higher faculty of Wisdom once claimed for man is now beyond ordinary sense and unassisted reason.*

## Argument

- Three Principles take on a new, stronger life without violent contest; three months of harmonious action give brilliant whiteness, three more a yellow, red or purpling tincture; the king is invested with the red garment, sin's reign finished. `II.1 ¶24` — QUOTED: Boehme
- Atwood calls this the process of working with the Vital Spirit, reiterated by Hermes, Democritus and others, and passed through many times. `II.1 ¶25` — ATWOOD
- Other matter only burns to gas and ashes; this mystical nature revives strengthened from each dissolution. `II.1 ¶25` — ATWOOD
- A failed stage is renewed by adding more Spirit; each digestion multiplies the white or red powder tenfold, in a tenth of the previous time. `II.1 ¶25` — QUOTED: the adept
- This gold's magnetical virtue draws divine increase; Art adds supernatural light, since only light multiplies. Hence it is called Microcosmic Sun, triumphal Chariot of Antimony, Stone of Fire. `II.1 ¶25, II.1 n21` — UNCLEAR
- The true tincture of Antimony is not shop antimony but extracted from the true mineral; that extraction is Alchemy's principal secret. Latin: the stone lies hidden in you and me. `II.1 ¶25, II.1 n22` — QUOTED: Basil Valentine
- Adepts will one day emerge as physicians of body and soul; now the Seal of God conceals the ground, and it must be entrusted person to person; the Work is easy, its wisdom the greatest mystery. `II.1 ¶25, II.1 n23` — QUOTED: Boehme
- Existence is the only mystery, one identity in all life; the principle of being within us should be knowable, as the apostle says God is near and to be sought. `II.1 ¶26, II.1 n24` — ATWOOD
- Vaughan: know the Protochemic Artifice before Wisdom; use hands not fancies, turn abstractions into extractions, pierce experimentally to the centre rather than judging by the crust. `II.1 ¶26, II.1 n25` — QUOTED: Vaughan
- Vaughan: a Spirit actuates every generation through a passive intrinsical principle and a chain linking visible and invisible; the Art unites a Particular Spirit to the Universal (not the divine spirit). `II.1 ¶26` — QUOTED: Vaughan
- Agrippa: a simple, pure mind, laid asleep from its present life, enters its divine nature, is enlightened and gains wonderful powers; the Operator of miracles is in us. Atwood: both passages allude to Alchemy. `II.1 ¶26, II.1 ¶27, II.1 n26, II.1 n27` — QUOTED: Agrippa
- Literal magic books are ridiculous without the key; alchemical records mislead those seeking abroad what is found only by seeking experimentally within. Manilius (our translation): men have the world within, each a small image of God. `II.1 ¶28, II.1 ¶29, II.1 n28` — ATWOOD
- Conclusion: man is the true laboratory, his life the subject, distillatory, distiller and distilled; Self-Knowledge is alchemy's root. The unwilling and gold-seekers are not invited. `II.1 ¶30` — ATWOOD
- Modern science (light as vital sustenant) tends to confirm the old creed, yet nothing now proves man a perfect microcosm or Self-Knowledge a way to Universal Nature, though once taught as proved. `II.1 ¶31` — ATWOOD
- Man ranked high not for body or elements but for a Divine Reason, occult principle of Causal Efficience, and a germ of Wisdom that, set alone, knows nature intuitively and works magically. `II.1 ¶32` — QUOTED: the cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience
- Atwood: such experience is departed or its conditions estranged; knowledge begins in sense, and unassisted reason ends negatively as the Subject Identity slides behind the mind. `II.1 ¶32` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **stage** — “mild action of the principles in harmony have produced a brilliant whiteness in the matter, which in three months more become a brilliant yellow, red, or purpling tincture” `II.1 ¶24` (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - **Plain (stated):** The principles take on new life and act without violent contest: three months more to brilliant whiteness, three more to a yellow, red or purpling tincture.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶24 gives the sequence and timing directly.
  - Also: “The same Three Principles gradually assume a new life, continues Boehme, infinitely more powerful in virtue, but without any violent contest” `II.1 ¶24`
- **result** — “the reign of sin is finished, and the king doth rule, and now he is invested wit the red garment” `II.1 ¶24` (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The red stage marks completion, figured as a king's coronation and the end of sin's reign; this chunk does not explain the king.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶24 places this exclamation directly after the red tincture is reached.
  - Also: “now the scarlet color is put on” `II.1 ¶24`
- **principle** — “That was the process of working with the Vital Spirit” `II.1 ¶25` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Atwood identifies the colour process just described as work with the Vital Spirit, and says it is repeated many times in practice.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25.
  - Also: “which also is many times passed through for the practical accomplishment” `II.1 ¶25`
- **subject** — “this mystical nature revives, fortified from every successive dissolution, renewing its Whole resolutely from either extreme by union” `II.1 ¶25` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Unlike ordinary matter, which burning only reduces to gas and ashes, the true subject comes back stronger after each dissolution.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25 contrasts it explicitly with 'every other matter'.
  - Also: “every other matter labors for this perfection in vain; it can only attain to combustion, heat, and temporary light” `II.1 ¶25`
- **procedure** — “This Spirit is so full of life, says the adept, that if the process fails in any stage, an addition of the same will renew it” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: the adept)
  - **Plain (stated):** A failed stage can be restored by adding more of the same Spirit.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25.
- **procedure** — “The white or red powder is increased tenfold in strength and quantity by each digestion of it with fresh i, wet with gas water, or oil of this antimony” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: the adept)
  - **Plain (stated):** Multiplication: each further digestion with fresh material multiplies the product tenfold, and each takes a tenth of the time of the last (a week down to a few hours).
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25; the added material is garbled in the text ('fresh i').
  - Also: “each digestion is made in tenfold shorter time than the preceding from a week to a few hours” `II.1 ¶25`
- **principle** — “adds to the natural effulgence a supernatural light; for what else but light should multiply?” `II.1 ¶25` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (stated):** What multiplies in the Stone is light: nature exhausts herself, and Art, by graduation, adds a supernatural light to the natural one; the gold's 'magnetical virtue' draws this increase.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25 states the rhetorical identification of the multiplying thing with light.
  - Also: “this gold is endued with a magnetical virtue, which by the inspissate fulgor of its tincture, draws the divine increase after it” `II.1 ¶25`
  - Also: “nature expends all her forces, but leaves the victory to Art” `II.1 ¶25`
- **sign** — “the Stone of Fire seen in bright lines, of equal attraction and repulsion, when made manifest, as it were, an armed magnet included and circulating in a perpetual heaven” `II.1 ¶25` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (stated):** How the Stone of Fire appears when made manifest: bright lines, equal attraction and repulsion, like an armed magnet circulating in a perpetual heaven. The chunk does not explain further.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25 describes the appearance; no further gloss.
- **subject** — “is not made of crude melted antimony, such as the apothecaries and merchants sell, but is extracted from the true mineral, as it is taken from the mountains” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: Basil Valentine)
  - **Plain (stated):** The 'antimony' of the tincture is not the commercial substance; the true matter must be extracted from a 'true mineral', and how to extract it is the principal secret.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25 states the negative and the secrecy; what the 'true mineral' is remains unexplained (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “how that extraction should be made, is a principal secret in which the whole art of Alchemy consists” `II.1 ¶25`
- **subject** — “Lapis noster inter duos monticulos nascitur; in te et in me at in nostri similibus latet” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: Kirchringius / M. Dunstani (per n22))
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Stone lies hidden in human beings ('in you and in me and in those like us').
  - Basis: Our translation of II.1 ¶25 (untranslated in the book); consistent with Atwood's conclusion in II.1 ¶28 and ¶30 that the subject is within man.
- **impediment** — “the Seal of God lieth before it, to conceal the true ground, unless a man knew for certain that it would not be misused” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - **Plain (stated):** The ground of the art is deliberately concealed against misuse; it cannot be gained by skill alone but must be entrusted from one person to another.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25.
  - Also: “there is no power to obtain, no art or skill availeth, unless one intrust another with somewhat (as Hermes and Arnold bear witness)” `II.1 ¶25`
- **principle** — “the Work is easy and simple, but the wisdom therein is great and the greatest mystery” `II.1 ¶25` (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operation itself is simple; the difficulty is in the understanding.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25.
- **principle** — “If, therefore, we contain within us a proper principle of being, why should not this, thus proximate, be known?” `II.1 ¶26` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Atwood argues the principle of our own being is near at hand within us and therefore knowable; the one mystery is Existence, identical in all life.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26.
  - Also: “life in every particular the same inscrutable Identity through all” `II.1 ¶26`
- **procedure** — “to change their abstractions into extractions” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - **Plain (stated):** Knowledge of the hidden foundation comes only through the practical 'Protochemic Artifice', by experimentally penetrating to the centre of things, not by speculation about their outward effects.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26: 'by means of this, and this only, the true foundation is discoverable'; 'use their hands and not their fancies'.
  - Also: “pierce not experimentally into the center of things” `II.1 ¶26`
  - Also: “use their hands and not their fancies” `II.1 ¶26`
- **agent** — “there is in nature a certain Spirit which applies himself to the matter, and actuates in every generation” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - **Plain (stated):** A Spirit drives every generation; it acts through a passive inner principle where it resides most directly, and through that reaches gross matter, by a chain linking visible and invisible.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26.
  - Also: “a passive intrinsical principle where he is more immediately resident than in the rest” `II.1 ¶26`
  - Also: “a certain chain or subordinate propinquity of complexions between visibles and invisibles” `II.1 ¶26`
- **result** — “a certain Art by which a Particular Spirit may be united to the Universal” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - **Plain (stated):** The aim of the Art is to unite an individual spirit with the universal one, thereby exalting and multiplying nature; Vaughan says he does not mean the divine spirit here.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26.
  - Also: “nature by consequence be strangely exalted and multiplied” `II.1 ¶26`
- **procedure** — “by a certain mysterious recreation and appeasing, the human mind, especially that which is simple and pure, may be converted and laid asleep from its present life” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Agrippa (endorsed by ATWOOD))
  - **Plain (stated):** By a 'mysterious recreation and appeasing' (means not explained), the mind is converted and laid asleep from its present life, brought into its divine nature, enlightened, and receives the virtue of wonderful effects; Atwood says this alludes to Alchemy.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26 (Agrippa); Atwood: 'Both these passages are in allusion to the art of Alchemy'. The nature of the 'recreation and appeasing' is not explained.
  - Also: “be brought into its divine nature, and become enlightened with the divine light” `II.1 ¶26`
- **operator** — “in us is the Operator of all wonderful effects” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - **Plain (stated):** The agent of the miraculous effects is within the human being, not external spirits.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26 contrasts this with magicians etc. working 'by spirits'; II.1 ¶27 verse.
  - Also: “in us, I say, is the Operator of miracles” `II.1 ¶26`
  - Also: “But the Spirit begetting all doth in us dwell” `II.1 ¶27`
- **operator** — “simple and pure” `II.1 ¶26` (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - **Plain (stated):** The mind best suited to this conversion is simple and pure.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26: 'especially that which is simple and pure'.
- **subject** — “that perfection which was to be found only by experimentally seeking at home within themselves” `II.1 ¶28` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The perfection alchemists describe is sought experimentally within oneself, not in external matter.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶28.
- **subject** — “Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art; his life the subject, the grand distillatory, the thing distilling and the thing distilled” `II.1 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The human being is the whole apparatus, operator and material of the art; the subject is his life.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶30; phrased as a question but defended in what follows.
- **principle** — “Self-Knowledge to be at the root of all Alchemical tradition” `II.1 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Alchemy rests on self-knowledge, formerly held to lead to knowledge of Universal Nature.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶30; II.1 ¶31 'Self-Knowledge would be a way to the knowledge of the Universal Nature ... this was taught and believed formerly'.
  - Also: “Self-Knowledge would be a way to the knowledge of the Universal Nature” `II.1 ¶31`
- **impediment** — “we invite not the unwilling, nor will these studies be found to reward the sordid seeker after riches or gold only” `II.1 ¶30` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Unwilling students and those after gold alone are excluded.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶30.
- **principle** — “identifying light, as the common vital sustenant” `II.1 ¶31` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Atwood cites modern science as treating light as the common sustainer of life, in accord through the human circulation with the planets and the medium of space.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶31.
- **agent** — “a Divine Reason, on occult principle of Causal Efficience, said to be originally resident in his life” `II.1 ¶32` (QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience)
  - **Plain (stated):** What set man apart was not his body but a hidden causal, divine rational principle in his life.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶32 contrasts this with physical constitution and the four elements.
- **result** — “the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom, which, when revealed and set alone, all the forms of things and hidden springs of nature become intuitively known” `II.1 ¶32` (QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience)
  - **Plain (stated):** Man holds in germ a faculty above ordinary perception which, once revealed and isolated, knows nature intuitively and works magically as nature's source.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶32.
  - Also: “it works magically withal, discovering latent properties as a principle, governing and supplying all dependent existence” `II.1 ¶32`
  - Also: “free perspicacity of thought in universal consciousness which is cognizant by rapport with essential being” `II.1 ¶32`
- **impediment** — “every attempt of the unassisted reason terminates negatively, as the Subject Identity slides evermore behind the retardant mind” `II.1 ¶32` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Ordinary reason, bound to sense, cannot grasp the Subject Identity; Atwood adds that if the experience of Wisdom was ever granted to man, it is now departed or its conditions are estranged.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶32.
  - Also: “it is now either wholly departed or the conditions are estranged” `II.1 ¶32`
  - Also: “Man from his birth employs sense prior to reflection” `II.1 ¶32`

## Terms

- **Three Principles** — The principles of the work that take on new life and act in harmony to produce whiteness then the tincture; Boehme also speaks of their 'mechanical part'. (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - “The same Three Principles gradually assume a new life” `II.1 ¶24`
  - “the mechanical part of the Three Principles” `II.1 ¶25`
- **Vital Spirit** — That which is worked with in the colour process; so full of life that adding more of it renews a failed stage. (ATWOOD)
  - “the process of working with the Vital Spirit” `II.1 ¶25`
  - “This Spirit is so full of life” `II.1 ¶25`
- **tincture** — The coloured product of the work (yellow, red or purpling); the true tincture of Antimony is the medicine of men and metals. (QUOTED: Boehme; Basil Valentine)
  - = medicine of men and metals: “this true tincture of Antimony, which is the medicine of men and metals” `II.1 ¶25`
  - “a brilliant yellow, red, or purpling tincture” `II.1 ¶24`
- **Antimony** (also: antimony) — Name used for the matter of the tincture; explicitly not the crude antimony sold by apothecaries, but extracted from 'the true mineral'. (QUOTED: the adept; Basil Valentine)
  - “oil of this antimony” `II.1 ¶25`
  - “not made of crude melted antimony, such as the apothecaries and merchants sell” `II.1 ¶25`
- **digestion** — The operation by which the white or red powder is multiplied tenfold each time. (QUOTED: the adept)
  - “each digestion is made in tenfold shorter time than the preceding” `II.1 ¶25`
- **this gold** — The finished product, having a magnetical virtue that draws divine increase. (UNCLEAR)
  - = terrestrial or Microcosmic Sun; triumphal Chariot of Antimony: “Whence it has been called likewise the terrestrial or Microcosmic Sun, the triumphal Chariot of Antimony” `II.1 ¶25`
  - = Stone of Fire: “and this is the Stone of Fire seen in bright lines” `II.1 ¶25`
  - “this gold is endued with a magnetical virtue” `II.1 ¶25`
- **magnetical virtue** — The drawing power of the gold by which it attracts divine increase; the Stone is likened to an armed magnet. (UNCLEAR)
  - “an armed magnet included and circulating in a perpetual heaven” `II.1 ¶25`
- **Art** — The human operation that completes what nature cannot, adding supernatural light by graduation; in Vaughan, an Art uniting a Particular Spirit to the Universal. (UNCLEAR)
  - “leaves the victory to Art” `II.1 ¶25`
- **Seal of God** — The concealment lying before the ground of the art to prevent misuse. (QUOTED: Boehme)
  - “the Seal of God lieth before it, to conceal the true ground” `II.1 ¶25`
- **Protochemic Artifice** — The practical art by which alone the true foundation is discoverable; prerequisite to the philosophy of Wisdom. (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - “a knowledge of the Protochemic Artifice; for that by means of this, and this only, the true foundation is discoverable” `II.1 ¶26`
- **Particular Spirit / Universal** — An individual spirit which the Art unites to the Universal; distinguished from 'the divine spirit'. (QUOTED: Vaughan)
  - “a certain Art by which a Particular Spirit may be united to the Universal” `II.1 ¶26`
- **Operator** — The worker of wonderful effects, located within us. (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - “in us is the Operator of all wonderful effects” `II.1 ¶26`
- **Self-Knowledge** — The root of alchemical tradition; formerly held to be a way to knowledge of Universal Nature. (ATWOOD)
  - “Self-Knowledge to be at the root of all Alchemical tradition” `II.1 ¶30`
- **laboratory** — Used of man himself as the place of the Hermetic work. (ATWOOD)
  - = Man: “Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art” `II.1 ¶30`
- **microcosm** (also: Microcosmic) — Man as one in whom the great world and its creatures may be discerned in summary; a compendium of created nature. (QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience (reported by ATWOOD, who says it cannot now be proved))
  - “man is a perfect microcosm, wherein, as it was said, the great world and all its creatures might be summarily discerned” `II.1 ¶31`
  - “man, say they, is demonstrated to be a compendium of the whole created nature” `II.1 ¶32`
- **Divine Reason** — The principle, said to be originally resident in man's life, for which he was ranked high. (QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience)
  - = occult principle of Causal Efficience: “a Divine Reason, on occult principle of Causal Efficience, said to be originally resident in his life” `II.1 ¶32`
- **Universal Reason** — Magnified by the ancients, with its ethereal vehicle; affords very meager evidence to the senses. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Universal Reason so magnified and its ethereal vehicle” `II.1 ¶32`
- **Faculty of Wisdom** (also: Wisdom) — A higher faculty in germ in man which, revealed and set alone, knows nature intuitively and works magically as her substratal source. (QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience)
  - = This Being: “This Being, moreover, or Faculty of Wisdom” `II.1 ¶32`
  - “the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom” `II.1 ¶32`
- **Subject Identity** — What unassisted reason fails to grasp, as it 'slides evermore behind the retardant mind'. (ATWOOD)
  - “the Subject Identity slides evermore behind the retardant mind” `II.1 ¶32`

## Ambiguities

- “extracted from the true mineral, as it is taken from the mountains” `II.1 ¶25`
  - Reading: A literal mineral ore, as opposed to refined commercial antimony.
  - Reading: A figure for something in man, to be extracted inwardly.
  - Basis: Reading A: the contrast with what 'apothecaries and merchants sell'. Reading B: the immediately following Latin 'inter duos monticulos ... in te et in me ... latet' (our translation) and Atwood's conclusion in II.1 ¶28, ¶30 that the subject is within man.
- “digestion of it with fresh i, wet with gas water, or oil of this antimony” `II.1 ¶25`
  - Reading: 'i' is a garbled word for the fresh material added in each digestion; the text gives no way to recover it.
  - Basis: Text corruption at II.1 ¶25.
- “when the mechanical part of the Three Principles passes into the hands of its proper manufacturers equally and generally in all countries” `II.1 ¶25`
  - Reading: The practical part of the art becomes widely known and practised.
  - Reading: Something else the chunk does not explain.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶25 gives only the consequence (adepts emerge as physicians of body and soul); 'mechanical part' and 'manufacturers' are not glossed.
- “by a certain mysterious recreation and appeasing” `II.1 ¶26`
  - Reading: A procedure of calming the mind into a sleep-like state.
  - Reading: Unspecified means; only the effect is described.
  - Basis: II.1 ¶26 says the mind is 'converted and laid asleep from its present life'; the means are not described.

## Translations

- `II.1 ¶25` “Lapis noster inter duos monticulos nascitur; in te et in me at in nostri similibus latet” → Our stone is born between two little hills; it lies hidden in you and in me and in those like us. *(ours)*

- `II.1 ¶24` 'continues Boehme' and 'The same Three Principles' — continue the account of the process from the preceding chunk.
