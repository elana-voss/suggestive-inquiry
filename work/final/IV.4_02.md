# IV.4_02

*Atwood answers objections to the Hermetic Wisdom (unprovable, irrelevant, too difficult, against temporal interests, unattractive, impious) and in doing so states its aim: only a vital inward means, not external improvement, cures the inbred evil of the mind; the Art resuscitates through self-knowledge the buried Root of Reason, which is Wisdom and the 'saving Salt', restoring the faculties under the rule of Light in obedience to the Divine Will.*

## Argument

- To observers drawn to externals, life and intellect apart from their operations seem absurd without tangible proof. Atwood offers no demonstration: inquiry answers skepticism, and Universals subsisting in the mind give probability. An 'enchanted fortress' guards internal Wisdom from curiosity and sense; sensual evidence cannot disprove 'another Experience'. `IV.4 ¶14` — ATWOOD
- Ancient Wisdom transcends but does not contradict reason: their basis is our summit. Faith, common to all, infers a reality beyond experience, so instinct rejects reducing Cause to antecedence. Striking on this Faith, she would awaken the imaginative centers so Reason seeks 'reminiscence in her root of Light'. `IV.4 ¶15` — ATWOOD
- Verse, unattributed: the 'faculty divine' is chained from birth, yet 'The beams pour in'. `IV.4 ¶16` — UNCLEAR
- The ancients used means to free the Divine Light held in thralldom; Wisdom came by arduous discipline. Modern institutions lack purifying or awakening rites; circumstances modify but do not recreate. Only a vital power, with introspective proof, reaches inbred evil. What generation binds only dissolution unlooses: Light overcomes darkness in the 'hypostatical alliance'; all other melioration is preparative. `IV.4 ¶17` — ATWOOD
- External progress breeds selfishness and leads from the First Source; without individual reformation by Wisdom and the Exemplary Light every plan fails. `IV.4 ¶18` — ATWOOD
- She seeks to promote investigation, not convince. Incredulity wisely bars premature practice. Earnest desire is needed, reason must direct faith, and faith is the loadstone drawing hope, desire and reason. `IV.4 ¶19` — ATWOOD
- Reason's proper object is its Source, buried in sense-life, which Hermetic Artifice resuscitates through self-knowledge. This Root of Reason is Wisdom and the 'saving Salt' philosophers excavated and exalted by circulating media. With this Stone the regenerate soul puts on a crown of light as a new body, ruling the elementary world and overcoming death. This philosophy immortalizes Reason; no faculty is repudiated; a refractory one causes suffering to those restoring 'their King', and all are renewed with him. The pursuit also discloses the occult springs of mythology and fable. `IV.4 ¶20` — ATWOOD
- Veneration finds awe before Divinity within the temple's veil. The highest privilege is to know the First Truth experimentally and revolve the Causal Light; this science shows all things in their constructive Causes. `IV.4 ¶21, IV.4 ¶22` — ATWOOD
- Those ruled by pride, avarice, ambition or ignorance should not enlist. Against Job: searching by one's own will fails, but 'by conditionating' in obedience to the Divine Will one enters the integral alliance. `IV.4 ¶23` — ATWOOD
- Those who think it impious should desist, lest pusillanimity follow; as St Paul says, a thing is unclean to him who esteems it so. `IV.4 ¶24` — ATWOOD
- Alchemy is holy; salvation is by the Divine Will alone, but the Art is 'a remedy of imperfect souls' offering 'means of assimilation'; whether knowing them is necessary she leaves open, but Adepts say God reveals them to his elect. `IV.4 ¶25` — ATWOOD
- Wisdom's Art supersedes common arts and comprehends every liberal science. `IV.4 ¶26` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **principle** — “the natural subsistence of Universals in the human mind may afford a ground of probability whence to proceed into their proof” `IV.4 ¶14` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** That universals are naturally present in the mind is offered as the starting point that makes the inquiry probable.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶14.
- **impediment** — “there is a strongly enchanted fortress about it, whose forces yield not either to impertinent curiosity or the peremptory demands of sense” `IV.4 ¶14` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The doctrine of internal Wisdom is guarded and cannot be reached by mere curiosity or by demanding sensory proof.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶14: 'it' is 'the ancient doctrine of internal Wisdom'; the paragraph contrasts this with 'sensual certainty'.
- **procedure** — “we have hoped to awaken the imaginative centers to such an accord as might stir Reason from her long lethargy to seek for genuine reminiscence in her root of Light” `IV.4 ¶15` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Atwood's own aim in the book is preparatory: by appealing to Faith and to records of transcendental experience, to rouse the imagination so that dormant Reason seeks real recollection in its own source of Light.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶15: done by 'Striking upon this Faith' and 'taking to witness some of the closer records of transcendental experience'. 'root of Light' resembles 'Root of Reason' (IV.4 ¶20), but the text does not state they are the same.
- **impediment** — “the use of means for removing the barriers by which the Divine Light is here held in thralldom” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** In this life the Divine Light is confined by barriers, and the ancients used definite means to remove them.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17; the verse in IV.4 ¶16 gives the parallel image of the 'faculty divine' chained from birth and bred in darkness.
  - Also: “the faculty divine Is chained and tortured, cabin’d, cribb’d, confin’d.” `IV.4 ¶16`
- **operator** — “it was avowedly a thing acquired, the reward only of peculiar and arduous discipline” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Wisdom is acquired only by a special, demanding discipline, unlike school logic or mathematics; no modern institution offers anything analogous, such as rites that purify or awaken the mind.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17.
  - Also: “no rites of purifying or awakening efficacy to the mind” `IV.4 ¶17`
- **principle** — “no power that is not vital can touch the evil that is inbred, or even discover it truly without introspective proof” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** External measures (education, circumstance) modify character but cannot remake it; only a living power can reach inborn evil, and it can only be truly detected by inward testing.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17.
  - Also: “all our circumstantial labours do not rectify the mind, or turn the inbred inclination out of life” `IV.4 ¶17`
  - Also: “they do not recreate” `IV.4 ¶17`
- **principle** — “That which generation binds dissolution only can unloose” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The evil fixed in the mind by birth can only be undone by a corresponding dissolution; this chunk does not say what the dissolution consists in.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17: follows 'no power that is not vital can touch the evil that is inbred' and is followed by 'the evil must be met by its proper antidote'.
- **agent** — “overcoming darkness by Light in the hypostatical alliance” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The proper antidote to inbred evil is Light overcoming darkness within a substantial union; the chunk does not define what is united (see ambiguities).
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17 calls this the evil's 'proper antidote'; compare 'supernal alliance' (IV.4 ¶20) and 'integral alliance and power' (IV.4 ¶23).
- **stage** — “every accessory means of melioration will be preparative to this which restores the human mind to integrity and universal science” `IV.4 ¶17` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Education and other improvements only prepare the way; the central work, the restoration by Light, returns the mind to wholeness and universal knowledge.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶17; repeated in IV.4 ¶18.
  - Also: “We may alter, and improve, and educate, and prepare the way with advantage; but the notion will be variable, and every plan defective without the Exemplary Light.” `IV.4 ¶18`
- **impediment** — “every advance we make in externals leads us further off from the First Source” `IV.4 ¶18` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Progress in external things, and the luxury it brings, weakens moral character and draws the mind further from its origin.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶18.
  - Also: “the increase of luxury, to which thought is now applied, enervates the moral character” `IV.4 ¶18`
- **impediment** — “Incredulity is the strongest barrier of possibilities over the world, no doubt wisely provided to prevent a too rapid movement of mind into practice, before it is well prepared and disposed to the pursuit of truth” `IV.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Disbelief blocks the work, but Atwood says it is a safeguard against entering practice before one is prepared.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶19.
- **operator** — “faith is the very attracting loadstone which hope pursues, and desire and reason, and the whole willing armament of Mind” `IV.4 ¶19` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator needs earnest desire, faith and reason working together: faith draws the other powers, and reason must direct faith.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶19.
  - Also: “Without an earnest desire of discovery, the liveliest faith will be frustrated, and labour will be vain unless reason give direction to the persuasion of faith.” `IV.4 ¶19`
  - Also: “conviction steeled to the purpose of her Motive Light” `IV.4 ¶19`
- **subject** — “For this same Root of Reason is Wisdom, and that saving Salt which philosophers were wont anciently to excavate and by so many circulating media to exalt” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** What the work is done on is the hidden source of the rational faculty, buried beneath ordinary sensory life; the text identifies this Root of Reason with Wisdom and with the philosophers' 'saving Salt'.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20 asserts the identity directly, right after 'the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source'.
  - Also: “the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source, which lies profoundly buried in this life of sense” `IV.4 ¶20`
- **procedure** — “this it is the province of Hermetic Artifice to resuscitate and bring through self-knowledge into the experience of life” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Art's task is to revive this buried Source of reason and, by way of self-knowledge, make it actually experienced.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20; 'this' refers to the Source buried in the life of sense in the preceding sentence.
- **procedure** — “excavate and by so many circulating media to exalt” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Salt (Root of Reason) is first dug out from where it lies buried, then exalted by means of many circulating media; this chunk does not explain what the circulating media are.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20: 'excavate' matches 'profoundly buried in this life of sense' in the same paragraph; 'circulating media' is not glossed.
- **result** — “The diadem of Wisdom is with this Stone, which, as a halo or crown of light, the regenerate soul puts on as a new body” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone is a crown or body of light that the regenerated soul assumes; in it the soul rules over and passes through the elemental world, overcoming evil, falsehood, ignorance and death.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20; whether 'this Stone' is the Salt itself or its product is open (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “wherein it can rule over the elementary world and pass through it, overcoming evil and falsehood, and ignorance and death” `IV.4 ¶20`
- **result** — “To the faculty of Reason, therefore, nothing is more attractive than this philosophy, which immortalizes it” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The philosophy makes Reason immortal.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20.
- **stage** — “if any one is refractory during the passage, it is the occasion of suffering to the better natures, which are all engaged voluntarily in the restitution of their King” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** During the work all the soul's faculties take part, under their rightful rule, in restoring their ruling source ('King'); any faculty that resists causes suffering to the others. Who the King is is not stated (see ambiguities).
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20: answers whether other faculties are despised; 'all are present together in obedience to their rightful rule and habitude'; the King is 'him who is their source'.
  - Also: “all are present together in obedience to their rightful rule and habitude” `IV.4 ¶20`
- **result** — “the brethren all are renewed together with him, and every dignified sentiment is set in accord, to testify and maintain the triumphant Monarchy of Light” `IV.4 ¶20` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The faculties need not finally be sacrificed: they are renewed together with their ruler and brought into harmony under the rule of Light.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20: 'brethren' follows the question of faculties sacrificed 'for the sake of him who is their source', answered by 'Such is not the ultimate necessity of the case'.
- **result** — “in the presence of Divinity, true awe is felt, and man discovers and knows the perfect goodness, which profanely without the temple’s veil he cannot, or unless the understanding is absolutely conjoined” `IV.4 ¶21` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Perfect goodness is known only within the sanctuary, in the presence of Divinity, or when the understanding is wholly conjoined; not from outside. 'Temple's veil' and 'conjoined' are not explained in this chunk.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶21.
- **result** — “to know the First Truth of things really and experimentally, and to revolve the Causal Light in permanence of intellection” `IV.4 ¶22` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The goal is direct, experiential knowledge of first truth and continuous contemplation of the causal Light; this science shows all things in their constructive Causes. Atwood adds, as something 'we are told', that nature's powers are then at our disposal, 'if we can believe'.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶22.
  - Also: “the springs of Universal Nature, with her growth and fruits, are at our delegate disposal, if we can believe” `IV.4 ¶22`
  - Also: “which exhibits all things in their constructive Causes” `IV.4 ¶22`
  - Also: “Identically penetrating, enters into the Whole of Existence” `IV.4 ¶22`
- **impediment** — “pride or avarice, or ambition or ignorance, accustomed to rule in this life, should disdain the subaltern station which the Divine Law assigns to them” `IV.4 ¶23` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Lower motives must accept a subordinate place; those ruled by them should not undertake the work, or they will be swallowed in their own delusion.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶23.
  - Also: “swallowed up in the gulph of their own overwhelming delusion” `IV.4 ¶23`
  - Also: “the viperous progeny of self-love” `IV.4 ¶23`
- **operator** — “by conditionating, in obedience to the Divine Will, he comes into the integral alliance and power” `IV.4 ¶23` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Searching by one's own will alone fails; the operator succeeds by placing himself under the required conditions in obedience to the Divine Will.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶23, set against 'man, searching in his own will alone, is incompetent to the Divine Discovery'.
  - Also: “man, searching in his own will alone, is incompetent to the Divine Discovery” `IV.4 ¶23`
- **impediment** — “lest it should become manifested forth in some practical form of pusillanimity, or faithless attempt to interrogate the profundity it fears” `IV.4 ¶24` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Anyone who thinks the research impious should desist, since that belief would show itself as cowardice or a faithless probing of the depths they fear.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶24.
- **principle** — “the Art is said to be a remedy of imperfect souls, and offers the means of assimilation” `IV.4 ¶25` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Art heals imperfect souls and supplies means of 'assimilation'; salvation itself is worked by the Divine Will alone, and whether knowing these means is necessary is left undecided. This chunk does not say what the soul is assimilated to.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶25, with 'the Divine Will operates its fulfillment alone'.
  - Also: “Adepts declare that He has always revealed them to his elected children” `IV.4 ¶25`

## Sources cited

- **the ancients** `IV.4 ¶15, IV.4 ¶17` — Supports: they insisted on means for removing barriers to the Divine Light; their Wisdom was acquired by discipline and transcends but does not contradict reason.
- **metaphysicians, inductively arguing** `IV.4 ¶15` — Criticized: their attempt to reduce the Idea of Cause to mere antecedence is opposed by human instinct.
- **unattributed verse** — “The beams pour in, and truth and skill may couch the blind.” `IV.4 ¶16` — Illustrates: the divine faculty is confined from birth, yet light can enter.
- **philosophers** `IV.4 ¶20` — Supports: they anciently excavated the saving Salt and exalted it by circulating media.
- **Job** — “no one by searching can find out the Almighty to perfection” `IV.4 ¶23` — Objection answered: true of man searching by his own will, but not of one conditionating in obedience to the Divine Will.
- **St Paul** — “to him that esteems a thing unclean, as St Paul says, it is unclean” `IV.4 ¶24` — Supports: the impurity lies in the thought of the accuser, not in the Art.
- **Hone soit qui mal y pense (unattributed)** — “Hone soit qui mal y pense.” `IV.4 ¶24` — Supports the same point against the charge of impiety.
- **Adepts** — “He has always revealed them to his elected children” `IV.4 ¶25` — Supports: God has always revealed the means of assimilation to his elect.

## Ambiguities

- “overcoming darkness by Light in the hypostatical alliance” `IV.4 ¶17`
  - Reading: A union of the Divine Light with the human mind.
  - Reading: The same union of all faculties under rightful rule called 'supernal alliance' (¶20) and 'integral alliance' (¶23).
  - Basis: ¶17 sets Light against inbred darkness and speaks of the Divine Light held in thralldom; ¶20 'supernal alliance' gathers all faculties; ¶23 'integral alliance and power' is entered by obedience to the Divine Will. The chunk does not say whether these alliances are one.
- “The diadem of Wisdom is with this Stone” `IV.4 ¶20`
  - Reading: 'this Stone' is the saving Salt / Root of Reason just named, so the three are identical.
  - Reading: The Stone is a further product of exalting the Salt.
  - Basis: The demonstrative 'this' points back to the sentence about the Salt, but no explicit identity is stated; 'exalt' could imply transformation into something further.
- “the restitution of their King” `IV.4 ¶20`
  - Reading: The King is Reason, the faculty this philosophy immortalizes.
  - Reading: The King is the Source or Root of Reason (Wisdom), 'him who is their source'.
  - Basis: IV.4 ¶20: 'To the faculty of Reason, therefore, nothing is more attractive than this philosophy, which immortalizes it'; earlier 'the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source'; later 'him who is their source'.
- “or unless the understanding is absolutely conjoined” `IV.4 ¶21`
  - Reading: Conjoined with Divinity.
  - Reading: Conjoined with its own source or with the other faculties.
  - Basis: The text does not say with what; ¶21 mentions 'the presence of Divinity', and ¶20 speaks of the faculties allied.
- “The Art of Alchemy is of all Arts distinguished as holt” `IV.4 ¶25`
  - Reading: The Art of Alchemy is the same as the 'Art of Wisdom, long distinguished as holy' of ¶24 ('holt' apparently for 'holy').
  - Reading: Two separate descriptions; no identity asserted.
  - Basis: Adjacent ¶24 and ¶25 both call an Art holy; the text does not explicitly identify them.

## Threads

- `IV.4 ¶14` 'the authorities already cited': earlier citations in the book offered as testimony for the doctrine.
- `IV.4 ¶14` 'For this we have laboured to supply means': the book's preceding work, offered as means for inquiry.
- `IV.4 ¶15` 'taking to witness some of the closer records of transcendental experience': evidence presented earlier in the book.
- `IV.4 ¶19` 'having nowhere undertaken to convince' and 'we have nowhere denied them' [the difficulties]: claims about the stance taken throughout the book.
