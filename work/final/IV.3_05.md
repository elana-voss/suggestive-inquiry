# IV.3_05

*Atwood presents mechanical Mesmerism as only the threshold of the temple of Divine Wisdom the ancients entered by a more scientific Handicraft; through a theosophist's account of the Fall she states the whole work as self-knowledge and the reduction of two natures (wrath fire and light fire, earthly and heavenly) to one, done by the Mercury within the matter while the qualified Artist only prepares the way; a chain of Scripture then promises Wisdom with material gifts as tokens, and she ends on the adepts' contempt for mere metallic gain.*

## Argument

- Scriptural faith bestowing Wisdom is unknown today; man no longer seeks the Truth in himself. Mechanical Mesmerism is only a first step before the temple of Divine Wisdom, which the ancients entered experimentally by a more scientific Handicraft. The materials remain; only will and faith are lacking to resuscitate the rejected Corner Stone. `IV.3 ¶37` — ATWOOD
- Without understanding himself and the evil enchanting him, man cannot grasp fall or regeneration, nor deliver matter from the curse. `IV.3 ¶38` — ATWOOD
- Unfallen Adam was led by the Eternal Word, his life a clear flame fed by divine substantiality. The Stone or tincture lies in God's sentence of enmity between Serpent and woman's seed, concerning man, then all creation; the head is bruised in soul and nature by a parallel process. `IV.3 ¶38, IV.3 n16` — QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed)
- Sting is wrath fire, seed light and love fire; both are in everything. Wrath ruled outward nature after the fall; light must shine through and subdue it into a servant, the two reharmonized. The tinctured Mercury turns death to life, making a heavenly body from an earthly. `IV.3 ¶38` — QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed)
- The whole work: Two Natures reduced to One; Earth turned in, Heaven out. The Mercury within does it; the Artist only prepares way and matter, needing understanding and faith, since one inwardly dead cannot raise matter from the dead. `IV.3 ¶38` — QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed; extent of quotation unclear)
- Only one already prepared by the Spirit, prudent, holy, true, holding the Key of David, is competent to minister. `IV.3 ¶39, IV.3 n17` — ATWOOD
- Solomon: God's counsel, hidden by the body's weight on the soul, is known only if Wisdom and the Spirit are given; Wisdom preserved the righteous through fall, Flood, fire on five cities, and the RED SEA. `IV.3 ¶39, IV.3 n18` — QUOTED: Solomon
- Once the Divine Light is loosened in life, all prospers while her Rule is kept; the Word comes line upon line, purifying and perfecting; every look and action is by rule and with power. Texts on hands, horns and Wisdom's crowns follow. `IV.3 ¶39, IV.3 n19, IV.3 n20, IV.3 n21, IV.3 n22` — ATWOOD
- Scripture also promises material fruits with Wisdom to him that overcometh: Tree of Life, Morning Star, White Raiment, New Name, throne. `IV.3 ¶40, IV.3 ¶41, IV.3 ¶42, IV.3 ¶43, IV.3 ¶44, IV.3 ¶45` — ATWOOD
- Scripture is obscure and mystical throughout, yet significant in material promises; when we begin to love the Spirit he sends these as tokens of love, and in conscious alliance potentializes the whole in each individual. `IV.3 ¶46` — QUOTED: Agrippa; the disciple (endorsed)
- This was the ground of ancient doctrine and of creeds now held in shadowy tradition; Solomon praises Wisdom, who is Understanding, above gold and rubies. `IV.3 ¶47, IV.3 n24` — ATWOOD
- Those who perfect the work gain Divine Wisdom, to be applied in obedience to the Divine Will for mankind's benefit, with wealth, power, health and promise of immortality; so they cared little for long life or riches beside what their art foreshowed; none should rest in metalline good like Midas. `IV.3 ¶48` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **principle** — “Mesmerism, as it is mechanically practiced in the present day, is a first step indeed, and this only before the entrance of that glorious temple of Divine Wisdom” `IV.3 ¶37` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Modern Mesmerism, done mechanically, is a first step that reaches only the entrance of the temple of Divine Wisdom; the ancients, by a more scientific practical art, entered it experimentally.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶37 states it directly.
  - Also: “a more scientific Handicraft enabled the ancients experimentally to enter” `IV.3 ¶37`
- **operator** — “the will only is wanting to discover the way, and faith to resuscitate the Corner Stone, which ignorance has rejected from immemorial time” `IV.3 ¶37` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Materials and foundations are available now; what the operator lacks is will and faith. The Corner Stone is not explained in this chunk.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶37: 'The materials are yet present with us; the foundations easily laid'.
- **operator** — “if man does not enter in to understand himself, and the evil under which he lies enchanted in this life” `IV.3 ¶38` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Self-knowledge, including knowledge of one's own fallen, 'enchanted' state, is the precondition for understanding fall and regeneration and for redeeming matter.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38 makes this the condition; ¶37 laments the lack of 'true knowledge of ourselves'.
  - Also: “nor does man any longer seek after the Truth that is in himself” `IV.3 ¶37`
- **principle** — “Herein the Philosophers’ Stone or tincture lyeth.” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Stone lies in the enmity between the Serpent and the woman's seed, i.e. in the conflict of wrath fire and light fire explained in the following sentences.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'Herein' follows God's sentence on the Serpent; the next sentences gloss sting and seed as the two fires.
- **principle** — “The bruising of the serpent’s head is done spiritually and corporally, both in nature and in the soul, and though in different degrees, yet by a parallel process in each.” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** One process of redemption runs in parallel in the soul and in physical nature; the work on matter corresponds to the regeneration of man.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38 directly; n16 titles the cited work 'Of the Analogy in the Process of the Phil. Work with the Redemption of Man'.
  - Also: “though this concerneth man, in the first place, yet secondly it concerneth the whole creation” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **agent** — “The serpent’s sting is the wrath fire, and the woman’s seed the light and love fire: these two are in every thing” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** Two contrary principles, a wrathful fire and a fire of light and love, are present in every thing; since the fall the wrath predominates in outward nature.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38 gives the identities and adds 'the former predominated in outward nature, by the fall'.
- **procedure** — “the latter must be raised up, and by its shining through the wrath, must subdue and keep it under” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The light principle must be raised until it governs the wrath principle, which is not destroyed but made to serve the light, so the two become one harmonized thing.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'taking away from it its predominant power, so that it may exercise its true natural office as a servant to the light'.
  - Also: “be one thing reharmonized by light and love, and re-introduced into Paradise” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **stage** — “when the dark poisoning Mercury is thus tinctured, his anguishing death is turned into triumphing life” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Mercury, dark and poisonous while wrath predominates, is transformed by the light: death becomes life, dark desire becomes desire of light.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38 directly.
  - Also: “his former dark desire into the desire of light” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **result** — “a heavenly body out of an earthly” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The outcome is pure love and light made substantial: an earthly body made heavenly.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'able to make a pure love and light substantially, viz., a heavenly body out of an earthly'.
- **principle** — “That Two Natures be reduced to One, as they were at the beginning.” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The whole work, summarily, is uniting a heavenly and an earthly matter into one of heavenly consistency, restoring the original unity.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'The whole work therefore consists summarily herein'; 'A heavenly and an earthly matter are to be mutually united'.
  - Also: “A heavenly and an earthly matter are to be mutually united and brought to a heavenly consistency.” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **procedure** — “Earth must be turned in, and Heaven out.” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The earthly side is to become inward and the heavenly side outward, reversing the post-fall condition in which wrath rules the outside.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: the wrath fire 'predominated in outward nature, by the fall' and the light 'must be raised up'; the phrase stands in the summary of uniting heavenly and earthly matter.
- **agent** — “The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The transformation is done by the Mercury already in the matter, which the text calls the workman within it.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'leave the work to be done by the workman which is in it already'.
  - Also: “leave the work to be done by the workman which is in it already” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **operator** — “the Artist is not to attempt it --- he cannot do it --- he is to prepare the way, the matter as is requisite” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator does not perform the transformation; his role is to prepare the conditions and the matter.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38 directly.
- **operator** — “which never can be done by one who is dead himself in his understanding and internal life” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Artist must have understanding and faith and be inwardly alive; one inwardly dead cannot raise matter from the dead.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'nevertheless, understanding and faith are required of him'.
  - Also: “understanding and faith are required of him” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **result** — “to redeem matter from the curse, and to raise it from the dead” `IV.3 ¶38` (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - **Plain (stated):** The aim of the work is to free matter from the curse of the fall and restore it to life.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶38: 'His design being no less than'.
  - Also: “deliver matter from the original curse, or be instrumental in the restoration” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **operator** — “he that the Spirit has already prepared, and endowed with prudence for the undertaking” `IV.3 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Only someone already prepared by the Spirit, prudent, holy and true is competent to minister in the work. 'Key of David' is not explained in this chunk.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶39 answers 'Who then is competent'.
  - Also: “he that hath the Key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth” `IV.3 ¶39`
- **impediment** — “For the corruptible body presseth down the soul, and the earthly tabernacle weigheth down that mind that museth upon many things.” `IV.3 ¶39` (QUOTED: Solomon)
  - **Plain (stated):** The body and a scattered mind hinder knowledge of heavenly things, which comes only as Wisdom given from above.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶39: 'And thy counsel who hath known, except thou give Wisdom and send thy Holy Spirit from above?'
  - Also: “And thy counsel who hath known, except thou give Wisdom and send thy Holy Spirit from above?” `IV.3 ¶39`
- **stage** — “When once the Divine Light is loosened in life, everything is prospered as long as her Rule is observed” `IV.3 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Once the Divine Light is released in the living subject, success follows provided its rule is kept, because the understanding can then put its motive energy into effect.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶39: 'for the understanding then bears its motive energy into effect'.
- **procedure** — “Line upon line, Precept upon precept, (purifying and perfecting) here a little and there a little” `IV.3 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Purification and perfection proceed gradually, by small successive steps.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶39: Atwood's own parenthesis '(purifying and perfecting)' glosses the cited verse, applied to the progress after the Divine Light is loosened.
- **sign** — “The whole individual is occupied; every look and action is by rule and with power” `IV.3 ¶39` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** In this state the operator's whole person is engaged, acting by rule and with power; the cited texts on the hands of the wise and horns from the hand illustrate this but are not further explained.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶39: the sentence is followed by 'as it is not likewise written, The Hands of the Wise are very heavy' and Habbakuk's horns from his hand and 'the hiding of his power'.
  - Also: “he had horns coming out of his hand, and there was the hiding of his power” `IV.3 ¶39`
  - Also: “The Hands of the Wise are very heavy” `IV.3 ¶39`
- **result** — “Nor are these the only passages in Scripture where with Wisdom a promise is given of more material fruits” `IV.3 ¶40` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Scriptural promises to 'him that overcometh' are read as including material as well as spiritual fruits of Wisdom. The individual images are not decoded in this chunk.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶40 introduces ¶41-45 so; ¶46 'significant in the promises even of material blessings and gifts'.
  - Also: “To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the Tree of Life” `IV.3 ¶41`
  - Also: “And I will give him the Morning Star.” `IV.3 ¶42`
  - Also: “He that overcometh the same shall be clothed in White Raiment.” `IV.3 ¶43`
- **principle** — “Clausum est Armarium --- the Scripture is obscure and mystical throughout” `IV.3 ¶46` (QUOTED: Agrippa)
  - **Plain (stated):** Scripture is to be read as veiled; even simple details carry profound meaning, including its promises of material gifts.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶46 glosses the phrase after the dash.
- **sign** — “then he sends us these things as tokens and pledges of his love” `IV.3 ¶46` (QUOTED: the disciple)
  - **Plain (stated):** Material gifts come as signs of the Spirit's love, sent when one begins to love the Spirit.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶46: 'For when we begin to love the Spirit'.
- **principle** — “in the conscious alliance becoming known, potentializes the whole in each individual universally without reserve” `IV.3 ¶46` (QUOTED: the disciple)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** When the alliance with the Spirit becomes conscious, the Spirit's productive virtue empowers the whole in the individual.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶46: the subject is the Spirit 'whose prolific virtue is the source of all things'; the alliance's other party is not named; ¶47 calls this 'the ground of ancient doctrine'.
- **result** — “this inestimable gift of Divine Wisdom” `IV.3 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Perfecting the work yields Divine Wisdom, freedom from human ills, wealth, power and health at command, a token of divine favor and a promise of immortality.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶48 lists these.
  - Also: “having the means of abundant wealth, power, and health at their command” `IV.3 ¶48`
  - Also: “sure promise of immortality in a life to come” `IV.3 ¶48`
- **result** — “human perfectibility, and a prospect of a happy immortality set before them in plain physical type and experience” `IV.3 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The physical results of the art are an experienced type foreshowing human perfection and immortality, valued above temporal gains; hence adepts with the medicine did not care to lengthen their days or display riches.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶48: 'that which, in the acquirement of these things, was by their art foreshown'.
  - Also: “philosophers, when they have obtained this medicine, have not cared to lengthen their days” `IV.3 ¶48`
- **impediment** — “rest in a mere metalline good, like Midas” `IV.3 ¶48` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Aiming only at metallic wealth is a degraded goal, preferring an earthly harmony to the higher one.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶48: 'preferring an earth-born harmony to the music of the spheres, was dishonored by the god'.

## Terms

- **Mesmerism** — As mechanically practiced today, a first step only before the entrance of the temple of Divine Wisdom. (ATWOOD)
  - “Mesmerism, as it is mechanically practiced in the present day, is a first step indeed” `IV.3 ¶37`
- **Handicraft** — The ancients' more scientific practical art by which they experimentally entered the temple of Divine Wisdom. (ATWOOD)
  - “a more scientific Handicraft enabled the ancients experimentally to enter” `IV.3 ¶37`
- **Corner Stone** — Rejected by ignorance from immemorial time, to be resuscitated by faith; not further explained here. (ATWOOD)
  - “faith to resuscitate the Corner Stone, which ignorance has rejected from immemorial time” `IV.3 ¶37`
- **regeneration** — Paired with the fall as ancient doctrine, understood only through self-knowledge. (ATWOOD)
  - “the ancient doctrine concerning the fall or regeneration” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **curse** (also: original curse) — The condition matter lies under since the fall; the work aims to redeem matter from it. (ATWOOD)
  - “deliver matter from the original curse” `IV.3 ¶38`
  - “to redeem matter from the curse, and to raise it from the dead” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Eternal Word** — Adam's leader, with dominion in him while in Paradisiacal innocency. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - “The Eternal Word was his leader, and had dominion in him” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **divine substantiality** — Pure spirit by which unfallen Adam's flame-like life was nourished. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - “nourished by that pure spirit of the divine substantiality” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Philosophers’ Stone** — Said to lie in God's sentence of enmity between the Serpent and the woman's seed; concerns man first, then all creation. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - = tincture: “Herein the Philosophers’ Stone or tincture lyeth.” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **wrath fire** — One of two principles in every thing; predominant in outward nature since the fall; to be subdued as servant to the light. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - = the serpent's sting: “The serpent’s sting is the wrath fire” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **light and love fire** — The other principle in every thing; must be raised up to shine through and subdue the wrath. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - = the woman's seed: “the woman’s seed the light and love fire” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Mercury** — The principle within the matter, 'dark poisoning' before tincturing, which itself performs the work of uniting the two natures. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - = the workman which is in it already: “The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself: the Artist is not to attempt it --- he cannot do it --- he is to prepare the way, the matter as is requisite, and leave the work to be done by the workman which is in it already” `IV.3 ¶38`
  - “the dark poisoning Mercury is thus tinctured” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **tinctured** (also: tincture) — Of the Mercury: transformed by light and love so that death becomes triumphing life. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - “when the dark poisoning Mercury is thus tinctured” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Two Natures** — A heavenly and an earthly matter, to be reduced to one as at the beginning. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - “That Two Natures be reduced to One, as they were at the beginning.” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Artist** — The human operator, who cannot do the work but prepares the way and the matter, and must have understanding and faith. (QUOTED: the theosophist)
  - “the Artist is not to attempt it --- he cannot do it” `IV.3 ¶38`
- **Key of David** — Held by the one competent to minister, who opens and no man shuts; not explained further. (ATWOOD)
  - “he that hath the Key of David” `IV.3 ¶39`
- **Divine Light** — Once loosened in life, makes everything prosper while 'her Rule' is observed (whether 'her' is the Light or Wisdom is unclear). (ATWOOD)
  - “When once the Divine Light is loosened in life” `IV.3 ¶39`
- **Wisdom** (also: Divine Wisdom) — Given by God with the Holy Spirit; personified as preserving and guiding the righteous and bringing riches, honor and long life; the gift obtained by perfecting the work. (ATWOOD)
  - = Understanding: “I, Wisdom, dwell with prudence, and find out knowledge of witty inventions. Counsel is mine and sound Wisdom; I am Understanding; I have strength.” `IV.3 ¶47`
  - “except thou give Wisdom and send thy Holy Spirit from above” `IV.3 ¶39`
  - “this inestimable gift of Divine Wisdom” `IV.3 ¶48`
- **medicine** — What philosophers obtain by the work; having it they did not care to lengthen their days or parade riches. (ATWOOD)
  - “when they have obtained this medicine” `IV.3 ¶48`

## Ambiguities

- “explains the theosophist” `IV.3 ¶38`
  - Reading: The theosophist's words run to the end of ¶38, including 'The whole work therefore consists summarily herein' and the Artist passage.
  - Reading: The quotation ends earlier and the summary of the whole work and/or the Artist's role (e.g. from 'His design being') are Atwood's own.
  - Basis: No closing quotation marks; 'therefore' could continue the cited argument or be Atwood's conclusion; marker (16), citing Freher, stands at the end of ¶38.
- “Earth must be turned in, and Heaven out.” `IV.3 ¶38`
  - Reading: Earthly nature made inward/subordinate and heavenly made outward/dominant.
  - Reading: A physical inversion of the matter's states, not specified here.
  - Basis: ¶38 speaks of wrath fire predominating in outward nature and light being raised; no direct gloss is given.
- “everything is prospered as long as her Rule is observed” `IV.3 ¶39`
  - Reading: 'her' refers to the Divine Light.
  - Reading: 'her' refers to Wisdom, personified as 'she' throughout the preceding Solomon passage.
  - Basis: ¶39: 'the Divine Light' is the nearest noun, but the immediately preceding passage repeatedly calls Wisdom 'she'.
- “The Hands of the Wise are very heavy” `IV.3 ¶39`
  - Reading: The operator's hands literally carry power in practice (cf. the Mesmerism of ¶37).
  - Reading: A figure for the effectiveness of the wise person's action generally.
  - Basis: ¶39 'every look and action is by rule and with power' and Habbakuk's hand image; ¶37 frames the discussion through Mesmerism; the text does not specify.
- “And His brightness was as the light, says Habbakuk” `IV.3 ¶39`
  - Reading: Footnotes refer to the markers as printed (n20 'Revel. 2:10' for the Habbakuk verse, etc.).
  - Reading: Footnotes are shifted by one: n20 belongs to (21) 'Be thou faithful into death', n21 'Proverbs 8' to (22), n22 'Revel. 2, 3' to (23) after ¶45.
  - Basis: Text names Habbakuk at marker (20) while n20 cites Revelation; marker (23) in ¶45 has no n23 in this chunk; the ¶41-45 promises fit 'Revel. 2, 3'.
- “adds the disciple” `IV.3 ¶46`
  - Reading: A disciple of Agrippa.
  - Reading: An unnamed disciple of the art or of the Spirit generally.
  - Basis: Follows Agrippa's words with 'adds'; no name given.
