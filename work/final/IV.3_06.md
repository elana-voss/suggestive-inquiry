# IV.3_06

*The closing peroration of the chapter: Atwood points past prophecies of a coming intellectual age to an inward search led by Conscience and the Idea of the Good, repeats her warnings (study first; success depends on the true Intention; the work is reserved to the few and falls through pride or private ends), and ends by declaring the Hermetic method and its end to be self-abandonment into union, the line returning into a circle, which she calls the true Christian Philosopher's Stone.*

## Argument

- Prophecies foretell a Fourth Monarchy, the Intellectual reign of Truth and Peace; but revelation is always at hand for one who knows how to investigate. If a cooperative age approaches, this enquiry may help fix the intellect's dominion in self-discovery of truth. `IV.3 ¶49` — ATWOOD
- The age worships temporal Utility yet feels its insufficiency. Look within, patiently, until Conscience reveals higher objects, which purify as they draw and finally transmute their worshipper into the Harmony and Beauty he dimly loved. `IV.3 ¶50` — ATWOOD
- Verse: keep the work ever complete in mind as aim and model until it is done. `IV.3 ¶51` — UNCLEAR
- The Idea of the Good, the Pure and the True, progeny of Divine Intellect, Moral beauty, is what all inwardly worship: our Polar Star. `IV.3 ¶52` — ATWOOD
- Verse: the reign of Mind will begin and man walk transparent as from a second birth. `IV.3 ¶52, IV.3 ¶53` — UNCLEAR
- Atwood repeats: no hasty trials without study of the Hermetic records and Laws of Art. Some things escaped her; others she reserved. The ignorant, avaricious, selfish, faithless or frivolous fail; only the right-minded and studious, persisting, succeed, each alone. Success depends on discovering the true Intention, latent in every mind but clear in few. `IV.3 ¶54` — ATWOOD
- Latin verse (n25: Palingenius): after long trial and cost they found the art of fashioning the Ethereal Stone, granted to few. `IV.3 ¶54, IV.3 ¶55, IV.3 n25` — QUOTED: Palingenius
- Even the favoured may let the work fall through too little desire or pride, so that Satan rules it. Scoffers without the Light of Reason cannot harm it; practitioners are warned: 'Enlarge not thy destiny', 'Neither break a superficies', lest the agent's sin multiply in the patient. Wisdom confounds whoever pursues private ends. `IV.3 ¶56` — ATWOOD
- Example: the Israelites suffered for rebellion; Deuteronomy warns the prosperous not to forget God or credit their own hand. `IV.3 ¶57, IV.3 n26` — ATWOOD
- Immortality is consummated only in union, abandoning the dual selfhood and its volition to the Divine Will; Jacob's self-abhorrence illustrates. The quotation ends: 'thine own right hand can save thee' (n27: Job 40:14). `IV.3 ¶58, IV.3 ¶59, IV.3 n27` — ATWOOD
- The wise passed through discipline, ordeal and humiliation; once freed from sense and passion, the final step is comparatively easy: by faith the Identity passes from separable selfhood into universal accord. Kabalah: go forth and return; close thy eyes and meditate. `IV.3 ¶60, IV.3 ¶61, IV.3 n28` — ATWOOD
- This is the Hermetic method and end: the line returns into a circle, a union that is Eternity; the true Christian Philosopher's Stone, epitome of the universe, confirmed in operation, visibility and luminous increase as the Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause. `IV.3 ¶62` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **procedure** — “with steady perseverance looking within, until Conscience reveal to us those higher objects of pursuit, and truer attractions” `IV.3 ¶50` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The search is turned inward, patiently and steadily, not outward; Conscience is what discloses the true objects to pursue.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶50 contrasts seeking 'vaguely around us for passing excitements' with 'looking within'.
  - Also: “the revelation of all things is always at hand for him who knows how to investigate” `IV.3 ¶49`
  - Also: “fix its dominion in the self-discovery of truth” `IV.3 ¶49`
- **result** — “purifying and corroborating as they draw, will, when at length they are worthily won, untie with and transmute their worshipper into that Harmony and Beauty, which, in the dim beholding, he venerated and loved” `IV.3 ¶50` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The higher objects purify and strengthen the seeker as they attract him and, once attained, transform him into the Harmony and Beauty he had dimly perceived and loved: transmutation applied to the person.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶50: the subject of 'transmute' is the 'higher objects of pursuit', its object 'their worshipper'. 'untie with' is copied as printed; the text does not correct it.
- **procedure** — “Be sure at least that ever in our mind It stand complete before us, as a dome Of light beyond this gloom” `IV.3 ¶51` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Keep the finished work constantly before the mind as aim and model, persevering until it is done.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶51: 'Be this our aim and model, and our Hands Shall not wax faint until the work is done.' The verse is unattributed and not explicitly tied to the Hermetic practice.
  - Also: “Near as our Hearts and perfect as the Heavens.” `IV.3 ¶51`
  - Also: “Be this our aim and model, and our Hands Shall not wax faint until the work is done.” `IV.3 ¶51`
- **principle** — “The Idea of the Good, the Pure, and the True is the alluring object which we all innerly worship” `IV.3 ¶52` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The inner object that attracts and guides the seeker is the Idea of the Good, Pure and True, which the text calls Moral beauty, offspring of Divine Intellect and 'our Polar Star', present though obscured by sense and selfishness.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶52 makes the identifications directly: 'the progeny of Divine Intellect', 'even Moral beauty', 'through the mists of sense and selfishness', 'our Polar Star'.
  - Also: “ever shines attractively --- our Polar Star” `IV.3 ¶52`
- **procedure** — “beware of hasty interpretation and trials of skill, without a proper foundation laid by study of the Hermetic records and scientific Laws of Art” `IV.3 ¶54` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Before any practical attempt, lay a foundation by studying the Hermetic writings and the laws of the Art; do not interpret hastily or experiment prematurely.
  - Basis: Direct warning, IV.3 ¶54.
- **impediment** — “In vain the ignorant, in vain the avaricious, the selfish, faithless, or frivolous, will in vain seek to percolate this Mystery.” `IV.3 ¶54` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Ignorance, greed, selfishness, faithlessness and frivolity make success impossible.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶54; the Latin verse of IV.3 ¶55 says the same of the profane (our translation).
  - Also: “quem scire profanes Haud quaquam licet, et frustra plebs improba quaerit” `IV.3 ¶55`
- **operator** — “The right-minded and studious alone benevolently, conscientiously, and rationally persisting to the end, will unravel it: and in this case each must labor singularly for himself.” `IV.3 ¶54` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator must be right-minded and studious, persist to the end benevolently, conscientiously and rationally, and do the work individually. The talent is given to few and needs fitting conditions to develop.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶54.
  - Also: “The talent is granted only to a few, and for them conditions are needful to draw it forth to increase” `IV.3 ¶54`
  - Also: “Sed tanto paucos dignantur munere divi” `IV.3 ¶55`
- **principle** — “The success of the experiment, as we have repeatedly shown, depends on the discovery of the true Intention.” `IV.3 ¶54` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Success of the work depends on finding the true Intention, which is present to some degree in every mind but not clearly distinguishable in all; this is why few succeed. This chunk does not say further what the Intention is.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶54: 'this being more or less involved in every mind, is not clearly distinguishable in all; nor are they many, therefore, but few only'.
  - Also: “nor are they many, therefore, but few only, who have been found to enter in by the narrow way of life” `IV.3 ¶54`
- **sign** — “to discern the rational possibility by the infallible touchstone of her Original Light” `IV.3 ¶54` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The capable few judge what is rationally possible by Nature's Original Light, used as an unfailing test. This chunk does not explain the Original Light.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶54: 'her' refers back to 'Nature' in 'heterogeneous elements of Nature'; 'touchstone' marks it as a criterion of discernment.
- **subject** — “Fingendi Lepidem Aetherium” `IV.3 ¶55` (QUOTED: Palingenius (endorsed))
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The worthiest art is that of fashioning an 'Ethereal Stone' (our translation). The verse does not explain it.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶55, on our translation only. Compare 'Philosopher's Stone' and 'Ethereal Hypostasis' in IV.3 ¶62; the text does not identify them with it.
- **impediment** — “lest desiring too little or presuming too far in self-sufficient pride, it should fall, and Satan establish a monarchy upon the most holy of works” `IV.3 ¶56` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Even a favoured operator can ruin the work, and turn it to evil, by too little desire or by proud self-sufficiency.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶56, reporting frequent injunctions on the sacred nature of the ministration, which Atwood endorses ('we advise him of our knowledge').
- **impediment** — “lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring unto the third or fourth generation” `IV.3 ¶56` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The operator's fault does not stay with him: it is multiplied in what he works on and carried into what that produces.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶56: given as the reason for 'Enlarge not thy destiny' and 'Neither break a superficies', addressed to one whose desire 'should lead him to practice'. Reading 'agent' as operator, 'patient' as the thing worked on and 'offspring' as product is inference; see ambiguities.
  - Also: “Ne tu augeas fatum --- Enlarge not thy destiny” `IV.3 ¶56`
  - Also: “Neither break a superficies, as the Pythagoric precept runs” `IV.3 ¶56`
- **principle** — “The Spirit of Wisdom runs lovingly by her own Rule to fulfill it; but leaves him to hopeless confusion who abandons the Divine purpose for the accomplishment of private ends.” `IV.3 ¶56` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The work proceeds by its own law when it serves the Divine purpose; pursuing it for private ends leads to confusion and failure.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶56.
- **impediment** — “lest thou say in thine heart my power the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth” `IV.3 ¶57` (QUOTED: the Lawgiver, in his book of Deuteronomy (endorsed))
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Success brings the danger of pride and of crediting oneself instead of God; gratitude and humility are required.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶57 brings in Deuteronomy, after the warnings of ¶56, so 'that no presumption might thereafter arise'; the application to the work is by analogy ('So the Israelites').
  - Also: “then thy heart be lifted, and thou forget the Lord thy God” `IV.3 ¶57`
  - Also: “that He might humble thee, and that He might prove thee, to do thee good at thy latter end” `IV.3 ¶57`
- **principle** — “For immortality is only to be consummated in union, and in the abandonment of the dual selfhood and its volition to the Divine Will.” `IV.3 ¶58` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The final aim, immortality, is reached only by union, which means giving up the divided self and its own will to the Divine Will.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶58, illustrated by Jacob's self-renunciation and the humbling of the proud.
  - Also: “Wherefore I abhor myself, cries Jacob (when all else being deprived, he renounced himself), and repent in dust and ashes.” `IV.3 ¶58`
  - Also: “Look on every one that is proud and abase him” `IV.3 ¶58`
- **procedure** — “passing through every ordained discipline, ordeal rite, and humiliation, in order that they might become conformable to the Divine Perfection they desired to approach” `IV.3 ¶60` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The preparatory path is a series of prescribed disciplines, ordeals and humiliations meant to make the person conform to the Divine Perfection.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶60.
- **stage** — “Once delivered from the exterior bondages of sense and heterogeneous desire, from the passions and false affections of this transitory life, the final step is declared comparatively easy” `IV.3 ¶60` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Two stages: first liberation from the senses, mixed desires and passions; then a final step that is comparatively easy.
  - Basis: Direct statement, IV.3 ¶60.
- **stage** — “as transcending by the energy of faith, from the separable selfhood, the Identity passes into universal accord” `IV.3 ¶60` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The final step: through faith, the individual identity leaves its separate selfhood and enters into accord with the universal.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶60 gives this as the content of 'the final step'.
- **procedure** — “Close thy eyes and meditate: and if thy heart fail thee, return again” `IV.3 ¶60` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (stated):** Meditate with eyes closed; if the heart fails, return again. The text joins this to 'going forth and returning' but does not explain it further.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶60, directly after 'To go forth and to return ... so says the Kabalah', and justified by the Latin of IV.3 ¶61 (n28: Sephir Jezirah, cap. 1). Whether the instruction is the Kabalah's words or Atwood's is unclear; see ambiguities.
  - Also: “To go forth and to return; therefore was the agreement cut off” `IV.3 ¶60`
  - Also: “Egredere et regredere, et propterea pactum praecisum est” `IV.3 ¶61`
- **result** — “The line returns to form a circle into its’ beginning, and they join not in time, for their union is Eternity.” `IV.3 ¶62` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The method's end is a return to the origin: end and beginning join in a union that is eternal rather than temporal.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶62 calls this 'the Hermetic method and its end' and closes with 'the Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause'; IV.3 ¶58 and ¶60 describe the end as union and passage into universal accord. Reading the line as the self returning to its source rests on these.
  - Also: “This is the work --- this is the Hermetic method and its end” `IV.3 ¶62`
- **result** — “This, reader, is the true Christian Philosopher’s Stone” `IV.3 ¶62` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Philosopher's Stone is this completed return and union itself, which Atwood calls the most exact epitome of the universe.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶62: 'This' refers back to the work, method and end just described (the line returning into a circle).
  - Also: “if it be a chimera, than is the universe itself not stable, of which it has been proven to be the most exact epitome” `IV.3 ¶62`
- **sign** — “is confirmed in operation, visibility, and luminous increase, when rising in rational supremacy over sense and finite reflection, the Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause” `IV.3 ¶62` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Stone is verified in practice by its working, by becoming visible and by increasing light, at the point where reason rises above sense and finite thought. 'Ethereal Hypostasis' is not defined here.
  - Basis: IV.3 ¶62 lists the confirmations directly, after testing by 'experimental reason' and analysis 'to the last extremity of contrite consciousness'.
  - Also: “having passed the test of experimental reason not only, but, analyzed to the last extremity of contrite consciousness” `IV.3 ¶62`

## Ambiguities

- “lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring unto the third or fourth generation” `IV.3 ¶56`
  - Reading: Technical: agent = the operator, patient = the subject worked on, offspring = the product of the work; the operator's fault passes into the matter and its product.
  - Reading: Moral: a general image of inherited guilt, the sinner's fault multiplied in those affected and their descendants, not mapped onto the operation.
  - Basis: Technical: addressed to one whose desire 'should lead him to practice' (IV.3 ¶56). Moral: 'unto the third or fourth generation' speaks of descendants, and ¶57 continues with the Israelites' punishment for sin; the text gives no gloss.
- “Neither break a superficies” `IV.3 ¶56`
  - Reading: A literal caution in the practice not to break some surface or boundary.
  - Reading: A figurative caution against overstepping a limit, parallel to 'Enlarge not thy destiny'.
  - Basis: The chunk gives no gloss; it sits beside 'Ne tu augeas fatum' as a warning to the would-be practitioner (IV.3 ¶56).
- “To go forth and to return; therefore was the agreement cut off” `IV.3 ¶60`
  - Reading: Going forth and returning in meditation, repeated: leaving and coming back when the heart fails.
  - Reading: The going forth from and return to the origin, as the line returning into its beginning.
  - Basis: First: immediately followed by 'Close thy eyes and meditate: and if thy heart fail thee, return again' (IV.3 ¶60). Second: IV.3 ¶62 'The line returns to form a circle into its’ beginning'. 'the agreement cut off' is not explained.
- “so says the Kabalah --- Close thy eyes and meditate: and if thy heart fail thee, return again; since therefore it is written,” `IV.3 ¶60`
  - Reading: The meditation instruction is part of the Kabalistic citation.
  - Reading: The instruction is Atwood's own, justified by the Kabalistic text that follows.
  - Basis: 'so says the Kabalah' closes the preceding sentence, but 'since therefore it is written' then grounds the instruction in the Latin of IV.3 ¶61 (n28).

## Threads

- `IV.3 ¶54` 'Having discussed thus much of the Hermetic Mystery, and suggested certain particulars concerning the Practice at this latter end': refers back to the preceding treatment of the Practice; 'we would repeat our warning' refers to an earlier warning against hasty interpretation and trials.
- `IV.3 ¶54` 'others we have designedly reserved': Atwood states some matters escaped her and others were deliberately withheld.
- `IV.3 ¶54` 'as we have repeatedly shown': earlier demonstrations that success depends on discovering the true Intention.
- `IV.3 ¶56` 'Injunctions likewise are frequent respecting the sacred nature of the ministration': injunctions said to be frequent; their source is not cited here.
- `IV.3 ¶62` 'of which it has been proven to be the most exact epitome': refers to an earlier proof that the Stone is the epitome of the universe.
