# III.1_07

*Atwood decodes the binding of drunken Saturn as watchful Intellect cutting off the sated Will, through repeated conflicts that prepare the Bath of Diana until the Identical Spirit arises; through Eirenaeus' verse, Helvetius and Synesius she then identifies the First Matter so produced with Diana, the Light, the secret water-fire and the soul drawn from its mine.*

## Argument

- Verse: drunken Saturn sleeps beneath the oaks; "fast bind the god." `III.1 ¶67` — QUOTED: unnamed verse
- Gloss: the Saturnian Will, revelling unrestrained through the subordinate faculties, grows drunk and sleeps; watchful Intellect cuts him off and sows his brazen strength. A rebellious armament springs up, fights for the Stone, and is by it annihilated and raised again. `III.1 ¶68` — ATWOOD
- So the Bath of Diana is prepared from the blood of many battles, until the Identical Spirit arises pure from its primaeval element, subject only to its own Law. `III.1 ¶68` — ATWOOD
- Verse: Mercury, first matter of metals, is a non-wetting 'water dry' holding the secret fire; dead, then purged and vivified with true sulfur, it unlocks gold into a healing Essence. `III.1 ¶69, III.1 n73` — QUOTED: Eirenaeus
- Argent Vive, unripe gold, yields the secret menstrual, mother of the Stone (fountain, May-dew, Eve); it washes the king; Adam quickens green. Freeing the soul imprisoned in Saturn raises 'our Moon'. `III.1 ¶69` — QUOTED: Eirenaeus
- The First Matter is generated by emancipating the Fontal Source: Diana, a Light blinding the irrational, the Fundamental Nature's wholeness personified. `III.1 ¶70` — ATWOOD
- Helvetius: this unforgettable Light; by experience the mystery is found only with Jehovah 'saturninely placed' at the world's centre. `III.1 ¶70, III.1 n74` — QUOTED: Helvetius
- There, made natural by art, every myth is displayed: Salamander, Aeneas' bath, Hercules reviving incombustible. `III.1 ¶70` — UNCLEAR
- These spectacles, instituted by Wisdom, lead souls from Lethe to the Freed Will; Divine Ecstasy kindled with its Source works the Hermetic miracle and exalts Mind. `III.1 ¶70` — ATWOOD
- Synesius: the Stone's Quintessence is the soul drawn by the magistery from its mine; the Water is sharpest vinegar, the Blessed Nature engendering all. `III.1 ¶70` — QUOTED: Synesius
- These plain words clarify the method; reason with perspicacious imagination reaches it. Kabalistic and Greek concordances follow. `III.1 ¶71` — ATWOOD

## Method

- **procedure** — “Saturn, with honey of the bees produced, Sunk in ebriety; fast bind the god.” `III.1 ¶67` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** When the Will has indulged itself to satiety and fallen asleep, it is to be bound — cut off by the Intellect.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68 glosses the image: the Saturnian Will 'becomes intoxicated ... and, as the image runs, from the effect he sleeps', and then Intellect 'prepares to cut him off'.
  - Also: “his desires are more than satisfied, and, as the image runs, from the effect he sleeps” `III.1 ¶68`
- **impediment** — “For the Saturnian Will, being allowed to revel without limitation or rational restraint, throughout te subordinate faculties, becomes intoxicated” `III.1 ¶68` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** 'Saturn' is the Will, which, left unrestrained by reason, spreads through and indulges the lower faculties.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68 names 'the Saturnian Will' as what the drunken Saturn of ¶67 signifies.
- **operator** — “It is then the watchful eye of Intellect, well advised and able, prepares to cut him off” `III.1 ¶68` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The restraining agent is the Intellect, which must be watchful, well advised and able, and acts at the moment the Will is sated and dormant.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68, explicit.
- **stage** — “drawing forth all his brazen strength, plants it in the newly-furrowed soil, whence springs another armament” `III.1 ¶68` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The Will's force, drawn out, is sown anew and gives rise to contending powers which the Stone itself repeatedly destroys and raises again: the work proceeds by successive dissolutions and revivals.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68: the armament, 'still rebellious, contending with each other for the self-same Stone, are by it once more annihilated and again raised up'. The sowing image is not further explained.
  - Also: “contending with each other for the self-same Stone, are by it once more annihilated and again raised up” `III.1 ¶68`
- **stage** — “So the Bath of Diana is prepared out of the blood of many battles, where the innocents suffer for the guilty” `III.1 ¶68` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The 'Bath of Diana' is the product of these repeated conflicts, with suffering and violent images along the way; Diana is identified in ¶70 with the First Matter.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68 ('So' links the bath to the preceding conflicts); III.1 ¶70 'this is Diana' of the First Matter. The chunk does not say what the 'blood' or 'innocents' are.
  - Also: “many barbarous images befall” `III.1 ¶68`
- **result** — “the Identical Spirit arises, pure, bright, and contrite, from its primaeval eleemtn, and free in legal subordination only to its own perfect Law” `III.1 ¶68` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The end of the conflicts: the spirit emerges purified from its original element, free and subject to no law but its own.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶68 states it as the outcome ('until ...'). What 'Identical' signifies is not explained in this chunk.
- **subject** — “The matter first of metals Mercury A moisture is which wetteth no the hand Yet flows, and therefore is named water dry” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** The first matter is called Mercury: a flowing moisture that does not wet, hence 'dry water', and explicitly not common (vulgar) water. It is akin to all metals, which hide a Mercury within.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 verse defines it and contrasts it with the vulgar water.
  - Also: “The vulgar is at every one’s command, But this is not the water we desire,” `III.1 ¶69`
  - Also: “This Matter is to metals all of kin All which do hide a Mercury within.” `III.1 ¶69`
- **agent** — “For in our water is our secret fire.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** The desired water itself contains (and is) the hidden fire of the work; the Mercury's body encloses an active spirit. This chunk does not say what the fire is.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 asserts it twice, the second time of Mercury directly ('This is our water, this our secret fire').
  - Also: “This is our water, this our secret fire.” `III.1 ¶69`
  - Also: “A body dost possess which doth enfold A Spirit inexpressible to act,” `III.1 ¶69`
- **principle** — “The life when gone, then dead it doth remain Till a new soul shall it reanimate.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Matter, once living and able to procreate all metals, is now dead and must be reanimated by a new soul.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 verse; repeated in 'For dead it is, though, fluent'. What the new soul is, is not said beyond the vivifying 'true sulfur'.
  - Also: “For dead it is, though, fluent” `III.1 ¶69`
- **procedure** — “He then who knows the parts of Mercury And can it superfluities decrease And with true sulfur it can vivify” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operator must know the Mercury's parts, remove its excess, and enliven it with 'true sulfur'; he can then open gold and re-solidify both into a healing Essence. 'Parts' and 'true sulfur' are not explained here.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 states the steps and their outcome; their meaning is not glossed.
  - Also: “May gold unlock and after recongeal Both to an Essence which all griefs canheal.” `III.1 ¶69`
- **subject** — “Here thou beholdest in a Subject Small, From this world’s isery a full protection.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The subject is small, yet contains all creatures in abridged, perfected form, and is a source of wealth, life and healing.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69: 'Here in a map, thou seest the creatures all Abridged, and reduced to their perfection'; 'Lo! Here a spring of wealth, a Tree of Life'.
  - Also: “Here in a map, thou seest the creatures all Abridged, and reduced to their perfection.” `III.1 ¶69`
  - Also: “Lo! Here a spring of wealth, a Tree of Life,” `III.1 ¶69`
- **subject** — “For Argent Vive is gold essential Only unripe, which, if thou canst prepare By art, it gives the secret menstrual: The mother of our Stone which is so rare.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** Argent Vive is gold in an unripe state; prepared by art it yields the 'secret menstrual', the mother (source) of the Stone, also named oil, unguent, marchasite and bright fountain.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 verse asserts these identities directly.
  - Also: “Our oil, our unguent, and our marchasite; Which we do name also our fountain bright.” `III.1 ¶69`
- **stage** — “with which our noble king Is wshed ad carried to the mountain tops, Where he the virtue of the Heavens receives, Which never after him, when fixed, leaves.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The fountain's distillation washes the 'king' and raises him, where he receives a heavenly virtue that, once fixed, stays permanently. The king and the 'fourfold spring' are not identified here.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69: the crystal fountain 'Distilling' washes the king; 'when fixed' marks permanence.
  - Also: “O crystal fountain! Which with fourfold spring Rubs down the valleys with its pearly drops” `III.1 ¶69`
- **agent** — “This our May-dew which our earth doth move To bring forth fruit, which fruit is perfect gold” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The same water, called May-dew, makes 'our earth' fruitful, and the fruit is perfect gold. 'Our earth' is not explained.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69: "This" follows the fountain stanza; taken as continuing its chain of names, but the antecedent is not explicit.
- **sign** — “He doth receive, who erst as dead was seen, And quickened first appears in colours green.” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The water is also 'Eve'; in union with her, 'Adam', who seemed dead, receives his soul, and the first sign of this revival is green colour. Adam is not identified.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69: "This is our Eve, whom Adam doth so love, That in her arms his soul ... He doth receive"; "This" taken as the same water (antecedent not explicit).
  - Also: “This is our Eve, whom Adam doth so love,” `III.1 ¶69`
- **procedure** — “in Saturn there is hid A soul immortal which in prison lies, Untie its fetters” `III.1 ¶69` (QUOTED: Eirenaeus)
  - **Plain (stated):** An immortal soul is imprisoned in 'Saturn'; releasing it produces a shining vapour called 'our Moon and sparkling Firmament'.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶69 answers its own 'How this? Even thus'. Relation to the binding of Saturn in ¶67–68 is not stated (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “then shall arise A Vapour shining, like pearl orient, Which is our Moon and sparkling Firmament” `III.1 ¶69`
- **result** — “the First Matter of the adepts said to be generated and produced by an emancipation of the Fontal Source; and this is Diana” `III.1 ¶70` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The First Matter is produced when the originating Source is set free by the process just described (¶68–69); it is Diana.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70: 'By such a vital and mysterious process' refers back; 'this is Diana' asserts the identity.
- **subject** — “she is the wholeness of the Fundamental Nature at once personified, the knot and link of all the elements o being” `III.1 ¶70` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** Diana (the First Matter) is the whole of the fundamental nature in one, containing and binding all levels of being, lower and higher.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, direct statement ('inferior as well as superior, which she contains within herself').
- **impediment** — “that refulgent Light which eclipses every other light but that of its proper Reason, and strikes the irrational intruder blind” `III.1 ¶70` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** The First Matter is a Light that only its own Reason can bear; whoever approaches without reason is blinded.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70 identifies First Matter with this Light; ¶71 likewise makes reason the faculty that attains the idea.
  - Also: “A light more splendid than the Sun and gold, and more beautiful than the Moon or silver, and more diaphanous than the purest crystal” `III.1 ¶70`
- **principle** — “this mystery is to be found; but only with the great Jehovah saturninely placed in the center of the world” `III.1 ¶70` (QUOTED: Helvetius)
  - **Plain (stated):** The mystery is found, by experience, only at the 'center of the world' where Jehovah is placed 'saturninely'; there, 'within most intimately', the 'Abyss of the Spoagyric artifice' is disclosed. The chunk does not explain these expressions.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70; the first-person 'we do assert, according to experience' continues Helvetius' speech. Meaning of the centre is open (see ambiguities).
  - Also: “There, within most intimately, the Abyss of the Spoagyric artifice is disclosed” `III.1 ¶70`
- **principle** — “There, in that region, no longer fabulous but by art made natural, is seen the Salamander casting out the ethereal waters, and washing himself in the flames” `III.1 ¶70` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (stated):** What the myths tell is, through the art, actually seen there: the ancient fables depict real experiences of the work. Individual images are not decoded in this chunk.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70 ('no longer fabulous but by art made natural'; 'there is displayed every mythological antique device').
  - Also: “as in a beautifully pictured series, there is displayed every mythological antique device” `III.1 ¶70`
  - Also: “the river Numitius, in which Aeneas, bathing, was absolved from his mortality” `III.1 ¶70`
- **result** — “magnanimous Hercules, having burnt all his maternal body upon a pile of wood, revives entire and incombustible, as the Phoenix on her pyre” `III.1 ¶70` (UNCLEAR)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** One spectacle: burning away the maternal body and reviving whole, fire-proof and god-like. Not explained further in this chunk.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, listed among the spectacles; 'and is changed into the likeness of an immortal god'.
  - Also: “and is changed into the likeness of an immortal god” `III.1 ¶70`
- **result** — “the games and choice spectacles which tradition commemorates as instituted by Wisdom, for the benefit of souls emerging from Lethe and Egyptian darkness to the glorious liberty of the Freed Will in life” `III.1 ¶70` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The mythic spectacles are experiences for souls passing out of forgetfulness and darkness into the freedom of the liberated Will, in this life.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, explicit statement of purpose.
- **agent** — “it is that kindling of Divine Ecstasy, in connection with its Source, that attracts the whole phenomenon of nature to its desire, and works the total miracle of the Hermetic Art in life” `III.1 ¶70` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The operative cause of the whole Art is a divine ecstasy kindled in connection with its Source; it draws nature to it, and exalts and confirms the mind through understanding of causes.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, direct statement.
  - Also: “exalts Mind by the understanding of Causes, and confirms it” `III.1 ¶70`
- **subject** — “the Quintessence and hidden thing of our Stone is nothing else than our viscous celestial and glorious soul, drawn by our magistery out of its mine” `III.1 ¶70` (QUOTED: Synesius)
  - **Plain (stated):** The hidden essence of the Stone is the soul itself, extracted by the work from where it lies buried; it generates and brings itself forth.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, Synesius' explicit identity; Atwood endorses it as 'These plain words' supporting her evidence (¶71).
  - Also: “which engenders itself and brings itself forth” `III.1 ¶70`
- **agent** — “that Water is the most sharp vinegar, which makes gold to be a pure spirit” `III.1 ¶70` (QUOTED: Synesius)
  - **Plain (stated):** The Water is a sharp solvent ('vinegar') that turns gold into pure spirit; it is the Blessed Nature that engenders all things.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶70, Synesius states the identities; endorsed in ¶71.
  - Also: “nay, it is that Blessed Nature which engenders all things” `III.1 ¶70`
- **operator** — “reason, aided by a perspicacious imagination, will attain readily to the idea, and research may further assist the faithful to confirm it” `III.1 ¶71` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (stated):** The method is grasped by reason working with a penetrating imagination, then confirmed by research.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶71, explicit.
- **subject** — “the development of this Material of Mind” `III.1 ¶71` (ATWOOD)
  - **Plain (interpreted):** Atwood names the subject under discussion a 'Material of Mind': the matter in question is mental.
  - Basis: III.1 ¶71 sums up the subject with this phrase; consistent with ¶68 (Will, Intellect, faculties) and ¶70 (soul as Quintessence).

## Sources cited

- **unnamed verse** — “Saturn, with honey of the bees produced, Sunk in ebriety; fast bind the god.” `III.1 ¶67, III.1 ¶68` — Illustrates; Atwood glosses it as Intellect cutting off the sated Saturnian Will.
- **Eirenaeus**, *Marrow of Alchemy* — “For in our water is our secret fire.” `III.1 ¶69, III.1 n73` — Supports: long verse on Mercury/first matter, the secret fire, Argent Vive, the menstrual, and the soul hidden in Saturn, leading into Atwood's account of the First Matter.
- **Helvetius**, *Vitulus Aureus* — “that most recreant Beauty can never be blotted out from my mind” `III.1 ¶70, III.1 n74` — Supports: 'the acute Helvetius' testifies to the transcendent Light and that the mystery is found, by experience, at the centre (extent of his words unclear).
- **tradition (mythological antique devices)** — “Such are a very few of the games and choice spectacles which tradition commemorates as instituted by Wisdom” `III.1 ¶70` — Illustrates: myths (Salamander, Aeneas in Numitius, Pactolus/Midas, Medea/Jason, Hesperides, Atalanta, Hercules, Phoenix, etc.) as spectacles of the soul's liberation, made natural by art.
- **Synesius** — “the Quintessence and hidden thing of our Stone is nothing else than our viscous celestial and glorious soul” `III.1 ¶70, III.1 ¶71` — Supports: 'the Greek saint' gives the summary statement; Atwood calls these 'plain words' supporting her evidence.

## Ambiguities

- “inasmuch transcendant, says the acute Helvetius (74), that that most recreant Beauty can never be blotted out from my mind” `III.1 ¶70`
  - Reading: Helvetius' words run from 'A light more splendid' through the mythic catalogue, ending before 'Such are a very few of the games'.
  - Reading: They end earlier (after 'disbelieved by fools and the illiterate' or after 'the center of the world'), the catalogue being Atwood's.
  - Basis: No closing quotation marks; 'my mind' and 'we do assert, according to experience' mark a first-person speaker; 'Such are a very few ... which tradition commemorates' and 'And it is that kindling of Divine Ecstasy' read as Atwood resuming.
- “in Saturn there is hid A soul immortal which in prison lies, Untie its fetters” `III.1 ¶69`
  - Reading: Same Saturn as ¶67–68 (the Saturnian Will): the Will is first bound, then the soul hidden in it is freed — two phases of one process.
  - Reading: An independent image in Eirenaeus' verse, not meant to be combined with the Saturnian Will.
  - Basis: ¶68 glosses Saturn as the Saturnian Will to be cut off; ¶69 untying fetters in Saturn; ¶70 'By such a vital and mysterious process ... an emancipation of the Fontal Source' joins them; no explicit identification.
- “only with the great Jehovah saturninely placed in the center of the world” `III.1 ¶70`
  - Reading: The divine principle hidden within, at the inner centre of the subject (cf. the soul hidden in Saturn).
  - Reading: A cosmic centre in a more literal sense.
  - Basis: ¶70 'There, within most intimately, the Abyss ... is disclosed' suggests interiority; ¶69 'in Saturn there is hid A soul immortal'; the chunk does not decide.
- “but by usurpation, in each particular universally and without return.” `III.1 ¶70`
  - Reading: The Blessed Nature is present in each particular thing only as appropriated by it, without returning to its source.
  - Reading: It engenders each particular thing universally and irreversibly.
  - Basis: Syntax of the closing clause of the Synesius passage is obscure; no further gloss in the chunk.
