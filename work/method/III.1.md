# Method material — III.1 Of the Experimental Method and Fermentation of the Philosophic Subject, According to the Paracelsian Alchemists and Some Others

Format of each point: plain meaning; strongest verbatim expression (ref); other expressions; label *(status; voice)*. *Endorsed* means Atwood adopts the cited view as her own.

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The first matter is a real substance of life, not an abstraction.** The alchemists' first matter has the same standing as the Greek "ethereal hypostasis": mind treated as "an absolute substratal matter also of existence" (III.1 ¶2). "claiming like extreme attributes and miraculous origin for their first matter, as do the Greeks, for that ethereal hypostasis we were before discussing" (III.1 ¶3). *(interpreted: the text draws a parallel, not a strict identity; ATWOOD)*

2. **In the universe the subject is the Ether; in man it is the Phantastic Spirit, figured as the Sphinx.** The Phantastic Spirit is the natural vehicle of life and the antechamber of Reason. The Sphinx stands for the Ether, guardian of the inner light, and Alchemy works on this same source. "In our vestibule, therefore, the Phantastic Spirit, which is the natural vehicle of our life, is situated" (III.1 ¶5). Also: "so is that Ether then the Sphinx of the Universe" (III.1 ¶5); "agreeable also do we find the art of Alchemy directed upon the same enigmatical source" (III.1 ¶6); "the Ethereal Spirit abandons her phantasy" (III.1 ¶20, where the Sphinx = the Ethereal Spirit). *(Sphinx = Ether stated; that the Phantastic Spirit is the subject of the work interpreted from ¶6; ATWOOD)*

3. **This spirit is double and receptive.** It is the top of instinctive mind and the base of rational knowledge, and it holds passively what the inner light is actively. "she is the summit of the irrational mind relying on instinct, and the basis whereon o build the rational and transcend opinion in indivisible science" (III.1 ¶6). Also: "And she is all things passively which the internal light is impassively." (III.1 ¶6). *(double nature stated; receptive role interpreted; ATWOOD)*

4. **The material is invisible and imperceptible to the senses.** "Material of our Magistry a substance insensible." (III.1 ¶7). Also: "A nature to search out which is invisible," (III.1 ¶7). *(stated; QUOTED: unattributed verse, adopted by Atwood)*

5. **The medicine is inside man, locked in.** "The true medicine, according to the Paracelsians, is bound in man" (III.1 ¶10). Also: "shut up as it may be milk, within the hard and solid nut" (III.1 ¶10). *(stated; QUOTED: the Paracelsians, endorsed)*

6. **What is freed is the highest intellect, imprisoned in the body.** "the flower of human intellect, triply imprisoned in the dark body’s hold" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "This it is the business of the philosopher to open and set free" (III.1 ¶13). "triply" is not explained. *(stated; ATWOOD)*

7. **The "Cave of Mercury" is the most hidden vapour of life; what is carried out of it is the root and true matter of the Art.** "the Cave of Mercury, which, according to the Hermetic interpretation, is Taenerus, the most hidden vapor of life" (III.1 ¶19). Also: "the mineral radix and true mater of the Hermetic Art" (III.1 ¶19). *(stated; ATWOOD reporting the Hermetic interpretation)*

8. **The whole matter is one thing; folly, phantasy, passion and Reason are its modes.** "the whole matter I know to be One only Thing" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "as folly, and phantasy, and passion are modes of being of the One Thing, and Reason is another" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; QUOTED: Hermes, endorsed; the modes are ATWOOD's)*

9. **The divine mind lies invisible and dormant in nature and becomes known only when set in motion** (the myth of Jupiter's grown-together feet). "his feet had grown together and that he was forced to live in solitude" (III.1 ¶23). *(stated by the cited scholar; QUOTED: Eudoxus in Plutarch; Atwood reads it as "an allegory of the Art")*

10. **The philosopher's Mercury is the impure vapour of life.** "the philosopher’s Mercury, i.e., of the impure vapor of life" (III.1 ¶24). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

11. **The material has two principles, male and female, to be taken spiritually.** They appear to be the body of gold and its consanguineous spirit that must be joined. "Take our two serpents, which are to be found everywhere on the face of the earth; they are a living male and a living female" (III.1 ¶50). *(spiritual sense stated in the passage; identification with the two natures interpreted; QUOTED: Cornelius Agrippa's ingenious disciple)*

12. **Salt is indispensable even when Sulfur and Mercury are known.** "yet without Salt no man can attain to this Sacred Science" (III.1 ¶61). Whether Salt is the impure Instrument is open (Open questions 25). *(interpreted; QUOTED: the Adept)*

13. **The Art works on an "identical representative" of the rebellious Principle, through which that Principle's seized forces are drawn out and spent.** "that identical representative of him which the Divine Art requires, in order that his stolen forces may be drawn forth and spent in the sanquinary conflict which he provokes in life" (III.1 ¶64). What the representative is, is not said. *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

14. **Mercury, the first matter of metals, is a "water dry" that does not wet. It is not common water, and all metals hide a Mercury within.** "The matter first of metals Mercury A moisture is which wetteth no the hand Yet flows, and therefore is named water dry" (III.1 ¶69). Also: "The vulgar is at every one’s command, But this is not the water we desire," (III.1 ¶69); "This Matter is to metals all of kin All which do hide a Mercury within." (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

15. **The subject is small, yet contains all creatures abridged and perfected.** "Here in a map, thou seest the creatures all Abridged, and reduced to their perfection." (III.1 ¶69). Also: "Here thou beholdest in a Subject Small," (III.1 ¶69). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

16. **Argent Vive is unripe gold. Prepared by art it yields the "secret menstrual", the mother of the Stone.** "For Argent Vive is gold essential Only unripe, which, if thou canst prepare By art, it gives the secret menstrual: The mother of our Stone which is so rare." (III.1 ¶69). Also: "Our oil, our unguent, and our marchasite; Which we do name also our fountain bright." (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

17. **Diana (the First Matter) is the whole of the fundamental nature in one, binding all levels of being.** "she is the wholeness of the Fundamental Nature at once personified, the knot and link of all the elements o being" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

18. **The hidden essence of the Stone is the soul, drawn out of its "mine" by the work.** "the Quintessence and hidden thing of our Stone is nothing else than our viscous celestial and glorious soul, drawn by our magistery out of its mine" (III.1 ¶70). Also: "which engenders itself and brings itself forth" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated; QUOTED: Synesius, endorsed in ¶71)*

19. **The subject is mental: a "Material of Mind".** "the development of this Material of Mind" (III.1 ¶71). *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **Two fires.** The fire of natural life, once released, stirs and alters. Only the purified inner fire perfects. External heat only cherishes the internal. "The fire of the natural life stirs up and, being manumitted, alters; but the internal alone is able, being purified, to perfect the work begun" (III.1 ¶43). Also: "L’externe feu de charbon Rend la matiere alteree, Mais l’interne et l’artheree Faira ton ouvrage bon" (III.1 ¶42); "be not so careful of the fire of the Athanor as of your internal Fire" (III.1 ¶14, Eirenaeus); "to govern his fire dexterously, so as to cherish internal heat with external" (III.1 ¶14); the opening motto "Hanc massam exterio tentum calor excitat ignis; Aethereo interior sed perfecit omnia fot." (III.1 ¶1; the Latin is corrupt and our rendering is: outer fire only excites, an inner ethereal [heat] perfects, so nothing should rest on it alone). *(¶43 stated, ATWOOD; ¶14 stated, QUOTED: Eirenaeus, endorsed; ¶1 interpreted on our translation, QUOTED: Tractatus Aureus)*

2. **The imagination has a lifting power that lets the phantastic spirit be assimilated to the divine.** "Her winds are images of the elevating power which the imagination possesses, by which likewise she is rendered capable of divine assimilation" (III.1 ¶6). *(stated; ATWOOD; "winds"/"wings" open)*

3. **"Antimony" means the fire of life in man, which does nothing excellent while shut in.** "our fire of life (called Antimony by adepts, can effect nothing comparatively excellent whilst it is immured" (III.1 ¶10). Also: "as fire which lies hidden in fuel, unless it be ignited, is good for nothing" (III.1 ¶10). *(stated; QUOTED: the Paracelsians, endorsed)*

4. **Natural reason is only an "iron key" at the threshold.** It is necessary to unlock the inner light, but in the work itself it is dull and does nothing. "as it were an iron key, intended to unlock the golden treasury of light within" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "natural reason, even the most acute, is dull here comparatively, and inoperative" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

5. **The spiritual intellect, once it knows itself, turns back on the outer life and transforms it. Atwood equates it with Eirenaeus' "internal fire".** "It is this vital perscrutinator, the internal fire of the sulfur of thy water, as Eirenaeus calls it, that, investigating scientifically, operates the whole change" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "this latter, returning with power upon the life without, proceeds to analyze and revolutionize" (III.1 ¶13); "proving all, as may be said, chemically by the fiery essence of its newly conceived Law" (III.1 ¶13). *(interpreted: perscrutinator = spiritual intellect rests on "this"; ATWOOD)*

6. **Eirenaeus' fire is the sulfur or seed of gold. It is imprisoned in a hard, dry body and set loose by "our water".** "Our fire is the true sulfur of Gold, says Eirenaeus, which in the hard and dry body is imprisoned but by the mediation of our water it is set loose" (III.1 ¶14). Also: "by rotting the moles of the body (i.e., of the ethereal body) under which it is detained" (III.1 ¶14); "this is the seed of gold, involved in many links, and held prisoner, as it were, in a deep dungeon" (III.1 ¶14). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus, endorsed; the parenthetical glosses UNCLEAR)*

7. **The higher Intellect purifies the phantastic spirit and raises it to the divine. Natural intellect cannot.** "Intellect above all things separates whatever is contrary to the true purity of phantastic Spirit; for it attenuates this spirit in an occult and ineffable manner, and extends it to Divinity" (III.1 ¶20). Also: "But the natural Intellect cannot do this" (III.1 ¶20). *(stated; QUOTED: Synesius, endorsed)*

8. **Mind is the true Separator: it both performs and governs the separation, and nothing foreign enters.** "we observe Mind to be the true Separator, the efficient as well as the regimen of the work, into which, as before shown, no foreign admixture is allowed to enter" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "Nor ought we, therefore, taking into consideration the human agency, to understand this decomponency of life in a mechanical sense" (III.1 ¶21); "that is the sequestatrix, which gives to every thing its form and essence" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

9. **The ancients' Gold, Sol, Sulfur and Tincture are names for supernatural Reason, which is Light: an "Intellectual sun".** Everyday reason is its image: what natural reason does in theory, this Reason does in essence. "it is of this the ancients speak, calling it Wisdom, Intellect, Gold, Sol, Sulfur, Tincture" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "For it is Light indeed, and an occult splendor of existence transcendentally pure" (III.1 ¶21); "these things which the natural reason as an image enacts theoretically, this supernatural reason is said to do as an archetype essentially" (III.1 ¶21); "so are we taught to conceive of the Intellectual sun" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

10. **Intellect is unmixed and rules over soul. It starts small and separates contraries.** "this comprehending Intellect made things to be separated" (III.1 ¶22). Also: "separated the dense from the rare, the hot from the cold, the lucid from the dark, and the dry from the moist" (III.1 ¶22); "it began from that which is small to exercise its comprehending power" (III.1 ¶22). *(stated; QUOTED: Anaxagoras, endorsed as confirming "its efficient operation")*

11. **Isis is the purified intelligence. Her act dissolves the vital medium, opens the hidden source and raises the Voluntary Efficient into intellectual remembrance.** "the purified spirit or intelligence, that is Isis, by dissolving the vital medium, opens the occult source, and draws the Voluntary Efficient upward into intellectual reminescence" (III.1 ¶23). Also: "That Intellect must be assumed which is most perfectly purified; the knowledge of which must be sought for in spirit or spirits" (III.1 ¶23, Aristotle, endorsed). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

12. **A small grain of purified Reason, already tried by fire, goes back into its own native life and turns all of it into its own light.** It acts as a ferment, as a "sulfuric Spirit" that makes the impure vapour of life putrefy. "a small grain of the metaphysical ferment leavens the whole lump" (III.1 ¶24). Also: "the metaphysical graft, already purified and passed the fire, re-enters to redeem its congenital life" (III.1 ¶24); "transmutes all into the substance of its own Aurific Light" (III.1 ¶24); "this acute discriminative sulfuric Spirit occasions a putrefaction of the philosopher’s Mercury" (III.1 ¶24). *(interpreted (ferment = purified Reason); putrefying action stated; ATWOOD)*

13. **After the loss, a self-sacrificing Heroic Will enters and stirs the "bitter waters" to win back what was lost.** "the Heroic Will enters in, self-sacrificing, and stirs the bitter waters, to redeem and reinstate the kingdom lost" (III.1 ¶30). "bitter waters" is not explained. *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

14. **The purifying agent is the true Light present in every man and perfected in the Savior. One ray of it can cleanse corrupted life.** "This is the true Light which lighteth every man that cometh into the world, which is in the Savior was perfected; one ray of which is able to cleanse this leprous life of ours" (III.1 ¶30). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

15. **Only an inborn "celestial instinct", the maternal intelligence, reveals the hidden tree of life and leads further to the paternal light.** "the celestial instinct only to reveal; that subtle Maternal; intelligence which originally conceived it" (III.1 ¶34). Also: "can alone lead into the yet more central, antecedent Paternal, light of life" (III.1 ¶34); "Materna agnoscit aves" (III.1 ¶35). *(claim stated; birds = Maternal intelligence interpreted; ATWOOD)*

16. **The agent is a separable, thrice-refined intellectual purpose.** It is personified by the heroes and by Hermes' "our Son", it passes through death and the Stygian waters, and it is the mustard-seed of Faith that finds the hidden Stone. "that heroic, separable, triply refined intellectual purpose, which has been so often and under so many names personified" (III.1 ¶36). Also: "This is that prolific Mustard-seed, and Light of divine Faith" (III.1 ¶36); "visiting the interior rectifying, discovers the occult Stone --- the hidden Medicine" (III.1 ¶36); "Such was the Caduceus of Hermes --- the Golden Bough, the ferrying Cup of Hercules" (III.1 ¶36). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

17. **The decisive principle is a "vertical separated Light": a divine ray that comes only from the will of someone already made divine.** "This vertical separated Light then we take henceforth to be the true alternative principle in the Divine Art" (III.1 ¶37). Also: "a Power which glancing forth from the capable will of such only as are divinized, is essentially Divine" (III.1 ¶37). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

18. **The secret Fire is the philosophers' key and was never openly revealed.** "seek to know this Fire with all thy soul, that so thou mayest attain to thy desire; for it is the key of all the philosophers" (III.1 ¶37). *(stated; QUOTED: Pontanus, endorsed; Fire = separated Light interpreted, see Open questions 18)*

19. **The divine Separator is a subtle working power inside the lovers of Art.** It may be pointed to but not handed over. "whose Separator is an artist of great subtlety in them" (III.1 ¶39). Also: "to give the divine Separator into the bestial hand is prohibited" (III.1 ¶39). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme)*

20. **The alchemical Moon is the Passive Intelligence.** Freed by art and joined to the seeking Reason, it answers the Reason's Will and goes with it through the abyss unharmed. "the Moon is singularly honored, for it is the Passive Intelligence which, freed by art and set in conjunction, responds to the Will of her seeking Reason" (III.1 ¶40). Also: "passes with him through the abyss, as it were the very kingdom of confusion, triumphant over all and unconfounded" (III.1 ¶40). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

21. **The philosophical Sun and Moon work the depths together, apparently as Power (Sun) and Wisdom (Moon).** "These are they which sound the depths together; the Sun and Moon, philosophical." (III.1 ¶45). Also: "the influence of the Moon, says Plutarch, seems to reflect the works of reason, and to proceed from Wisdom" (III.1 ¶45). *(interpreted: the Sun/Power and Moon/Wisdom mapping is not stated; ATWOOD with QUOTED: Plutarch)*

22. **Sol is the soul.** "give it Sol, viz., the soul, that conjoining they may conceive a body according to that soul" (III.1 ¶48). *(stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme)*

23. **The dissolution sets loose two fires, one natural and one "against Nature", which feed on each other: "the greatest poison".** "the one being natural and the other a fire against Nature" (III.1 ¶56). Also: "being of equal origin, they prey upon each other incombustibly" (III.1 ¶56); "adepts describe it indeed as the greatest poison" (III.1 ¶56). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

24. **The newly roused, wrathful Efficient of the dissolution goes by the adepts' names Green Lion, Typhon and Firedrake, and during mortification Black Toad.** "Adepts call him the Green Lion, Typhon, Firedrake; or during the mortification, he is their venomous Black Toad" (III.1 ¶56). Also: "the newly roused Efficient is exceeding wrathful" (III.1 ¶56). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

25. **The dissolution appears to be caused by vital heat that art stirs up in the blood.** "the action of the vital heat stirred up artificially within the blood" (III.1 ¶58). Also: "ignites and opens for itself a passage, endeavoring forthwith to absorb the circulating light" (III.1 ¶58). *(stated, with Atwood's own hedge "appears"; ATWOOD)*

26. **The one unclean thing in the Stone is the Instrument that drives the gold to putrefy. It is evil but necessary, and once returned it gives the work its force and completeness.** "the Instrument moving the gold to putrefy" (III.1 ¶61). Also: "it is called by them Typhon, Satan, Aquafoetida, Ignis Gehanna, Mortis Immundities, etc." (III.1 ¶61); "though impure in the beginning, and manifestly evil, it is nevertheless a necessary ingredient" (III.1 ¶61). *(stated; ATWOOD endorsing the Alchemists)*

27. **The heavenly agent must be measured so that it changes the moisture from earthy to transparent without driving it off.** "Let the heavenly power or agent be such in the place of generation, or mutation, that it may alter the humidity from its earthly complexion to a fine transparent form or species" (III.1 ¶55). *(interpreted as a rule of measure; QUOTED: Lully)*

28. **"By crafte occasionate": the inborn (connate) spirit, through its attractive grace, subdues the self-willed agent and brings it to mortify itself. With the solar light eclipsed, a "Lunar Vulcan" plays Isis's part.** "it is by the attractive grace of the connate spirit that the self- willed agent is finally seen to be subdued and betrayed to self-mortification" (III.1 ¶63). Also: "the Lunar Vulcan acts a principal part, as Isis in the Mysteries" (III.1 ¶63); "by a conscience moving contrite in the Law of her Light" (III.1 ¶63). *(gloss of "By crafte occasionate" stated; Lunar Vulcan = connate spirit interpreted; ATWOOD)*

29. **The Green Lion: "green" means unripe. Enclosed with the Sun, it devours and eclipses him, turning red to white, yet its heat brings him to more than natural perfection.** "But our Lyon wanting maturity, Is called greene for unripenes trust me," (III.1 ¶65). Also: "And suddainely can hym devoure, If they be both shut in one towre: And hym Eclipse that was so bryght, And make thys redde to turne to whyte:" (III.1 ¶65); "He bringeth hym to more perfection, Than ever he had by Natures direccion." (III.1 ¶65). *(meaning of "green" stated; the rest interpreted; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

30. **The Lion is the priest who weds Sun and Moon (both born from him). Their King feeds only on the Lion's Blood.** "The Lyon ys the Preist, and the Sun and Moone the wedd, Yet they were both borene in the Lyons Bedd;" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "And tis as strange that thys Kings food, Can be nothing but thys Lyons Blood;" (III.1 ¶65); "A wonder a Lyon, and Sun and Moone, All these three one deede have done:" (III.1 ¶65). *(King's food stated; uniting role interpreted; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

31. **The fable heroes (Cadmus, Jason, Orpheus, Aeneas) stand for the Rational Ferment.** "The example given of Cadmus, from the Greek fable, identified him with Jason, Orpheus, Aeneas, and the rest, who represent the Rational Ferment;" (III.1 ¶66). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

32. **The Intellect, watchful and able, cuts off the Will once the Will has gorged itself and fallen asleep.** "It is then the watchful eye of Intellect, well advised and able, prepares to cut him off" (III.1 ¶68). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

33. **The desired water contains, and is, the secret fire; the Mercury's body encloses an active spirit.** "For in our water is our secret fire." (III.1 ¶69). Also: "This is our water, this our secret fire." (III.1 ¶69); "A body dost possess which doth enfold A Spirit inexpressible to act," (III.1 ¶69); "This our May-dew which our earth doth move To bring forth fruit, which fruit is perfect gold" (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; May-dew as the same water interpreted; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

34. **What works the whole Hermetic miracle is divine ecstasy kindled in union with its Source.** "it is that kindling of Divine Ecstasy, in connection with its Source, that attracts the whole phenomenon of nature to its desire, and works the total miracle of the Hermetic Art in life" (III.1 ¶70). Also: "exalts Mind by the understanding of Causes, and confirms it" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

35. **The Water is a sharp solvent that turns gold into pure spirit: the Blessed Nature that engenders all.** "that Water is the most sharp vinegar, which makes gold to be a pure spirit" (III.1 ¶70). Also: "nay, it is that Blessed Nature which engenders all things" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated; QUOTED: Synesius, endorsed)*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **The healer must first be healed. The physician must be reborn from divine light.** "that he would heal others, or hope to impart any superior efficacy, should first of all heal or be healed himself" (III.1 ¶9). Also: "Physician, heal thyself" (III.1 ¶9); "a physician should be born of the light of grace and spirit of the invisible divinity" (III.1 ¶10, Crollius, endorsed). *(stated; ATWOOD and QUOTED: Crollius)*

2. **Preparation of the self comes first: purification, then gradual mastery of the passions and alignment of the lower powers with reason.** "by an effectual baptismal purification, they also prepared the way, and by a gradual subjugation of the passions and adaptation of the subordinate powers to reason" (III.1 ¶11). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

3. **Consult the divine intellect only about purification, liberation and the salvation of life, never about trifles.** "they consulted it about things which pertain to purification, liberation, as Iamblicus tells us, and the salvation of life" (III.1 ¶12). *(stated; QUOTED: Iamblicus, endorsed)*

4. **The operator needs devout persistence, contemplation of causes, first-hand experience and divine grace. The work is laborious.** "by a holy assiduity of preparations, joining thereto diligent contemplation of natural causes" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "shall refuse no pains or difficulties to get experience by the industry of his handy work" (III.1 ¶13); "if the grace of the most high be infused into him" (III.1 ¶13); "the attainment of the Tree of Life is laborious, and the fruit of intellectual research" (III.1 ¶13); "Learn and look for the first foundation, says the monk, which nature holds concealed; search for it even with thine own eyes and hand" (III.1 ¶13); "the way to Wisdom is by patience and rational inquiry" (III.1 ¶13, Atwood's summary). *(stated; QUOTED: Crollius, Van Helmont, Basil Valentine, endorsed; ATWOOD)*

5. **Only those in whom the spiritual intellect is awakened (the "passport") can complete the work. The operator must be a lover of Wisdom.** "they only who have obtained this passport can attain to the Magistery of life" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "they only, literally speaking, can enter in through the narrow gate" (III.1 ¶13); "he must be a lover of Wisdom who can set her free" (III.1 ¶13). *(interpreted: passport = awakened intellect is not explicit; ATWOOD)*

6. **A right mind, a motive allied to the goal, the predestined conditions and the right order of operation are all required.** "But if he have not a right mind, and unless the predestined conditions and the order of operation be observed, all will be in vain" (III.1 ¶19). Also: "let him enter with a prudent motive, well understanding and allied to what he seeks" (III.1 ¶19); "the power will remain hidden, despite of every effort, in a pusillanimous uncongenial soul" (III.1 ¶19). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

7. **The investigator of the Sphinx must be trained and chosen, and must know how to examine her essence.** "having been duly educated and singled out, knows how and wherewith to investigate her peculiar essence" (III.1 ¶20). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

8. **The operator's free will takes part and can help or hinder. Man can both find and bring about the Causal Nature.** "The free will flourisheth and is conversant in virtue, and is either friend or foe in our works" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "there is a free power in the creature to its mutual affection and destruction" (III.1 ¶21); "man should be able not only to find the Causal Nature but to effect it" (III.1 ¶21, Hermes). *(stated; QUOTED: Paracelsus; Hermes)*

9. **The secret Fire is found only by wholehearted, profound meditation.** "But profound meditation alone can give it to thee so thou mayest discern, and not otherwise" (III.1 ¶37). *(stated; QUOTED: Pontanus, endorsed)*

10. **The true Inquisitor is trained to know what, where and how to obey, inquire, will and hope.** "that he may know what, and where, and how he ought to obey, and inquire, and will, and hope" (III.1 ¶38). Also: "all the care that is taken to train the true Inquisitor, that he may obtain the passport clear" (III.1 ¶38). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

11. **First requirement: surrender to divine love and grace.** "that they first seek God’s love and grace, and resign up themselves to become wholly one with that: else all their seeking is but a delusion" (III.1 ¶39). *(stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme)*

12. **The operator never works alone. The divine can be held only by "mediate occupation", through a guardian of the Light, as the fable heroes are always helped.** "the heroic adventurer with all his divine equipment, though he loses his first companion, never goes through the labors alone; but is aided by stratagem and wise counsel in the way" (III.1 ¶40). Also: "the representative understanding, measure, and guardian of the Light" (III.1 ¶40). Whether the helper is an inner faculty or a second person is open (Open questions 1). *(stated as to the fable; application interpreted; ATWOOD)*

13. **In the dark stage, the operator meets evil only by willing sacrifice and humbling of the Selfhood.** "voluntary sacrifice and humiliation of the Selfhood under the exemplary cross of Christ" (III.1 ¶60). Whose Selfhood is not stated. *(interpreted as operator requirement; ATWOOD)*

14. **The seeker must first know how to rule the fire. The teaching is deliberately veiled for safety.** "That they which do this Art desire, Should first know well to rule their Fyre:" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Swords to keep fro mad Mens hand:" (III.1 ¶65); "Whose noble practice doth them teach To veil their art with misty speech;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

15. **Only those who have the foundation within themselves can know it. The frivolous cannot receive it.** "known, as the Adept says, only to the Wise, because they alone can know it who have it in themselves" (III.1 ¶66). Also: "The irrational and frivolous-minded cannot Receive this truth, because it depends exactly upon the knowledge of That which is most abstruse in them." (III.1 ¶66). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

16. **The method is grasped by reason working with a penetrating imagination, then confirmed by research.** "reason, aided by a perspicacious imagination, will attain readily to the idea, and research may further assist the faithful to confirm it" (III.1 ¶71). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter gives no single linear sequence. It gives several overlapping accounts, listed here in the order they appear.

**A. Preliminaries (¶3–¶13)**

1. **The experiment is done on, and seen within, the operator's own inner being.** "to have observed in the experimental development of their own internal being, the whole procedure of the occult nature into evidence" (III.1 ¶3). Also: "with her universal efficient by the Light of Wisdom thenceforth revealed" (III.1 ¶3). *(stated as "A few" alchemists' claim, which Atwood treats as the foundation of Hermetic philosophy)*

2. **The method is a "Vital Experiment": an alteration of the seeker's life, continuous with what the Mysteries worked on those seeking wisdom and immortality.** "the vital alteration that was deemed necessary and operated in the Mysteries upon those who were desirous of wisdom and immortality" (III.1 ¶4). Also: "Let us examine yet further into the Method of this Vital Experiment" (III.1 ¶4). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

3. **First step: set the mind in motion.** "the first step of philosophy is to set the mind a-going" (III.1 ¶8). *(stated; QUOTED: unnamed, endorsed)*

4. **The Spirit must first be made to conceive the effects it is to produce.** How this is done is not said. "before the Spirit can be expected to yield any rational or pure effects, she must be made to conceive them" (III.1 ¶9). *(stated; QUOTED: philosophers, endorsed; its placement as a step interpreted)*

5. **The material is worked while still unmanifest, with nothing foreign added: life acts on life.** "This Material, whilst yet immanifest, they worked, and worked with by itself alone; joining self to self" (III.1 ¶8). Also: "vita vitam concipit, natura naturam vincit ac superat, patefacit, gignit et renovat" (III.1 ¶8; untranslated in the book, our rendering: life conceives life, nature conquers nature). *(interpreted; ATWOOD; within-one vs between-persons open)*

6. **Mesmerism is only a first key.** It opens onto the vestibule but shows only the maze of life, and gives no knowledge of how to amend "the Thing". "This Mesmerism, in respect of our Mystery then, may be regarded as a first key which, opening into the vestibule, affords a view within the sense’s prison, but of the labyrinth of life only" (III.1 ¶8). Also: "mesmerizing one another but without the important knowledge how to alter and amend the Thing" (III.1 ¶8). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

7. **Nature is questioned by reason from within, as by a lawful guest, not taken apart by force.** "the doors being opened from within, they enjoyed and took advantage of their entrance as lawful guests" (III.1 ¶12). Also: "put the question to Nature rationally" (III.1 ¶12); "sphered in the magian circle of her own light" (III.1 ¶12). *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

8. **Living causes are traced back to Deity. From there supernatural results are worked, aiming only at the Central Light until the hidden Divinity is manifest.** "having set the chain of vital causes in action, succeeded in tracing them to their last efficient link in Deity" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "until the hidden Divinity was moved into experience and made manifest in effect and power" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

9. **Two parts: find the hidden "first foundation" by experience, then contemplate spiritually how nature is perfected by resolving itself, so that pure is separated from impure.** "the contemplation which absolves the Second part of our admonition is celestial" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "considering how nature may be helped and perfected by resolution of itself, and how the destruction and compaction are to be handled" (III.1 ¶13); "the pure may be separated from the impure" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; QUOTED: Basil Valentine, endorsed)*

**B. Fermentation, grafting, purgation (¶9–¶10)**

10. **The Vital Spirit ferments of its own accord, and this changes the Passive Subject. Moderns neither exploit nor complete it.** "the spontaneous fermentation which the Vital Spirit undergoes, and the change that is thereby effected in the Passive Subject" (III.1 ¶9). *(stated; ATWOOD; not described further)*

11. **An exact ancient art of "grafting and transplanting" grew and refined the Spirit by degrees, up to a fifth, "in as many representative vessels or forms".** "that exact art of grafting and transplanting which the ancients practiced, and by means of which a growth and sublimation of the Spirit was effected" (III.1 ¶9). Also: "even to a third, fourth, and fifth degree of concentrated essentiality in as many representative vessels or forms" (III.1 ¶9). *(stated as to the effect; method interpreted; ATWOOD)*

12. **Purgation separates the pure life from its impure surroundings, like metal from ore. Freed, it flows out as a panacea.** "by a due purgation, the pure life is separated, as metal from the dark and sordid ore" (III.1 ¶10). Also: "a pure panacea from the god of Light" (III.1 ¶10). *(stated; QUOTED: the Paracelsians, endorsed)*

13. **The separation is a trial by fire, guided by a "natural inbred chemist" that puts each hidden virtue in its place and strips off impure envelopes.** "the natural inbred chemist teaches to segregate every mystery into its own reservacle, and to free the medicine from those scurvy envelopes wherein naturally it is wrapped" (III.1 ¶10). Also: "physic and pyrotechny, says Crollius, cannot be separated" (III.1 ¶10). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Crollius, endorsed)*

**C. Eirenaeus' regimen (¶14)**

14. **Prepare the first water, join it to the body in the right proportion, seal it until the "infant" forms, govern the fire and wait for signs.** "to prepare the first water, and to join it to the body in a just pondus, to shut it up in its vessel philosophically, until the infant be formed" (III.1 ¶14). Also: "But the means to discover this is not a light work, it requires a profound meditation" (III.1 ¶14). *(stated (sequence only, not decoded); QUOTED: Eirenaeus, endorsed)*

15. **The freed fire becomes visible at a third stage, the "Third Menstrual", after the body's elements separate. Without the two first menstruals it cannot be reached.** "after separation of the elements (of the same body) it appears visibly in our Third Menstrual" (III.1 ¶14). Also: "he that knows not our two first menstruals is altogether shut out from attaining to the sight of this Third and last" (III.1 ¶14). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus; menstruals not explained)*

**D. Finding and taking the matter (¶15–¶20)**

16. **The prize (Golden Bough) is found by looking upward and then taking it in hand. It yields only to one called by fate.** "well directing him how to look from beneath upwards, and take it in hand" (III.1 ¶15). *(interpreted; ATWOOD; that it cannot be won by force rests only on our translation of ¶16)*

17. **Like assimilates like: the genuine gold body is thrown into a "bottomless" Mercury, so that gold is assimilated, not made.** "Take, therefore, that body which is gold (not a brazen ferment), and throw it into Mercury" (III.1 ¶19). Also: "Fire loves fire, say they, not to make gold, but to assimilate it" (III.1 ¶19); "such a Mercury as is bottomless, or whose center it can never find, but by discovering its own" (III.1 ¶19). *(stated; QUOTED: the adepts; terms not decoded)*

18. **The art of Oedipus: the Sphinx is subdued, meaning the Ethereal Spirit gives up its fantasy and yields clear understanding.** "This is the art of Oedipus which, well conducted with the Sphinx, ends in her subjugation; in other words, the Ethereal Spirit abandons her phantasy, and yields the clear light of understanding" (III.1 ¶20). Also: "that deformed and limping Oedipus for example; so that he was able to vanquish the Metaphysical Monster" (III.1 ¶4). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

**E. Separation (¶21)**

19. **Separation is the main object of the Art. It is the same as the Mysteries' ordeal of the death of the natural life, running from suffering through dread, dissolution and corruption to the resurrection of the pure Ether into Light.** "appears to be identical with that which is described as taking place in the Mysteries, when the great ordeals are passed through during the decomposition and death of the natural life" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "Separation is the greatest miracle in philosophy, and that magic the most singular by which it is effected" (III.1 ¶21, Paracelsus); "from the beginning in suffering, succedent dread, dissolution, and corruption, to the final resurrection of the pure Ether into Light" (III.1 ¶21); "This Separation is indeed the primary object of the Art" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; identification ATWOOD's, hedged "appears"; QUOTED: Paracelsus)*

20. **Separate subtle from gross, gently and wisely. What is separated rises and descends again, gaining the strength of both higher and lower.** "Thou shalt separate the earth from the fire, the subtle from the gross, gently, with much sagacity; it will ascend from earth to heaven, and again descend from heaven to earth" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "will receive the strength of the Inferiors and of the Superiors" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; QUOTED: Hermes (Smagardine Tablet), endorsed)*

21. **The Intellectual sun strips away acquired false forms, gradually grinds out what is foreign, and fills the passive Spirit with itself.** "separating and rejecting the false forms and elementary qualities which supervene through generation, assimilating the whole inferior life by continual trituration of its foreign tincture" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "by pure infusion of Itself to the passified Spirit throughout" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

**F. The ferment, the death of the medial life, repeated rebirth (¶23–¶37)**

22. **First half of the work: universal Reason is revealed in a purified intelligence.** "that perfect beginning of Wisdom, which is half of the perfect whole" (III.1 ¶23). Also: "Dimidium facti qui bene coepit habet" (III.1 ¶24). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

23. **The purified graft re-enters its native life as a ferment and makes the impure vital vapour putrefy** (Agent 12; Signs 5).

24. **A first, intermediate "celestial" life must be cut off in its prime and buried, leaving only a memory that drives the work. The deaths of the heroes' friends depict it.** "the celestial medial life which, in the order of divine rites, precedes the heroic work, is by necessity cut off, even in its prime" (III.1 ¶26). Also: "when perfected at all points, is shut up and buried" (III.1 ¶26); "Achilles does not appear at all in arms, nor has he these, until after Patroclus, his bosom friend, is slain" (III.1 ¶25); "so Hyanthe died, so Hylas at the fountain, Adonis, Misenus, Elpenor, Patroclus, too, before the heroic virtue was brought into act" (III.1 ¶28). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

25. **The drive comes from loss: Divine Law takes the spirit's understanding-light away at full strength, leaving Intellect isolated and longing.** "It needs a motive and excitation; and this is given by artifice of the Divine Law depriving it when in full vigor of its Understanding Light" (III.1 ¶28). Also: "all but the hallowed memory burning to retrieve" (III.1 ¶26); "So Eurydice was lost to Orpheus and Proserpine by Pluto’s stratagem" (III.1 ¶28). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

26. **The verse Atwood says "divulges the artificial method": a volatile lad is drowned in the Stygian lake, crucified by Lemnos' God, dissolved in a warm womb, penetrated by a spirit, and revived by degrees in gold and silver.** "Take this Arcadian slippery lad that’s apt to fly. And in the glittering Stygian lake, drowned let him die" (III.1 ¶27). Also: "Then in a warm womb placed, his taint dissolve, Whose dropping limbs a spirit shall devolve, To him and penetrate" (III.1 ¶27); "Dead by degrees, shall bring to life anew All clad in robes of gold and silver hue." (III.1 ¶27). *(interpreted; figures not decoded; QUOTED: Palingenius, endorsed)*

27. **Repeated deaths: each life dissolves, corrupts and rises improved in the next. Even the heroic agent must die.** "So each succeeding life must die, as transplanted in the next, it dissolves, corrupts, and rises into a better form" (III.1 ¶30). Also: "But Achilles, too, must die and suffer, as was predestinated, before the fatal gates" (III.1 ¶30); "how otherwise should that which is sown be quickened unless it die?" (III.1 ¶30). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

28. **Begin with a small representative portion, not the whole: "Ramus, non arbor".** "Ramus, non arbor --- The bough, not the whole tree, is to be taken" (III.1 ¶32). Also: "with a certain portion of gold, as a representative of universal gold" (III.1 ¶32, Plotinus). *(stated; ATWOOD with QUOTED: Plotinus, Anaxagoras)*

29. **One stage is getting past the senses so that one "sees within". Even then the tree of life is still to be found.** "him who has already passed the turbulent waters of the senses’ medium and sees within" (III.1 ¶34). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

30. **The "stone of Fire" (Antimony) is cooked with the microcosm's own fire. Where cosmic fire stops, the microcosm's fire starts a new generation.** "our stone of Fire (which is Antimony) ought to be boiled and maturated with the corporeal fire of the microcosm" (III.1 ¶37). Also: "in the preparation of Antimony consists the Key of Alchemy" (III.1 ¶37). *(stated; QUOTED: Basil Valentine, endorsed)*

31. **Three things are needed: the key, the method of using it ("Manual Operation"), and the strength to turn it. The key opens both the first and the last gate.** "He who obtains this key and knows the method, which is called Manual Operation, by which to use it, and hath strength to turn the same" (III.1 ¶37). Also: "this opens the first gate, this will also unlock the last, which leads into the palace of the king" (III.1 ¶37). *(stated; QUOTED: Kirchringius, endorsed)*

**G. Conjunction, mutual death, reunion (¶40–¶50)**

32. **The Passive Understanding, gained by contemplating itself, directs the analysis and supplies most of the means.** "The mode of analysis however is directed, and the means for the most part provided by the Passive Understanding gotten in transcendental contemplation of herself" (III.1 ¶40). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

33. **Both the spiritual agent and the spiritual patient must be prepared. They must be equal, like natures, and each needs the other.** "Both therefore have to be prepared --- the spiritual agent and the spiritual patient" (III.1 ¶41). Also: "It is not lawful therefore, in this work, to conjoin unlike natures; but, in order to bettering in the offspring equal Spirits are allied" (III.1 ¶41); "both need the help of the other, for the precepts demand a medium" (III.1 ¶41). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

34. **Two "Argent vives" are put to death together.** "it behooves us to mortify two Argent vives together" (III.1 ¶43). Also: "the Argent vive of Auripigment and the Oriental Argent vive of magnesia" (III.1 ¶43). *(stated; QUOTED: Hermes; that they are the agent/patient pair is interpreted)*

35. **The Rational Efficient finds the defect of its first conception and dissolves the bond of the natural life.** "it proceeds even to a dissolution of the Vital Bond, continually imaging its revelation in act" (III.1 ¶47). Also: "the Rational Efficient, armed with a bright intelligence, discovers the evil of its first conception, now appearing manifold within the veil" (III.1 ¶47). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

36. **Sol and Luna are brought through anguish into death until the compound returns to the centre of the will. It then hungers for a body, Sol (the soul) is given to it, and they conceive a body. Atwood: mutual death of agent in patient, then reunion "under another law".** "bring them through an anguish into death, and bruise that composed magical body so long until it becomes again that which it was before in the center of the will" (III.1 ¶48). Also: "enter I even to the cross in the Fourth Form, there you have Sol and Luna together" (III.1 ¶48); "then it becometh magical and hungry after nature. It is a longing in the eternal desire, and would fain have a body" (III.1 ¶48); "by death and contrition of the agent in the patient, and vice versa, the old life is finally crucified" (III.1 ¶49). *(stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme, glossed by ATWOOD; "Fourth Form" not explained)*

37. **Summary: a genuine rational analysis, repeated many times, changes the Vital Spirit until the whole natural-born constitution dissolves. That dissolution is the condition for new life.** "the object was to produce an alteration in the Vital Spirit and that this was operated by a true Rational Analysis, which, repeatedly passed through, leads on to a dissolution of the whole natural born hypostasis" (III.1 ¶50). Also: "is the condition proper to induce a new life and growth into consciousness" (III.1 ¶50). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

38. **The "metaphysical body of gold" is joined with a spirit of its own kind, and the two are worked on each other until one conceives by the other.** "joining it with a spirit that is consanquineous and proper to it, and circulating these two natures one upon the other, until one have conceived by the other" (III.1 ¶50). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

39. **The serpents allegory.** First labour: tie the two in a love knot and shut them in the "Arabian Caraha". Second, harder labour: besiege them with the fire of nature, every outlet closed, and continue patiently. Then strengthen the fire until the Phoenix appears, and feed the Phoenix with the father's fire and the mother's ether. "Thou must incamp against them with the fire of nature, and be sure thou dost bring thy line round about. Circle them in, and stop all avenues, that they find no relief." (III.1 ¶50). Also: "tie them in a love knot and shut them up in the Arabian Caraha" (III.1 ¶50); "Continue this siege patiently" (III.1 ¶50); "Feed this bird with the fire of his father and the ether of his mother; for the first is meat, the second is drink" (III.1 ¶50); "Be sure to understand this secret; for fire feeds not well unless it be first fed." (III.1 ¶50). *(sequence stated; meaning interpreted; QUOTED: Cornelius Agrippa's ingenious disciple)*

**H. Dissolution, putrefaction, mortification (¶52–¶63)**

40. **Spirit washes the soul. The three stay together until the body dissolves and putrefies, then spirit and soul rejoin "In the extreme or last heat".** "Until the noble body is dissolved, and putrefy and separate from them;" (III.1 ¶52). Also: "Then after some time the spirit and soul come together In the extreme or last heat" (III.1 ¶52). *(sequence stated in the verse; QUOTED: Aquarium Sapientum, which Atwood calls "the constant doctrine")*

41. **The light of faith enters the natural-born life, meets the self-willed Identity and is stopped. Good and evil contend; for a time the opposing side prevails and the light is eclipsed and dissolved.** "the contest of good and evil commences in the soul" (III.1 ¶53). Also: "moving at length penetrating to meet the self-willed Identity within, is arrested" (III.1 ¶53); "an eclipsation of the light takes place, and a dissolution of its body" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

42. **The one ethereal life-substance splits into three: soul, spirit, body; paternal, maternal, proceeding; sulfur, mercury, salt. The image is Typhon scattering Osiris.** "the ethereal hypostasis is divided against itself and brought to a separation even as these three" (III.1 ¶53). Also: "Typhon killed Osiris, his uterine brother, and scattered his members to the four winds, and usurped his rightful throne; but Isis, recollecting, hides them in a chest" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD; item-by-item mapping open)*

43. **The soul (sulfur) is displaced and floats on the ethereal waters, which soften the dominant sensual life and ready it for returning reason.** "is carried aloft to float upon the ethereal waters" (III.1 ¶53). Also: "made to feel the want of the light it had rejected" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

44. **Catch and fix the volatile part as it rises, so that it stays instead of flying off.** "Take, my son, the flying bird and drown it flying" (III.1 ¶53). Also: "not by flying away indeed but truly by forbearing to fly" (III.1 ¶53). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Hermes)*

45. **The fiery agent is deliberately allowed to grow and show itself fully, and is mortified only when the time is ripe.** "this fire is suffered to grow profusely" (III.1 ¶56). Also: "his hair was suffered to grow whilst yet he tarried in Ethiopia" (III.1 ¶56). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

46. **The Efficient reduces the body of Light (Osiris) to a vapour, "the Four Winds", which condenses at the top of the vessel and runs down continually.** "reducing the foreign body of Light, which is Osiris, to a mere vapor, called by the philosophers, on account of its origin, the Four Winds" (III.1 ¶56). Also: "condensing together at the top of the vessel in the form of drops, runs down continually, day and night, without ceasing" (III.1 ¶56). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

47. **The two contending principles destroy each other and, cooked in their own venom, become a Fifth Essence.** "the two dragons do bite one another very cruelly" (III.1 ¶58). Also: "being decocted totally in their own venom, are turned into a Fifth Essence" (III.1 ¶58); "Sal gave Sulfur a mortal wound, out of which, instead of blood, came forth, as it were, most white milk" (III.1 ¶56, Sendivogius). *(interpreted; QUOTED: French adept; Sendivogius)*

48. **The unclean Instrument is brought back "through the natural Alembic" and then gives the work its force and completeness. Dissolution removes the Dragon's blackness from the threefold nature.** "take away the cloud from the water, the blackness from the sulfur, and by dissolution thou shalt obtain a triumphant gift" (III.1 ¶61, Hermes). Also: "The Dragon dwells in all the threefold nature, and his houses are the darkness and blackness that is in them" (III.1 ¶61). *(Instrument's return stated, ATWOOD; Hermes interpreted)*

49. **Gold is dissolved into a mineral water by heat and moisture that art brings about.** "Ryght so to water minerall reduced ys our gold" (III.1 ¶62). Also: "Wyth heat and moisture by Crafte occasionate Wyth congelation of the Spyrite" (III.1 ¶62). *(stated; QUOTED: Ripley)*

50. **The connate spirit subdues the self-willed agent, and Isis reconciles the opposites** (Agent 28; Result 11).

**I. Stripping the Lion; the Green Lion regimen (¶64–¶65)**

51. **The Orient Animal is stripped of its skin (its "garment of Light") by the Hand, not by violence.** "For the Orient Animal must be stripped of his skin, not with arrows or clubs, but with the Hand, as Adepts say" (III.1 ¶64). Also: "the whole garment of Light must be dissected, shorn, and the signal of victory be heroically transferred" (III.1 ¶64). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Adepts; ATWOOD)*

52. **Feed the Lion with Sol and Luna, enclose them cleanly and keep them in gentle heat until they unite.** "Whan thow hast they Lyon with Sol and Luna well fedd, And layd them clenly in their Bedd; An easie heate they may not misse, Till each the other well can kisse;" (III.1 ¶65). *(interpreted; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

53. **When the skin forms, draw off the Lion's Blood and feed it to the King after he rises.** "Than mus thow draw from thence away, A right good secret withouten any nay: Wych must serve to doe thee good’ For yt ys the Lyons Blood:" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "And therewith must be the King fedd, When he ys risen from the dead:" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

54. **Keep the fire gentle through the black stage. After the white separation raise it one degree until the matter is dry, then remove the residue and bed the "young Chylde".** "Take heede yow move hym not with yre, But keepe hym in an easy fyre;" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Your fire one degree increasing; Untill yow well may se thereby, Your matter to grow very dry:" (III.1 ¶65); "The excrements be tane away; Prepare a Bed most bryght and shine For to lodge this young Chylde in:" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; "Bed" = vessel interpreted; same source)*

55. **Imbibition (giving the dried matter drink) is the concealed key, "the whole subtill warke".** Add a portion of "Lyons sweate", grind, seal, dry, and repeat, drying up each dose before the next. "Thus do olde Fathers hide it from a Clearke, Because in it consisteth the whole subtill warke;" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "For Philosophers of tyme old, The secret of Imbibition never out tould;" (III.1 ¶65); "A portion of your Lyons sweate Must be given it for to eate:" (III.1 ¶65); "But evermore looke that you dry Up all hys drinke, that none lye by," (III.1 ¶65). *(steps stated; imbibition = giving drink interpreted; same source)*

56. **Schedule: six imbibitions, eight days apart, with a seventh as rest. At the ninth, seal for six weeks under tempered heat until black turns white.** "Six tymes thy Imbibtions make, The seaventh that Saboath’s rest betake: Eight dayes twixt ilke day of the six, To dry up moist and make it fix;" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Then at the nynth tyme thy Glasse up seale, And let him stand six weeks each deale: With his heate tempered so right, That Blackness passed he may grow white;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

57. **Do not ferment at the white. Continue to red, then prepare a ferment of pure gold.** "Which if thow wilt Ferment for Whyte, Thereby thow gainst noe greate prifitt; For I assure thee thow needest not dred, To proceede with fire till all be Redd;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

58. **Prepare the ferment by repeating the opening procedure separately: Luna alone with the Lion's blood for white, Sol alone for red. Feed a white King with Luna and a red one with Sol.** "Worke Luna by her selfe alone, With the blood of the Greene Lyon: As earst thow didst in the begining, And of three didst make one thing," (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Of Sol for Redd, of Luna for White, Each by himself let worke tight;" (III.1 ¶65); "If he be of Collour White, Feed hym than with Luna bright; If his flesh be perfect Red, Than with the Sun he must be fedd," (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

59. **Add the ferment as "one fourth parte" to the Magnet, joined warm, then seal and circulate through black, white and red.** "Your Ferment one fourth parte must be, Into your Magnet made evenly, And joyne hem warme and not cold," (III.1 ¶65). Also: "For raw to ripe you may be bold Have disagreement soe have heate and cold:" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; base of the fraction open; same source)*

60. **Multiplication: keep ferment continually in the fire so the Medicine keeps increasing and the work need not be restarted.** "I say to thee that in noe fashion, It’s so well Multeplied as with continuall Firmentation:" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Therefor in the fyre keepe Firment alway, That thy Medicine augment mayst aye;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

**J. Binding Saturn; producing the First Matter (¶66–¶70)**

61. **The Opposive Principle draws in the other faculties, revels with them, becomes sated, and is then more easily trapped, as drunken Saturn was bound by his son on a goddess's advice.** "As it is told of Saturn, likewise, that he was inebriated when he was bound in fetters by his son; and by the advice of the goddess, too, according to Orpheus, the subtle stratagem was contrived." (III.1 ¶66). Also: "rapidly attracting them when it is freed, and reveling with which, it becomes satiated and more easily ensnared" (III.1 ¶66). *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

62. **When the Will is sated and asleep, bind it: the Intellect cuts it off.** "Saturn, with honey of the bees produced, Sunk in ebriety; fast bind the god." (III.1 ¶67). Also: "his desires are more than satisfied, and, as the image runs, from the effect he sleeps" (III.1 ¶68). *(stated via Atwood's gloss in ¶68; verse QUOTED: unnamed)*

63. **The Will's strength, drawn out, is sown again. It produces contending powers that the Stone repeatedly destroys and raises again. From the blood of these battles the "Bath of Diana" is prepared.** "drawing forth all his brazen strength, plants it in the newly-furrowed soil, whence springs another armament" (III.1 ¶68). Also: "contending with each other for the self-same Stone, are by it once more annihilated and again raised up" (III.1 ¶68); "So the Bath of Diana is prepared out of the blood of many battles, where the innocents suffer for the guilty" (III.1 ¶68). *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

64. **Know the parts of the Mercury, remove its excess, and enliven it with true sulfur. Then it can open gold and re-solidify both into a healing Essence.** "He then who knows the parts of Mercury And can it superfluities decrease And with true sulfur it can vivify" (III.1 ¶69). Also: "May gold unlock and after recongeal Both to an Essence which all griefs canheal." (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; terms not glossed; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

65. **The fountain washes the "king" and raises him, and he receives a heavenly virtue that stays once fixed.** "with which our noble king Is wshed ad carried to the mountain tops, Where he the virtue of the Heavens receives, Which never after him, when fixed, leaves." (III.1 ¶69). *(interpreted; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

66. **An immortal soul is imprisoned in "Saturn". Untie it, and a shining vapour arises, "our Moon".** "in Saturn there is hid A soul immortal which in prison lies, Untie its fetters" (III.1 ¶69). Also: "then shall arise A Vapour shining, like pearl orient, Which is our Moon and sparkling Firmament" (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

67. **The First Matter is produced by setting the originating Source free.** "the First Matter of the adepts said to be generated and produced by an emancipation of the Fontal Source; and this is Diana" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

(III.1 ¶10). *(interpreted: the text does not call it a sign; ATWOOD)*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Errors to avoid in working on the inner life: taking dreams for revelation, instinct for insight, unconsciousness for the goal, and leaving the spirit passive.** "if they had mistaken dreams for revelations, instinct for intellectual vision, and insensibility for the highest good" (III.1 ¶8). Also: "left Nature to dream on and take her rest without exerting a thought to probe or prosper her ability" (III.1 ¶8). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

2. **Judging spiritual causes from outward effects (licking "the shell") cannot succeed** (Principles 3). "as long as men continue to lick the shell after their fashion, presuming to judge of hidden celestial things which are shut up in the closet of the matter" (III.1 ¶8). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

3. **Mesmerists reach the vestibule but lack the knowledge to amend "the Thing"** (Procedure 6).

4. **Ordinary life imparted gives ordinary results.** "If, ten, we go out at once to throw our common lives to common lives, what wonder we have only common results?" (III.1 ¶9). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

5. **Beginners wander among errors before gaining experimental knowledge. Success needs a right, rectifying spirit from the start.** "he wanders awhile in the region of chimeras" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "investigation, once begum in a right rectifying spirit, enters" (III.1 ¶13); "the discovery is difficult, and reputed tedious by many who have spared no labor either of body or mind in the research" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

6. **Seeking what is beyond nature's strength; words without experience.** "Seek not for that in nature which is an effect beyond her strength" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "Let not any one imagine either that we can be satisfied with mere words, who rather exact documents proved by experience" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; QUOTED: the Alchemists; Basil Valentine)*

7. **Lack of a right mind, of the conditions or of the order; a "pusillanimous uncongenial soul"** (Operator 6).

8. **Natural intellect cannot purify the phantastic spirit.** "But the natural Intellect cannot do this" (III.1 ¶20). *(stated; QUOTED: Synesius)*

9. **Free will can be a foe** (Operator 8).

10. **If phantasy prevails it turns the whole inner life to folly.** "the phantasy, if suffered to prevail, will convert all to her own folly in the internal life" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

11. **An impure, that is private rather than universal, motive. Impure reason must be sent back and reworked.** "if it be not pure, in other words, if the motive be not universal, it must be returned, to work, and resolve, and meditate, and prove" (III.1 ¶32). Also: "if the reason be but pure, it will penetrate according to its purity, and gather growth" (III.1 ¶32). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

12. **Finding the true Separator is the greatest difficulty. The tree of life stays hidden even from one who sees within.** "To find the true Separator is described, in fact, as the greatest difficulty" (III.1 ¶32). Also: "The tree of life covered over, indeed, with the dark oblivion of this natural outbirth, is latent and difficult to find" (III.1 ¶34). *(stated; link to the hidden tree interpreted; ATWOOD)*

13. **Knowing the matter, fire and furnace is not enough. Adepts failed for years until they found the singular key; missing the door wastes everything.** "experimenting even after he had attained a general knowledge of the matter and method of its use" (III.1 ¶37). Also: "had both fire and furnace indicated to him by Abraham the Jew, wandered in the wilderness of uncertainty for three several years" (III.1 ¶37); "I was entrusted with the Key of Power, by a secret revelation from Almighty God" (III.1 ¶37, Madathan); "if you miss the door, all your course will be in error" (III.1 ¶37, Kirchringius). *(stated; ATWOOD and QUOTED sources)*

14. **Imposing one's own blind purpose without divine illumination, instead of patiently setting conditions and inquiring. Sophistry, avarice and ignorance do not lead.** "he who presumes without the revelation of the Divine Light to introduce his own blind purpose, instead of conditionating and inquiring patiently, will be in danger of falling into infinite snares" (III.1 ¶38). Also: "Sophistry, it will be observed therefore is no leader in this Art, or avarice, or ignorance," (III.1 ¶38). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

15. **The divine Separator must not be given to the unworthy** (Agent 19).

16. **Power without Wisdom destroys; intellect without "the Hands" cannot act.** "the prophetic power unfolds the simplicity of truth and takes away the variety of that which is false; but the arrow-darting power exterminates everything, furious and wild" (III.1 ¶45). Also: "Intellect is therefore useless for the purposes of action when deprived of the subserviency of the Hands" (III.1 ¶45). *(stated; ATWOOD with QUOTED: Proclus, Synesius)*

17. **Seeking the Mystery in the outward spirit gives only an imperfect product.** "if you would seek the Mystery, seek it not in the outward spirit; you will there be deceived" (III.1 ¶48). Also: "in this world you will find only the drossy child, that is altogether imperfect" (III.1 ¶48); "You must seek in another world for the pure child that is without spot" (III.1 ¶48). *(stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme)*

18. **At the critical point of dissolution, too strong a fire would cut the Spirit from the body and the soul would leave; a dissolved element might also escape. Constant care is needed.** "by force of too strong a fire" (III.1 ¶55). Also: "Unremitting care and attention are enjoined at this critical juncture" (III.1 ¶55); "lest either of the dissolute elements should escape from its legitimate attraction" (III.1 ¶55). *(stated; ATWOOD citing Lully)*

19. **The rising light must keep a trace of the body, or the soul is lost** (Principles 22).

20. **The dissolving agent is "the greatest poison". While unresolved, in its natural evil state, it does no good.** "when this poison is resolved into a subtle water, it coagulates our Mercury into most pure silver" (III.1 ¶61, Maria). Also: "no good can come until it is overtaken and resolved" (III.1 ¶61). *(stated; ATWOOD and QUOTED: Maria)*

21. **In the dark stage, the tempter and original Sin show themselves in full** (Operator 13). "there is the tempter Evil of the Son of Man made manifest" (III.1 ¶60). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

22. **The Divine Light suffers violent restraint, opposition and overturning as it passes through the will (Typhon as Seth, Bebo).** "importing a certain violent, forcible restraint, contrariety, and subversion" (III.1 ¶63). Also: "all which Osiris, i.e., the Divine Light, suffers in passing through the voluntary axle in the regeneration" (III.1 ¶63). *(stated; ATWOOD citing Plutarch)*

23. **The Lion is not common quicksilver; working with that fails.** "For they weene that our Lyon ys Common Quicksilver, but truly they miss:" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "Therefore leave off ere thou begin, Till thow know better what we meane;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

24. **Imbibition must keep a middle course: too wet lengthens the work, too dry kills the Child.** "Wherefore the meane to hold is best, Twixt overmoyst and too much rost [roft];" (III.1 ¶65). Also: "For if yow make hym drink too free, The longer will your workeing be, And yf you let hym be too dry, Than for thirst your Child may dye;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

25. **Imitators who knew only how to make Magnesia found nothing at projection.** "Soe many men thought they had had perfeccion, But they found nothing in their Projeccion:" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

26. **The Lion's pursuit deceives and ruins most. The unlearned should desist, and God may withdraw the blessing.** "For hym to hunt that ys a prety wyle, Yet by hys Craft he doth most Folke beguile, And hem devour and leave hem full of care," (III.1 ¶65). Also: "But if so be that thow be lay, And understond not what I say, Keepe Councell then and leve thy Toy," (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; same source)*

27. **The mind's other faculties are drawn away into the Opposive Principle.** "the associates are taken to signify the other faculties of the mind originally attendant on this, but which are drawn away afterwards into the vortex of the Opposive Principle" (III.1 ¶66). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

28. **The Will, unrestrained by reason, spreads through the lower faculties and indulges itself.** "For the Saturnian Will, being allowed to revel without limitation or rational restraint, throughout te subordinate faculties, becomes intoxicated" (III.1 ¶68). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

29. **The First Matter is a Light that blinds anyone who approaches without reason.** "that refulgent Light which eclipses every other light but that of its proper Reason, and strikes the irrational intruder blind" (III.1 ¶70). *(stated as description; its reading as a danger interpreted; ATWOOD)*

## The result

1. **Intellectual illumination from the divinity within, given when the right conditions are provided.** "that Intellectual Ilumination which proceeds, when the conditions are duly offered, from the Divinity within" (III.1 ¶5). Also: "a power of purifying and informing the Mind much more beneficial and lasting than this pertaining to the mortal body" (III.1 ¶11). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

4. **Self-knowledge: knowing where one comes from, what for, and what one is allied to.** "that man may know himself; whence, what for, and whereto, he is allied" (III.1 ¶23). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

6. **The earthly is raised into the celestial, and the celestial into the divine.** "the terrestrial is bettered in the celestial, and the celestial, descending and overcoming, is conceived into the divine" (III.1 ¶30). Also: "convert it to the purest spiritual extreme" (III.1 ¶30). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

7. **A permanent "body", changed from a corporeal to a spiritual state.** "even to the order of bodies permanent, being changed from a corporeal to a spiritual extreme" (III.1 ¶41). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

8. **The new life from mutual death and reunion is the Quintessence, the Mercury and the Elixir of Life. With the Divine light it draws, it becomes the philosophers' living gold, transmuting and multiplying.** "which life is a very real and pure Quintessence, the Mercury so much sought after, even the Elixir of Life" (III.1 ¶49). Also: "needs only the corroborative virtue of the Divine light which it draws, in order to become the living gold of the philosophers, transmuting and multiplicative" (III.1 ¶49); "So the Will springs up in Paradise with fair golden fruits." (III.1 ¶48, Boehme). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

9. **The Stone is a principle of regeneration that reverses the vital order: the sensible becomes hidden, and the hidden universal reason of life becomes manifest and powerful.** "this miraculous principle of regeneration; by which the relationships of the vital elements are exchanged" (III.1 ¶50). Also: "the sensible medium, which in this present birth is dominant, being made occult; and the occult supernatural reason of life, which is catholic, becoming manifest in self-evidence and power" (III.1 ¶50). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

10. **Nature placed on the horizon of Eternity, end joined to beginning.** "thou hast placed nature in the horizon of Eternity" (III.1 ¶50). Also: "Unite the end to the beginning as the flame is united to the coal" (III.1 ¶50). *(stated; QUOTED: Cornelius Agrippa's ingenious disciple)*

11. **The opposed principles end reconciled, circulating together in balance, with Typhon gradually mastered.** "the opposive principles are, through her artful intercession, finally reconciled, and remain together, circulating with her in equilibriate accord" (III.1 ¶63). Also: "tempered by the benign offices of Isis, he, Typhon, is likewise gradually enthralled" (III.1 ¶63). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

12. **The resolved poison fixes "our Mercury" into pure silver. The purged stone reaches whiteness as a powerful Elixir.** "pass fro’ the darknes of purgatory to lyght Of Paradyce, in whytenes Elixir of great might" (III.1 ¶62). Also: "Rejoicing in the beauty and whytenes of his wife" (III.1 ¶62); Maria (Impediments 20). *(Maria stated; Ripley interpreted)*

"And Circle all till yt be wonne, By passing degrees every each one: Both black and whyte, and also redd, Than of the Fire heere have noe dread;" (III.1 ¶65). *(stated; QUOTED: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon)*

14. **With Original Sin overcome, a New Life arises whose lasting virtue is the Corner Stone, the first material foundation of the Art.** "the New Life thence arises whose quintessential virtue, imperishable and perpetually victorious, is the Corner Stone or first Material foundation of the Hermetic Art" (III.1 ¶66). Also: "The evil of the Original Sin being overcome by so many subtle stratagems" (III.1 ¶66). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

15. **The Identical Spirit rises pure from its original element, subject only to its own Law.** "the Identical Spirit arises, pure, bright, and contrite, from its primaeval eleemtn, and free in legal subordination only to its own perfect Law" (III.1 ¶68). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

16. **A healing Essence; a spring of wealth and a Tree of Life.** "May gold unlock and after recongeal Both to an Essence which all griefs canheal." (III.1 ¶69). Also: "Lo! Here a spring of wealth, a Tree of Life," (III.1 ¶69). *(stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus)*

18. **Souls pass from forgetfulness and darkness to the freedom of the liberated Will in this life. ## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Man's natural intelligent spirit corresponds to the universal Ether. Each is an outer vestibule around a hidden inner light.** "for what the natural intelligent Spirit is in man, that Ether is in the universe" (III.1 ¶5). Also: "the commonly diffused Ether is as a vestibule or vehicle in respect of the universal soul of the world" (III.1 ¶5). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

2. **Imitate Nature in art.** "Naturam in primisimitabere in arte, magister." (III.1 ¶1). *(our rendering; QUOTED: Tractatus Aureus, motto)*

3. **Spiritual causes are known only by direct experience on their own ground, not by inference from outward effects. The ancients worked "a priori", from inward principles, not by inductive fact-gathering.** "How then can we hope for an understanding of spiritual causes without entering in upon the proper ground of their experience?" (III.1 ¶8). Also: "Is not experience the basis of true knowledge, and rational experiment the proper road to attain it?" (III.1 ¶8); "return with her instruments to work as formerly on the a priori ground" (III.1 ¶8). *(first stated; a-priori reading interpreted; ATWOOD)*

4. **Theory precedes practice; use the mind as well as the hands.** "in the Hermetic art, theory ought to precede practice" (III.1 ¶9). Also: "be sure to add reason to thy experience, and to employ thy mind as well as thy hands" (III.1 ¶9, Vaughan). *(stated; QUOTED: philosophers, endorsed)*

5. **What passes from operator to subject is life, and its quality decides the result.** "That much depends on the quality of life imparted, general observation teaches" (III.1 ¶9). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

6. **Nature must be helped beyond her ordinary course, by ingenuity and industry.** "you must help her, that she may exceed her common course, or all is to no purpose" (III.1 ¶13). Also: "for the Mercury of the Wise comes not but by help of ingenuity and industry" (III.1 ¶13); "Lay the line to thy thoughts and examine all patiently, and infer from experience" (III.1 ¶13). *(stated; QUOTED: the Alchemists, endorsed)*

7. **Like assimilates like** (Procedure 17).

8. **A freed individual will acting on its own vehicle transmutes it from dull impurity into clear universal intelligence: "the magic action of mind in her own spheres".** "the efficient force of an individual freed will upon the vehicle of its motive cause, separating, refining, and transmuting it from an impure, dull consistency to the clear light of universal intelligence" (III.1 ¶20). Also: "the magic action of mind in her own spheres" (III.1 ¶20). *(stated; ATWOOD reporting the adepts)*

9. **Nothing is discovered unless the matter is decomposed, and nothing perfected without the whole "introversion".** "nothing is discovered except the matte be decomposed, and because it is not perfected unless the whole introversion is passed through" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "not corporally or imprudently sought after, but is through patience and Wisdom to be discovered according to their causes and their occult operation" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

10. **Whichever mode prevails converts the inner life to itself. Reason, prevailing, acts as a ferment and raises everything into its light (the adepts' "Head Stone").** "this Reason gaining the ascendancy, would gather all up, as a ferment, into the superior essence and traction of her own Light" (III.1 ¶21). Also: "abundantly celebrating the virtues of their Head Stone" (III.1 ¶21). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

11. **Knowledge of the universal Cause comes from experiment inside the individual; inner and cosmic processes cooperate.** "by the microcosmic experiment into It, the knowledge if the macrocosmic Cause also was derived" (III.1 ¶23). *(interpreted; ATWOOD)*

12. **Reason needs illumination from God to be free of error.** "reason, unless it be illuminated, is not free from error, and Light is not given to reason except God impart it" (III.1 ¶23). Also: "human reason, as a ray of light, reflected apart from its originating focus, is halting and impotent" (III.1 ¶23). *(stated; QUOTED: the Magian, endorsed; ATWOOD)*

13. **The vital spirit cannot be fundamentally improved until the first medial life is dissolved.** "Nothing is done radically to meliorate the Vital Spirit previous to this dissolution of the first medial life" (III.1 ¶28). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

14. **Law of spiritual generation: the parent is bettered in the offspring, up to a fourth or fifth generation.** "it is the Law especial of spiritual generation that the parent is bettered in the offspring, even to the fourth generation, or fifth, if this happily should be attained" (III.1 ¶30). *(stated; ATWOOD; compare the "fifth degree" of grafting, III.1 ¶9)*

15. **Knowledge and the working cause both arise within. They are one in principle but treated as two in practice "for the sake of offspring".** "It is from within that the knowledge springs together with the true efficient" (III.1 ¶40). Also: "which are indeed one in principle, but in their practical operation and for the sake of offspring are distinguished and separately represented in the Art" (III.1 ¶40). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

16. **Keep the refined universal reason distinct from the coarse ether and confused understanding. Nothing gives a virtue it lacks, though what it has can be magnified.** "distinguish, in our conception, the pure from the impure, the rectified spirit of universal reason and its intelligence from the gross ether and perplexed understanding" (III.1 ¶43). Also: "For there cannot come of any thing that virtue which it has not; though that which it has indeed may be improved and magnified." (III.1 ¶43). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

17. **Contemplation and action alternate: when the higher eyes see, the lower are closed.** "there are two pairs of eyes, and it is requisite that the pair which are beneath should be closed when the pair which are above them perceive" (III.1 ¶45). *(stated; QUOTED: Synesius)*

18. **Only with Wisdom and Power joined can the Will descend safely into the region of Power.** "these two concurring, Wisdom with Power in subtle and firm texture of divine splendor and prophetic companionship, the Will may descend in safety to the abodes of Power" (III.1 ¶45). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

19. **The lasting, transmuting union depends on the "first unity" and first love. Without that love the elements never unite inwardly.** "Without this love the elements will never be married; they will never inward and essentially unite; which is the end and perfection of magic" (III.1 ¶50). Also: "you seek an insoluble, miraculous, transmuting, uniting union; but such a tie cannot be without the first unity" (III.1 ¶50); "to create and to transmute essentially and naturally without violence is the proper office of the first power, the first wisdom and the first love" (III.1 ¶50). *(stated; voice UNCLEAR: Atwood or Vaughan)*

20. **The governing rule is the regeneration of light out of darkness: the sense-born spirit is dissolved, then raised into its prototypic form by an affinity that art supplies.** "the regeneration of light out of darkness, of life from death" (III.1 ¶53). Also: "the solution of the sense-born spirit and its subsequent sublimation, by a preponderant affinity artificially endowed, into the transparent glory of its prototypic form" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

21. **Nature is moved by reasoned experiment together with the light of faith, not by theory alone or by mechanical art.** "rational experiment and the light of faith" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

22. **The rising light keeps a "fermental odor of the body", so the soul does not leave altogether.** "a fermental odor of the body" (III.1 ¶53). *(stated; ATWOOD)*

23. **The impure agent is necessary: evil at first, it gives the finished work its force** (Agent 26).

## Open questions

1. **Is the work done within one person, or between persons?** "joining self to self" (III.1 ¶8). *Within one being*: "worked with by itself alone" (¶8); "their own internal being" (¶3); "It is from within that the knowledge springs" (¶40); the Moon as an inner "Passive Intelligence" (¶40). *Between persons*: the comparison with "mesmerizing one another" (¶8); "throw our common lives to common lives" and "quality of life imparted" (¶9); the operator who "never goes through the labors alone" and is aided by a "guardian of the Light" (¶40), and the "mediate occupation" of ¶40; Boehme's "unless one doth entrust another with somewhat" (¶39). The chapter does not decide.

2. **"Passive Subject"** (III.1 ¶9): the recipient in the mesmeric trance (mentioned in the sentence before), or the philosophic subject in general (chapter title, ¶1). Bears on question 1.

3. **"as many representative vessels or forms"** (III.1 ¶9): laboratory vessels, living persons or bodies to which the Spirit is grafted, or a symbolic sense. Note the parallel with "fourth generation, or fifth" (¶30), which the text does not connect.

4. **"this, their Initial Principle"** (III.1 ¶3): the first matter, or the claimed inner observation of occult nature. Both antecedents are in ¶3.

5. **Corrupt motto** (III.1 ¶1): the outer/inner fire contrast is clear, but what agent "perfecit" names is not. It agrees with ¶42–43, which carries the point independently.

6. **"Her winds"** (III.1 ¶6): a slip for "wings" (the Sphinx's body has just been described, the sense is "elevating", and the chunk has other typos), or "winds" as printed.

7. **"the natural inbred chemist"** (III.1 ¶10): an inner agent (the fire of life "bound in man") or Nature personified ("natural", "teaches"). Compare Boehme's "Separator is an artist of great subtlety in them" (¶39).

8. **Eirenaeus' parenthetical glosses** "(i.e., of the ethereal body)", "(of the same body)" (III.1 ¶14): Atwood's insertion or part of the quoted text. If Atwood's, they are her own decoding of "body" as the ethereal body.

9. **"our water" vs "the first water"** (III.1 ¶14): the same, or distinct menstruals (¶14 distinguishes "two first menstruals" from the Third). Also, "it" in "Seek it in the house of Aries" is either the internal Fire or the sulfur/material; Aries, Saturn and Mercury are not explained.

10. **"Salt have lost its savor"** (III.1 ¶20): natural intellect (it follows "But the natural Intellect cannot do this") or a general warning about the agent's potency.

11. **"the vehicle of its motive cause"** (III.1 ¶20): the Ethereal/phantastic Spirit (just discussed) or the body (¶13–14).

12. **"which is the part especial of Intellect"** (III.1 ¶21): the sequestatrix, or the Separation as a whole.

13. **"the sol receives"** (III.1 ¶23): misprint for "soul" (favoured by context), or "sol" as printed.

14. **What is "the true Separator" whose finding is the greatest difficulty** (III.1 ¶32)? (a) The vertical separated Light/Fire/key of ¶37 ("separable" ¶36, "separated" ¶37, the key adepts failed to find); (b) the pure, universal reason just discussed in ¶32; (c) what the hidden tree of ¶32–34 figures. Related: ¶21 calls Mind "the true Separator", and Boehme (¶39) speaks of a "divine Separator" within. The chapter never states the identity across these passages.

15. **"Arcadian slippery lad"** (III.1 ¶27): the volatile vital spirit or impure vapour of life (¶24, ¶28), or the celestial medial life that must die (introduced in ¶26). Compare Hermes' "flying bird" drowned flying (¶53), which the text does not link.

16. **Aeneas leaving the bough in Tartarus** (III.1 ¶30): the agent itself must be given up at the threshold (alongside "Achilles, too, must die"), or a simple parallel of completion.

17. **"true alternative principle"** (III.1 ¶37): "alternative" as altering/transmuting, or as a second principle beside another. Not glossed.

18. **Antimony and the Fire.** Is Basil's "stone of Fire (which is Antimony)" (¶37) the vertical separated Light, or a substance in Basil's own terms? Is Pontanus' "Fire" (¶37) the same as the separated Light (placement only)? The Paracelsians' "fire of life (called Antimony by adepts" (¶10) uses the same name; the text does not connect ¶10 and ¶37.

19. **"mediate occupation" / "guardian of the Light"** (III.1 ¶40): see question 1. Also, the fable helpers (Ariadne, Medea, the Sybil) are not explicitly identified with the Moon/Passive Intelligence; the link is by sequence only.

20. **"Istud est vas Hermetis, quod Stoici occultaverunt, et non est vas nigromaniticum sed est Mesnura Ignis tui"** (III.1 ¶40): untranslated; we read "Mesnura" as *Mensura* (our rendering: the measure of thy fire). Whether the vessel is the Passive Understanding rests on placement only.

21. **Which pair is which?** The two Argent vives (¶43), Sol and Luna (¶44, ¶48), the philosophical Sun and Moon (¶45), and agent and patient (¶41) are all paired just before "These are they which sound the depths together" (¶45). No explicit mapping is given, including which Argent vive, or which of Sun/Moon, is agent and which patient. The Sun = Power and Moon = Wisdom reading of ¶45 is inferred.

22. **"Our stone comes with Light, and with Light it is generated; and then it brings forth the clouds, or darkness"** (III.1 ¶49): a stage order (light, then a dark phase), or a cosmological statement about Nature's middle principle.

23. **"the fire of nature"** (III.1 ¶50): the same as "The fire of the natural life", which alters but cannot perfect (¶43), or a different fire. Note also that "Black Toad" names the serpents' first transformation (¶50) and Typhon during mortification (¶56); the text does not connect them.

24. **Voice of ¶50's closing passage** ("you seek an insoluble, miraculous, transmuting, uniting union"): Atwood's own, or Vaughan's (n56, Lumen de Lumine). No quotation marks.

25. **Salt.** Is Salt the impure necessary Instrument (¶61; Sal mortally wounds Sulfur ¶56), or the body principle of the triad (¶53), cited to stress that all three are needed? Relatedly, "Hermes, alluding to the same" (¶61) refers either to Salt or to the unclean principle generally.

26. **The triad mapping** (III.1 ¶53): term by term (soul = paternal = sulfur; spirit = maternal = mercury; body = proceeding = salt) or loosely parallel. Only sulfur = soul is confirmed.

27. **"until the later prevailing for a period"** (III.1 ¶53): "later" = "latter", i.e. evil prevails for a time (fits the eclipse that follows), or a temporal sense.

28. **"Three souls I had, and all my own; but two Are fled"** (III.1 ¶54): the three principles of ¶53, two departing in the dissolution, or poetic expression of the in-between state.

29. **What "it" is in "Entering it for the dissolution, adepts describe it indeed as the greatest poison"** (III.1 ¶56): the dissolving agent/fire (fits the rest of ¶56), or the abyssal contest of the "metaphysical Embryo" (nearest antecedent).

30. **Are the wrathful Efficient (¶56) and the unclean Instrument (¶61) one principle?** Both are called Typhon and both cause putrefaction; the text does not state the identity.

31. **"it constitutes the natural Alembic, and returned, it constitutes"** (III.1 ¶61): a printing duplication (the returned Instrument constitutes the work's force), or literally that the Instrument also constitutes the natural Alembic.

32. **Leviathan** (III.1 ¶63, no gloss): the self-willed agent that force cannot master ("the hope of him is in vain", fire imagery), or the reconciled invincible power ("sorrow is turned into joy before him", "a king over all the children of Pride").

33. **Orient Animal** (III.1 ¶64): the rebellious Principle or its "identical representative" ("For" links it causally, and "pelle sua leonine" leads into the Lion Hunt), or a separate image. "with the Hand": actual manual operation (compare Kirchringius' "Manual Operation", ¶37, and Basil's "eyes and hand", ¶13), or a figurative agency. Not glossed.

34. **Green Lion details** (III.1 ¶65): (a) whether the Lion's Blood or the Lion itself is "the Kings Father and Mother"; (b) whether "Lyons sweate" is the Lion's Blood drawn off earlier; (c) whether "one fourth parte" is a quarter of the whole or a quarter of the Magnet. Also: is the Green Lion here the same as the wrathful Efficient called Green Lion in ¶56? The name is shared; the text does not state the identity.

35. **Saturn across ¶66–¶70.** Is the soul hidden "in Saturn" whose fetters are untied (Eirenaeus, ¶69) the same Saturn as the bound Saturnian Will (¶66–68), giving two phases (bind the Will, then free the soul within it)? Atwood's "By such a vital and mysterious process" leading to "an emancipation of the Fontal Source" (¶70) joins them, but there is no explicit identification. Helvetius' "Jehovah saturninely placed in the center of the world" (¶70) may be an inner centre ("within most intimately") or a cosmic one.

36. **Diana across the chapter**: "the Doves of Diana" as the signal (¶14, Eirenaeus), "the Bath of Diana" (¶68), and Diana = First Matter (¶70). The text does not connect ¶14 with ¶68–70.

37. **Where Helvetius' words end** (III.1 ¶70): before "Such are a very few of the games", or earlier, in which case the mythic catalogue (Salamander, Aeneas, Hercules) is Atwood's own.
