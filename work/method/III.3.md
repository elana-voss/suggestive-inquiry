# Method material — III.3 Of the Manifestations of the Philosophic Matter

(Chapter heading in the text: "Of the Manifestation of the First Matter, and its Information by Light." (III.3 ¶1))

Format for each point: plain meaning; then the strongest verbatim expression with its ref, and other expressions after "Also"; then *status; voice*. *Ours* means a translation from the chunk readings, not given by the book.

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **One Matter, which is Nature as a single Unknown Identity.** All things have one matter. Experience shows it to be simple. Under every quality and form it stays one identity. Chemists know that water, air, earth and gold are not elements, but they have never found the true element. — "The Matter of all things is One and proved simple in the experience" (III.3 ¶65). Also: "Nature remains one and the same Unknown Identity through all" (III.3 ¶65); "but Her, the true element, they have never found" (III.3 ¶65); "All is identical --- need we repeat it? --- in the Universal Identity" (III.3 ¶67). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **The Matter as Isis: nothing in herself, able to take every form.** At the decisive point the Matter is called Isis, the goddess with a thousand names. Atwood says the many names mean it can understand and receive any Form the Supreme Reason impresses on it. In herself she is no particular thing. She comes before animal, vegetable and mineral and is the mother of all. Her birth and complexion are miraculous. — "Then she is Isis, the Divine I Am, by the Greeks called Myrionymous, or the goddess with a thousand names" (III.3 ¶62). Also: "hereby to denote the capacity with which such a Matter is endowed of understanding, and of being converted to all or any of the Forms or degrees of specific Law" (III.3 ¶62); "As respects herself, she is Nothing" (III.3 ¶62); "her birth, according to the Adepts, is singular and not without a miracle" (III.3 ¶62). — *stated; ATWOOD (citing the Adepts for the miraculous birth)*. "Then" refers back to the extremity of ¶61 (see Procedure A6).

3. **The Rosicrucian Goddess: the First Matter, which looks like poison at first.** All things are generated from this subject. At first sight it looks like poison and bears the name "flying eagle". Its parents are the philosophical Sun and Moon. Atwood introduces the speech as describing the same many-named goddess. — "Though at the first sight thou art poison adorned with the name of the flying eagle; thou are the First Matter: the Seed of Divine benediction, in whose body there is heat and rain" (III.3 ¶63). Also: "I am a Goddess for beauty and extraction, famous, born out of our own proper sea, which compasseth the whole earth, and is ever restless" (III.3 ¶63); "O, most excellent subject! Out of which all things in the world are generated" (III.3 ¶63); "Thy parents are the Sun and Moon (philosophical)" (III.3 ¶63). — *stated; QUOTED: fraternity of the Rosy Cross (via Vaughan, n45), presented by Atwood*

4. **The Dragon, called Mercury, whose husband is philosophical Gold.** The subject speaks as a dragon present everywhere. It names itself the Mercury of the philosophers, the Egg of nature and the Old Dragon. It unites all contraries. Inwardly it is the Light of nature. — "I am called of the philosophers Mercury: my husband is Gold philosophical" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "I am the Egg of nature, known only to the Wise" (III.3 ¶64); "I am the Old Dragon that is present everywhere on the face of the earth" (III.3 ¶64); "I am, within, the Light of nature; I am dark and bright" (III.3 ¶64); "I am the carbuncle of the Sun, a most noble clarified Earth" (III.3 ¶64); "I am well known and yet a mere nothing" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated (the self-identifications); QUOTED: fraternity of the Rosy Cross*

5. **Everywhere, free and despised, yet hidden and given to few.** The subject is in plain sight every day, costs nothing and is held in contempt. Its outward appearance hides it from the wicked, and it is given only to a few. — "this mystery, because of the malice and wickedness of men, is given only to few; notwithstanding it lives and moves every day in the sight of the whole world" (III.3 ¶63). Also: "I am a poisonous dragon, present everywhere, and to be had for nothing" (III.3 ¶64); "hidden from the wicked, because of thy habit and virgin vestures, which are scattered over the whole world" (III.3 ¶63); "mean and of no account in the eyes of the vulgar" (III.3 ¶50); "A matter easy to be found, And yet most hard to come by" (III.3 ¶76); "A secret of secrets pardye, That is most vile and least set by" (III.3 ¶76). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross; Khunrath (n35); Experience and Philosophy*

6. **Wisdom is the substance of the Stone.** Atwood asserts that the divine Wisdom praised in Scripture is the substance of the Stone. That Wisdom was created by God, poured on all his works, given to those who love him, and is the Worker of all things. — "such a Wisdom (shall we not believe it?) was the worthy object of all Hermetic Philosophy, and the miraculous substance of its transmutative Stone" (III.3 ¶50). Also: "For Wisdom, which is the Worker of all things, taught me" (III.3 ¶49); "he hath given her to them that love him" (III.3 ¶48). — *stated; ATWOOD (the scriptural lines are QUOTED: Ecclesiasticus, Solomon)*

7. **The Stone as Ruach Elohim given a body.** The Stone is the Spirit that moved on the waters, conceived and made into a sensible body in the formless matter of the greater world. As "the Son" it is universal, triune and hermaphrodite, and can be perceived by the senses. — "The philosopher’s stone, says the mysterious adeptest, is Ruach Elohim, which moved upon the face of the waters" (III.3 ¶50). Also: "conceived and made body, truly and sensibly, in the virgin womb of the greater world, viz., that Earth which is without form and water" (III.3 ¶50); "universal, triune, hermaphrodite; visible, sensible to hearing, to smell, local and finite" (III.3 ¶50); "setting aside all idea of anything individually human" (III.3 ¶50). — *stated; QUOTED: the mysterious adeptest (Khunrath per n35), cited by Atwood to answer her own question*

8. **Salt, the most ancient principle of the Stone, named Wisdom.** Salt is the Stone's oldest principle. The Salt of Saturn, "the Universal son of Nature", reigns in all things. Salt has been given the name of Wisdom. — "Salt, that most ancient principle of the Stone; whose nucleus in the Decad guard in holy silence" (III.3 ¶50). Also: "The Salt of Saturn, the Universal son of Nature, has reigned, does reign, and will reign naturally and universally in all things" (III.3 ¶50); "not without weighty cause has Salt been dignified with the name of Wisdom" (III.3 ¶50). — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath (n35)*. Whether Salt is the Stone or a part of it: see Open questions.

9. **The Water of the Adepts: the product of the mind's spiritual poles joined at their source.** The famous Water is the miraculous product of the spiritual poles of mind joined at their source. All the Adepts' names refer to it. It is reborn in a body that is visible, tangible, local and finite. — "That was the Water so much magnified by the wise Adepts, the miraculous product of the spiritual poles of mind in sublime conjunction at their source" (III.3 ¶73). Also: "this was their Stilla roris, Lac Virginis, Elixir, Aqua Vitae, Azoth, Prima Materia Lapis et Rebis" (III.3 ¶73); "regenerate in its once assumed body, visible, tangible, and sensible to every sense, local and finite" (III.3 ¶73); "And this shall stand, said that noble clerke, For the Water within our werke" (III.3 ¶74, Norton). — *stated; ATWOOD (verse QUOTED: Norton)*. "That" refers back to the conflux of Altitude and Profundity in the Calx (III.3 ¶72).

10. **Other "Waters" named in the chapter.** The text does not equate these with point 9 or with each other.
    - The Tears of Isis are "that blessed Water". Atwood insists they were real, substantial experience, not vision: "the Tears of Isis, even that blessed Water which nature sheds divinely for the world" (III.3 ¶3); "experience there was present with power and effect in substance to bear it witness" (III.3 ¶3). — *stated; ATWOOD*
    - The whole labour is for one Water that blackens, dissolves, congeals, putrefies and makes things germinate: "labour only for that Water which burns to blackness, dissolves and congeals" (III.3 ¶60); "It is that which putrefies and causes germination" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated properties, identity not given; QUOTED: Synesius*
    - The golden water is drawn from above, because ripe Mercury rises: "Our golden water, says the adept, is not found in wells, nor in profundities, but in higher places" (III.3 ¶17); "for Mercury, being ripe, arises to her superior habitation" (III.3 ¶17). — *stated; QUOTED: the adept (Mercury’s Caducean Rod, n7)*
    - The gold of the Wise is a clear water: "the gold of the Wise to be by no means vulgar gold; but it is a certain water clear and pure, on which is borne the lightning of the Lord" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; QUOTED: the author of Lucerna Salis*
    - The Water of Might carries life and light: "the Water of Myght, Which beareth ingression of Lyfe and Light" (III.3 ¶16). — *stated by the verse; QUOTED: Pierce, the Black Monk*
    - Azoth, "The Universal", can be touched and seen: "With thy hands thou shalt touch, and with thy eyes thou shalt see Azoth! The Universal!" (III.3 ¶9). — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath*. Azoth also appears in Atwood's list of names for the Water (III.3 ¶73).

11. **The starting point of this stage: the purified Vital Spirit.** The chapter begins with the Vital Spirit already purified by sacrifice of everything foreign to it. Its active and passive powers are united, and it is about to generate the fullness it contains. — "the Vital Spirit theurgically purified and freed through sacrifice of all foreign attractions" (III.3 ¶3). Also: "having power active and passive in hypostatic union always about to generate the infinite fullness which it contains and draws" (III.3 ¶3); "carrying along with us the body of our Sphinx, subdued and contrite" (III.3 ¶3). — *stated; ATWOOD*

12. **The vessel is the gross body.** Fire, air and water are worked inside their "earthly vessel", which the text glosses as the gross body. — "contained in one vessel in their earthly vessel, i.e., in their gross body or composition" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "I take them and then I leave them in one alembic, where I decoct and sublime them" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; QUOTED: the Benedictine Monk (Mehung in Vaughan, n14)*. Compare Nature's vessels "of her own ethereally wise construction" (III.3 ¶65). The text does not link the two.

13. **What is sought lies at the centre of every being, hidden by the senses.** Every creature has at its centre a spirit from the world's origin. When the sensual principle dominates, that divine origin is hidden from consciousness. — "The centre of every Being is a spirit from the original of the world" (III.3 ¶52). Also: "And so far we stand even now in the great mystery, in the Mother of all beings" (III.3 ¶52). — *stated; ATWOOD*. In the same vein: "the exemplary Logos is hidden, --- slain from the foundation in the exterminating fiat of our Identity" (III.3 ¶79). — *interpreted (that the Logos is hidden in the individual); ATWOOD*

14. **The matter is impalpable and only dimly conceived at first.** — "That which is impalpable and imperfectly conceived at first" (III.3 ¶80). — *stated; ATWOOD*

15. **One Thing, the first Material Mother.** One confused, dark mass that held all Nature was made first. It is called the first Material Mother, and the four elements were divided out of it. It cannot be destroyed. — "One Thing I made when there was none; A mass confused and darkly clad, That in itself, all Nature had" (III.3 ¶76). Also: "It pleased me the same to call, The first Material Mother of all" (III.3 ¶76); "One tings was employed, Which shall not be destroyed" (III.3 ¶76). — *stated; QUOTED: Experience and Philosophy (Ashmole, n49)*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **The Divine Fiat blesses the reunion and creates the new hypostasis.** When the Soul is drawn back into the Passive Spirit, the Divine Fiat blesses the union. A new hypostasis is created out of the darkness. In the same Fiat the dominance of the senses is subdued. — "the Divine Fiat comes mercifully to bless the union, and a new hypostasis is created out of the darkness to abide" (III.3 ¶10). Also: "when the sensual dominant is occultated in the Divine Fiat and subdued" (III.3 ¶15). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **A heavenly fire, not common fire.** The working fire is "heavenly". It rouses the elemental fire as the matter calls for a fitting Form. No tools or ordinary fire are used. Khunrath names the Fire of Divine love and an inner and outer fire in sympathy with an "Olympic Fire". — "For I have my heavenly fire, which excites and stirs up the elemental one, according as the matter desires a more becoming agreeable Form" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "separated by the Fire of Divine love in the regeneration of the three principles" (III.3 ¶9); "with the internal and external fire in harmonious sympathy with the Olympic Fire" (III.3 ¶9). — *stated; QUOTED: the Benedictine Monk; Khunrath*. The Olympic Fire is not explained.

3. **The Rational Light moves the Spirit, and the Spirit overcomes the corporeal.** Once discovered, the Rational Light sets the Spirit in motion. The Spirit penetrates and overcomes the corporeal, which the text glosses as the dominance of the senses, and absorbs it so that it is glorified. — "The Rational Light, once discovered and set in motion, actuates the Spirit, and the Spirit, in its turn penetrating, overcomes the corporeal, which is the sensual dominant, in the regeneration" (III.3 ¶23). — *stated; ATWOOD*

4. **Desiring fire draws in Light and makes it Water and Blood.** Fire, as desire, draws the Light's virtue into itself and makes it an essence, namely Water. The red fire-form (holding sulphur) and the thin water-form (the desiring tincture) join and become Blood. Its warmth is life, and the Thin Water of Life proceeds from it. — "the property of the fire is a desiring, and thereby it attracteth the virtue of the Light unto itself, and maketh it an essence, viz., Water" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "both of which contracting together into one, are converted into Blood" (III.3 ¶24); "It cometh forth from anguish into the meekness of the light" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme (Turned Eye, n16)*. An interjection of unclear voice notes that common chemistry condenses light into a fluid form.

5. **Intellectual Light, descending from a centre in a magnetic series.** Light comes down from a single centre through a surrounding vapour as a living chain. Because celestial nature, aerial spirit and the soul's aura differ only in degree, Light can unite them and raise them into immortality. — "may be carried by a certain intellectual Light into the supreme horizon, and so swallowed up of immortality" (III.3 ¶27). Also: "the Light, striking in a rapid coruscation from the center to the circumference, depends from the solitary unit through the surrounding vapor, in a vital magnetical series" (III.3 ¶27). — *stated (the union); interpreted (that the series is what makes the union possible); QUOTED: Vaughan, endorsed by Atwood ("has well described")*

6. **The divine Mind (Poemander) and the Word.** Poemander is the Mind of God and is identified with the Light. The Word that comes from it, joins Nature and separates the elements is the Son of God. The Mind is always present and teaches whoever holds it in mind. It enters the pure and keeps out evil. — "I am that Light, the Mind, they God, who am before that moist nature, that appeareth out of the darkness, and that bright and lightful Word from the Mind is the Son of God" (III.3 ¶54). Also: "I am, quoth he, Poemander, the Mind of the Great Lord" (III.3 ¶52); "Have me again in thy mind, and whatsoever thou wouldst learn, I will teach thee" (III.3 ¶52); "being the Porter and Door-keeper, I shut up the entrance of evil" (III.3 ¶57). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander in Hermes' account, offered by Atwood as testimony*

7. **Wisdom carries Reason and the Demiurgic Fire.** Wisdom carries the universal Reason and bears and measures the creative Fire. Sense-bound conception hides that Fire. — "For this Wisdom is the vehicle of the Catholic Reason in Identity, the bearer and measure of the Demiurgic Fire" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*

8. **The Fire of nature and the Fire of Life.** What the Matter brings forth by the Fire of nature, lawfully conceived, is Orus. The Rosicrucians call the mystery the knowledge God breathed into nature by his Word: the Fire of Life. — "that which she brings forth by the Fire of nature lawfully conceived, is Orus, the Philosophic Sun" (III.3 ¶62). Also: "a knowledge which God Almighty, by his Word, breathed into nature; a miraculous power, the blessed Fire of Life" (III.3 ¶63). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶62); QUOTED: Rosy Cross (¶63)*

9. **The subject's own water and fire.** The subject holds its own dissolving and combining agents and supplies both male and female faculties. — "My water and my fire dissolve and compound" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "I give thee faculties both male and female, and the powers both of heaven and earth" (III.3 ¶64). — *interpreted (as the work's agents); QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

10. **Sulphur is the Word of God, acting independently of human will.** The active member of the alchemical Three is the Word of God in the regeneration. — "The first, I the regeneration, is the Word of God independent without all human will, miraculously conceived and confessed Divine in the new birth" (III.3 ¶77). — *stated; ATWOOD* (the full triad is under Identities).

11. **The Divine Will and the spirit in the Darkness, in the cosmogonies.** In the account of creation, the Divine Will alone produces itself into material form. A spirit's breath stirs the inert mass, so that like parts separate from unlike and attraction begins. — "the Divine Will, then alone operating, says the Kabalistic Interpreter, produces itself into a material form and recreation" (III.3 ¶43). Also: "the breath of the tremendous spirit that dwells in the Darkness had not gone forth and put the lifeless mass in agitation" (III.3 ¶46); "It was then the congenial parts began to dissever from their heterogeneous associates, and to seek a mutual embrace" (III.3 ¶48). — *stated as cosmogony; interpreted as bearing on the work (Atwood says Hesiod's imagery fits "the Alchemical descriptions", III.3 ¶42); QUOTED: Kabalistic Interpreter; metropolitan of Memphis*

12. **Reflection and Divine Experience.** These are hailed at the point where life returns to its First Source. — "Most mighty and surpassing magic of Reflection. --- And thou august Mother of all things, Divine Experience." (III.3 ¶79). — *interpreted (as the powers at work); ATWOOD*. In the "Tablet" verse Experience is the creative power that framed all things from nothing: "That thou art the very same, That didst from nothing all things frame" (III.3 ¶76). — *QUOTED: Experience and Philosophy*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **Purity: nothing profane enters.** The work succeeds when human conditions are made like the divine causes and aimed at the same end. The profane are excluded, and a mind swollen with crime cannot draw the healthful waters. — "When divine Causes and human Conditions which are assimilated to them are coordinate to one and the same end, the perfection of such works overflowing returns a pure and most bright reward" (III.3 ¶5). Also: "Ite profani!" (III.3 ¶5); "Nihil ingreditur profanum." (III.3 ¶6); "Cui scelerum viru mens moribunda tumet" (III.3 ¶12). — *stated (¶5); interpreted (the Latin, ours); UNCLEAR (¶5–7); QUOTED: Orpheus Eucharisticus (¶11–12)*

2. **Sanctity, union with God, piety.** The immortal essentiality cannot be reached without a sanctified mind and purified soul, united to God. The Mind comes only to the holy, pure and merciful, and Wisdom is given to those who love God. The Rosicrucian subject is known only to the pious and modest. — "it is impossible for you to attain this immortal essentiality, unless you become sanctified in mind and purified in soul, so as to be united to God, and to become one spirit with Him" (III.3 ¶28). Also: "I, the Mind, come into men that are holy and good, and pure and merciful, and that live piously and religiously" (III.3 ¶57); "Behold, I show you the fear of the Lord, and the love of Him, with unfeigned obedience" (III.3 ¶28); "known only to the Wise, such as are pious and modest" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; QUOTED: Roger Bacon; Poemander; Rosy Cross*

3. **Theurgic preparation through refining ordeals.** Whoever seeks the First Principle must be theurgically prepared and pass through ordeals described in chemical terms. In this sentence it is the seeker himself who is refined and raised. — "he must be theurgically prepared, and pass through many preliminary ordeals, corrosive tests, and fiery solutions and dissolutions refining, in order to raise himself" (III.3 ¶59). — *interpreted (that the chemical terms apply to the person); ATWOOD*. *Theurgically* is not defined in the chapter.

4. **Sacrifice, mourning, faith: besieging Self-Will through death.** The operator re-enters freed, with prepared intellectual faith, mourning, and aware of his self-sacrifice. He besieges Self-Will and prevails even through death, with the Divine Will in his favour. — "they who sow in tears shall reap in joy" (III.3 ¶20). Also: "with the prepared Light of intellectual faith, mourning, and, like another Achilles, conscious of self-sacrifice, to besiege the fortress of Self-Will in life, prevailing at length through death and every obstacle" (III.3 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD (expounding the Adepts' maxim)*

5. **Contrition is required.** The hardened conscience feels no contrition and yields nothing unless struck by fire, as flint gives a spark only to steel. — "the flinty conscience yields no chalybeate, feels no contrition, but by the fiery well-tempered steel" (III.3 ¶11). — *interpreted (contrition as a required condition); ATWOOD*. See also Signs 3.

6. **The will must break its compact with the senses.** Overcoming the will's bond to sense takes great effort, and this struggle is the meritorious part of the work. — "nor is it any trifling exertion which the will has to make to overcome the compact which it has made to sense; but herein consists the meritorious struggle of the powers" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*

7. **Faith and "ideal conception" lead experimental inquiry.** The inquirer starts from a faith that is real, however small, and nourishes it until practical experience confirms it. Atwood says this is not credulity but a liberty of thought that carries its own evidence. — "without faith, without the ideal conception, nothing is or can be proven; for is not this, in fact, the leader of all experimental inquiry?" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "let us believe in it, and nourish and educate, that it may increase with knowledge, and finally prove its own reward in practical experience" (III.3 ¶64); "The faith we invite is no blind credulity, but such a liberty of thought" (III.3 ¶64); "having within them a substantial evidence of the thing hoped for, will believe and know too" (III.3 ¶59); "the percipient right- believer will not be deceived" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; ATWOOD*

8. **Self-knowledge.** The mystery of our Image is to be reflected into, so that we know our true selves. Whoever has Mind must know himself immortal and learn what he is made of. Atwood: without self-knowledge the Divine Idea cannot be reached. — "that great and miraculous mystery of our Image, which it behooves us to reflect into, rather than profanely to discuss; that we may know our true selves" (III.3 ¶25). Also: "let the man endued with mind, mark, consider, and know himself well" (III.3 ¶57); "let him that is endued with Mind know himself to be immortal" (III.3 ¶56); "We cannot, profanely as we live without the knowledge of ourselves, attain to the Divine Idea" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated (¶25 purpose, ¶78); interpreted (¶25 as a step of the work); ATWOOD; QUOTED: Poemander*

9. **Belief in one's own powers; knowing, willing, hoping.** One must believe nothing is impossible, see oneself as immortal, and join knowing, willing and hoping. — "If thou art able to believe in thyself, that nothing is impossible, but perceivest thyself to be immortal" (III.3 ¶78). Also: "But to be able to know, and to will, and to hope, is the straight way and the divine way proper to the Good" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander*

10. **Courage, magnanimity and a charitable motive.** The work must be done with great courage. It is given so that the poor may be relieved, not for perishing gold. — "The mysteries of my art are to be performed magnanimously and with great courage, if thou wouldst have me overcome the violence of the fire" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "that they may relieve the poor with my treasures, and not set their mind on gold that perisheth" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

11. **Exact knowledge of the matter.** The first gate is knowing the true matter, and it is the bitterest conflict. Working without exact knowledge is ruinous (see Impediments 8). — "The first gate was the knowledge of the true matter, and here arose the first, and that a most bitter conflict" (III.3 ¶63). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

12. **Knowledge comes by intuition and inward unfolding, not outward teaching.** Solomon knew the Living Light by Conscious Intuition, taught by Wisdom. Atwood suggests the Memphian mysteries were taught by passing the lesson inward to unfold. The artist grasps the hidden device either "by spiritual aid" or by his own experiment. — "not by outward teaching or rational inference from effects, but by the Conscious Intuition, as he relates it, of one only might" (III.3 ¶49). Also: "by passing it inwards to the evolution of its proper mystery, thence to emanate and recreate" (III.3 ¶44); "unless the artist shall have rightly conceived either by spiritual aid or himself experimenting in a triune furnace spherically round" (III.3 ¶11). — *stated (¶49, ¶11); interpreted (¶44, offered as "more probably"); ATWOOD*

13. **The Art is a midwife, not a maker.** The Stone, or the Water, brings itself forth regeneratively. The Art's "obstetric hand" assists the birth. — "made manifest by itself regeneratively by the obstetric hand of the Physico-Chemical Art" (III.3 ¶50). Also: "made manifest of itself regeneratively, by the obstetric hand of the physico-chemical art" (III.3 ¶73); "drawn from three heads by the obstetric hand of the physico-chemical Art" (III.3 ¶35). — *interpreted (from "by itself" plus the midwife image); QUOTED: Khunrath (¶50); ATWOOD (¶35, ¶73)*

14. **Faithful perseverance; the timing belongs to God.** — "work faithfully, as the philosopher tells us, until it be the Divine pleasure to make it appear" (III.3 ¶80). Also: "demur not at the expense of time; otherwise thou shalt gain no advantage" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Synesius*

15. **Secrecy.** — "whose nucleus in the Decad guard in holy silence. Let him who hath understanding understand" (III.3 ¶50). — *stated (the injunction); content unexplained; QUOTED: Khunrath (n35)*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter has no single sequence. It sets out general operations (A) and several partial sequences from different voices (B, in chapter order).

Across these sequences one pattern recurs. It is *interpreted*, and each step is grounded in the points cited:
1. separation of the principles (A1, A2; B1, B6);
2. an extremity or darkness, read by Atwood as contrition (A6; B2, B3; Signs 3–4);
3. reunion or conjunction (B2, B6, B7, B8, B10);
4. light and colours appearing (B2, B3; Signs 5–6);
5. a fixed, indissoluble union (B7, B8, B10).

### A. Operations

1. **Dissolve and coagulate; the threefold secret in balance.** After the Vital Principles have been separated by Art, the threefold secret is applied: attraction, repulsion and circulation are brought into exact balance. Khunrath's formula, the Monk's and Atwood's closing exhortation repeat dissolve-and-congeal. — "great care and diligence is needed at this juncture to apply the threefold secret of the Art; so that the hypostatic principles of attraction and repulsion and circulation may be brought into a perfect equilibriate accord" (III.3 ¶9). Also: "Seek Three in One, and again seek One in Three, dissolve, congeal" (III.3 ¶9, Khunrath); "Solve et Coagula, reiterates the Benedictine Monk, Dissolve and Coagulate" (III.3 ¶24); "to dissolve, coagulate, resolve, refine, and regulate" (III.3 ¶80, ATWOOD). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶9 first sentence, ¶80); QUOTED: Khunrath; Benedictine Monk*

2. **Separate and rejoin soul or spirit and body.** Several voices give the same operation. Spirit is separated from "water" and allowed to putrefy. Then the dregs are discarded and the spirit is brought back and rejoined. Soul is drawn out of the body and returned to the same body until the four elements are mixed. The flying soul is returned to earth. — "the extraction of the soul out of the body, and the reduction of it afterwards upon the same body; until the whole be drawn to a commixion of the four elements" (III.3 ¶60). Also: "cause that the spirit be separated from the water, that it may putrefy and be like a grain" (III.3 ¶29); "let him reduce and bring back the spirit again from the deep into the water and make them be joined again" (III.3 ¶29); "The flying soul to th’ earth returned, The soaring bird descended" (III.3 ¶68); "Separate, therefore, the thick from the thin artificially" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated (Synesius, Sendivogius); interpreted (Eirenaeus verse, Rosy Cross); QUOTED: Synesius; Sendivogius; Eirenaeus; Rosy Cross*. Sendivogius is called "enigmatic" and his "water" and "spirit" are not decoded.

3. **All the named operations are one decoction.** The many operation names in the books do not stand for separate procedures. All mean cooking nature until she is perfected. Extraction of soul, spirit or body is the same calcination, which signifies the "operation of Venus". — "to dissolve, to calcine, to tinge, to whiten, to renew, to bathe, to wash, to coagulate, to imbibe, to decoct, to fix, to grind, to dry and to distill are all one" (III.3 ¶60). Also: "signify no more than to decoct nature until such time as she be perfected" (III.3 ¶60); "to extract the soul, or the spirit, or the body, is nothing else than the aforesaid calcinations in regard they signify the operation of Venus" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated; QUOTED: Synesius*. "Venus" is not explained.

4. **Gentle, long, patient coction in a sealed vessel.** Decoct the Water gently, little by little, until its false tincture becomes a perfect form of light. Do not grudge the time. Keep the vessel closed. — "Decoct it gently by little and little, until it have changed its false tincture into a perfect form of light" (III.3 ¶60). Also: "wholly employ thyself in the coction of this water, and demur not at the expense of time" (III.3 ¶60); "Shut thy vessel well that it may not breathe out, so that thou mayest bring it to some effect" (III.3 ¶60); "Ce qi a ete attire doit etre cuit si long temps d’une certaine mainere de repetition" (III.3 ¶18; ours: what has been drawn must be cooked a long time by a certain manner of repetition). — *stated (the instruction); interpreted (what the vessel and heat are); QUOTED: Synesius; Mystere de la Croix*

5. **No manual labour or common fire.** — "Apply nomanual labor" (III.3 ¶9). Also: "without the help of hammer, tongs, or file; without coals, smoke, or fire, or bath; or the alembics of the sophisters" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; Benedictine Monk*

6. **Force the matter to its extremity: the Proteus method.** Matter deliberately pushed toward annihilation runs through every shape. If the force is kept up, it restores itself. Atwood glosses the "extremest degrees" as the last extremity of life, just before rebirth by the Light within. — "if any skillful minister of nature shall apply force to matter, and by design torture and vex it in order to its annihilation" (III.3 ¶61). Also: "running through the whole circle of transformations and completing its period, it restores itself, if the force be continued" (III.3 ¶61); "That is, in the last exigence of life; when it is about to be born again from out the oblivion of this worked and its defilements, by attraction of the recreative Light within" (III.3 ¶61). — *stated; QUOTED: Lord Bacon (Fable of Proteus, n44), endorsed by Atwood, who calls it "the Art of Democritus"; gloss ATWOOD*

7. **Torture the Eagle, bleed the Lion; boil milk and blood.** Two opposed principles are pressed until the Eagle gives tears and the Lion gives blood. Combined, these are the treasure. The pair devour each other yet love each other, and take on the nature of a fire-proof Salamander. — "Torture the Eagle till she weeps; and the Lion being weakened, bleeds to death" (III.3 ¶63). Also: "The blood of this Lion incorporated with the tears of the Eagle is the treasure of the whole earth" (III.3 ¶63); "Out of my breast I pour forth milk and blood; boil these two till they are turned into silver and gold" (III.3 ¶63). — *interpreted (Eagle and Lion are not decoded); QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

8. **Conversion of the elements: exchanging active and passive.** The work imitates creation. All diversity comes out of the One and returns to it, and this is called conversion of the elements. It means making the active passive and the passive active, so that the hidden becomes manifest. The Quadrature of the Circle is achieved only this way. — "a conversion of the elements in this respect is just to make active passive and passive active; the occult becoming manifest and the manifest occult in inverse order of conception" (III.3 ¶29). Also: "in no other way but by a transmutation of the hypostatic relations, as in a circulating medium making passive active and active passive" (III.3 ¶30). — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **Circulation of the Vital Spirit in three periods.** Atwood says, hedging ("it would seem"), that the whole secret is the circulation of the Vital Spirit under a threefold Law, carried through in three periods. — "the whole secret of this discovery, it would seem, consists in the sanquinary circulation of the Vital Spirit; in which there is a threefold Law, as before explained, which has to be revolutionized also in three period" (III.3 ¶72). — *stated (hedged); ATWOOD*. For the periods see B12.

10. **Inward procedure: senses held back, the Light conceived in mind.** Hermes' testimony gives the inward conditions. Thought is busied with "the things that be", the understanding is raised, and the bodily senses are held back. The Light is conceived in the mind and gazed on steadily. One believes oneself to be of Life and Light. The Mind stops the body's operations and shuts out evil. — "Conceive well the Light in thy Mind, and Know It" (III.3 ¶54). Also: "my understanding lifted up, --- all my bodily senses being entirely holden back" (III.3 ¶52); "for a long time, we looked steadfastly one upon the other" (III.3 ¶55); "believe thyself to be of the Life and Light, thou shalt pass back into Life" (III.3 ¶56); "before they give up their bodies to the death of them, they hate their senses, knowing their works and operations" (III.3 ¶57). — *stated; QUOTED: Hermes/Poemander, offered by Atwood as testimony of regeneration (III.3 ¶52)*

11. **Enlarge oneself to understand God.** Leap beyond every body and beyond time, and take all creatures, elements, places and times into oneself. Like is known by like. — "Increase thyself into an immeasurable greatness, leaping beyond every body, and transcending time, become Eternity, and thou shalt understand God" (III.3 ¶78). Also: "judge of this by thyself, assimilating, for the like is intelligible by the like" (III.3 ¶78); "become higher than all height, lower than all depth, comprehend in thyself the qualities of all the creatures" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander, introduced by Atwood with "Yet" against the impossibility she has just stated*

12. **Sacrifice of the material nature supplies a body to the Divine.** Matter is non-being produced by severance from real being. When the material nature is gratefully given up in the reunion, it supplies a body for the Divine, draws the Powers in and unfolds them into visible action. — "the sacrifice that is gratefully provided of the material nature in their reunion, as supplying body to the Divine, excites the Powers to participation" (III.3 ¶77). Also: "conceives them when they accede, and consciously unfolds them into visibility and act" (III.3 ¶77). — *stated; ATWOOD*

13. **Return along the wheel to the First Source.** The work runs backward, from created individuality to the initial germ and then to the First Source. — "We must pass the eternal wheel of the vicissitudes of things, from the manifest created individuality, back into the Initial germ" (III.3 ¶79). Also: "until life, as an ocean tide flowing to its extreme boundary, returns to refund its treasury again its First Source" (III.3 ¶79). — *stated; ATWOOD*

14. **Give the released light to a proper recipient.** — "when that light is again taken and given to a proper recipient, it is made a thousand times more fit and apt to bring forth excellent and abundant fruits" (III.3 ¶30). — *stated; ATWOOD*. No further detail is given.

15. **One fixed fixes the unfixed.** — "To make by one fixt, one unfixed fixt" (III.3 ¶36). — *interpreted (literal reading; terms not decoded); QUOTED: Enigma Philosophicum*

### B. Stage sequences given in the chapter (chapter order)

1. **Khunrath: separation, new Chaos, inner motion, removal of sin, Azoth.** When the process is rightly carried through, a "new Chaos" appears. After the separation the operator feels an inner motion and weeps for joy. Original sin is removed, and Azoth is seen and touched. — "the new Chaos of the Universal Nature of the of the new world will then appear to be unfolded and separated" (III.3 ¶9). Also: "(motum in te, experis internum et pro gaudio, lachrymabis!)" (III.3 ¶9; ours: you will feel an inner motion within you and weep for joy). — *stated (English); interpreted (Latin, ours); QUOTED: Khunrath*. Khunrath also warns that "the animated spirit cannot be conjoined to the body" (III.3 ¶9); see Open questions.

2. **Atwood: extremity, reunion, Fiat, visible light.** At the last extremity of tribulation, the faith and desire of the Passive Spirit draw the Soul back. This is the first link of the "magnetic series". The Divine Fiat creates a new hypostasis. The fiery soul is held again in the "liquid crystal of the understanding ether", and its light streams out. Only in this state does it first become subject to the artist. — "When in the last extreme of tribulation and departing life, the returning faith and desire of the Passive Spirit attracts the Soul again into herself, the first link in the chain of the magnetic series moves" (III.3 ¶10). Also: "the fiery soul suffers itself again to be imprisoned, as by a lawful magic in the liquid crystal of the understanding ether" (III.3 ¶10); "in which state it is first made subject to the artist" (III.3 ¶10). — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **Atwood: conjunction under the cross, darkness, colours, germination, marriage.** Everything is sown under the cross. In the hours of conjunction there is darkness (see Signs 4), then all colours as a token of reconciliation (see Signs 5). Fire and water mix and germinate, and Harmony is born of the Spirit. This is the marriage of Mind and Universe: solitary Thought unites with the simple Understanding of its ether. — "All is sown under the cross and completed in its number" (III.3 ¶13). Also: "the fire and water are commingled, and, falling together under the cross, germinate, and the beautiful Ideal of Harmony is born of the Spirit" (III.3 ¶13); "This is the union supersentient, the nuptials sublime, Mentis et Universi; the Thought solitary unites itself to the non-being, or simple Understanding of its ether" (III.3 ¶15). — *stated (sequence and marriage gloss); interpreted (the "cross", tied by ¶15 to "the crucifixion of the cerebral life"); ATWOOD*. Ancient marriages that prefigure it: "of Peleus and Thetis, of Earth and Heaven" (III.3 ¶15).

4. **Mars by Venus; the vapour caught in nets.** In the conjunction of Mars and Venus a spirituous vapour rises and must be caught with subtle nets. The vitriol left behind yields living philosophic gold. — "il s’eleve une vapeur tres spirituesuse et necessaire a un grand ouvrage: ilffaut prendre cette vapeur avec des filets bien subtils" (III.3 ¶11). — *interpreted (ours); QUOTED: Mystere de la Croix*. Mars, Venus and the nets are not explained.

5. **Pierce the Black Monk's verse; colour sequence (Khunrath).** Sublimed arsenic and calcined mercury are ground with the Water of Might. Everything runs to bright water, is fixed on the fire, and is fed until strong. Khunrath gives the colours: ashy, white, yellow, then the Stone. — "Alle runnyth to Water bright and schene; Upon this fire they grow together Till they be fast and flee no whyther" (III.3 ¶16). Also: "Then feed them forth with thine own Hand, With meat and bread, tyll they be strong" (III.3 ¶16); "after the ashy colour, and the white, and the yellow, thou shalt behold the Stone" (III.3 ¶19). — *stated (sequence); interpreted (terms not decoded); QUOTED: Pierce, the Black Monk; Khunrath*

6. **Putrefaction, then generation (the Monk).** After putrefaction comes generation. The incombustible sulphur works on the cold, crude quicksilver until they become one body. — "after putrefaction succeeds generation, and that because of the incombustible sulphur that heats or thickens the coldness and crudities of the quicksilver" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "at last it is united to the sulphur and made one body therewith" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; QUOTED: the Benedictine Monk*

7. **Two conjunctions: Mercury first, then Gold; then the offspring.** In the first conjunction Spirit (Mercury) dominates. In the second the Soul's Light (Gold) dominates. Gold's power is sealed at first and it does not know itself. Once penetrated by the mercurial Spirit, it sends out its Light. Mercury, now passive, conceives an offspring more perfect than either parent, "a branch of unlike shape to its parents". — "In the first conjunction the Spirit predominates; in the second the Soul, i.e., its Light; which two are, by adepts, called Mercury and Gold" (III.3 ¶30). Also: "the formal virtue of Sol is sealed; his sulphur is imprisoned, so that he is not aware of it, does not feel or know himself" (III.3 ¶30); "until penetrated by the mercurial Spirit, then he sends forth his Light; to which the Mercury, in turn becoming passive, conceives and bears an offspring more perfect than either parent" (III.3 ¶30); "that Conjunction will generate a branch of unlike shape to its parents" (III.3 ¶29, Sendivogius). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶30)*

8. **The geometric figure: circle, square, triangle, circle; the Fifth Essence.** Make a circle of male and female, then a square, then a triangle, then a circle again, and the Stone results. Atwood calls this the geometric procedure with the "Metaphysical Embryo". Beyond four degrees a Fifth Essence is made: the Ethereal Quadrangle becomes a Circle of golden light. — "Fac ex mare et foemina circulum; inde quadrangulum; hinc triamgulum, fac circulum, et habebis lapidem philosophorum" (III.3 ¶31). Also: "the geometric method of procedure with the Metaphysical Embryo, through its complex parts" (III.3 ¶37); "the Ethereal Quadrangle becomes a Circle of golden light in eternity" (III.3 ¶32); "Feomina masque unus fiant tibi circulus, ex quo Surgat habens aequum forma quadrata latus." (III.3 ¶38). — *stated (Atwood's naming of what it symbolizes); interpreted (the figures, rendering ours); QUOTED: Maier; ATWOOD*

9. **Lucerna Salis: spirit through the alembic, black earth, dissolution, reunion.** The spiritualized gold passes through the Alembic and leaves a black earth. From "thirst" that earth dissolves into thick water and oil. Only then can spirit and soul reunite with it into one Thing that cannot be separated. — "by means of the spirit it passes through the Alembic, its earth remaining black" (III.3 ¶32). Also: "By reason of the thirst it has, it dissolves and is dissevered, which benefits it very much" (III.3 ¶32); "if it did not become water and oil, its spirit and soul could not unite and mingle with it" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; QUOTED: the author of Lucerna Salis*

10. **The Tetractys: fourfold from three heads, indissoluble.** The final Conjunction consummates the Microcosm. It is the circulated Form of Gold, Ripley's "tetraptive" conjunction and the Pythagorean Tetractys. It is drawn from three principles and cannot be dissolved again. — "Tetractys, fourfold, drawn from three heads by the obstetric hand of the physico-chemical Art and without possibility of dissolution any more" (III.3 ¶35). Also: "This then is the Conjunction in which all the mysteries of the Microcosm have their consummation --- the true circulated Form of Gold" (III.3 ¶33); "for those principles so joined together of God, man cannot any more put asunder" (III.3 ¶35). — *stated; ATWOOD*

11. **Ascent from image to Archetype (Platonic); Poemander's ascent.** The soul associates with its Efficient, passes from itself as image to the Archetype, and if it falls away it rises again through Intellect and Wisdom. After "the return", the sense-body is dissolved, the senses go back to their sources, the passions stay lowest and the rest rises to the eighth sphere, Intellect. — "if he further passes from himself as an image to the Archetype, he will then attain the end of his progression" (III.3 ¶26). Also: "through virtue proceeding to Intellect and Wisdom, and afterwards to the Principle of things" (III.3 ¶26); "in the resolution of the material body of sense, this body itself is given up to alteration, and the form which it hath becometh invisible" (III.3 ¶57); "anger and concupiscence remain lowest in the irrational life, and the rest strive upward by harmony" (III.3 ¶57). — *stated; QUOTED: the Platonic Successor; Poemander*. Whether ¶57 describes death or regeneration: see Open questions.

12. **Synesius: two luminaries, then one.** After soul and body are commixed, two luminaries appear: a fixed, male one below and a volatile one above. When the lower rises upon the upper, an incomparable Luminary comes out. — "there are made manifest two luminaries, the one fixed, the other not; whereof the fixed which is the male remains below, and the volatile remains above, moving itself perpetually" (III.3 ¶60). Also: "until that which is below rises upon that which is above, and all being substantiated, there then issues forth an incomparable Luminary" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated; QUOTED: Synesius*

13. **The five gates (Rosy Cross).** (1) knowing the true matter; (2) preparation, to obtain the eagle's embers and the lion's blood, which yields water, blood and a spiritual bright body; (3) the fire that ripens the medicine; (4) multiplication and augmentation by proportions and weights; (5) projection. — "for the center of the middlemost tree in the Terrestrial Paradise, entering in by five litigious gates" (III.3 ¶63). Also: "The second was the preparation by which the matter was to be qualified, that they might obtain the embers of the eagle and the blood of the lion" (III.3 ¶63); "it produceth water and blood, and a spiritual bright body" (III.3 ¶63); "The third gate is the fire which conduceth to the maturity of the medicine" (III.3 ¶63); "The fourth gate is that of multiplication and augmentation in which proportions and weights are necessary" (III.3 ¶63); "The fifth and last gate is projection" (III.3 ¶63). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

14. **Dimensional unfolding: point, line, square, cube.** The Spirit's emanative Law unfolds Nature from the point to the line, to the square and to the cube, which is the foundation of the new physical whole. — "from the point proceeding into the line, from the root into the square superficies, and from the square by multiplication into that cubic form which is the supernatural foundation of the New physical Whole" (III.3 ¶67). — *interpreted (as stages of the work); ATWOOD*

15. **Eirenaeus' verse: death of lion and eagle, burial of king and queen, timed colours.** After the battle the lion and eagle die and the lion is revived. The compound is reduced and the soul returned to earth. King and queen are buried together as one. Then a timetable follows: 90 days to the black crow, 30 more to the bright dove, and 20 more until the soul needs "no fire defender". The result is a silver tincture and fixity against fire. — "Twice forty days do come and go, To which twice five are added; These do produce a perfect crow" (III.3 ¶68). Also: "The Lyon dead revived; The Eagle’s dead which did him slay" (III.3 ¶68); "The king and queen contumulate, And joined as one together" (III.3 ¶68); "Twice fifteen more produce a dove, Whose wings are bright and tender" (III.3 ¶68); "Twice ten more make the soul above To need no fire defender" (III.3 ¶68). — *stated (sequence and counts; the arithmetic is ours); interpreted (figures not decoded); QUOTED: Eirenaeus (Ripley Revived, n46)*

16. **Atwood: struggle with the passions, then the Light drawn out of the Archaeus.** The heroes' ordeals are the soul's struggle with its passions (Impediments 4). Only after that struggle does the Light hidden in the Archaeus come out as a dry splendour, drawn by artificial heat and exhalation. — "until, by artificial force of heat and exhalation, the Light, so long hidden and enshrined in the Archaeus, comes forth as a dry splendour, surviving through all" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*. What the heat and exhalation are is not explained.

17. **Profundity, Altitude, Latitude; the Calx.** The three periods of the circulation are named. Profundity is the subjective life, the water below. Altitude is the objective Light, the ether above. Latitude is the two united at their extreme poles. Their conflux in a Calx produces the physical Tetractys through a contrite experience. — "the Profundity is the subjective life, the water that is below; the Altitude is the objective Light, the ether that is above" (III.3 ¶72). Also: "Altitude and Profundity, being united at their extreme poles, make Latitude; and so the wheel of Life is turned about" (III.3 ¶72); "the conflux of these two is in a Calx, out of which, as from a rocky fountain, the physical Tetractys springs through the contrite experience into life" (III.3 ¶72). — *stated; ATWOOD*

18. **Atwood: Thought, Light, Life.** — "Thought emanating Light, as by Intelligence excruciated, Life springs forth with motion, feeling itself to Be." (III.3 ¶79). It parallels the Zohar sequence of Thought shut up in itself, then Spirit (named Intelligence), then an articulate voice from the Mind (III.3 ¶78). — *interpreted (as a stage sequence; "excruciated" not explained); ATWOOD; QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar*

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **The first manifestation is a Water, the Tears of Isis, experienced "in substance".** — "admiring at the Tears of Isis, even that blessed Water which nature sheds divinely for the world" (III.3 ¶3). Also: "For not all was vaporous vision, as we have shown, or mere ideality on the internal ground; but experience there was present with power and effect in substance to bear it witness" (III.3 ¶3); "I could perceive a silent dew Steal down her cheeks" (III.3 ¶4, Vaughan). — *stated; ATWOOD*. Whether "in substance" means physical: see Open questions. The phrase is echoed at the chapter's close: "where Experience is present with Power, and Effect in substance to bear them witness" (III.3 ¶79).

2. **The light becomes visible and the matter becomes workable.** When the soul is held again in the understanding ether, its light streams out visibly. Only then is the matter first subject to the artist. Making it appear is the hidden crux (Impediments 1). — "Then it is Lux manifeste et visibilis ad oculum, as the adept says, in which state it is first made subject to the artist" (III.3 ¶10). — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **Inner motion in contrition; tears of joy.** The Light appears in great darkness, which Atwood glosses as the contrition of the sensible nature in the conscience, where a peculiar motion is felt. Khunrath promises an inner motion and tears of joy once the separation is done. — "the Light is made manifest in great darkness, viz., in the contrition or distress of the sensible nature in the conscience, where a peculiar motion is present" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "even then, as Jacob Boehme says, cometh the Power of Christ in the midst of such a motion" (III.3 ¶24); "motum in te, experis internum et pro gaudio, lachrymabis!" (III.3 ¶9, Khunrath; ours). — *stated (¶24 gloss); interpreted (¶9 Latin); ATWOOD; QUOTED: Boehme; Khunrath*

4. **Blackness: Night, Saturn, the Head of the Crow; black shows the spirit fixed.** In the conjunction a darkness falls, and several names are listed as present in it. The blackness is welcome: it shows the spirit is fixed. — "Darkness will draw over the face of the Abyss, Night, Saturn and the Antimony of the Wise will be present, Obscurity and the Head of the Crow in the various hours of conjunction" (III.3 ¶13). Also: "The sable blacke is not disdained Which shows the spirit’s fixed" (III.3 ¶68); "These do produce a perfect crow, Whose blackness cheers hearts sadded" (III.3 ¶68); "that Water which burns to blackness" (III.3 ¶60); "its earth remaining black" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶13); QUOTED: Eirenaeus; Synesius; Lucerna Salis*. The individual names in ¶13 are not decoded.

5. **All colours: the rainbow, the peacock's tail, a token of reconciliation.** After the darkness all colours appear, like a rainbow after a storm. They are a token of reconciliation in the "Microcosmic Heaven" and a sign of grace. Later colour marks: ashy, white, yellow (Khunrath); silver stained with gold, and violet (Eirenaeus). — "all the colours of the world will be apparent; also Iris, God’s messenger, and the tail of the peacock" (III.3 ¶13). Also: "the same beautiful token of reconciliation is made apparent in the Microcosmic Heaven" (III.3 ¶13); "jusqu’a ce qu’il montre les couleurs de l’arc en ciel; signe de grace et de reconciliation" (III.3 ¶18); "The Argent field with Or is stained, With violet intermixed" (III.3 ¶68); "all colours shine in me and all metals, by the beams of the Sun" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶13); QUOTED: Mystere de la Croix; Eirenaeus; Rosy Cross*

6. **A Light about the summit, in the North.** Atwood glosses the Adept's phrase. In darkness, when the head-centred life is crucified and the senses are subdued, a Light arises about the summit. Rightly returned and multiplied, it becomes substantial in life. — "Lo! Behold I will open to thee a mystery, cries the Adept, the bridegroom crowneth the bride in the North!" (III.3 ¶15). Also: "In the darkness of the North, out of the crucifixion of the cerebral life, when the sensual dominant is occultated in the Divine Fiat and subdued, there arises a Light wonderfully about the summit" (III.3 ¶15). — *stated (gloss); QUOTED: the Adept, glossed by ATWOOD*. What "summit" refers to: see Open questions.

7. **Heavy drops in the receiver.** — "et que les goutes pesantes tombent dans le fond du vase recipient; quasi comme un mercure commun distille" (III.3 ¶18; ours: and heavy drops fall to the bottom of the receiving vessel, almost like distilled common mercury). — *interpreted (ours); QUOTED: Mystere de la Croix*. The work is called "Aimantique" (magnetic).

8. **The pattern of the universe appears in the Subject Waters.** Once Divine Light pierces the matter, the pattern of the whole universe appears in the "Subject Waters", like an image in a mirror. Footnote 27 treats it as the appearance of the Universal Idea in the mind. Atwood ranks the physical counterpart (a body's form seen in the vessel after decomposition) as only a secondary witness. — "no sooner, it is said, does the Divine Light pierce to the bosom of the matter, but the pattern of the whole universe appears in those Subject Waters, as an image in a glass" (III.3 ¶39). Also: "this appearance of the Universal Idea in the mind" (III.3 n27); "would be but as a secondary vestiment or witness of that which is in the Archetypal mind creatively efficient" (III.3 n27). — *stated; QUOTED: unnamed ("it is said"); ATWOOD (n27)*

9. **Hermes' vision: infinite Light, darkness turning into a moist nature, separation by the Word.** First all becomes Light. Then a darkness descends and turns into a troubled moist nature. A holy Word joins Nature, pure fire flies upward and air follows, while earth and water stay mingled below. Fire is held in its station by a great Power. — "I saw an infinite Light, all things were become Light, both sweet and exceeding pleasant" (III.3 ¶53). Also: "there was a darkness made in part, coming down obliquely, fearful and hideous" (III.3 ¶53); "Then from that Light a certain holy Word joined itself unto Nature, and out flew the pure and unmixed fire from the moist nature upward on high" (III.3 ¶53); "the fire is comprehended or contained in and by a most great Power, and constrained to keep its station" (III.3 ¶55). — *stated (as vision); not related to the Art by the text; QUOTED: Hermes*

10. **The body asleep while the mind is awake.** — "For the sleep of the body was the sober watchfulness of the mind; and the shutting of my eyes the true Sight; and my silence great with child and full of good" (III.3 ¶58). — *interpreted (as a sign of the illuminated state); QUOTED: Poemander/Hermes, which Atwood calls the "operative revelation of Intellect" (III.3 ¶59)*

11. **Under the revealed light the passions appear monstrous.** Faults that a deadened conscience treats as trifles appear "without metaphor" as monstrous. The inward man actually suffers them, and they leave slowly. — "by the revealed light of equilibriate justice, are monstrous; and, without metaphor, in their imaged atmosphere appear terrific" (III.3 ¶71). Also: "do force the inwardly placed man to suffer sensibly" (III.3 ¶71); "For they do not suddenly depart, or easily, even from him in whom the exemplary virtue is revealed" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*

12. **End-of-phase signs in the verse.** The dews that fell for six weeks stop, and the swollen toad bursts. — "The showers cease, the dews, which fell For six weeks, do not rise; The ugly toad, that did so swell, With swelling, bursts and dies" (III.3 ¶68). — *interpreted (not decoded); QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

13. **The order of nature is inverted in the subject.** It unites contraries. — "In me the order of nature is oftentimes inverted in colour, number, weight and measure" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "I am father and mother, youthful and antique, weak yet powerful, life and death, visible and invisible" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

14. **Once found, it meets one everywhere.** — "it will meet thee everywhere thereafter and everywhere be seen of thee, plain and easy, even when thou dost no longer expect or look for it" (III.3 ¶78). Also: "For there is nothing which is not the Image of God" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Failing to make the matter appear.** The "device of Vulcan" is deeply hidden. Even with the right material the labour is vain if the artist cannot make the matter appear. — "Which subtle device of Vulcan most profoundly hidden unless the artist shall have rightly conceived either by spiritual aid or himself experimenting in a triune furnace spherically round, his labours are declared to be vain" (III.3 ¶11). Also: "even though working in the right material, if he cannot cause it to appear" (III.3 ¶11); "If the horse’s strength be yet denied, in vain he will strike upon the mountain" (III.3 ¶11). — *stated; ATWOOD*. The furnace and the horse: see Open questions.

2. **Saving one's gold, that is, resting in the first fruits of reason.** The "gold" to be destroyed is the first product of one's reasoning. Whoever rests satisfied in it loses his labour, like Midas. Whoever destroys it gets it back a hundredfold. — "he that saves his gold, that is to say, remains satisfied in the first fruits of his reason, loses his labour, and is deceived, like Midas" (III.3 ¶22). Also: "Never grudge, then, that thou hast destroyed thye gold, says Eirenaeus, for he that thus destroys loseth it not, but soweth good seed in good earth" (III.3 ¶22). — *stated (gloss); ATWOOD (gloss); QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

3. **The dominance of the senses hides the divine original.** The corporeal, which the text glosses as the sensual principle, hides the divine origin from consciousness. Sense-bound conception hides the Demiurgic Fire so completely that man does not suspect it and thinks his bondage is freedom. Love of the body causes death. A soul shut up in the body cannot know God. — "by the corporeal, i.e., the sensual principle which is predominant in the mundane conception, the divine original is obscured and separated off from the consciousness" (III.3 ¶52). Also: "that Fire which the sensual conception occultates, and so forcibly restricts, that man does not suspect it even" (III.3 ¶71); "but in his willing thralldom, fancies himself at liberty" (III.3 ¶71); "the cause of death is the love of body" (III.3 ¶56); "But if thou has shut up thy soul in the body, to abuse it" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Poemander (¶56, ¶78)*

4. **The household enemies: passions, desires, vices.** The heroes' ordeals record the soul's long, fearful struggle against its own passions. — "the difficulties and dangers that the soul must endure overcoming her household enemies within the stronghold of life" (III.3 ¶71). Also: "Nor are they few, but many and fearful ones that have to be encountered" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*

5. **The wicked are given over to the tormenting Demon.** From the wicked the Mind withdraws. A Demon torments them with ever-increasing fire and insatiable desire. — "giving place to the revenging Demon, which, applying unto him the sharpness of fire, tormenteth such a man sensibly" (III.3 ¶57). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander*

6. **Profane living without self-knowledge; the inversion of thought.** Living profanely, without self-knowledge, one cannot think the Divine Idea, because God's creative Thought is the inversion of ours. — "We cannot, profanely as we live without the knowledge of ourselves, attain to the Divine Idea; either to entertain or think It self-actively is impossible" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; ATWOOD*

7. **Haste and excess heat at the start; an unsealed vessel.** — "have great care at the beginning, that thou burn not its flowers and its vivacity, and make not too much haste to come to an end of thy work" (III.3 ¶60). Also: "Shut thy vessel well that it may not breathe out" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated; QUOTED: Synesius*

8. **Working without exact knowledge of the matter: poison, ruin, loss of the senses.** — "if thou dost not exactly know me, thou wilt with my fire destroy thy five senses" (III.3 ¶64). Also: "A most pernicious quick-poison comes out of my nostrils, which hath been the destruction of many" (III.3 ¶64); "in which attempt many have lost their labour and their substance" (III.3 ¶64). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross*

9. **Ordinary chemistry cannot catch the true element.** It escapes ordinary tests and vessels and is held only in vessels of Nature's own construction. — "she eludes their tests and closest vessels; all except those of her own ethereally wise construction" (III.3 ¶65). — *stated; ATWOOD*. What these vessels are is not said.

10. **Seeking the Art in fancies; the sealed fountain.** — "The sophister still failing, Who seeks in fancies for to find Our Art so much concealed" (III.3 ¶68). Also: "That ‘t is a fountain sealed, Which one thing only can unlocke; This one things learn to know" (III.3 ¶68). — *stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

11. **Reading only the letter; the veil on the heart.** Unbelief comes from ignorance of Scripture's hidden sense. The veil is taken away when the heart turns to the Lord. — "when Moses is read, the veil is upon their heart" (III.3 ¶59). Also: "Does not all our unbelief, as the common faith, arise in ignorance?" (III.3 ¶59); "nor does any look, as Agrippa says, under the Bark of the Law" (III.3 ¶59); "unhappy he, who mistakes the vestment for the Law itself" (III.3 ¶59). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the Apostle; Agrippa; the Kabalist (endorsed)*

12. **Falling away from the vision, which can be recovered.** — "when falling off from the vision of God, if he again excites the virtue which is in himself and perceives himself to be perfectly adorned, he will again" (III.3 ¶26). — *stated; QUOTED: the Platonic Successor*

13. **Words cannot convey the subject.** — "how hardly should words avail, even the most significant, to convey a tangible idea of that which is beyond and inverse to all sensible experience" (III.3 ¶51). — *stated; ATWOOD* (see Principles 9).

## The result

1. **Regeneration of body, soul and spirit; original sin removed.** — "thou wilt surely understand that the original sin has been removed --- separated by the Fire of Divine love in the regeneration of the three principles --- body, soul and spirit" (III.3 ¶9). — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath*

2. **The Stone as King, glorified, a carbuncle of inseparable parts.** The Stone leaves its glass tomb in a glorified, regenerate body, like a shining carbuncle whose parts are inseparably joined. The son of Art becomes a crowned King. — "our King and Lord of hosts go forth from the chamber of his glassy sepulcher, into this mundane sphere, in his glorified body, regenerate and in perfection perfected; as a shining carbuncle" (III.3 ¶19). Also: "whose parts, most subtle and most pure, are inseparably blent together in the harmonious rest of union into one" (III.3 ¶19); "henceforth thou art a Crowned King, resting over the Fountain and drawing from thence the Auripigment, dry without moisture" (III.3 ¶17); "who hast the Sun for thy Diadem and the Moon Crescent for thy Garland" (III.3 ¶17). — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; the Hermetic Master*. The Crowned King images are not explained.

3. **The Spirit born of the Soul, clothed with Divine Essentiality; this qualifies the Adept.** The product is a Spirit born of the Soul, in which lie the Image of God and all understanding. Giving up its own will, it enters God and is clothed with Divine Essentiality. Atwood adds that this Essentiality qualifies and sanctifies the true Adept and is the material of the Corner-Stone. — "this is the true Spirit which is born of the Soul, wherein is the Image of God, and the Divine Virgin of God’s wisdom consisteth" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "casting away the cunning fire of its own soul, putteth its will into God; then it dwelleth with Him in power, and is clothed with the Divine Essentiality" (III.3 ¶24); "And this Essentiality it is which qualifies the true Adept; which sanctifies even as it qualifies" (III.3 ¶24); "It is this material of the Corner-Stone which links reason to Divinity" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; QUOTED: Jacob Boehme, endorsed and extended by ATWOOD*

4. **A spiritual body, the corruptible made incorruptible.** — "It is sown a natural body, it is raised a spiritual body" (III.3 ¶24). Also: "For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality" (III.3 ¶27); "and so swallows up the same, that it is glorified and transfigured, occultating the body in more luminous manifestation" (III.3 ¶23, ATWOOD). — *stated (¶23); interpreted (scriptural verses as the work's end); QUOTED: the Apostle; "the Aristotle" (as printed)*

5. **The Fifth Essence: embodied yet not subject to gross corporeity.** Beyond the four degrees comes a Fifth Essence, distinct from all of them yet sharing in all. It has a body but is not bound by the laws of gross bodies. — "beyond all the four precedent degrees of perfection there is made a Fifth Essence, which neuter from all, yet partaking of all in perpetuity of union" (III.3 ¶32). Also: "though they have bodies, yet are not subject to those laws of gross corporeity which fetter bodies unregenerate" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD*

6. **The philosopher's Mercury: fixed and compact yet penetrating and tinging.** — "the philosopher’s Mercury is a system of wonders ponderous, fixed, and, as a petrification from water is, exquisitely compact; yet penetrative withal and communicative of tincture" (III.3 ¶32). Also: "projected on the imperfect metal of any life, dissolves, drawing away the foundation into itself" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD*

7. **The indissoluble One Thing; the Tetractys.** See Procedure B10. Lucerna Salis says the same: "of them One Thing is made which rises to a consummate perfection; the parts thereof being so firmly joined together that they can never after be separated" (III.3 ¶32). — *stated; QUOTED: Lucerna Salis; ATWOOD (¶33–35)*

8. **Whoever squares the Circle holds the one Principle and sees the Archetypal Law of Light.** He has reduced all things, thoughts, passions and actions to one Principle and can recompose every particular from it. He sees the original causal order as in a mirror, the same in the individual as in the Great World. — "having before himself, as in a glass, the great Archetypal Law of Light, in which are all things causally ranged in the order in which they were originally distributed and set apart" (III.3 ¶39). Also: "having resolved all sorts and ideas of things, all thoughts, passions, and actions to one and the same Principe" (III.3 ¶39); "Perfect in the Microcosmic Unit as in the total Deity of the Great World" (III.3 ¶39). — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **Orus, the Philosophic Sun; an incomparable Luminary.** — "that which she brings forth by the Fire of nature lawfully conceived, is Orus, the Philosophic Sun" (III.3 ¶62). Also: "there then issues forth an incomparable Luminary" (III.3 ¶60). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Synesius*

10. **Medicine and transmutation.** Reaching the fourth gate gives a universal medicine for all diseases. Projection (the fifth gate) serves only for metals. The Salamander cures men and metals, and the Dragon turns base metals into gold. — "most glorious, full, rich, and highly elevated is he who attains but to the fourth gate; for his has got an universal medicine for all diseases" (III.3 ¶63). Also: "The fifth gate serves only for metals" (III.3 ¶63); "which, if it remains in the fire without any detriment, cures all the diseases of men and metals" (III.3 ¶63); "by which thou mayest turn copper, iron, tin, and lead into most pure gold" (III.3 ¶64); "ce qui vous donnera un Ophtalmique et Antiepileptique merveilleux; et meme quelque chose de plus si le Seigneur vous ouvre les yeux" (III.3 ¶18; ours: which will give you a marvellous ophthalmic and antiepileptic, and even something more if the Lord opens your eyes). — *stated; QUOTED: Rosy Cross; Mystere de la Croix*. A silver tincture and fixity against fire: "Tincture to give of silver fine" (III.3 ¶68). — *QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

11. **The dry splendour: the Tincture of the Sapphiric Mine, the Subtendent; the principle preserved like Noah.** The Light drawn from the Archaeus is the Tincture of the Sapphiric Mine and the Subtendent, which in seed is equal to the whole it came from. In this water the principle of all survives destruction and can reproduce everything tenfold. — "And this is that Tincture of the Sapphiric Mine before alluded to, and that Subtendent which is found seminally equal to the whole of the parts whence it is derived" (III.3 ¶71). Also: "in this water, when destruction has done its worst with the elements of life, the principle of all is artificially preserved, as Noah in the Ark" (III.3 ¶71); "that has power to reproduce all and each with tenfold perfection and increase out of itself" (III.3 ¶71). — *stated; ATWOOD*

12. **True free Will: the Almighty Magnet freed.** Free will is not unmotivated choice but the operative power released from bondage and drawn up to the Light above, so that effects follow the will. — "That was free Will, not the motiveless chimera which human fancy has been prone to coin, but the operative Almighty Magnet freed from Tartarean bondage and obscurity" (III.3 ¶71). Also: "effects follows the voluntary Axle in a necessitous full accord" (III.3 ¶71); "So life is perfected in Wisdom, and the Will springs up in Paradise with fair golden fruits" (III.3 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

13. **The human hypostasis becomes universal intelligence.** Through self-perception and voluntary surrender of self, the human being becomes "the All". — "the human hypostatis becomes, through a self-perceivance, into universal intelligence, and of its own voluntary resignation, from the nothingness of self oblivion, to be the All" (III.3 ¶77). — *stated (hedged: "we may be enabled to conceive perhaps"); ATWOOD*

14. **The Fontal Reason is discovered with creative powers.** — "in the regeneration this Reason is said to be discovered, as, upon the dissolution of the natural life, it arises through the self-perceivance, with creative attributes and powers" (III.3 ¶52). — *stated (as what "is said"); ATWOOD*

15. **Arrival at Intellect, the eighth sphere; illumination; teaching others.** — "until, being naked of all operations, it cometh to the eighth sphere, which is Intellect" (III.3 ¶57). Also: "becoming a guide unto many, I sowed in them the words of Wisdom" (III.3 ¶58). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander/Hermes*

16. **Experience and illumination; Christian regeneration as the worthiest end.** — "That was the Experiment that led our Fathers into Experience, and illumination in the Divine Antecedent of all life" (III.3 ¶61). Also: "with Christian regeneration for its most worthy end" (III.3 ¶61); "are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord" (III.3 ¶59, the Apostle). — *stated; ATWOOD*

17. **The First Principle reached: nature's Flower, through which it is Deity.** — "That which is the most united in nature, and to its Flower, and That through which it is Deity" (III.3 ¶59). — *stated; ATWOOD*

18. **The highest aim: to be the Understanding of the Light and one with the First Mover.** — "To Be the Understanding of that Light, of which all Nature is the Efflux, to move One with the First Mover, and Be His Will" (III.3 ¶78). — *interpreted (posed as a question); ATWOOD*

19. **Reason becomes an immortal Light, Mistress of Life; *our most noble Mercury*.** — "until reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light in the periphery of her fiery essence remains immortal, and is the Mistress of Life" (III.3 ¶80). Also: "Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus, et Deus nunquam creavit rem nobiliorem sub coelo praeter animam rationalem." (III.3 ¶81; ours: this is our most noble Mercury, and God never created a nobler thing under heaven besides/except the rational soul). — *stated (¶80, ATWOOD); interpreted (¶81, UNCLEAR voice)*

20. **Inner and outer worlds agree.** — "the External and Internal Worlds were seen to blend together in confluent harmony, proving and establishing each other, and leaving reason nothing more to doubt of, or the senses to desire" (III.3 ¶82). — *stated; ATWOOD*

21. **The whole work known; the gates of God's treasure opened.** — "you may obtain the knowledge of the beginning, the middle, and the end of the whole work" (III.3 ¶28). Also: "He will open to you the gates of His treasure, the like of which is not to be found on earth" (III.3 ¶28). — *stated; QUOTED: Roger Bacon*

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Corruption comes before increase; the natural comes before the spiritual.** Seed decays before it springs up. Ferment transforms natures by breaking them down and spiritualizes them. What is destroyed is sown and returns increased. — "by a contrition and fretting of their elementary particles, natures are transformed, and their bodies spiritualized and preserved by the assimilating must or leaven" (III.3 ¶23). Also: "The germ of all Being is indeed corrupted before it is brought forth" (III.3 ¶23); "Howbeit that is not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural, and afterwards that which is spiritual" (III.3 ¶23); "It is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption" (III.3 ¶24). — *stated; ATWOOD (with Pauline verses)*

2. **Preservation through destruction: the dying and reviving god.** The principle of all survives destruction. The dying-and-reviving god of the ancient rites is the symbol of the physical secret of regeneration and of the preservation of the One Thing. — "In hac aqua rosa latet hieme" (III.3 ¶71; glossed by the text itself: "in this water, when destruction has done its worst with the elements of life, the principle of all is artificially preserved"). Also: "The god, dead and revived, is a principal character in all their ceremonial rites" (III.3 ¶69); "the Arkite Mysteries have reference to the physical secret of the regeneration throughout the Gentile Mythology" (III.3 ¶69); "The heart of Bacchus, swiftly slaughtered king, Was saved in Aether" (III.3 ¶70, Proclus). — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **All from the One and back to the One; the work imitates creation.** — "For as in the Beginning there was said to be one only matter of all things, so in this imitative process all diversities of things are seen to proceed from and return to this only One" (III.3 ¶29). Atwood reads Hesiod's births as the successive states of the ethereal essence: "indicative of the several estates of the Ethereal Quintessences arising one above another, called forth by the light and heat of the superincumbent mind" (III.3 ¶42). God's Will made the elements by copying the Archetype: "which taking the Word and beholding the beautiful world in the Archetype thereof, imitated it" (III.3 ¶56, Poemander). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Poemander*

4. **Three in one: body, soul and spirit as three relations of one life.** The three are born out of one another, die within one another and are strengthened by one another. They differ only as agent, patient and their common offspring. Vaughan: the three are one in substance and so can be perfectly united. — "body, soul, and spirit --- the three hypostatic relations --- are born in it, one out of another: conjoin, die, and are mortified, one within the other; are fortified and increased, the one by the other" (III.3 ¶67). Also: "differing only, in respect one from the other, as agent, patient, and that universal offspring which is the All in all, without foreign admixture" (III.3 ¶67); "These three, being but One substantially, admit of a perfect hypostatic union" (III.3 ¶27, Vaughan). — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Vaughan (endorsed)*

5. **The active–passive exchange.** See Procedure A8. — *stated; ATWOOD*

6. **The sun is born twice, by Nature and by Art; the work is twofold yet indivisible.** Nature leaves the "sun" imperfect and Art perfects it. The work is two yet one and admits no division except a "mathematical" one. The verse calls this "the primary secret". — "There is no sun but which is twice begot; Nature and Arte the parents first begonne" (III.3 ¶36). Also: "Art then, what Nature left, in hand doth take, And out of one a twofold worke doth make" (III.3 ¶36); "It must be two yet make it one and one, And you do take the way to make it none" (III.3 ¶36); "Lo here, the primary secret of this Arte" (III.3 ¶36). — *stated (two births, Art perfects); interpreted ("make it none"); QUOTED: Enigma Philosophicum*

7. **Number, weight and measure are the dimensions of Nature.** — "Thou hast disposed all things, in number, weight, and measure. --- For these are the length, and breadth, and profundity of Nature, which the Spirit in her emanative Law displays" (III.3 ¶67). — *stated; ATWOOD*

8. **Read for the hidden sense.** The story of the Law is its garment. Every word hides a mystery, and its Soul is the Law itself. Without it nothing is truly known. — "The story of the Law is the vestment of the Law; unhappy he, who mistakes the vestment for the Law itself" (III.3 ¶59). Also: "each word of the Law has a meaning and cloaks a mystery entirely sublime" (III.3 ¶59); "Without which nothing is truly known; whose Experience is All" (III.3 ¶59, ATWOOD). — *stated; QUOTED: the Kabalist (endorsed by ATWOOD)*

9. **The adepts' names explain nothing; approach by analogy with one's own life.** The philosophers keep swapping "Wisdom" and "Stone", which explains the unknown by the more unknown. The thing is beyond and inverse to sense and becomes manifest only when thought, through understanding, focuses it like a lens. Atwood therefore approaches it by analogy with the mystery of one's own life. — "it has been the custom of philosophers to ring the changes from Wisdom to their Stone, and from the Stone to Wisdom" (III.3 ¶51). Also: "a baffling about of terms, ignotum per ingotius" (III.3 ¶51); "which is neither hard nor soft, not tangible nor visible, nor comprehensible by common sense" (III.3 ¶51); "until thought, by understanding (as light by the focus of the familiar lens, producing combustion), has brought it forth into effect and flame" (III.3 ¶51); "Thus considering the inverse problem, analogically however, we arrive at a more familiar conception" (III.3 ¶52). — *stated (the critique, the approach); interpreted (the lens: comprehension or production); ATWOOD*

10. **Thought is the principle; God's Thought inverts ours; knowing Him is self-annihilation.** — "Thought is the Principle of all that is; but it is as first Unknown and shut up in itself" (III.3 ¶78, Zohar). Also: "For the Thought which is of God creative is the inversion of our thought; and to know Him in It is self-annihilation in the life which is eternal" (III.3 ¶78, ATWOOD). — *stated; QUOTED: the Rabbi in Zohar; ATWOOD*

11. **Like is known by like; the perceiver is one with the Mind.** — "That which, in thee, seeth and heareth the Word of the Lord, and the Mind, the Father, God, differ not one from another, and the union of these is life" (III.3 ¶54). Also: "for the like is intelligible by the like" (III.3 ¶78). — *stated; QUOTED: Poemander*

12. **Matter is non-being, severed from real being.** — "By a severance from real being, non- being, that is to say matte, s produced" (III.3 ¶77). — *stated; ATWOOD*. How this relates to the One Matter of ¶65: see Open questions.

13. **The visible springs from the invisible.** — "For visibles here are said verily to spring out of that which is invisible, as from the precedent nothingness Something is produced" (III.3 ¶79). Also: "Verbum invisible fiet palpabile et germinabit ut Radix." (III.3 ¶79, the Sybil in Boissard; ours: the invisible Word will become palpable and will germinate like a Root). — *stated; ATWOOD*

14. **Experience is the test.** Philosophy is proved by experience, and the operative revelation cannot be made credible to those without it. — "And if experience be truly, as it is said to be, the proper test of philosophy, then was not theirs the right and true philosophy" (III.3 ¶61). Also: "They who cannot imagine will disbelieve without experience" (III.3 ¶59). — *stated; ATWOOD*

15. **The dissolved body congeals Mercury.** — "Corpus solutum est aqua perennis congelans Mercurium perpetua congelatione." (III.3 ¶21; ours: the dissolved body is a perennial water congealing Mercury with perpetual congelation). — *interpreted (ours); UNCLEAR voice*

16. **Divine I Am = Substant Unity, the source of all increase.** — "in the Divine I Am, is by the Kabalah signified the Substant Unity of all that is; the fountain of Universal nature and her Exemplary Law" (III.3 ¶79). — *stated; ATWOOD (endorsing the Kabalah)*

## Open questions

1. **Physical or inward?** "experience there was present with power and effect in substance to bear it witness" (III.3 ¶3).
   - Physical: the Water is materially real. Basis: "With thy hands thou shalt touch, and with thy eyes thou shalt see Azoth" (¶9); "visibilis ad oculum" (¶10); the Water "visible, tangible, and sensible to every sense, local and finite" (¶73); Stone "visible, sensible to hearing, to smell" (¶50).
   - Inward but real. Basis: the Light appears "in the contrition or distress of the sensible nature in the conscience" (¶24); the Water is the "product of the spiritual poles of mind" (¶73); "This is the union supersentient" (¶15); the vessel is "in their gross body" (¶24).
   - The chapter does not settle this. It is the main question for the method.

2. **What are the "Three"?** "Seek Three in One, and again seek One in Three" (¶9).
   - Body, soul and spirit (same paragraph).
   - Attraction, repulsion and circulation (the threefold secret just before).
   - Possibly also the "threefold Law" of the Vital Spirit's circulation in three periods (¶72, "as before explained"), whose content lies outside this chapter.

3. **Khunrath's warning.** "the animated spirit cannot be conjoined to the body, nor, in the other hand, will the body be reunited to the spirit" (¶9).
   - A warning not to rejoin spirit and body before the separation is rightly done.
   - A garbled or condensed line, in tension with the reunion of the Soul (¶10) and with separate-and-rejoin elsewhere (¶29, ¶60).

4. **"a triune furnace spherically round" (¶11).**
   - A physical apparatus ("experimenting").
   - The threefold person: three principles body, soul and spirit (¶9); the vessel is "in their gross body" (¶24); "Apply nomanual labor" (¶9).

5. **The horse.** "If the horse’s strength be yet denied" (¶11).
   - The "spiritual aid" just named.
   - The force of contrition, the "fiery well-tempered steel".
   - Not explained.

6. **"summit" and "higher places".** "a Light wonderfully about the summit" (¶15); golden water "in higher places" (¶17).
   - The head: "crucifixion of the cerebral life" (¶15).
   - The culmination of the work.
   - Literal high places (the tree-top analogy, ¶17).

7. **"the body of our Sphinx, subdued and contrite" (¶3).**
   - The operator's own sensual nature: compare "subdued" (¶15) and contrition (¶24).
   - The matter carried into the next stage.

8. **Mercury: agent or product?**
   - Mercury = Spirit, acting first on Gold (¶30), versus "the philosopher’s Mercury" that is fixed and projectable (¶32).
   - Versus the Dragon-Mercury (the subject, ¶64).
   - Versus Mercury as the prepared selfhood (¶77).
   - Versus "Mercurius noster" possibly = rational soul (¶81).
   - The text does not reconcile these uses. Readings: one Mercury in different states; or distinct referents under one name.

9. **"Hic est Mercurius noster nobillissimus" and "praeter animam rationalem" (¶81).**
   - "Hic" points back to reason made immortal Light (¶80), so our Mercury is the perfected rational soul.
   - "praeter" read as "except", so Mercury and the rational soul are distinct.
   - No speaker or gloss is given.

10. **Is Salt the Stone or a part of it?** "The Salt of Saturn, the Universal son of Nature" (¶50).
    - The same as "The Son" (the Stone) in the same passage.
    - A constituent principle ("most ancient principle of the Stone").
    - ¶77 makes Salt the resulting Bodily Substance of Sulphur and Mercury.

11. **"Matter" in two senses?**
    - The One Matter, the true element (¶65).
    - "non- being, that is to say matte, s produced" (¶77).
    - Readings: the same Matter viewed as severed from real being; or a distinct, lower sense.

12. **King and queen; lion and eagle (¶68, ¶63).**
    - The active and passive (Sulphur and Mercury), per ¶77.
    - Soul and body ("For soul and body so combine, The spirit interceding", ¶68).
    - Not decoded.

13. **Male and female; the "four precedent degrees".** Male and female (¶31) and the four precedent degrees (¶32).
    - Male and female = the Mercury/Spirit and Gold/Soul "parents" of ¶30.
    - The four degrees = the four figures of ¶31, or four elements united as the "Ethereal Quadrangle"/Tetractys (¶32, ¶35).
    - Not stated.

14. **"It must be two yet make it one and one, And you do take the way to make it none" (¶36).**
    - A warning that dividing the work destroys it.
    - An instruction that right joining resolves the two into a neutral unity ("neuter from all", ¶32).

15. **Death or regeneration?** "in the resolution of the material body of sense" (¶57).
    - Physical death and the soul's ascent afterwards.
    - The dissolution of natural life in regeneration, as Atwood frames the Poemander (¶52).

16. **Who perceives in "self-perceivance"?** (¶52, ¶77)
    - The Fontal Reason perceives itself.
    - The individual perceives itself and so discovers the Reason.

17. **The lens simile (¶51).** "has brought it forth into effect and flame" (¶51).
    - Epistemic: thought makes the subject comprehensible.
    - Operative: thought actually produces or actualizes it. Basis: "creative attributes and powers" (¶52); "Most mighty and surpassing magic of Reflection" (¶79).

18. **"Reflection" (¶79).**
    - Mental self-reflection (¶78 "judge of this by thyself").
    - The reflux of life to its source (the tide image just before).
    - Compare "reflect into" one's Image (¶25).

19. **The two reverse poles of the hidden Logos (¶79).**
    - God's Thought and ours (¶78 "inversion of our thought").
    - The manifest individuality and the Initial germ.
    - Two opposed principles within the individual.
    - Compare "the spiritual poles of mind" (¶73) and Altitude/Profundity "united at their extreme poles" (¶72). The text does not link them.

20. **"sanquinary circulation of the Vital Spirit" (¶72).**
    - Literally in the blood. Compare Boehme's Blood, whose warmth is life (¶24).
    - A figurative, blood-like circulation.
    - Hedged by "it would seem".

21. **"the operation of Venus" (¶60); "mars par Venus" (¶11).**
    - A name for the extracting calcination as one operation.
    - A particular agent called Venus.
    - The chapter glosses neither.

22. **Is ¶60 all Synesius?**
    - The text names Synesius.
    - The footnotes cite Lumen de Lumine, Norton and Eirenaeus for earlier sentences (n41, n42), so it may be a composite.
    - Also: "and not that to dissolve" is probably a misprint for "note" (parallel "Note further").

23. **Time and timing.**
    - Eirenaeus gives day counts: 90, then 30, then 20 days (¶68).
    - Synesius says "demur not at the expense of time" (¶60).
    - Atwood says "until it be the Divine pleasure to make it appear" (¶80).
    - Whether the counts are literal is not stated.
