# Method material — III.2 A Further Analysis of the Initial Principle and Its Eduction into Light

Labels: `[stated|interpreted · voice]`. "Endorsed" means Atwood adopts the cited view as her own. All refs are to III.2.

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The Matter is "our Identity".** The Hermetic material is the practitioner's own essential identity, reached by direct intellectual contact, not by imagination. It is what the Mysteries everywhere denote.
   — "intellectual contact of the Thing itself, which is our Identity. This is the true Hermetic material" (¶7); also "this is the Matter everywhere alluded to, and so often denoted in the Mysteries" (¶7).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **The "one thing in which are all" is the Identity made in regeneration.** God did not make all things from absolute nothing: He made "something", the one thing containing all. Atwood says (hedged: "appears") that this is the Identity produced in regeneration. In Boehme, the first something is a dark cloud condensed into a water, the One Thing.
   — "this One Thing appears to be nothing less or more than that Identity which is made in the regeneration" (¶16); also "God of nothing created something which was made that one thing in which are all" (¶16); Boehme: "this first something was a certain cloud or darkness, which was condensed into water; and this water is that One Thing in which all things are contained" (¶15).
   `[stated · ATWOOD; Boehme endorsed]`

3. **Not common water but a universal oily vapour; a water that does not wet.** The Matter defined by the old alchemists is a subtle, unctuous vapour present everywhere. Sendivogius saw and felt it as a water, white as snow, that did not wet the hands.
   — "it is no common water, but an unctuous mineral vapour, universally subsisting" (¶7); also "the Water of our Sea, the Water of Life, not wetting the hands" (¶7; again n43); "that water was as white as snow" (¶7, n43).
   `[stated · the old Alchemists and Sendivogius, endorsed by Atwood (rhetorical "is not this the Matter")]`

4. **A white salt, a magic earth with its own fire.** The same Matter is the Arabians' Flower of White Salt; it is a "salt" that is a water that does not wet. It is Van Helmont's living white soil that no tool has dug, and the magic earth in which the recreative fire dwells. It is also the Magnesia shown in the Mysteries.
   — "this salt is the Water that wets not the hands" (¶8); also "Flos Salis Albi --- the Flower of White Salt" (¶8); "the white sand, Quellum, which Van Helmont speaks of as manifesting itself forth in a vivid vital soil, which spade or mattock never pierced" (¶8); "This is the true magic earth wherein is the recreative fire" (¶8); "that identical Magnesia that was exhibited in the Mysteries" (¶8).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

5. **Mythic figures name the same material.** It is what Orpheus bade be taken from the cave of Mercury; the power borne by the Centaur Cheiron; what Silenus personifies; Pan. These images are asserted, not explained.
   — "that recommended by Orpheus to be taken in the cave of Mercury, and carried in both hands away" (¶7); also "this is the power borne by the Centaur Cheiron" (¶7); "The same in Silenus is satirically personified the most venerable preceptor of the God of Wine; and this is Pan" (¶7).
   `[stated (identities) · ATWOOD; images undecoded]`

6. **The hidden ground seems "nothing" only to us.** Before it is conceived, the principle is nothing to consciousness. It looks like nothing because we cannot conceive what does not appear, not because it is absent. It is a divine, active darkness: visible yet incomprehensible.
   — "Ain, i.e., to say, Nihil quoad nos; nothing or no entity as respects us" (¶10, Reuchlin); also "it is nothing as respects the consciousness before it is conceived" (¶15, ATWOOD); "Dionysius styles it caligo divina, because, as he says, it is obscure and humanly incomprehensible, though visible indeed" (¶13); "Tenebrae activae" (¶13, Lumen de Lumine).
   `[stated · Reuchlin, Dionysius, Lumen de Lumine; Atwood concurring ¶15]`

7. **The Kabalists' "nothing" is the Cause of causes.** Creation "from nothing" means from primitive non-Being: not conceivable as cause or essence, yet the first Cause, prior to the universe and not bodily, which is Wisdom. Atwood says this agrees exactly with the Hermetic philosophy.
   — "by non-Being they mean that which is neither conceivable as cause nor as essence, but yet is in fact the Cause of causes" (¶32); also "it is that which we call the primitive non-Being; because it is anterior to the universe" (¶32).
   `[stated · the Rabbi, endorsed ¶33]`

8. **One universal pure matter between the causes and their gross effects.** The Greeks who knew the Whole and the Alchemist agree on one universal pure matter, the origin of all living and bodily things. Though hidden, it can be made perceptible to the senses and shown in practical effects. It lies between the first causes and their gross effects.
   — "a certain pure matter subsisting about Intelligibles which is universal, and the proved origin of every vital and corporeal existence" (¶46); also "though occult in nature, it can be made manifest to sense even, and exhibited in divine and practical effects" (¶46); "a certain pure and divine Matter mediately subsisting between primary and secondary causes and their gross effect" (¶49).
   `[stated · ATWOOD ¶46; Plotinus/Porphyry summary, endorsed ¶49]`

9. **The persisting subject of bodies.** Under all change there is one subject. It differs from bodies, takes on forms and reflects them, and is never destroyed. Below the corruptible elements is a living, formless, immortal principle.
   — "the subject-matter however --- that which receives the forms and reflects them --- always remains the same" (¶47); also "a Nature primarily vital which is also formless, indestructible and immortal, as being the principle of other things" (¶49).
   `[stated · Plotinus/Porphyry summary, endorsed]`

10. **Celestial elements: an earth within heaven.** The ancients' "elements" are not the common elements but prior, living, celestial ones. The Hermetic "earth" lies within their "heaven", as Plato's does, and is not dead soil. The highest heaven is the Spirit of universal nature, the invisible limit of every body, known only when consciousness is joined with it. Hermes calls it Quintessence. Plato's perfect, ageless Whole is this ethereal Quintessence.
    — "their investigation concerned the prior Elements of Life; which some, openly distinguishing, call the Celestial Elements" (¶36); also "call it magical, the Earth of the Wise, Olympus, Our Earth" (¶36); "in the conscious alliance only becomes known" (¶36); "The same is called by Hermes, a Quintessence" (¶36); "Plato yet more plainly alluding to the Etheral Quintessence" (¶38).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

11. **Known only by negation.** The Matter is said to be one yet all things, everywhere yet nowhere. That is, it is no sensible thing, not a multitude, and not an object of reflective thought. It has no positive attributes. It is the last and lowest effect, in which the attributes of the First Cause are reversed, and the text says this is proved in experience.
    — "neither form, nor quality, nor corporeal, nor reason, nor bound; but a certain Infinity" (¶53, the Greeks); also "they say it is one so far as one; depriving the idea of multitude and dual, i.e., reflective, contemplation" (¶52); "the last and lowest effect, in which all the attributes of the First Cause are not only deficient but reversed" (¶54, ATWOOD).
    `[stated · the Greeks endorsed; Plato, Aristotle (¶53); ATWOOD ¶54]`

12. **The First Matter is the abyssal Darkness God brings forth.** At the new birth, Light springs out of a Darkness. God brings that Darkness forth as First Matter, to give contrast to His revelation.
    — "the abyssal Darkness, which is then alone before its Creator; and is brought forth by Him for a First Matter" (¶56).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

13. **The pure Passive Nature: an imageless mirror.** The subject must be wholly passive to intellect, as a mirror is to images. It is pure and undetermined, empty to thought yet in being the source of all life. Atwood identifies it with the alchemists' Matter. (That the "ethereal glass" is this Passive Nature is inferred; see Open questions.)
    — "the Passive Nature ought indeed to be a thing of this kind, pure and indeterminate" (¶60); also "so is this ethereal glass to intellect" (¶60); "as it were nothing in the Idea yet in Being all things" (¶60); "Such was the Matter so often celebrated by the Alchemists" (¶60).
    `[stated (Passive Nature = Matter) / interpreted (ethereal glass = Passive Nature) · ATWOOD]`

14. **The Identic Salt: what survives the dissolution.** An indestructible "salt" survives the full dissolution of the being. It is the true seed of this world and the next, and it immediately renews the dissolved parts.
    — "adepts have concluded this Identic Salt to be the true grain, since it cannot be annihilated, but survives the wreck of the whole dissolute Being throughout" (¶20); also "this mystical substance, this root of the world, returning immediately upon the dissolution of its parts, renews them" (¶20).
    `[stated · ATWOOD (reporting adepts)]`

15. **First matter as an all-containing water.** In Valentine, the first matter is a water containing all, which the drying of its own fire and air forms into earth. In the analysis, metals reduce to "water", which is their first pure matter.
    — "this water therefore containing all, which by the dryness of its fire and air is formed into earth, is the first matter of all things" (¶19, Basil Valentine); also "all metals, likewise, may be reduced into water, that is, into their first pure matter" (n38, ATWOOD).
    `[stated · Basil Valentine; ATWOOD]`

16. **The First Matter is what remains beyond all things.** When one has passed beyond every image and thing, what remains is what is prior to all things: the First Matter.
    — "that which thus remains to him who passes beyond all things, is That which is prior to all things, and the First Matter" (¶66).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

17. **The Philosophic Subject must have the ass's qualities.** Patience, content with little, simple understanding, a clean heart without anger, willing labour: these must be found in the "Philosophic Subject", or it cannot do the work.
    — "All which qualifications are, in their similitude, very applicable and necessary to be found in the Philosophic Subject" (¶64); also "There is no creature, concludes the panegyrist, that is so able to receive divinity as an ass" (¶64, Agrippa).
    `[stated · ATWOOD applying Agrippa]`

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **The Fire of Intellect, with Life.** Only Life, and the Fire of Intellect purified and strengthened at its source, finds the true Matter.
   — "no other Fire but that of Intellect, sublimed and fortified in its efficient source" (¶62); also "Life and nothing but Life" (¶62).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **The recreative fire inside the Matter.** The fire living in the magic earth acts of itself. It draws the Matter's parts together, solidifies them and stops their flowing.
   — "this fire binds the parts thereof spontaneously to himself, coagulates them, and stops their flux" (¶8).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

3. **Each thing's own imperceptible fire.** In the vessel, everything was moved by its own fire. That fire was internal, not sensible, and ethereal at that stage.
   — "All things were actuated with their own appropriate fire; but in very truth imperceptible as yet, and ethereal." (¶25).
   `[interpreted · the Adeptus (Grasseus, n26)]`

4. **The Tincture.** In the Celestial Light is a spirit or property. It is like water but burns like oil, and it is the source of the material world and of all growth, present in metals and stones. It generates nothing itself but makes others fruitful, and it does nothing of its own accord. It is as subtle as thought, and human thought arises from it. All things arose from it through the Divine Imagination. Atwood endorses the testimony.
   — "a Substance like water which yet is no water, but such a spirit or property: but it burns more like a kindred oil, and is called by many the Tincture" (¶16); also "this Tincture is the source of the material world, and gives to all essences virtue to grow" (¶16); "amongst all the children of nature it only is a virgin, and has never generated anything out of itself" (¶16); "It is not God, but it is God’s friend; for it works not of itself" (¶16); "For it is as subtle as the thoughts of ma, and his thoughts do even arise from thence" (¶16); "All things are thence arisen through the Divine Imagination" (¶16).
   `[stated · Boehme, endorsed ¶17]`

5. **The central magnet's whole voluntary force.** Only the whole willed force of the "central magnet", called "its proper reason", can bring the freed, imagining vitality to rest. Whose will this is, the operator's or the vitality's own centre, is open (see Open questions).
   — "To arrest this imaginative flux of freed vitality, we may well conceive that it need the whole voluntary force of the central magnet; and that this alone, which is its proper reason, can compel it to repose" (¶24).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

6. **The Efficient present in all.** Number and figure produce nothing real without the active cause that is present in everything. The highest causes have a surplus power that is present, hidden, even in material things.
   — "nothing solid, long, short, or square, not the smallest grain of sand without the Efficient which is in all" (¶43, ATWOOD); also "an exuberance of power is always present with the highest causes" (¶49, Iamblichus); "it is nevertheless present with material natures immanifestly" (¶49).
   `[stated · ATWOOD; Iamblichus]`

7. **The primitive Ether: an attracting emptiness, an understanding.** The primordial cloud, a concentration of Divinity, gives birth to the primitive Ether. That Ether is an attracting vacuum, or understanding, which draws the central active power out into will and operation.
   — "the primitive Ether, which is the pure attracting vacuum, or understanding whereby the central efficient is drawn forth to will and operation" (¶13).
   `[stated · some modern Kabalists]`

8. **The "superior stars" are the Ideas of the Divine Mind.** Things below are infused from them as light and start as a watery vapour.
   — "the superior stars [i.e., Ideae Divinae Mentis]" (¶19); also "infused from the superior stars as light, and to take their beginning from them in the form of an aqueous cloud, fume, or vapour" (¶19).
   `[stated · bracket gloss ATWOOD in Valentine's text; English rendering of the Latin is ours]`

9. **The dew of light.** A white dew shaken from the head of the hidden principle wakes the dead to new life. The chapter links it to its theme only through whiteness and does not apply it to the practice.
   — "From his head he shakes a dew which awakens the dead, and brings them to new life" (¶3); also "Thy dew is the dew of light" (¶3).
   `[stated · Rabbi Ben Jocahi]`

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **Experiential self-knowledge.** Only those who have turned inward by direct experience to know themselves can recognize the ground. The Whole is grasped in self-knowledge, by contact and intuition rather than reasoning. A faith able to tell true testimony from fanatical delusion is also needed.
   — "They only who have entered experimentally within to know themselves, have been satisfactorily able to recognize the ground" (¶4); also "an experimental conception of its reality in the self-knowledge" (¶46); "not so much therefore an object of reason as of contact and intuition" (¶46).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **Assimilation to the principle.** The seeker cannot know the principle from outside but must become like it, rising to what is most unified in nature. Whoever cannot do this should not investigate real Being.
   — "if any one wish to discover the One Principle he must become first assimilated to it" (¶50, Proclus); also "If, however, you are unable to keep pace with it, and to become assimilated to the whole Intelligible Nature, you should not investigate anything pertaining to real Being" (¶59, Porphyry).
   `[stated · Proclus, endorsed; Porphyry]`

3. **Purgation through trial.** Before the vision, the adepts got past the illusions of the senses and of selfhood, fought every opposing deception, and were tested again and again. Olympiodorus reads Plato's Tartarus as the place of the soul's trial.
   — "they had passed the turbulent delusions, not of sense only but of the selfhood, and having combated every sinister disguise in opposition, were proved and reproved" (¶28, ATWOOD); also "Tartarus is the place of the soul’s trial, where the balance of existence is struck, and imperfections are made manifest" (¶9, Olympiodorus).
   `[stated · ATWOOD; Olympiodorus]`

4. **Reason as charioteer.** Without reason in control, the soul takes on the temper of the body. Ruled by reason, it does not yield and is assimilated to the Supreme virtue.
   — "souls, in which reason does not preside as a charioteer, are changed according to the subject temperament of the body; but when reason has the dominion, the soul does not yield" (¶9).
   `[stated · Olympiodorus]`

5. **Inward prayer and freedom from worldly business.** Fervent inward prayer frees the mind from earthly concerns so that it can attend to the inspirations needed to search nature.
   — "an abundance of thoughts, which my internal fervent prayer to God suggested, had set me loose and wholly free from terrene business" (¶19).
   `[stated · Basil Valentine]`

6. **Total commitment: risking one's life.** Boehme kept seeking through repeated rebuffs and resolved to risk his life rather than stop, and then the gate opened. Atwood warns against casual seekers who risk nothing, and against relying on an alert mind or an entranced sleepwalker.
   — "I resolved, he says, rather to put my life to the utmost hazard than to give over and leave off; the gate was opened to me" (¶16, Boehme); also "without risking anything, or devoting his life, as philosophers did formerly, to the pursuit" (¶62, ATWOOD); "some entranced sleepwalker will reveal the truth to him" (¶62).
   `[stated · Boehme; ATWOOD]`

7. **The Light of God and the regenerate eye.** Natural ability cannot understand the Tincture without the Light of God, but to those "in the Light" it is plain. Boehme sees with eyes "wherein life generates", the new man, as against his external man.
   — "the understanding and capacity is not in nature’s own ability without the Light of God" (¶16); also "it is very easy to be understood by those who are in the Light, to them it is easy and plain" (¶16); "with those eyes wherein life generates in me, for the new man speculates into the midst of the astral birth or geniture" (¶16).
   `[stated (Light of God) / interpreted (new man as regenerate faculty) · Boehme]`

8. **Receptive non-striving.** The required attitude is still receptivity, not intrusion. Actaeon intruded and was hunted by his own thoughts. The sleeping Endymion received the goddess's presence.
   — "to the sleeping Endymion she vouchsafed her willing presence, and the vast benefits of her love" (¶57); also "Actaeon, who for his presumptuous intrusion was disgraced by the goddess and hunted thereafter by his own distracted thoughts" (¶57); "Quaeres multum et non invenies" (¶58).
   `[interpreted · ATWOOD]`

9. **Human aid is indispensable.** Nature has no hands, no sufficient intellect and no free will. Without a human operator's magical solution and strengthening, the Spirit cannot leave its outer forms.
   — "Without the magical solution and human aid to fortify, the Spirit is not able to forsake her extraneous forms" (¶61); also "she has no hands (45), nor intellect sufficient, nor a free will" (¶61).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

10. **Dormant faith, roused by images.** We are asleep in bodily darkness, yet a faith in us still answers to the truth. The Neoplatonists' images serve to wake it.
    — "images are admirably adapted to stimulate that faith, dormant as we now are in the corporeal darkness" (¶46).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter gives no single ordered procedure. It gives a compact formula (solution, sublimation, fixation, ¶64) and several partial sequences (¶20, ¶28, ¶54–56, ¶64–66). They are listed here in chapter order.

1. **Point of Light, unknown until separated.** In the Zohar, manifestation begins with a point of Light. While that point stays inward and undivided, the principle stays unknown. It develops only by separating things within itself. This is cosmological, not applied to practice in the chapter.
   — "he begins by producing a point; but, whilst the point of Light remains within subjective and inseparate, he is unknown" (¶3).
   `[stated · Zohar]`

2. **The white work.** The matter whitens step by step: hoary, then silver, then snow-white. The fire is to be governed with great care. Full whiteness completes the "white work".
   — "your matter will become white as snow. Then is your elixir perfect as to the white work" (¶5); also "he matter will become white like a hoary man, whose aged complexion resembles ice; it will also whiten more afterwards, like silver" (¶5); "Govern your fire with a great deal of care" (¶5).
   `[stated · author of the Lucerna Salis, endorsed]`

3. **Bodies turned into the vapour; the vapour is the Stone.** The work reduces bodies to the universal vapour, and that vapour is the Stone. How the reduction is done is not stated here.
   — "Bodies, therefore, say they, are to be turned into such a vapour, and this vapour is the Stone" (¶7).
   `[stated · the old Alchemists, endorsed]`

4. **Dark Aleph made light.** When the hidden principle actually shows itself in human apprehension, its darkness becomes light.
   — "is dark Aleph converted into light Aleph" (¶10); also "Si tu, Deus meus, illuminaveris me Lux fiunt tenebrae meae." (¶11).
   `[stated · Reuchlin]`

5. **Kindling of divine enthusiasm in the seer.** Paradise opens in the seer, and the Light from which all things come is created when divine enthusiasm is kindled in union with its source.
   — "by that kindling of Divine enthusiasm in conjunction with its source, the soft lenient Light was created" (¶12); also "So Paradise was opened in the Seer" (¶12).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

6. **Return from an artificially induced void of life.** The Identity is made in regeneration. A dissolved, void state of life is induced by art, and the Identity forms as life comes back from it into experience. For the creature, this is the first divine manifestation out of the abyss: the Spirit held in a new, closed circulation.
   — "the reprocedure into experience out of the dissolute void of life when artificially induced" (¶16); also "the first divine manifestation out of the abyss, when the Spirit is brought forth into a new circulatory confine" (¶16).
   `[interpreted · ATWOOD]`

7. **Valentine's allegory: flight, fall into the sea, rescue, mountain.** Valentine flew too near the sun, fell into the sea, was saved by one who stilled the waters, and climbed a mountain that rose from the abyss. In the generation he describes, light enters as vapour, is dried until water loses its rule, and fire and air then rule. The chapter does not decode the images one by one. Atwood says only that the whole allegory displays the analysis (next point).
   — "when I soared too near the sun, my feathers with its vehement heat were consumed, and I fell headlong into the depths of the sea" (¶19); also "one hastened to my assistance who commanded the waters should be still; and instantly in that deep abyss appeared a most high mountain" (¶19); "this vapour is dried, that the wateriness may lose its dominion, and the fire next by help of the air retain the ruling power" (¶19).
   `[interpreted · Basil Valentine]`

8. **The metaphysico-chemical analysis: separation, introspection, reunion.** The analysis of the "Universal Subject" has three moments: the vital elements are separated, looked into, and reunited in their ethereal accord.
   — "In this allegory the whole metaphysico-chemical analysis of the Universal Subject is displayed --- the separation, introspection and reunion of the vital elements in their ethereal accord" (¶20); also "[viz., in the metaphysico-chemical analysis]" (¶19).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

9. **Binding the freed, changing matter.** The freed substance runs through endless forms, and the philosophers chain it while it contends with fire. Entreaty does not work; only force and bonds do (¶23, Latin, our rendering). ¶24 then names the central magnet's will as what arrests the flux.
   — "The philosophers chain up their matter with a strong chain or band when they make it to contend with fire" (¶22); also "that mode of binding is said to be best which makes use of manacles and fetters" (¶22).
   `[interpreted · Hermes; ATWOOD]`

10. **One thing, slightly helped, strengthened by degrees.** In the vessel, all diversity comes from one thing with very little help. It is strengthened gradually and at last settles into one body.
    — "so many things, and diverse in kind, and of such perfect particulars, should proceed from one only thing; and that, with very small assistance" (¶25); also "the artist faithfully affirmed to me that all those diversities would settle at last into one body" (¶25).
    `[stated · the Adeptus (Grasseus)]`

11. **Experimental ingress to the Vital Radix.** The adepts entered the vital root by direct experiment, not theory, and found the universal origin of nature, intellectual and material, arising within themselves.
    — "by an experimental ingress, as they acknowledge, to the Vital Radix, have discovered the catholic original of nature" (¶28); also "through the arising spectacle of the Creative Majesty within themselves" (¶28).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

12. **Philosophic fermentation: threefold division.** Life itself goes through the fermentation. Its substance is wholly changed and divided into three principles (Van Helmont's Salt, Sulphur, Mercury) as unlike parts are separated and the forms of the "Medial Spirit" are put out.
    — "as Life passes through the philosophic fermentation, its substance is entirely transmuted, and the threefold property is developed, with a dividing of the heterogeneous parts, by an extinguishing of the forms and properties of the Medial Spirit" (¶28); also "resolved into these three principles, which Van Helmont also calls Salt, Sulphur, and Mercury" (¶28).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

13. **Near-annihilation, new seed, new generation.** After the division comes an almost total destruction of the old life. At its extreme it draws in a new seed and a new generation begins. This is the road to the "Night of Hippocrates", which leads into the "Day of Orpheus".
    — "a procedure towards a radical destruction, almost annihilating the components of the former life, which at length, in its extreme exigence, draws a new seed to begin a New Generation" (¶28); also "this is the way of the recedure to the Night of Hippocrates, leading thenceforth into the Day of Orpheus" (¶28).
    `[stated (destruction/new generation) / interpreted (Night = destruction, Day = new generation) · ATWOOD]`

14. **Ascent by gradual assimilation.** Atwood endorses the ancients' claim that a practical method exists. By gradual assimilation one rises from forgetful bondage to a more or less direct view of the first Cause. The profane did not know it.
    — "there are methods by which an ascent may be affected from the oblivious bondage of this existence, and, through a gradual assimilation, to a survey more or less immediate of the Causal Source" (¶33); also "belonged to an experience and method of observation, unknown to the profane multitude" (¶33).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

15. **Segregating the ethereal on the ruins of mortal life.** The art separates the ethereal principle out of ordinary, composite mortal existence.
    — "which art once taught them to segregate, and establish upon the ruins of this mortal and dislocated existence" (¶38).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

16. **Casting off the superfluous; sacrificing the old nature.** The separated Psychical Quintessence throws off what is superfluous, and the old nature is sacrificed so that it can begin anew. The hecatomb symbolizes this. Approaching the highest secrets requires expiation.
    — "cast off the superfluity, sacrificing the old nature to begin anew" (¶44); also "neither are the secrets of the highest causes approached without a mean of expiation" (¶44).
    `[stated (casting off, expiation) / interpreted (hecatomb = this sacrifice) · ATWOOD]`

17. **Artificial corruption; the mystical analysis, inward and outward.** Corruption, above all artificially induced corruption, shows that things are composites over a persisting subject. Atwood reads Plotinus' pot-to-gold-to-incorruptible-water passage as the mystical analysis, which holds in two senses. Inwardly, the "iron spirit" in the blood is cleansed and "aurified", that is, illuminated. Outwardly, a metal's radical moisture, worked on by a fermenting agent, passes into a more perfect form.
    — "This therefore Corruption manifests (especially the artificial)" (¶48, Plotinus summary); also "an iron pot should be resolved into gold, but gold into water; and the water, being incorruptible, will require no analogous process" (¶48); "Here Plotinus doubtless makes an allusion to the mystical analysis" (n38); "by a reducation of the iron spirit in the blood, it becomes cleansed from its foreign oxide and aurified --- that is, illuminated by contrariation of its Form" (n38); "The radical moisture of the metal likewise, obeying the fermentive virtue of such a test when applied, may be made to pass away, as the tradition runs, from its own Form into that which is more integral and perfect" (n38).
    `[stated (n38 glosses) · ATWOOD; interpreted (artificial corruption as part of the art) · Plotinus summary]`

18. **The Theurgic Art makes the pure Matter manifest.** The pure divine Matter is made manifest and communicated through the Theurgic Art, in secret visions. The procedure is not described.
    — "by means of the Theurgic Art it is made manifest and imparted through arcane and blessed visions" (¶49).
    `[stated · Plotinus/Porphyry summary, endorsed]`

19. **Withdrawal into one's own essence.** Plotinus rouses himself out of bodily awareness, turns toward himself, detaches from externals and retires into the depths of his essence.
    — "I am roused from body and converted to myself, and being separated from externals, retire into the depths of my essence" (¶50).
    `[stated · Plotinus]`

20. **Contrary predicates gather the mind.** Paired contradictions (one yet all things; everywhere yet nowhere) are a deliberate device. They strip away notions taken from outside and leave the mind collected and neutral.
    — "on endeavoring by means of contrary peculiarities to gather the mind up into a neutrality about itself" (¶52); also "in order to exterminate from the apprehension those notions which are externally derived" (¶52).
    `[stated · the Greeks, endorsed]`

21. **Ablative reasoning: through reason into ultimate ignorance.** Matter is known by subtraction, negating every predicate of Being, so that touching it is a kind of not-knowing. Reason is used and then passed through, not discarded.
    — "not slighting reason indeed, but passing through it, beyond every bound and finite probability in order to conceive that kind of ultimate ignorance, which is the Infinity of Life" (¶54, ATWOOD); also "by a sort of defective or ablative reasoning" (¶53, Plato); "to pass into contact with it, is to be in a certain respect ignorant" (¶53, Aristotle).
    `[stated · ATWOOD; Plato; Aristotle]`

22. **Obscure oblivion: the Original of Life.** With no object, and with all thought and reflection sunk, the mind's eye becomes one with darkness, as the eye without light does. This darkness is the origin of life and stands to true Being as night to day.
    — "that obscure oblivion which is the Original of Life" (¶54); also "so the mental eye, observant of no attracting object, thought, reflection, and all that in sensibles resembles light being submerged" (¶54); "as silence to sound, as night to day" (¶54).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

23. **The Night of Matter, and the recoil into true Being.** In the Night of Matter the mind passively receives the formless. The intellect, having already analysed Being into its parts, sinks into the Abyssal Subject: it feels but no longer understands. Pained by the void, the soul pulls back, gathers round its last remaining unity, and passes from nonentity into true Being.
    — "When therefore the mind is in the Night of Matter" (¶55); also "sinking into the Abyssal Subject, feels, but understands not her intellection any more" (¶55); "the soul retracts, rallying about her last deserted Unit, and not enduring any longer to stop at nonentity, becomes into true Being" (¶55).
    `[stated · UNCLEAR (the philosopher or Atwood)]`

24. **The new birth: Light out of the abyssal Darkness.** At the new birth the extremes meet, and Light springs out of the dark abyss.
    — "extremes are said to be present at the new birth, when Light springs forth to manifestation out of the abyssal Darkness" (¶56).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

25. **Rest in one's own essence.** Investigate nothing else and settle in your own essence: you are then assimilated to the intelligible whole. Drop the idea of being "greater" and you become universal, as you were before.
    — "being established in yourself and in your own Essence, you will be assimilated to the Intelligible Universe" (¶59); also "omitting this idea of greatness, you will become universal, as you were universal prior to this" (¶59).
    `[stated · Porphyry, cited in support]`

26. **Searching Nature experimentally.** The adepts of many faiths searched Nature experimentally with their own reason, found the Matter, and used it.
    — "having searched into Nature by their proper Reason experimentally, found Her’s; and used it" (¶60).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

27. **Opening before the Stone.** The subject must first be opened, that is dissolved, before the Stone can be made.
    — "Ought they not first to be opened and prepared by the most ancient cook of the planets?" (¶61).
    `[interpreted · Nature rebuking Madathan, endorsed]`

28. **Dissolution of the Vital Spirit.** The true Matter is found by dissolving the life-spirit and releasing it from its natural tie. This is to be done the way the wise mean it, not sophistically and with no corrosive (our rendering of the Latin).
    — "a dissolution of the Vital Spirit and alienation of its natural bind" (¶62); also "Nullo corrosivo adhibito" (¶63); "Et nequaguam sophistico" (¶63).
    `[stated (¶62) · ATWOOD; interpreted, from our translation (¶63) · Lucerna Salis Phil.]`

29. **Solution, sublimation, fixation, repeated.** The whole art is three operations. Solution dissolves and liquefies the enclosed Spirit. Sublimation volatilizes and washes it. Fixation, after calcinations, reunites it in a more permanent form. These are repeated many times, at great cost of bodily and mental labour.
    — "solution, sublimation, and fixation" (¶64); also "after calcinations there is a reunion into a more permanent form of Being" (¶64); "these processes are reiterated many times, and many labours of body and mind have to be undergone" (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

30. **Reduction to simple Being (the ass).** The Sphinx carried into the temple on an ass signifies that the subdued nature must be brought down to a simple state of being, without passion, will, imagination, purpose or reflective thought.
    — "carried within the temple upon the back of an ass" (¶64); Atwood's gloss: "the simple estate of Being to which such a nature is to be reduced by deprivation of all passion, will, imagination, purpose, or reflective thought" (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

31. **Patient suffering under the cross of dissolution.** The ass also stands for the long suffering that follows. Wisdom appears only once the conquered elements go back through dissolution.
    — "the conquered elements return under the humiliating cross of dissolution" (¶64); also "bearing and bringing forth the burden of the divine mystery" (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

32. **Leaving all the statues: entering the Adytum.** The aspirant leaves the temple statues behind to enter the inner sanctuary. In the same way, the mind must give up every image and thought, self-made or impressed from outside, to hold the simple Unity of Light. Plotinus: first become established in Intellect and separate yourself from the body and its defilements.
    — "leaves behind him all the statues in the temple" (¶66); also "separate body from the man and its defilements" (¶66, Plotinus); "should first become established in Intellect, when he will e able through it, as an image, to behold the Archetype" (¶66).
    `[stated · ATWOOD; Plotinus, endorsed]`

33. **Experiment carries out the inversion.** Logic only circles reason turning back on itself; the experiment actually performs that turn.
    — "experimentally proving, it effects that inversion" (¶67).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **Demonic forms in vision at first.** The Matter first appears in visions of demonic forms. It is not known until the mind's eye, turned back and purifying itself, reaches its source.
   — "which in demoniacal forms is at first in vision made apparent, nor known until the eye of mind, regardant and purifying, meets its First source" (¶7).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **Whiteness.** The ground shows as white, all colours united. The matter whitens to snow (point 2 of the procedure). The water that does not wet is snow-white. The dew of light is white like diamond. True Being is white.
   — "As all colours in their prismatic unison are white, just so is the Universal Nature, described as appearing in the evolution of her Fontal Light" (¶5); also "the aspect of this dew is white, as the diamond is white, the colour which contains all" (¶3); "The Platonists have declared true Being to be white" (¶9); "that water was as white as snow" (¶7).
   `[stated · ATWOOD; Ben Jocahi; Platonists; Sendivogius]`

3. **Protean colours and forms.** Once freed, the substance shows the observing eye a run of lights and colours: green to red, red to black, then countless colours. It also takes endless forms: vegetable, stone, animal, gem, metal. In the vessel, the salt turns white, red, yellow, purple.
   — "Proteus-like runs from one complexion of light into another, and from this colour to that" (¶20); also "as he runs forth from green to red and from red to black, receding thenceforth into a million of colours and transmigrating species" (¶20); "one while into a vegetable, and then into a stone or into some strange animal" (¶22); "now white, now red, then yellow and purpled, and, as it were, chamletted with various colours" (¶25).
   `[stated · ATWOOD (¶20); the adept, Fama (¶22); Grasseus (¶25), colours not explained]`

4. **A world in miniature, circling in the vessel.** The matter in the sealed vessel moves in a circle and shows an image of the whole world: earth amid clear waters, fruits, metals.
   — "a certain mass moving circularly, or driven round about, and representing the very figure of the great world" (¶25); also "the earth was to be seen standing of itself in the middest of all, compassed about with most clear waters" (¶25).
   `[interpreted (that this is the matter in the vessel, per ¶26) · Grasseus]`

5. **Signs of the Mercury/Lunaria.** Its water rises against the fire of nature, shines by night and is viscous by day. Not explained.
   — "whose water gets up against the fire of nature, and shines by night, but by day has a glutinous, viscous faculty" (¶25).
   `[stated · Grasseus]`

6. **Dream-like vision of creation.** In a state "without the day", Van Helmont saw the earth from its void creation to the flood. Atwood calls this perceiving the simple element of nature. Such images are read in the inner "sky of our Archaeus", from the soul's own starry heaven or from a bodily spirit.
   — "at length without the day and beyond the beginning of the night, I saw, as in a dream, the whole face of the earth" (¶17); also "by a mysterious hand he was led along into a perception of the simple element of nature" (¶17, ATWOOD); "in the sky of our Archaeus, aspectual Ideas are deciphered" (¶17).
   `[stated (vision) / interpreted (Archaeus explanation) · Van Helmont]`

7. **Dismay, pain at the void, fear.** At the far end of the descent the intellect is dismayed, understands only obscurely, is pained by the void and fears being put outside the order of things.
   — "Intellect, having already analyzed and separated the component parts of Being, becomes dismayed about the sensation of her extreme life" (¶55); also "pained with the void of the retreating infinitude (such being the divine decree), and as if afraid of being placed out of the order of things" (¶55).
   `[stated · UNCLEAR]`

8. **Felt, not known.** The subject cannot be pictured as a thing or as nothing. It is met only as felt experience: consciousness passing back into its source without a limiting idea.
   — "may be felt indeed, not known --- but as an escape of consciousness into its primal source without ideal limitation" (¶60); also "every imagination concerning it will be false" (¶60).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

9. **No subject and object.** While the union lasts, knower and known are not two.
   — "there are not two things as of subject and object in the consciousness, but the life understanding and the light understood are one" (¶66); also "at one and the same time illuminates and is illuminated" (¶66).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

10. **Vaughan's First Matter.** An eye-witness description of "the same Matter": fat, pearl-bright and crystal-clear, a vast stream falling in dead silence, an oil of watery complexion. Vaughan was told it was the First Matter. Not decoded.
    — "A fat mineral nature it was, he says, bright like pearls, and transparent like crystal" (n43); also "they came down with a dead silence like the still soft air" (n43); "it was not common water but a certain kind of oil of a watery complexion" (n43).
    `[stated · Vaughan, adduced by Atwood]`

11. **Four concentering winds.** The poet's image of the four winds meeting is offered as an apt figure of the arising celestial element. Not explained.
    — "the arising circulation of Mature from the four concentering winds" (¶36).
    `[interpreted · ATWOOD citing a poet]`

## Impediments and dangers

1. **The worldly, outward-bound mind.** Ordinary consciousness cannot even imagine occult truth and calls it fanciful.
   — "the worldly mind is shut out from the imagination even, of occult truth" (¶4); also "who are chained to these exterior surfaces, content with the bare tradition of a life to come" (¶12).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **Consciousness cut off from its essence.** Natural waking consciousness is separated from the essence beneath it. It perceives nothing of the true self and cannot reach true Being by ordinary reasoning.
   — "consciousness in this natural life of ours is separated off from the antecedent essentiality, so that we perceive in reality nothing of our true selves" (¶50); also "by no ordinary process of rational contemplation is the mind able to conceive of this nature or the infinitude of true Being" (¶50).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

3. **Imagination.** Imagination hinders. Only those who look into the mind without it can credit the adepts. Picturing the Eternal Essence as located or related to something puts a fantasy between it and the seeker. Every imagination of the Matter is false.
   — "able to glance freely, without imaginative hindrance, into the capability of mind" (¶28, ATWOOD); also "you separate yourself from the perception of it, by receiving as a veil the phantasy which runs under your conjectural apprehension of it" (¶59, Porphyry); "every imagination concerning it will be false" (¶60).
   `[stated · ATWOOD; Porphyry]`

4. **Striving defeats itself.** The First Matter flies from whoever tries hard to see it. Thought bounds and so misses the Infinite, and desire is opposed to the presence it wants. The First Matter is probably present but goes unperceived by self-active effort.
   — "the desire it, in this instance, diametrically opposed to the presence of the thing desired" (¶57); also "flies from him, indeed, who wishes intently to behold; for the thought, as circumscribing boundary, eludes the Infinite" (¶57); "it is rather probably present, but is not perceived by him who strives self-actively to comprehend it" (¶57).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

5. **Selfhood.** "Non-being" is glossed as the subjective selfhood. Whatever it adds makes one poorer, and dismissing it restores sufficiency.
   — "When, therefore, anything is added from non-n\being (i.e., from the subjective selfhood) a place is afforded to poverty as an associate" (¶59); also "Hence, dismissing non-being, you will then become sufficient" (¶59).
   `[stated · Porphyry (the gloss may be Atwood's)]`

6. **Abstraction without experiment.** Pure logic (Kant, Fichte, Schelling) reaches the threshold, the void, but cannot realize it. Logic cut off from experiment turns on itself and decays into empty dispute. The Oracle warns against stooping toward the precipice.
   — "stopped short nevertheless of objective realization on their own ground; there being arrested, faithful and as it were in view, without a means of passage to the promised shore" (¶31); also "losing this substantial ground and aim therefore, the Organon began to work upon itself" (¶42); "Stoop not down, for a precipice lies below in the earth" (¶15); "But there thy intellectual notions check When in this path exploring" (¶14).
   `[interpreted (¶31: what is lacking is experiential contact; ¶15 precipice) / stated (¶42) · ATWOOD; the Oracle; Parmenides]`

7. **Lapse back into discursive reason; difficulty of carrying it out.** The state is lost when the mind falls back into step-by-step reasoning. What is seen inwardly is hard to bring into the external man and into words.
   — "falling off from Intellect into the discursive energy of reason" (¶50, Plotinus); also "I could very hardly apprehend the same in my external man, and express it with my pen" (¶16, Boehme).
   `[stated · Plotinus; Boehme]`

8. **Acting against nature.** Any operation against nature makes the Tincture escape, and once gone it does not naturally come back.
   — "if anything be done against the right of nature, then it readily flies away" (¶16); also "when it goes out of a thing it comes not into it again naturally, but it stays in its ether" (¶16).
   `[stated · Boehme]`

9. **Nature holds no pure Matter and cannot free herself.** Even the purest natural thing is mixed with forms brought in from outside. Nature is magically bound and cannot loosen the coagulation that shuts in her Inner Light. Left alone, she circles back into herself.
   — "the purest is defiled with the imagination of Forms externally introduced" (¶61); also "all is mixture and an adulterous manifestation" (¶61); "Nature is bound magically, nor is she able of herself to loosen the bond of coagulation" (¶61); "generate in monotonous retrogression, always circulating into herself" (¶61).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

10. **The spirit's initial resistance.** At the start, the spirit is earthy, heavy and full of fancies, and it rebels at its source.
    — "terrestrial, heavy, fantastic, and proves rebellious everywhere at its Source" (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

11. **Impurity and mixture repel Divinity.** Nothing defiled, unstable, unreceptive, selfish or impure can draw down the Divine.
    — "nothing that is defiled by information, or inconstant, or impassive, or selfish, or impure can attract Divinity" (¶64); also "All mixed unguents are hateful to Minerva." (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD; Callimachus (n48)]`

12. **Vicarious sacrifice is useless.** An outward substitute offering does nothing while the "Prototypes" it stands for go on feeding in the Philosophic Field.
    — "the vicarious dedication of huge beasts will not avail whilst their Prototypes remain feeding and fattening in the Philosophic Field" (¶44).
    `[interpreted · ATWOOD]`

13. **Hiddenness.** The First Matter is invisible in nature, so few find it. The first unity is concealed in its own generation.
    — "It is invisible in nature and therefore there are few that find it" (n43); also "the first unity is occultated in its generation and does not appear" (n43).
    `[stated · Vaughan]`

## The result

1. **Real contact, not dream.** The discovery is real. Fantasy and desire are dropped and taken up into direct intellectual contact with the Thing.
   — "every phantastic desire and imagination is alienated and merged in intellectual contact of the Thing itself" (¶7); also "the discovery of it is no dream, if we may believe the experienced" (¶7).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

2. **The only True Thing, the happiest life.** What was nothing to consciousness becomes the one True Thing. It is known only negatively and is the perfect possession of the happiest life.
   — "it becomes that only True Thing of which we can affirm nothing, whose theology is negative; but which constitutes the perfect possession of the most happy life" (¶15).
   `[stated · Dionysius, endorsed]`

3. **Boehme's vision: ground and groundless, the three worlds within.** In a quarter of an hour Boehme saw the ground and the groundless void and the Three Worlds in himself. He then wrote from an inner book whose three leaves are the Principles of Eternity, and says it was the Spirit of God, not he, who did it.
   — "the Bysse, or ground or original foundation; and the Abysse, that is without ground, or fathomless or void" (¶16); also "I knew and saw in myself all the Three Worlds" (¶16); "in one quarter of an hour I saw and knew more than if I had been many years at the University" (¶16); "my book hath only three leaves, the same are the Principles of Eternity" (¶16).
   `[stated · Boehme]`

4. **The Tincture mastered: metals transmuted.** Though imperceptible, the Tincture can be overpowered and used. Pure, it makes gold of iron or copper and multiplies.
   — "it may well be overpowered and used, especially in metals: there it can of itself, being pure, make gold of iron or copper, and makes a little grow to be a great deal" (¶16).
   `[stated · Boehme]`

5. **Fixity in the Final Cause.** The changes go on until the Matter has worked out its own restoration and Art fixes it permanently in its final end. The diversities settle into one body.
   — "until the Matter has worked out its own restitution and is brought by Art into the supernatural fixity of its Final Cause" (¶22); also "all those diversities would settle at last into one body" (¶25).
   `[stated · ATWOOD (hint of Democritus); Grasseus]`

6. **The clarified hypostasis: Forms arise of themselves.** In the purified substance all forms arise spontaneously, really and not in imagination. This is the true Genesis of Light.
   — "this clarified hypostasis (shall we not believe in it?), is the stage of all Forms, and here they are spontaneously produced" (¶26); also "as the true Genesis of Light" (¶26).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

7. **Light meeting Light: the Causal Truth.** The vision of the Causal Truth comes when the inner light meets the light it seeks and knows itself alone. It unfolds a threefold creative law: mineral, vegetable, and the Divine Image in man.
   — "when Light meeting Light, apprehends itself alone" (¶28); also "the triple mystery of its creative Law throughout, from the infernal motive wheel which is the origin of the mineral kingdom, through the whole intermediate paradisiacal vegetable growth, up to the final concord of the Divine Image in man" (¶28).
   `[interpreted (Light meeting Light = inner light meeting its object, cf. ¶31) / stated (triple law) · ATWOOD]`

8. **The Psychical Quintessence.** What is found is a psychical quintessence, the "Tincture of the Sapphiric Mine". It is threefold and equal to the whole dissolved compound it came from.
   — "that miraculous Psychical Quintessence, known to the wise as the Tincture of the Sapphiric Mine" (¶44); also "being in its own threefold segregated essentiality equal to the whole dissolute compound whence it arises" (¶44).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

9. **Light in the ultimate analysis: all reducible to gold.** The final analysis of anything yields Light, its formal essence, which gold has most of. Hence all things can be brought to "gold".
   — "Light, which is the formal essence of all things, and most abundant in gold, is found in the ultimate alchemical analysis of every existing thing" (n38); also "All things may be reduced to gold according to this doctrine" (n38).
   `[stated · Albertus Magnus via Becher, endorsed]`

10. **A receptacle of Divinity.** The pure Matter becomes a complete vessel in which the divine shows itself.
    — "an entire and fit receptacle for the manifestation of Divinity" (¶49).
    `[stated · Iamblichus]`

11. **Identity with a divine nature.** Plotinus' withdrawal ends in seeing an admirable beauty, becoming one with a divine nature, and resting.
    — "I energize according to the best life and become the same with a nature truly Divine" (¶50); also "I then perceive an admirable beauty" (¶50); "fix myself in this sublime eminence as in an ineffable harbour of repose" (¶50).
    `[stated · Plotinus]`

12. **Matter returned to consciousness through the Freed Will.** The Matter stays "Nothing" until it comes back into consciousness through a freed will. Then it shows Life, Wisdom, Plenitude and the Supreme Cause. "Rational Boundary" is not explained.
    — "until returning by its Rational Boundary in the Freed Will to consciousness, t makes manifest the Life, Wisdom, Plenitude, and Supreme Cause of all" (¶60).
    `[interpreted · ATWOOD]`

13. **Wisdom made manifest after dissolution.** Wisdom appears only after the patient suffering under the cross of dissolution (procedure point 31).
    — "the conquered elements return under the humiliating cross of dissolution" (¶64).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

14. **The soul arrives at itself; union with the Efficient leaves a remnant; contents come back raised.** Turning back from non-being, the soul finds itself. Whoever becomes one with the Efficient keeps something of it. After the union, earlier mental contents come back as secondary things, raised and multiplied.
    — "it arrives not at another thing but at itself" (¶66); also "whoever thus becomes One by mingling with the Efficient, will have a remnant of it with himself" (¶66); "there recurs That also which was in the mind prior to the union, exalted and multiplied" (¶66).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

15. **The generative power reunited with mind.** The first movement of the withdrawn ("ebbing") life back from its farthest retreat brings the estranged generative power back into union with mind. The Platonists call the soul's first such motion "the true origin of matter".
    — "this primary motion of the ebbing life from its ultimate recessure recreates" and "the Generative Virtue, which was alienated, becomes re- united to mind" (¶66); also "her first motion towards this from her ultimate artificial recessure is the true origin of matter" and "when the generative virtue is drawn up into intellectual alliance with the medial life and light" (¶9).
    `[stated (¶66) · UNCLEAR; interpreted (¶9 referents) · the Platonists]`

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Whiteness is the simple ground of diversity.** All things in God's hand are white, a simple ground on which things can be recreated without confusion.
   — "all things in the hand of God are white, says the Magian, He colours them according to His pleasure" (¶5).
   `[stated · Paracelsus, endorsed]`

2. **Mind and matter are consubstantial.** The sages' different first principles (fire, water, earth, Intellect, Being) differ only in expression. The substance is one, mind and universal matter together.
   — "the mind is not without the matter (the universal element we mean), nor that matter without the mind" (¶35); also "all things, however various in manifestation, are consubstantial in their Cause" (¶35); "the substance alluded to by them all is the same" (¶34).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

3. **Like knows like.** To grasp the Indefinite, the knower must become indefinite. Reason then passes out of itself into a void of thought.
   — "if the similar is perceived by the similar, the Indefinite also will be apprehended by the Indefinite" (¶53); also "will pass out from itself into an undefined void of thought" (¶53).
   `[stated · Plotinus]`

4. **Self-knowledge requires losing all other knowledge.** To know oneself, all other knowledge must be removed. Then the self too is wiped out as it is drawn up into the First Cause.
   — "For Self-knowledge is impossible unless every other knowledge is deprived" (¶56).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

5. **Death is the way of Life.** The descent into the Night of Matter and nonentity is the way into true Being.
   — "Death is the way of Life" (¶55).
   `[interpreted (scope) · ATWOOD]`

6. **The fall teaches.** The soul's descent is not wasted: it carries back experience of its fallen state, which makes the good clearer.
   — "For the experience of evil produces a clearer knowledge of good" (¶50).
   `[stated · Plotinus]`

7. **One magnetic chain from darkness to light.** Being is one continuous chain, with infinite light at one end and the darkness of Matter at the other: the ladder from Tartarus to Heaven.
   — "this darkness, therefore, to be conceived at the opposite extreme of the magnetic chain, which is extended a non gradu ad non gradum" (¶54); also "this is that ladder of Celsus and of Zoroaster which reaches from Tartarus to the highest Heaven" (¶54).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

8. **The bounding hypostasis is the Absolute Identity.** The ground that abstract reason reaches only as empty "pure Being" turns out, when touched by the inner light, to be the Absolute Identity, prior to the split of subject and object. Logic reaches by inference what the Rabbis reach by ontological experiment. It is the same truth, the one "in light", the other "in life".
   — "that very hypostasis which bounds reason in transcendental abstraction, when met by contact of the inquiring light within, is that Absolute Identity which it seeks after" (¶31); also "that truth which common logic arrives at by abstraction as an inferential necessity, is the same which the Rabbis, ontologically experimenting" (¶29); "the one in light, the other in life, the one by inference, the other in Absolute Identity" (¶67).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

9. **Revelation held in consciousness becomes efficient.**
   — "fontal revelation which, when entertained in consciousness, becomes efficient" (¶28).
   `[stated · ATWOOD]`

10. **Presence with oneself is presence with Being.** Being present to oneself, that is to one's rational part, and absent from externals, is being present with true Being.
    — "to be present with that which is present with himself, that is to say, with his rational part, and to be absent from that which is external to him" (¶59); also "when any one is present with that which is present in himself, then he is present with true Being, which is everywhere" (¶59).
    `[stated · Porphyry]`

11. **The receptive nature must be pure.** The Passive Nature must be free of images and mixture so that it reflects the Divine Light without distortion.
    — "that there may be no falsehood or commixture of images, but the Truth only" (¶60).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

12. **One thing yields all; every part holds the whole.** The unit must be absolute, the One that is the fountain of all being. Each part holds the whole in proportion, as a fragment of loadstone or a single spark does.
    — "it is necessary that the point or unit should be established as something absolute" (¶45); also "the One of these philosophers is the Fountain of all being" (¶45); "every portion of existence to be continent and comprehended proportionally of the Great Whole" (¶45); "each particular spark of fire contains the principle and developing force of the entire kindred element" (¶45).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

13. **Twofold order; matter from mind.** Sensible things are images of an intelligible exemplar, and matter is the offspring of mind.
    — "a twofold order of natural procedure --- the one intelligible, and the other sensible" (¶38); also "matter likewise is the progeny of mind" (¶40).
    `[stated (¶38) / interpreted (¶40) · ATWOOD; Anaxagoras endorsed]`

14. **Two natures: changing and immutable.** One nature is sensible and always changing. The other, the principiating substance, never changes. Atwood cites this for arresting the flux.
    — "the other nature or principiating substance is incorruptible, immutable, and always subsistent" (¶24).
    `[stated · Reuchlin]`

15. **Corruption belongs only to composites; formless matter has worth.** Change and corruption show a persisting subject. Formless matter is valuable when it is turned toward the divine exemplaries. Establishing this pure Matter is the precondition for any participation in superior Being.
    — "This Indefinite is not therefore everywhere to be despised" (¶49); also "unless a foundation of this kind be first established" (¶49).
    `[stated · Plotinus/Porphyry summary, endorsed; who speaks in the last clause is UNCLEAR]`

16. **The union is real experience.** Plotinus treats the union as a real entry of the understanding into its source, not a spectacle or a theory.
    — "a true experimental ingress of the understanding essence to its source" (¶66).
    `[stated · Plotinus, endorsed]`

17. **The doctrine is veiled; read its spirit.** The teaching was hidden in fables, symbols and poetry, or in abstruse diction, because it is hard to put into words and to keep it from the vulgar. The Mysteries forbade revealing it. The Neoplatonists speak of principles, never of the practice. Aristotle opposed the letter of his predecessors, not their spirit.
    — "Many, therefore, adopted fables, symbols, similitudes, enigmas, and the license of poetry they also called in aid" (¶40); also "forbearing direct allusion to the Practice, they feared less to speak of Principles and the procedure of mind" (¶46); "his opposition is uniformly directed to the letter rather than to the spirit of their doctrine" (¶41).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

18. **Contraries consubsist in the Divine Original.** Opposites (small and great, deficient and exceeding) always coexist in the origin. Hence the Matter is both a thing and nothing, and imagining it as either one alone is false.
    — "Add to this, that contraries are always consubsistent in the Divine Original" (¶60); also "every imagination concerning it will be false, either that it should appear in the conception as any particular thing, or contrariwise as nothing; for it is both" (¶60).
    `[stated · ATWOOD]`

19. **Aristotle's logic as a channel for the Divine Intellect.** In its original intent, the logic fixed experiment and unfolded the birth of the Divine Intellect into manifestation. That is "the syllogism" worth seeking.
    — "by means of the categories, as by a congenial channel, the birth of the Divine Intellect into life and manifestation" (¶41); also "That was the syllogism so important to be sought after" (¶42).
    `[interpreted · ATWOOD]`

20. **The theorem as symbol.** Pythagoras' theorem stands for the separated essence being equal to the whole it came from.
    — "the subtendent of a right-angled triangle is equivalent to those parts which contain it" (¶44).

## Open questions

1. **Is the subject the operator's own being, or a substance in a vessel?** For an inward subject: "our Identity" (¶7); the Identity "made in the regeneration" (¶16); "within themselves" (¶28); the "Philosophic Subject" must have the ass's moral qualities (¶64); the First Matter is what remains to the mind beyond all images (¶66). For an outward one: the consecrated vessel, furnaces and colours (¶25), which Atwood calls "the whole Hermetic laboratory" (¶26); the Matter "can be made manifest to sense even" (¶46); metals reduced to water (n38). n38 says the doctrine holds "both in an internal and in an external sense", which suggests both apply. The chapter never says how they relate.
2. **Central magnet (¶24).** (a) The operator's will and reason, binding the freed vitality, matching the philosophers' chains of ¶22–23 and the word "voluntary". (b) A centre within the vitality itself ("its proper reason"). The referent of "its" is not fixed.
3. **"Artificial recessure" / "dissolute void of life when artificially induced" (¶9, ¶16, ¶66).** (a) The soul's farthest natural withdrawal into body, from which it first turns back (¶9's context of bodily temperament). (b) A withdrawal produced by the art: "artificial" (¶9) and "artificially induced" (¶16) both point to an operation, and ¶66's "ultimate recessure" uses the same image. The chapter does not describe how the void is induced.
4. **The Protean colours (¶20–22, ¶25).** (a) Inner visions of the freed vitality: "before the regardant eye", "imaginative flux" (¶24). (b) Physical colour changes in a substance: pearl, gem and metal (¶22), and the salt in the vessel (¶25). Related: the first appearance "in demoniacal forms is at first in vision made apparent" (¶7).
5. **"Clarified hypostasis" (¶26).** (a) The matter in the Adeptus's vessel (¶25–26). (b) The inner vital substance reached by "experimental ingress" (¶28), since ¶31 uses "hypostasis" for what the inner light meets.
6. **Endymion's "sleep" vs. the rejected sleepwalker (¶57, ¶62).** ¶57 commends the sleeping Endymion's receptivity, while ¶62 dismisses reliance on "some entranced sleepwalker". (a) "Sleep" means non-striving receptivity in general. (b) It means a sleep-like state with thought submerged (¶54). Either way, ¶62 shows it is not trance-revelation by another.
7. **Night of Hippocrates / Day of Orpheus (¶28).** (a) Night = the radical destruction; Day = the new generation (immediate context). (b) Night = the whole fermentation; Day = the vision of Light (¶26 "Genesis of Light", ¶28 "Light meeting Light"). The names are not explained.
8. **Valentine's flight and fall (¶19).** (a) A coded stage: excess heat leading to dissolution into the "sea", part of the "separation". (b) Narrative only. ¶20 says the whole allegory displays the analysis but maps no single image.
9. **Do the chapter's sequences describe one process?** These sequences are given separately: separation, introspection, reunion (¶20); fermentation, threefold division, near-annihilation, new seed (¶28); passing through reason, Night of Matter, recoil into true Being, new birth (¶54–56); solution, sublimation, fixation, then reduction to simple Being and the cross of dissolution (¶64); leaving all images, then union (¶66). Their parallel structure suggests one process, but the chapter never states that they are the same.
10. **Prototypes in the Philosophic Field (¶44).** (a) The gross "old nature" of the compound that must itself be sacrificed (same paragraph). (b) The operator's unpurified nature, which outward rites cannot expiate ("Psychical" Quintessence, ¶44; self-knowledge, ¶46).
11. **"the most ancient cook of the planets" (¶61).** (a) The agent of the dissolution, possibly the Fire of Intellect of ¶62. (b) Some other, unnamed agent. The text makes no identification.
12. **"the strength of sin is in the Law of dual generation" (¶62).** (a) Ordinary generation, which mixes forms (¶61 "Nothing therefore is generated truly"). (b) The duality of subject and object, which is overcome in union (¶66).
13. **"defiled by information" (¶64).** (a) By forms impressed from outside (¶61). (b) By acquired learning, against the ass's "simple, indifferent understanding" (¶64).
14. **"Ethereal glass" (¶60) and "ethereal element" (¶53).** Are these the Passive Nature or Matter? The context favours yes, but neither identity is stated outright. The glass could instead refer to the "Divine Original" of the preceding sentence.
15. **"Death is the way of Life" (¶55): scope.** (a) Narrow: the descent into nonentity leads to true Being. (b) It extends to the obliteration of selfhood in the First Cause (¶56, introduced by "For").
16. **The precipice (¶15).** (a) A warning not to grasp the infinite ground with discursive notions (¶14). (b) A warning against descending toward the earthly.
17. **Saturnian Monarchy of the Freed Will (¶7).** Is the thing "once circumscribed in Intellect" the Monarchy or the Matter (Pan)? Compare the "Freed Will" through which the Matter returns to consciousness (¶60).
18. **"dedicated to the Ternary" (¶25).** (a) The threefold principle of ¶28 ("triple mystery", "threefold property"); compare the "threefold" Quintessence (¶44). (b) An unexplained triad.
19. **Voice boundaries.** Where the unnamed philosopher stops and Atwood resumes in ¶55; whether the final sentence of ¶66 (Generative Virtue reunited) is Plotinus or Atwood; whether "(i.e., from the subjective selfhood)" in ¶59 is Porphyry or Atwood's gloss. These decide whether the Night of Matter sequence and the closing result are Atwood's own claims.
20. **"He" who leaves the labyrinth (¶22).** (a) The Identic Salt of ¶20, personified. (b) The spirit (n22 "Ubi vero spiritus excessit"). They may be one, but the text does not say so.
21. **"Found Her’s" (¶60).** (a) Nature's own Reason, parallel to "their proper Reason". (b) Nature's Matter.
