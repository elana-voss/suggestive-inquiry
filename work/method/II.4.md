# Method material — II.4 The Mysteries (Concluded)

## The subject (what the work is done on)

- **The soul holds within itself everything it needs to know.** Knowledge is not brought in from outside. It is already present and has to be woken.
  "all things are in us psychically, that is to say, in the efficient Reason of our life" (II.4 ¶29). Also: "the knowledge of all things is compact in you" (II.4 ¶19, Agrippa); "he created the soul as a reasonable tree full of forms and knowledges" (II.4 ¶19, Agrippa).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n22) and Agrippa (n13). Atwood adopts both.

- **The thing to be freed is a hidden Reason, latent in our present life, like fire in unlit fuel or gold in ore.** The alchemists give names to the enclosing ore, or else to the hidden thing itself; which of the two is unclear (see Open questions).
  "the total Reason is in this life of ours hidden, as the fire in fuel that is not kindled, or as gold in the dark ore unseen" (II.4 ¶39). Also: "our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone, celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam, in which we are all now as dead" (II.4 ¶39).
  — The hiddenness is stated. The referent of the alchemical names is interpreted (basis: ¶39, where the names follow the fuel/ore similes, and "in which we are all now as dead" fits a container). ATWOOD.

- **A prepared vessel, cleansed in advance, in which the new light is born.** Atwood gives a chain of names: Flammel's seven mountains, Esdras' outgoings of paradise, Sendivogius' Planetary Sphere, the Sapphiric tincture. She says this is the "cleansed Augean", meaning the cleaned stable of Hercules' labour.
  "that most famous tincture of the Sapphiric Mine: which is in truth the cleansed Augean, the already prepared medial receptacle of the newborn light" (II.4 ¶9). Also: "Those seven mystic mountains, whereupon grow the roses and lilies, are the outgoings of paradise mentioned in Esdras" (II.4 ¶9).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **A purified "new vessel" is consecrated in Light.** Atwood says it is not a lamp or teaching device. It holds, sustains and measures, and it lights a perpetual flame inside itself that burns in balance around the fuel of life. The text does not name the vessel.
  "the new vessel, purified and holy, is brought into the Eleusinian temple, to be consecrated in Light" (II.4 ¶18). Also: "a far more pellucid gas-lamp, an infallible gasometer, able to hold and sustain and measure simultaneously, even within itself to kindle a perpetual flame" (II.4 ¶18); "shining in equilibriate constancy about the sufficient fuel of all life" (II.4 ¶18).
  — interpreted (what it is: see Open questions); ATWOOD.

- **R.C.'s fountain, an image the text does not decode.** A perennial, still-dripping fountain in which Diana bathes, with Venus as handmaid and Saturn going before. It describes the "habitation" that philosophers visit. The text warns it says too much to the one who understands and too little to the inexperienced.
  "fontem aperuit perennis aquae adhuc stillantem, in quo Diana se lavat, cui Venus ut Pedissaqua et Saturnus ut anteambulo, conjunguntur" (II.4 ¶11). Also: "Intelligenti nimium inexperto minimum hoc erit dictum" (II.4 ¶11). Both translations are ours.
  — not decoded; QUOTED: R.C. (via Vaughan).

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

- **Union is brought about by real divine media acting of themselves, not by thought.** The Synthemata are the theurgic aids. Once the preparation is complete they do their own work, and the operator's intellect adds nothing.
  "it is indeed by divine Media, and not a mere conception of the mind or metaphysical abstraction, either, that Theurgists are conjoined to the Divine nature" (II.4 ¶31). Also: "the Synthemata, i.e., the Theurgic aids and media themselves, perform by themselves their proper work" (II.4 ¶31); "the things which properly excite the Divine will being the Divine Synthemata themselves" (II.4 ¶31).
  — stated; ATWOOD (first sentence), then QUOTED: Iamblichus (n23), endorsed.

- **The rites act through the "passive Ether" as their medium.** They unfold the newly conceived life of the soul. The chunk does not say what the Ether is. ¶29 speaks of "their Ether" in souls and ¶40 of the "ethereal vehicle", which suggests the soul's own subtle vehicle.
  "So did Theurgic rites, by the medium of the passive Ether, unfold the embryo vigour of her newly conceived life" (II.4 ¶36).
  — The medium is stated. That it is the soul's vehicle is interpreted (basis: ¶29 "equilibriate circulation in their Ether"; ¶40 "the ethereal vehicle"). ATWOOD.

- **Without the "Pontic Medium" and theurgic help, ordinary consciousness cannot be transcended.** The theurgic ascent goes "through appropriate media" and cannot happen without them. The text does not explain the "Pontic Medium".
  "Without the Pontic Medium, without Theurgic assistance we are unable to transcend the consciousness of this life" (II.4 ¶49). Also: "by Theurgic rites, an ascent was made through appropriate media and a gradual assimilation, which without these could not be effected, to the knowledge of the First Cause" (II.4 ¶56).
  — stated (the necessity); "Pontic Medium" is unexplained. ATWOOD.

- **Fire is the purest element. It is utterly quiet, present unseen in everything, and moves only when God's will stirs it.** It contains the reasons of life and understanding. In man it is the rational soul, infused into the vital spirit, and it is what makes him a microcosm.
  "In it are the reasons of life and understanding, which are distributed in the first infusion of man’s life, and these are called the rational soul" (II.4 ¶39). Also: "This soul was of that most pure fire, infused by God into the vital spirit" (II.4 ¶39); "This nature is of all the most quiet and like unto a chariot, when it is drawn, it runs; when it is not drawn, it stands still" (II.4 ¶39).
  — stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius, "the adept philosopher" (n26). Endorsed: Atwood builds the hidden-Reason passage directly on it.

- **All the gods are aspects of one Demiurgic Fire, shown through Intellect in Light.** Minerva is its lucid understanding, Apollo its golden ideality, Venus its beauty and attraction, Mars its concentrated force, Diana its compact purity, Mercury its varied thought and imagination, and Jupiter its fabricative virtue.
  "what are all the gods but manifestations of this same Fire germinating through the projecting energy of Intellect distinct in Light?" (II.4 ¶26). Also: "Minerva is sometimes called Phosphor, as being the bearer and measure of the Demiurgic Fire" (II.4 ¶26).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **A central attracting power moves the ascent: the "central magnet" or "Rational Magnet".** When Reason is set up as monarch, the lower powers move only with it. Atwood also likens it to the Presters of Intellectual Fire following their First Cause.
  "when the central magnet moves to the ascent" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "the Almighty force that moves about the Rational Magnet, and how the Presters of Intellectual Fire follow in radiant order the will of their First Cause" (II.4 ¶38); "to these Intellectual Presters of Intellectual Fire all things are subservient by the persuasive will of the Father" (II.4 ¶33, Chaldaic Oracle).
  — interpreted (basis: ¶38, which sets the magnet beside the monarch whose "leading motive" the lower powers follow; ¶36, where they "venerate this Reason"). ATWOOD. See Open questions.

- **The Golden Branch is something drawn only to its native soil.** It goes in through death and darkness, takes root, and blossoms into a light that spreads through the rest of life. The text does not name it.
  "that Golden Branch, which, attracted from the first to its native soil, indifferent to every other lure, through death and darkness enters; and taking root at last, gathers strength to germinate and blossom, as a radiant flower" (II.4 ¶7).
  — interpreted (basis: it directly follows "nothing but the subtlety of an immortal essence, and that by regeneration, can pass into immortality", ¶7). ATWOOD. See Open questions.

- **The goddess of the Mysteries is Nature, the universal life-source.** Atwood says Apuleius' praise of Isis applies to the Universal Nature.
  "I, who am nature, the parent of all things" (II.4 ¶17, Apuleius). Also: "All which is readily admissible of the Universal Nature" (II.4 ¶19, Atwood); "whose one divinity the whole orb of the earth venerates under a manifold form" (II.4 ¶17).
  — stated; QUOTED: Apuleius, endorsed by ATWOOD at ¶19.

- **The Meadow is the generative power of life and holds the first causes of life and of forms. Its water is the symbol of vivification.**
  "the Meadow is the prolific power of life, according to Plato, and of all various reasons, and is the comprehension of the First Efficient causes of life and the generation of Forms" (II.4 ¶16). Also: "bear water which is the symbol of vivification" (II.4 ¶16).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n8), citing Plato.

- **The light seen in the true initiations was the Light of Life itself, which could be kindled and strengthened.** It was not a drug effect or a stage effect, and the visions were not mere symbols.
  "was the Light of Life which these could kindle and fortify" (II.4 ¶56). Also: "nor were the visions or gods attending on those Mysteries dead images, nor mere symbols" (II.4 ¶56).
  — stated; ATWOOD. Whether "these" means the initiations or the ancients is open.

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

- **Purification through sacred rites and pure living, with piety and justice.**
  "being purified by sacred institutions and enjoying undefiled fruits" (II.4 ¶3, Proclus). Also: "even for us, who are initiated and who have learned to perform all acts of piety and justice" (II.4 ¶16, Aristophanes).
  — The rites and fruits as a precondition are interpreted (basis: ¶3, where they are the conditions under which Hercules "at length obtained an establishment among the god"). QUOTED: Proclus and Aristophanes, both used approvingly.

- **Purity of mind and heart, no prejudice, and contemplation of the One.**
  "persevere in purity if mind and heart" (II.4 ¶40). Also: "suffer not therefore former prejudice to debar thee" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; QUOTED: Orphic Fragment (n28).

- **Lifelong dedication, obedience, worship and chastity.** The promised reward is life extended beyond its fated limit.
  "by sedulous obedience, religious services, and inviolable chastity, you shall know that it is possible for me to extend your life beyond the limits appointed to it by fate" (II.4 ¶17). Also: "the remaining course of your life must be dedicated to me, even to the boundary of your latest breath" (II.4 ¶17).
  — stated; QUOTED: Apuleius (Isis to Lucius).

- **Body and soul must be pure and freed for the visions in Pure Light.**
  "being ourselves pure and liberated from this surrounding vestment, which we denominate body, and to which we are bound like an oyster to its shell" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; QUOTED: Plato, Phaedrus (n18).

- **The operator fulfils the preparatory conditions and then stops working intellectually.** His good dispositions and purity count only as contributing causes ("concauses").
  "when we do not energize intellectually (all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)" (II.4 ¶31). Also: "it is requisite to consider these and all the best dispositions of the soul, and also the purity pertaining, as certain concauses" (II.4 ¶31).
  — stated; QUOTED: Iamblichus (n23), endorsed.

- **Voluntary renunciation of sensual habit, together with actively stirring the divine virtue within.**
  "rising in voluntary abnegation above the evil and sensual habitude of this life" (II.4 ¶52). Also: "by exciting the divine virtue within, they became simultaneously elevated" (II.4 ¶52).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Capacity varies, so results come in degrees.** Good habits of thought and natural inclination make the Spirit fitter "to sublime". Earlier initiations prepare for later ones.
  "the possession of the best habits of thought in this life, and natural inclination, render the Spirit better adapted to sublime" (II.4 ¶54). Also: "there are many degrees of Divine illumination; nor were the rites of Eleusis found to be equally efficacious for all" (II.4 ¶54).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **The knowledge comes only to those whom God has inspired with his Wisdom.**
  "they are manifest to philosophers into whom the incomprehensible Deity has inspired his own Wisdom" (II.4 ¶39).
  — stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius (n26).

- **A penetrating sight that reaches every centre and finds itself in the source.**
  "having a sight like that of Lynceus, penetrating all centers, discovers himself in That finally which is the source of all" (II.4 ¶50).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Secrecy.** Divine things are not to be told to the uninitiated. They endanger unpurified souls, and only those who have perceived them can understand them.
  "that mandate of the Mysteries which forbids that divine things should be divulged to the uninitiated" (II.4 ¶56). Also: "beyond the early danger to unpurified souls, there remains this objection, that such things cannot be understood by the multitude" (II.4 ¶56).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

*Composite order of the chapter (interpreted).* The chapter gives several partial sequences that fit together as follows:
1. descent into generation;
2. purification and discipline;
3. turning inward;
4. the lower vision "in Hades";
5. a death-like liberation into elysian light;
6. Elysian intellection, which is not the top grade;
7. passage through the gods to contemplation of the Unity, with "translation";
8. the Paternal Port, union in identity;
9. repeated ascents and descents until the whole life is irradiated.

Basis: ¶19 and ¶29 put the Hades vision before the elysian light. ¶15 says "though there is a grade above". ¶41 says "Not such as was with science, or that vision that was in Elysium, but more beautiful". ¶39–¶40 have the Unity and translation. ¶52 has the circulations.

- **Freeing the whole person from the bond of birth, in three stages.** Dragging Cerberus up from hell is glossed as this.
  "he dragged up Cerberus from hell, that is to say, he liberated the whole individual entity through a threefold evolution from the bond of its earthly geniture, and established it finally in the most exalted life" (II.4 ¶3).
  — stated; voice UNCLEAR (Olympiodorus or Atwood). The three stages are not named.

- **Two gates: the soul goes down into generation and comes back up to the divine.** This is the framework of the whole work.
  "the northern gate pertains to souls descending into the realms of generation, and the southern to souls ascending to divinity" (II.4 ¶7). Also: "That to the North is pervious to mankind, The sacred South t’ immortals is consigned" (II.4 ¶6, Homer).
  — stated; QUOTED: Porphyry (n2), endorsed.

- **Climb the mountain of the Seven and look down from the top. At the summit is a royal herb named after all three kingdoms.** This chunk does not explain the seven or the sixth.
  "betake thyself to the mountain of the Seven, where there is no plain, and look down from the highest downward to the Sixth, which you will see afar off; in the topmost height, you will find a royal herb triumphing" (II.4 ¶9). Also: "which some call mineral, some vegetable, some saturnine" (II.4 ¶9); "For it is either and all" (II.4 ¶9, Atwood).
  — not decoded beyond the identity chain under Subject; QUOTED: Flammel (n4), with Atwood's gloss.

- **The inward turn of the mind's eye.** The mind's eye stops looking in through natural life from outside. It passes through that life, is purified to its centre, turns and rises, and then, as a unity, takes the whole periphery as a reflector of its own light.
  "having passed through this, purifying to the center, is converted and raised; and, as a Unit, now regards the circumference transitively, including it as an understanding or reflector, as it were, to the focus of her light" (II.4 ¶15). Also: "a fountain, not flowing outwardly, but circularly scattering its streams into itself" (II.4 ¶15, Porphyry).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Dissolving the spirit in its own kind, unmixed, makes the material principle visible.** Atwood reports this as what the alchemists did experimentally. She defers the treatment of these "material rewards", and of "this Water", to later.
  "reproducing the whole material principle to sense and visibility from the dissolution of the spirit in its proper kind without alloy" (II.4 ¶16).
  — The report is stated. Its nature (physical or psychic) is interpreted and open. ATWOOD.

- **Self-Knowledge: in a purified soul the inner light reveals itself, and the mind's eye, watching steadily, is suddenly drawn into contact with it.** Atwood names this the method.
  "This is the method and arcane principle of Self- Knowledge, and the narrow way of regeneration into life" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "whose spontaneous revelation in a purified soul" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Lower stage: the vision "in Hades".** It belongs to the co-nascent spirit and is undergone without much disturbing ordinary life. Its figured images come from the mind's own imagination.
  "to this spirit the vision in Hades is allied which is born be through without much disturbance of the common life" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "such as the mind before beheld in Hades from its own self-shadowing creative fancy" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated (wording corrupt: "born be through"); ATWOOD.

- **Higher stage: a death-like liberation.** When the central magnet moves upward, an "expiration" takes place. There is agony as of death, the circulation oscillates, and the soul, joined with its vehicle, passes free of the body into elysian light. Lucius' rosy crown and his shedding of the ass's hide mean this.
  "when the central magnet moves to the ascent, this expiration is described as taking place; a liberation is effected through agony, as it were of death" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "the circulation oscillates, and the soul, coalescing with its vehicle, transcends free from corporeal hindrance into the elysian light" (II.4 ¶19); "That was the rosy crown of which the Hierophant was to assist Apuleius’ Lucius to partake" (II.4 ¶19); "gently pluck the roses as if you intended to kiss his hand, and immediately divest yourself of the hide of that worst of beasts" (II.4 ¶17, Apuleius).
  — stated; ATWOOD (her gloss of the Apuleius image).

- **Enter into yourself, cast off forgetfulness, wait for the True Light, and pass "from the light to light".** Atwood glosses the steps as the senses, then reason, then faith, then the glorification of all.
  "entering not either into the schools of other philosophers, but into yourself, ye shall know all things" (II.4 ¶19, Agrippa). Also: "Cast out the drink of Lethe, you who have made yourselves drunken with forgetfulness, and wait for the True Light" (II.4 ¶19, Agrippa); "from the light of the senses to the illumination of reason, and from reason through its topmost faith into the substantive glorification of all" (II.4 ¶19, Atwood).
  — stated; QUOTED: Agrippa (n13), with ATWOOD's gloss.

- **Three grades of initiation: telete, then muesis, then epopteia.** In telete one is initiated by the perfective gods. In muesis one sees "with closed eye", that is with the pure soul, through the connective gods. In epopteia one is fixed as a spectator of the intelligible, through the collective gods. All the steps end at the Paternal port.
  "For telete precedes muesis, and muesis, epopteia" (II.4 ¶23). Also: "we view with closed eye, i.e., with the pure soul itself (muoumetha) entire and stable appearances" (II.4 ¶23); "there are indeed many steps of ascent, but all of them extend to the Paternal port and the Paternal Initiation" (II.4 ¶23).
  — stated; QUOTED: Socrates in Phaedrus (n15 cites Proclus), endorsed.

- **Turning inward in stages: first the soul perceives herself, then understanding and the Reason of beings, then, in her innermost "Adytum of Life", the gods "with the eye closed".** Then the faculties are stilled and gathered into union with the One.
  "when she converts herself to herself, she evolves her own Essence, and the vivific reasons which she contains" (II.4 ¶29). Also: "she proceeds into her interior recesses and into the Adytum of Life, as the great theologist declares, she perceives, with the eye closed, as it were, the genus of the gods" (II.4 ¶29); "having our individual powers at rest, to revolve harmoniously round it, to excite all the multitude of the soul to this union" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n22), endorsed.

- **The rites act like a midwife.** They wake the intellect into recollection and bring the whole nature to birth in Light. Wisdom, like a mother, first educates and then sets the soul to practise virtue and theoretic discipline until its final conversion.
  "awakening intellect into reminiscence and filling it with the conscious reasons of things manifest and occult; and as it were by an obstetric hand and action, bringing forth the total nature and ornamenting it with Light" (II.4 ¶36). Also: "exercising him in every virtue and theoretic discipline for the final conversion and accomplishment of his soul" (II.4 ¶36).
  — stated; ATWOOD. ¶37 duplicates ¶36 and is not a separate point.

- **Reason is established as monarch.** When discipline is complete, the lower powers line up around Reason and move only with it.
  "the inferior powers will so venerate his leading motive that they will move only according to his movement, pursuing constantly in observant order his infallible rule" (II.4 ¶38). Also: "the whole inferior powers will range in harmonious concord about their proper rule" (II.4 ¶36, Porphyry).
  — stated; ATWOOD and QUOTED: Porphyry (n25), endorsed.

- **The hidden Reason wakes only when its whole enclosing circulation is dissolved. It then rises reversed, perfect and alone, and this is the alchemists' Salt and Mercury.**
  "nor can be awakened to reminiscence without a resolution of the whole circulatory confine, when I arises identically reverse, perfect, and alone" (II.4 ¶39).
  — The condition is stated. What "circulatory confine" means is interpreted (basis: ¶39, where the Reason is hidden "in this life of ours"; ¶40, where all life but the intellectual is "buried in profound oblivion"). ATWOOD.

- **Ordered passage through all the intermediate causes, the "gods", to contemplation of the highest Unity.** Once every irrational, downward-pulling tendency is overcome, the soul holds reason complete and desires only her own essence in the Absolute.
  "having passed orderly through the whole progression of Intelligible Causes, by the Greeks called gods, were next promoted to a contemplation of their Highest Unity" (II.4 ¶39). Also: "having vanquished every irrational and gravitating inclination, the soul, holding the circle of reason complete" (II.4 ¶39); "possessing all, except her own identic essence, desires this now alone" (II.4 ¶39).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Contemplating the indwelling Unity is the last preparation for "translation".** The rite buries the body up to the head, meaning that all life but the intellectual sinks into oblivion while the intellect rises.
  "His contemplation, then, of the indwelling Unity was the final preparative to translation" (II.4 ¶40). Also: "the whole body should be buried, except the hear; sublimely signifying that the total life, with exception of that which is intellectual, should be buried in profound oblivion" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; ATWOOD. "Translation" is not defined.

- **Become an "Intellectual World", approach the Maker, grow familiar with him through unbroken intellectual energy, and so be united.** Proclus' sequence is wandering in generation, then purification and the light of science, then intellectual energy alone shining forth.
  "For uninterrupted energy about anything calls forth and resuscitates our dormant Ideas" (II.4 ¶41). Also: "For after wandering about generation, after the purification and the light of science; intellectual energy alone, by the Intellect that is in us, shines forth" (II.4 ¶41).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n30), endorsed.

- **Total inner stillness, then an averted, reverent regard, then a return down into intellect and reasoning.**
  "let the air and the universe be still (within us)" (II.4 ¶41). Also: "to remove from ourselves multiform knowledge, exterminate all the variety of life, and in perfect quiet approach near to the Cause of all" (II.4 ¶41); "with nearly closed eyes, adoring, as it were, the rising sun" (II.4 ¶41); "again from this divine tranquility descending into Intellect, and from Intellect, employing the reasonings of the soul" (II.4 ¶41).
  — stated; QUOTED: "the Platonic Successor", endorsed. The closed-eye manner is interpreted (basis: ¶41 "since it is not lawful for any being whatever intently to behold Him"; ¶47).

- **The Intelligible is grasped with the "Flower of thy mind": an emptied mind, a turning eye, not a fixed one.**
  "There is something Intelligible which it behooves thee to understand with the Flower of thy mind" (II.4 ¶43). Also: "not fixedly but having a pure turning eye, Extend the empty mind of thy soul towards the Intelligible" (II.4 ¶47); "for it exists beyond the mind" (II.4 ¶48).
  — stated as instruction. "Flower of thy mind" is otherwise unexplained (interpreted). QUOTED: the Divine Oracle, endorsed.

- **Repeated cycles.** The Vital Spirit goes up and comes down again and again, through Intellect to Wisdom to the First Principle, and gains virtue with each ascent until the whole life is irradiated.
  "it is said to be lawful for the Vital Spirit to descend and ascend in successive circulations until she terminated her flight in the Principle of things" (II.4 ¶52). Also: "proceeding through Intellect to Wisdom, they arrived at the First Principle; and again descending thence, increasing in divine virtue by each ascent, until the total life was irradiated" (II.4 ¶52); "Tunc ire ad mundum archetypum saepe atque redire" (II.4 ¶53; "then to go often to the archetypal world and return", our translation).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

- **When the new light arises, colours lost in darkness come back, the poison is neutralized, and a suspended circulation starts again in conscious balance.** What the colours and the circulation are is not explained.
  "no sooner does this arise than all the vegetable colours, before obliterated in darkness, return to neutralize their poison and restore the suspended circulation to a conscious equilibriate accord" (II.4 ¶9).
  — interpreted ("this" = the newborn light); ATWOOD.

- **At awakening, the dragon shuns the light and the "dead son" lives.** The images follow Lucius waking in fear, joy and sweat as the sun rises. They are not explained.
  "The dragon shuns the sun’s beams which look through the crevices, and the dead son lives" (II.4 ¶18). Also: "seized with fear and joy, and in an excessive perspiration" (II.4 ¶18, Apuleius).
  — not decoded; ATWOOD.

- **The inner light shows itself in a sequence: light with harmonious understanding, then a rarefied radiant form, then sudden contact.**
  "It also emits light accompanied with harmony of intellection, and finally exhibits a form of such rarified effulgence that the eye of mind, all the while faithfully regarding, is drawn to contact suddenly, unable longer to sustain itself alone" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Those who see the Divine Nature become languid, like fish pulled into clear air, and lose the use of their co-nascent spirit.**
  "the initiated, when surveying it, are affected in the same manner as fishes, when they are drawn upwards from the dark and turbid waters into the diaphanous clear air" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "becoming languid as soon as they perceive it, and deprived of the use of their co- nascent spirit" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; QUOTED: Iamblichus (n12), endorsed.

- **A flow of spirit comes of its own accord from the hidden energy of the Elysian life (the river Eridanus).**
  "this indicates the prolific flow of spirit which accedes spontaneously from the occult energy of such a life" (II.4 ¶16).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Test of a true vision: the gods appear as simple light or idea, never as figured images.** Figured images belong to the Hades stage and to the mind's own imagination.
  "reveal themselves each in his particular form or essence of light, but by no means extend themselves as figured phantasms, such as the mind before beheld in Hades from its own self-shadowing creative fancy" (II.4 ¶29). Also: "emits illuminations, true representations of himself" (II.4 ¶29, Iamblichus); "they exhibit to Theurgists a Fire which is itself, to itself, visible" (II.4 ¶29, Iamblichus).
  — stated; ATWOOD with QUOTED: Iamblichus (n20).

- **The lower powers stop moving on their own initiative; their motions dissolve near Reason.**
  "the motions themselves will be dissolved through their proximity to the reasoning power" (II.4 ¶36).
  — stated; QUOTED: Porphyry (n25), endorsed.

- **The divine arrives suddenly and by itself, after a self-forgetting openness.** It is not produced by effort.
  "He comes, says Plotinus, suddenly alone, bringing with him his own empyreal universe and total deity, in one" (II.4 ¶50). Also: "these are preparatory steps only to the self-oblivious amplitude of conception which precedes Him" (II.4 ¶50).
  — stated; QUOTED: Plotinus, with ATWOOD's framing.

- **Certainty comes only from direct contact.** When intellect actually sees, it can affirm, and the affirmation is true for it. Doubters are like a sleeper who, on waking, clings to his dreams.
  "If therefore Intellect should by any means be enabled to come into visive contact with its vision, as if begetting an experience, it would then assert; and the assertion, as respects itself, would be true" (II.4 ¶49).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Visible radiance spreads from the freed centre out to the senses and the body.** Atwood's examples are Bacchus, Osiris, Apollo, Socrates, Orpheus, Achilles, and Jason with the Fleece.
  "diffusing itself from the freed center outwardly center outwardly until it moved the dark circumference of sense itself" (II.4 ¶54). Also: "So Osiris appeared in shining garments, as Apollo, all over radiant" (II.4 ¶54); "and Jason, on his return from Colchis, with the Golden Fleece" (II.4 ¶55).
  — interpreted as a sign of completion (basis: ¶54 "freed center" and "circumference of sense"; ¶50 centre and circumference). ATWOOD.

## Impediments and dangers

- **The passions keep a person bound to the life of the senses and stop the hidden labours from being finished.**
  "being detained by his passions in the sea of sense" (II.4 ¶3).
  — interpreted (basis: ¶3, where Theseus "was unable to finish" for this reason); voice UNCLEAR.

- **Turning outward cuts us off from the higher life without our noticing.**
  "converted to externals, we desert our best life unconsciously" (II.4 ¶9). Also: "Heaven’s exiles straying from the orb of light" (II.4 ¶10, Empedocles).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Foreign impressions, passions and accidents defile the soul's receptive purity, so it becomes unaware of its inner light.**
  "receiving the impressure of foreign forms, passions, accidents, and evil generations, the passive purity is defiled and obscured, and unconscious of that inner light which lives in reality" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Forgetfulness, the drink of Lethe, together with ignorance.**
  "oblivion, the mother of ignorance, stepped in" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; QUOTED: Agrippa (n13), endorsed.

- **Phantasms.** Varied images may be thrown up before the gods appear. They come from nature and the demiurgic intellect, not from the gods.
  "if various phantasms are hurled forth before the presence of them; nor if they, being uniform, should in their appearance be multiform" (II.4 ¶29). Also: "For nature and the demiurgic intellect extend corporeal formed images of things corporeal, sensible images of things intelligible" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n21).

- **Theory alone fails.** Abstract thought, contemplation, desire, enthusiasm and abstraction are only preparation.
  "what would hinder those who philosophize theoretically from participating of this union? Which they do not" (II.4 ¶31). Also: "Theoretic contemplation, sensible attraction, continuity of active thought, all are alike inadequate" (II.4 ¶49); "But not reason, nor enthusiasm, nor ardent desire, nor an intellectual conception, nor abstraction, as we are taught, conjoins theurgist with the One" (II.4 ¶50).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **In natural life no single principle rules, and each passing motive takes over in turn.**
  "the government of the natural life is oligarchical, almost an anarchy" (II.4 ¶38). Also: "each motive rising, as it were, becomes a usurper of a vacant throne" (II.4 ¶38).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Nothing compound or impure survives close approach to the divine Fire.** Even the earlier, partial unions strained the ethereal vehicle to its limit.
  "Fire will not suffer anything that is compounded to come near to it: but is the death and separation of everything that is compounded" (II.4 ¶39, Sendivogius). Also: "previous unions, which were but partial and instantaneous, as it were, tried the ethereal vehicle to its utmost susceptibility" (II.4 ¶40, Atwood); "how much less, therefore, can the compound creature, approaching to the Fiery Centre, live?" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius, restated by ATWOOD.

- **A threefold barrier keeps out the uninitiated: sense, ignorance and unwillingness.**
  "a threefold barrier of sense, ignorance, and disinclination, from the discovery of truth" (II.4 ¶40). Also: "Neither is it said to be lawful for the pure to be touched by the impure" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **In the sensible world being is broken at the circumference into separate, opposed parts, through self-will.** The cure is to return to the centre and recognise the error.
  "nor does the devious wheel of life obey her axle any more, until returning into it, she perceives her error and the transgression that was made in self-will" (II.4 ¶50). Also: "the equilibrium of being is broken everywhere at the circumference for manifestation" (II.4 ¶50).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Having the fire is not enough. Many have it and even perceive it, but cannot draw it out into manifestation.**
  "there are many more thrysus bearers than Bacchic souls; which is to say, that many had the fire indeed, and were able even to perceive it, who were without the power to discover and draw it forth to manifestation" (II.4 ¶54).
  — stated; ATWOOD, citing Plato.

## The result

- **A state above bodily life and generation: the Islands of the Blessed or Elysian Fields, a life "in open day".**
  "a condition of being, therefore, which transcends this corporeal life and generation is denominated the Islands of the Blessed; and these are the same with the Elysian Fields" (II.4 ¶3). Also: "having vanquished an obscure and terrestrial life, he afterwards lived in open day" (II.4 ¶3).
  — stated; QUOTED: Olympiodorus (n1), endorsed.

- **Elysium is the region of Divine Ideas, which the alchemists call their garden.** There, ideas coming down from above take form. The soul there is dead to this life and alive to that one ("nox corporis").
  "this meadow of Divine Ideas, or Pratum, as the Oracle denotes it, is a place well known to philosophers; the Alchemists in general call it their garden" (II.4 ¶9). Also: "the rendezvous of all spirits, where Ideas as they descend from above, are conceived and incorporated" (II.4 ¶9, Vaughan); "the true Terrestrial Paradise, which some have called nox corporis, the night of body or corporeal sleep" (II.4 ¶9); "we live their death and die their life" (II.4 ¶9, Heraclitus); "souls dead indeed to this life, yet more alive in that" (II.4 ¶9).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **The adept lives in the body without being bound by it.**
  "the Brahmans of India dwelling on the earth and not on the earth; they were guarded without walls invisibly, and possessing nothing, they enjoyed all things" (II.4 ¶11).
  — interpreted (offered "To clear the prospect" of the philosophers' habitation, ¶11); QUOTED: Apollonius via Vaughan.

- **Elysian intellection makes knower and known one, as far as consciousness allows, and reveals the pattern-image of universal nature.** It is still not the highest grade.
  "there is an assimilation established, as near as may be in consciousness, of the self-knowing and the self known" (II.4 ¶15). Also: "though there is a grade above; yet this is the Intellection in Elysium where the exemplary Image of the Universal Nature also is revealed as in that Athanor of Hermes before mentioned, or furnace having a glass to it" (II.4 ¶15); "eternity is consubsistent only with the permanence of intellection itself" (II.4 ¶15).
  — stated; the Athanor is a comparison, not an identity. ATWOOD.

- **A new inner life grows in Elysium.** The understanding, standing before its archetype, is educated and brings every virtue and faculty into order under reason. Its activity produces Light through calm contact with the first Principle.
  "an unfolding of the embryo life, the nourishment and education of the understanding vehicle now standing in open presence before its Archetypal Light" (II.4 ¶16). Also: "perfects all the new-born attributes, as of justice, beauty, charity, hope, every faculty, sentiment and desire in orderly relation under the dominion of reason" (II.4 ¶16); "generates Light through a perpetual tranquil and quiet contact with the Principle of things" (II.4 ¶16).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Practical fruits of the inner light: virtue, knowledge, health, long life, riches and felicity.**
  "whose spontaneous revelation in a purified soul imparts virtue with understanding, and universal knowledge, health of body, and long length of days" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "riches as from the Causal fountain of all things, and felicity in communion with all" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **The Epopteia, the full revelation: Universal Nature, its secret foundation, and the soul's essence, origin, hindrances and end.**
  "the Pantheistic revealment of the Universal Nature, her secret foundation, the soul’s essence, origin, hindrances, and proper end" (II.4 ¶21).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Real union with the divine, not a priest's illuminated image.**
  "the initiated were conjoined with the total deity and intellectual perfection of their leaders, and were replenished with the divine essentiality" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; ATWOOD (against Warburton).

- **The awakened Reason is the alchemists' Salt of the Wise, Mercury of the Philosophers and Secret of Secrets: God's own magistery.**
  "This is the Sal Sepientum et Mercurius Philosophorum; their Secretu, Secretorum" (II.4 ¶39). Also: "hoc magisterium nihil aliud est, nisi Arcanum et secretum secretorum Dei altissimi et magni" (II.4 ¶39, Morieni; "this magistery is nothing other than the arcanum and secret of secrets of the most high and great God", our translation).
  — stated; ATWOOD, with QUOTED: Morieni (n27).

- **The end: the Paternal Port.** It is discovery of the Father, which means meeting him, being one with him and seeing him, "the Alone with the Alone", in an undefiled union that goes beyond the Elysian vision.
  "For discovery is this, --- to meet with him, to be united to Him, and to see Him Himself --- the Alone with the Alone" (II.4 ¶41). Also: "locating the soul in the Father, as in a port, purely establishing her in fabricative intellections, and conjoining Light with Light" (II.4 ¶41); "This, then, is the Paternal Port and the discovery of the Father, according to Proclus, viz., an undefiled union with him" (II.4 ¶41); "the rebirth and recreation of this principle in the Free Ether, prepared for it, is the end and plenitude of initiatory rites" (II.4 ¶25, Atwood).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n30), endorsed, and ATWOOD (¶25).

- **Knowing in identity.** Subject and object disappear, and the soul becomes one in substance with what she knows.
  "consubstantial, becomes herself the ultimate object as she was before the subject in simultaneous accord" (II.4 ¶42). Also: "returning from thence into herself with all her beams concentrated, addressing the Great Archetype, He becomes known" (II.4 ¶42); "nor as before, either in separation of subject and object; but absolutely, in Identity" (II.4 ¶42).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Total transparency.** Every part contains the whole, and thinker and thought are one.
  "And all things in that ultimate circulation are diaphanous, nothing dark or resisting" (II.4 ¶50). Also: "The sun which is there is all the stars, and again each star is the sun" (II.4 ¶50); "Thus that which thinks and understands, and the thing understands, and the thing understood are one" (II.4 ¶50).
  — stated; QUOTED: Plotinus (extent of his voice is open), endorsed.

- **Freedom from bodily suffering, a foretaste of eternal life, and a whole life irradiated.**
  "obtained together with a liberation from these, a foretaste simple, beatific, and secure, of the life which is eternal" (II.4 ¶52).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **One becomes co-operator with the Father and can do all things.**
  "Cujus tunc Co-operator effectus potest Omnia." (II.4 ¶54).
  — interpreted; this rests on our translation alone, and the referent of "Cujus" is unresolved. Voice UNCLEAR.

- **By-products: the elixir and gold.** They are "vital products" of the "Vital Experiment", shown to the world without their source. Further "material rewards" and "this Water" are promised for later.
  "These intermediate fruits and fragments, having been exhibited at intervals to the world, without a discovery of their source, have given rise to much astonishment of common chemistry after the elixir and gold. Both of which are vital products" (II.4 ¶58). Also: "intending to speak of these material rewards of initiation hereafter, and of this Water especially" (II.4 ¶16).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

## Principles (general laws of the work)

- **The way down is easy and the way back up is narrow. Only what is essentially immortal passes, and only after regeneration.**
  "The descent, therefore, is allowed on all hands to be easy; but the ascent otherwise; the gate indeed being so narrow, close, and difficult to discern, that there be few, and they immortals only, that are able to pass through" (II.4 ¶5). Also: "nothing but the subtlety of an immortal essence, and that by regeneration, can pass into immortality" (II.4 ¶7).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **We are drawn out of the universal life-source, not cut off from it.** Present life is a faint copy of the real inner life.
  "we are not cut off from this fountain, but attracted out from it; which supplies all things with life perpetually, so that we are what we are by its influence" (II.4 ¶19). Also: "of which the present life is a mere vestige and a comparative diminution of existence" (II.4 ¶19).
  — stated; ATWOOD ("if we may believe the experienced").

- **The alchemists proved by experiment, on ontological ground, that Nature is one throughout.**
  "who, experimentally searching, were said to prove the Universal Identity of Nature on the ontological ground" (II.4 ¶16).
  — stated as report; ATWOOD.

- **Wisdom is clearness, and clearness comes from light.**
  "what is Wisdom? We reply, that it is a certain clearness, as being that which renders all things conspicuous" (II.4 ¶26). Also: "From whence was this word clearness denominated? We reply, from light" (II.4 ¶26).
  — stated; QUOTED: Ascelpius Trallianus (via Taylor), endorsed.

- **The soul's wholeness comes from balanced circulation in its own Ether.**
  "the being entire is derived to souls from equilibriate circulation in their Ether; which contains, and is connective of all the Divine genera" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Visions produced while the mind is still acting on its own are not to be trusted.** Enthusiasm is to be kept distinct from fanaticism, and imagination from the light of the true gods.
  "dismiss all visions which take place during the imperfect self-activity of the human mind as arbitrary and untrustworthy" (II.4 ¶29). Also: "between enthusiasm and fanaticism, between the shadowy world of imaginative vision and the light of the true gods" (II.4 ¶29).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **Divine activity moves itself. It takes nothing from the person undergoing it.**
  "things pertaining to the gods are moved by themselves, and do not receive from an inferior nature (i.e., to say, from the regardant subject) the principle of their energy" (II.4 ¶31).
  — stated; QUOTED: Iamblichus (n23), with a gloss probably by Atwood, endorsed.

- **The Fire lies quiet and hidden until God's will stirs it, as fire lies in a flint.**
  "Thou hast an example of this in the flint, in which there is fire, and yet is not perceived, neither doth appear until it is stirred up by motion" (II.4 ¶39). Also: "is not moved unless it be stirred up by the proper will of the Most High" (II.4 ¶39).
  — stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius (n26), endorsed.

- **Like sees like.** The regenerate soul's regard is "hypostatical", not from outside.
  "their regard was not extraneous but hypostatical, as of like to like" (II.4 ¶40). Also: "the initiated were nowhere considered as mortal men, in respect of their souls, which were regenerate" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **The method is passive.** The soul does not seize Deity by its own activity; it is raised by love, hope and faith. Muesis comes through a silence that faith imparts, above every "Gnostic energy".
  "Plato beautifully unfolds the passive method of the Divine Intuition, and the three elevating causes of love, hope, and faith" (II.4 ¶40). Also: "muesis, in short, is neither through intelligence nor judgment, but through the uncial silence imparted by faith" (II.4 ¶40, Proclus n29); "do the Initiated self- actively comprehend the life of Deity; for that would be indeed in inversion and a submerging of the Creator in the creature" (II.4 ¶40).
  — stated; ATWOOD, with QUOTED: Proclus (n29), endorsed.

- **The goal is reached neither by opinion nor by syllogistic science, but by direct intellectual vision and contact.**
  "But it is that which subsists according to Intellectual Vision itself: a contact with the Intelligible, and a union with the Demiurgic Intellect" (II.4 ¶41). Also: "neither is it scientific; for this is syllogistic and composite" (II.4 ¶41).
  — stated; QUOTED: Proclus (n30).

- **Natural reason holds the goal only by faith. Proof requires the experience itself.**
  "which the natural reason echoes, but by a necessity of faith only, since it cannot pass into the superstantial proof" (II.4 ¶49). Also: "affirmation implies a doubled testimony in subject and object" (II.4 ¶49).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

- **The consummation of the Mysteries is the ground of Hermetic philosophy. The Mysteries are sketched here to show how the Sacred Art and Alchemy connect, and what their "proper Subject" is.**
  "And this was the consummation of the Mysteries, the ground of the Hermetic philosophy, prolific in supernatural increase, transmutations and magical effects" (II.4 ¶52). Also: "to indicate the connection of the Sacred Art and Alchemy, and inasmuch as modern revelation would permit, the nature of that Art and proper Subject of this philosophy" (II.4 ¶58); "all things therein were provided by them for the improvement and perfection of human life" (II.4 ¶57, Epictetus).
  — stated; ATWOOD.

## Open questions

- **The Golden Branch (¶7).** (a) It is the immortal essence itself. (b) It is the power or process of regeneration that carries the essence through death and darkness. Basis: it follows directly on "nothing but the subtlety of an immortal essence, and that by regeneration, can pass into immortality". The text names neither.
- **The "new vessel" consecrated in Light (¶18).** (a) It is the purified soul or its vehicle. Basis: ¶19 "spontaneous revelation in a purified soul" and "the soul, coalescing with its vehicle"; ¶29 "equilibriate circulation in their Ether", which echoes ¶18 "equilibriate constancy". (b) It is the subject or container of the work in general. Basis: ¶18 names only a vessel that holds, sustains, measures and kindles a flame. It also stands beside ¶9's "prepared medial receptacle of the newborn light". The text does not link the two explicitly.
- **"central magnet" (¶19) / "Rational Magnet" (¶38).** (a) The ruling Reason within the soul. Basis: ¶38, the monarch whose "leading motive" the lower powers follow; ¶39 "our Loadstone". (b) The divine First Cause, with the ordered soul only a faint image of it. Basis: ¶38 "the Almighty force" and "the Presters of Intellectual Fire follow in radiant order the will of their First Cause".
- **Physical or inner operation? "dissolution of the spirit in its proper kind without alloy" (¶16).** (a) A physical experiment making a material principle visible. Basis: "experimentally searching", "to sense and visibility". (b) Material effects of the same inner, Elysian state. Basis: it is framed as accord with "the metaphysician", "on the ontological ground", and as "material rewards of initiation". This bears on ¶58's "Vital Experiment" and "vital products". The material is deferred to later chapters.
- **Referent of "our Iron, our Red Earth, our Loadstone, celeberrimus ille microcosmos et Adam" (¶39).** (a) The hidden Reason itself. (b) The life or ore that hides it. Basis: "in which we are all now as dead" suits a container, while the similes and the next clause concern the Reason.
- **"when I arises identically reverse, perfect, and alone" (¶39).** (a) "I" is a misprint for "it", the hidden Reason. Basis: grammar and subject. (b) It is a literal "I", the true self arising. Basis: the soul's "own identic essence" later in ¶39.
- **"circulatory confine" (¶39) and "resolution".** What exactly is dissolved is not explained. Related circulation language appears in: ¶9 "restore the suspended circulation"; ¶19 "the circulation oscillates"; ¶29 "equilibriate circulation in their Ether"; ¶50 "ultimate circulation" versus sensible circulation broken at the circumference; ¶52 "successive circulations". Whether these all name one process is not stated.
- **"which is born be through without much disturbance" (¶19).** (a) "borne through": the Hades vision is endured without much disruption. (b) "born": it arises without much disruption. The text appears corrupt.
- **"the uncial silence imparted by faith" (¶40).** (a) A misprint for "unical", meaning unifying. Basis: union with the One and "the ineffable Unknown". (b) Read as printed, with unclear sense.
- **"Pontic Medium" (¶49).** It is unexplained. (a) The same thing as "Theurgic assistance", in apposition. (b) A separate requirement. Basis: the parallel "Without the Pontic Medium, without Theurgic assistance"; ¶56 "appropriate media".
- **"translation" (¶40).** It is undefined. It is the last step after contemplation of the indwelling Unity, and its rite is burial up to the head. Its relation to ¶19's "expiration" and to ¶41's Paternal Port is not stated.
- **The Ether (¶25, ¶29, ¶36, ¶40).** The text speaks of a "Free Ether, prepared for it" (where the paternal principle is reborn), "their Ether" (the soul's wholeness), the "passive Ether" (the medium of the rites) and the "ethereal vehicle". The text does not say whether these are one.
- **Rank of Elysium.** ¶3 presents the Elysian Fields as the goal. ¶15 says "though there is a grade above", and ¶41 says the Paternal union is "more beautiful" than "that vision that was in Elysium". Elysium therefore seems to be an intermediate stage, though the chapter also uses Elysian language for the goal (¶19 "the elysian light").
- **Antecedent of "which Vaughan describes as the rendezvous of all spirits" (¶9).** It refers either to the garden or to the royal herb ("For it is either and all, which Vaughan describes as the rendezvous of all spirits"). The syntax allows both.
- **Voices.** It is unclear whether the Cerberus gloss (¶3) is Olympiodorus or Atwood. "The Platonic Successor" (¶41) is either Proclus, named just before, or unnamed. It is unclear whether Plotinus' voice runs beyond "He comes, says Plotinus, suddenly alone" (¶50) to "the subsistence of all". "the great theologist" (¶29) is either Proclus or someone he cites. "asks the philosopher" (¶40) is Plato or Proclus (n29). It is unclear whether the parenthetical glosses in Proclus (¶16 "subjective identity" / "objective entity") are Atwood's.
- **"Cujus tunc Co-operator effectus potest Omnia" (¶54).** "Cujus" may refer to the "Cunctarumque patrem rerum" of ¶53 or to the First Principle of ¶52. Either way the claim rests only on our translation.
- **"these" in "the Light of Life which these could kindle and fortify" (¶56).** It means either the true initiations or the ancients.
