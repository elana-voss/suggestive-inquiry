# Method material — IV.4 The Conclusion

The chapter recapitulates the whole Inquiry (¶2–¶13), answers objections (¶14–¶26), and closes with final directions to the qualified seeker (¶27–¶32). Almost everything is in Atwood's own voice; many points look back to earlier chapters ("it has been shown", "we have shown").

## The subject (what the work is done on)

- **A Universal Subject, nourishable in man.** The "grand Hermetic secret" is that one universal subject runs through nature and can be fed and increased inside a human being. A related statement describes a Subtle Nature that pervades everything and can be altered by art. The text does not say outright that the Subtle Nature and the Universal Subject are the same (see Open questions).
  "there is a Universal Subject in nature, and that Subject is susceptible of nourishment in Man" (IV.4 ¶10). Also: "there is a Subtle Nature pervading the universe, which is the All in every thing and susceptible of artificial alteration through all" (IV.4 ¶12).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Man is the laboratory.** The work is done in and on the human being, not in outside apparatus. Man is the apparatus Nature devised for distilling her Spirit.
  "Man was the proper laboratory of the whole Art" (IV.4 ¶3). Also: "the most perfect chemical apparatus, devised by Nature for the distillation of her Spirit" (IV.4 ¶3).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Life is the core.** Life is the centre of the whole Mystery, and discovering life was what drew the philosophers in.
  "And Life is the nucleus of the whole Hermetic Mystery" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "the primary attraction which led them in for the discovery of life" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Three kingdoms bound together in man.** Animal, vegetable and mineral life are bound together in man's natural identity. Only animal life is conscious. The vegetable and mineral lives are known only to those who have gone inward by experiment.
  "the diverse kingdoms of nature, as it were, bound together in the threefold enchantment of his natural Identity" (IV.4 ¶4). Also: "known only to those who have entered experimentally within to prove the hypostatic action and passion of the working essences in life" (IV.4 ¶4).
  *stated; reported as what the adepts found ("it has been shown"), endorsed by ATWOOD*

- **The Root of Reason, buried in the life of the senses, which is Wisdom and the "saving Salt".** What reason properly aims at is its own Source. That Source lies deeply buried under ordinary sensory life. The text identifies this Root of Reason with Wisdom and with the Salt the philosophers dug out and exalted.
  "For this same Root of Reason is Wisdom, and that saving Salt which philosophers were wont anciently to excavate and by so many circulating media to exalt" (IV.4 ¶20). Also: "the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source, which lies profoundly buried in this life of sense" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

- **The life-fire.** The life circulating in man is a pure fire held in an incombustible ethereal vapour, and the Universal Efficient is in that fire. The adepts found this by "vital analysis".
  "the life of man therein circulating to be a pure fire incorporated in a certain incombustible ethereal vapour" (IV.4 ¶4). Also: "the Universal Efficient was in this fire" (IV.4 ¶4).
  *stated; reported as the adepts' discovery, endorsed by ATWOOD*

- **Everything needed is inside man.** Man has within himself the whole ferment, the motive power and the principle of improvement the work needs.
  "having besides the whole fermentative virtue, motive, and principle of vital melioration and every requisite complete within himself" (IV.4 ¶3).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The ray of motive Light, which is the Form of Gold and the Stone.** A living, active light is the true essence of gold and the only universal principle of growth and perfection. When it becomes dominant in the circulation, it becomes concrete in life, and then it is the transmuting ferment, that is, the Stone.
  "that ray of motive Light, pure, vital, and efficient, we have shown to be the true Form of Gold, the alone universal principle of increase and perfection" (IV.4 ¶9). Also: "the same which in the circulatory system, becoming dominant, is made concrete in life; and is the transmutative ferment --- even the Philosophic Stone" (IV.4 ¶9).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Light is the Key.** Light opens the Mystery. Without it one can neither enter nor explain it. The intricate "wards" of the lock were built by the Key and for it alone. What the wards are is not stated (see Open questions).
  "the Key thereof is Light" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "If, however, the Key is wanting, how may we presume to enter" (IV.4 ¶32); "those mirific wards which were constructed by it, and for it to pass through, and for it alone" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *Key = Light is stated; the reading of the wards is interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **Light overcoming darkness in the "hypostatical alliance".** The proper cure for inborn evil is Light overcoming darkness inside a substantial union. The chapter does not say what is united with what.
  "overcoming darkness by Light in the hypostatical alliance" (IV.4 ¶17).
  *interpreted (basis: ¶17 calls this the evil's "proper antidote"); ATWOOD*

- **The "catholic torch".** Once kindled, a universal agent kindles the Light of Universal Nature. It works through the three kingdoms, outwardly or inwardly, morally or physically, "according to the application", and it brings things to their full strength by voluntary assimilation. Above all it rejoices in the upright mind of man. The text never says what the torch is.
  "The catholic torch, miraculously kindled, kindles the Light of Universal Nature" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "either externally or internally, morally or physically, or in all these ways, according to the application" (IV.4 ¶32); "by its voluntary assimilation, increasing and promoting things to their utmost boundary of strength" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

- **A true lover of Wisdom.** The work needs a philosopher of the old kind who puts Wisdom above everything else and is studious, simple, ardent and open to truth as it appears. The philosophers sought Wisdom for her own sake.
  "a true lover of Wisdom, who, for her sake, will devote everything else, studious, simple, ardent, and withal suscipient of appearing truth" (IV.4 ¶8). Also: "it needs a philosopher, one of the antique mould" (IV.4 ¶8); "The philosophers sought after Wisdom for her own sake" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Reason and right purpose, developed and purified.** The faculties required are reason and right intention, and they have to be developed. Reason must be purified and set apart by the Art for the encounter with temptation. The worker must be upright in thought and action.
  "man by his especial prerogative of Reason and rectitude of purpose is able, by a development of these" (IV.4 ¶12). Also: "the Reason also, purified and singled out by Art for the encounter" (IV.4 ¶9); "in conscious rectitude of thought and action" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Hard, special discipline.** Wisdom is acquired only through a peculiar and demanding discipline, quite unlike school logic or mathematics. No modern institution offers anything comparable, such as rites that purify or awaken the mind.
  "it was avowedly a thing acquired, the reward only of peculiar and arduous discipline" (IV.4 ¶17). Also: "no rites of purifying or awakening efficacy to the mind" (IV.4 ¶17).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Earnest desire, and faith directed by reason.** Without a real desire to discover, faith comes to nothing, and labour is wasted unless reason directs faith. Faith is the magnet that draws hope, desire and reason after it.
  "faith is the very attracting loadstone which hope pursues, and desire and reason, and the whole willing armament of Mind" (IV.4 ¶19). Also: "Without an earnest desire of discovery, the liveliest faith will be frustrated, and labour will be vain unless reason give direction to the persuasion of faith" (IV.4 ¶19); "conviction steeled to the purpose of her Motive Light" (IV.4 ¶19).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Obedience to the Divine Will, not one's own will.** Searching by one's own will alone fails. Success comes from putting oneself under the required conditions in obedience to the Divine Will. At the end, the worker submits his own power to the rule of the universal Reason (whose Rule this is depends on an unclear antecedent, see Open questions).
  "by conditionating, in obedience to the Divine Will, he comes into the integral alliance and power" (IV.4 ¶23). Also: "man, searching in his own will alone, is incompetent to the Divine Discovery" (IV.4 ¶23); "he submits his own omnipotence to the omniscience of its Rule" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *¶23 stated; ¶32 interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **Natural fitness, worthiness and a "congenial Sight".** The book is addressed to people naturally drawn to the pursuit and judged worthy of it. The Light itself attracts them. Some need no argument because they have instinctive faith. The work cannot be carried on without a fitting inner faculty of perception, which is not defined.
  "we have sought to attract those chiefly to whom such a pursuit would be naturally pleasing, and such as have been customarily esteemed worthy of the reward" (IV.4 ¶30). Also: "for those to whom it is addressed the Light will attract" (IV.4 ¶31); "an instinctive faith and appreciation" (IV.4 ¶30); "nor can it be prosecuted without a congenial Sight" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *the requirements are stated; the gloss on "Sight" is interpreted. ATWOOD*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter gives no complete sequence. Words that mark explicit order are: Ether, then elaboration, then effect (¶2); first key, then vestibule, then inner halls (¶5); "preparative to" (¶17); "through dissolution into a new birth" (¶12); excavate, then exalt (¶20); first entrance, then volatilize, then return (¶32). The points below follow the chapter's order.

- **Outline of making the Stone: Ether, then elaboration, then effect.** The Stone starts from the free Ether, is worked up by an artificial process, and ends as a visible effect.
  "from its foundation in the free Ether, through an artificial process of elaboration, into manifest effect" (IV.4 ¶2).
  *stated; ATWOOD (summarizing her witnesses)*

- **Explore the hidden capacity by going inward.** The Hermetic technique aims to explore man's concealed power, and the hidden lives are known only to those who have "entered experimentally within".
  "This hidden capacity it has been shown to be the purpose of the hermetic artifice to explore" (IV.4 ¶4). Also: "who have entered experimentally within" (IV.4 ¶4).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Preliminary stage: the vestibule, with Mesmerism as a first key.** The ancient Mysteries were real practices that led the knowing spirit back to its origin. Mesmerism matches the Mysteries and Alchemy in their *preliminary* practice, so it opens only the vestibule. The inner halls of Light are open to philosophers only. The Sphinx image is not explained.
  "a first key opening to the vestibule of this Experiment" (IV.4 ¶5). Also: "real inductions of the Understanding Spirit to its Source" (IV.4 ¶5); "open to philosophers only the inner halls of Light" (IV.4 ¶5).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Accessory means only prepare.** Education and other improvements only prepare the way. The central work, Light overcoming darkness, is what restores the mind to wholeness and universal knowledge.
  "every accessory means of melioration will be preparative to this which restores the human mind to integrity and universal science" (IV.4 ¶17). Also: "We may alter, and improve, and educate, and prepare the way with advantage; but the notion will be variable, and every plan defective without the Exemplary Light" (IV.4 ¶18).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Atwood's book as a preparation: rousing Reason.** By appealing to faith and to records of transcendental experience, Atwood hopes to wake the imagination so that dormant Reason looks for real recollection in its own root of Light.
  "we have hoped to awaken the imaginative centers to such an accord as might stir Reason from her long lethargy to seek for genuine reminiscence in her root of Light" (IV.4 ¶15).
  *interpreted (that this is a preparatory stage); ATWOOD*

- **The method is an ascent, the making of the Fire.** Atwood calls the method an ascent and identifies it with the artful making of Prometheus's Fire. Her book's order of exposition hints at it indirectly. What "genealogical" means is open.
  "the genealogical method of ascent; even that artistic fabrication of the Fire which Prometheus received from Vulcan" (IV.4 ¶8). Also: "we have spoken of principles with reference to practice, and in an order indirectly indicative of" (IV.4 ¶8).
  *the identity is stated ("even"); the meaning is open. ATWOOD*

- **The vital experiment, a "pyrotechnical induction".** The fire-working experiment brings powers, the regenerative origin and temptations into conscious awareness. In it the ancients found their whole philosophy.
  "by this pyrotechnical induction, powers were revealed to conscience, the whole regenerative original and those temptations" (IV.4 ¶9). Also: "In the course of this vital experiment the ancients discovered the whole of the philosophy they teach" (IV.4 ¶9).
  *interpreted (basis: life is fire in ¶4; method is "fabrication of the Fire" in ¶8); ATWOOD*

- **Dissolution and voluntary self-annihilation.** The operator carries his inner life through a dissolution into a new birth. At her Initial Source, Nature works her own annihilation by her own will and survives, ruling on her redeemed Light. Whose nature this is remains open. The principle that what generation binds only dissolution can unloose (Principles) points the same way.
  "bear the life within him through dissolution into a new birth" (IV.4 ¶12). Also: "after operating voluntarily about her own annihilation, to survive and establish a stable monarchy upon her redeemed Light" (IV.4 ¶6).
  *¶12 stated (as a supposition Atwood argues for); linking ¶6 to it is interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **Revive the buried Source through self-knowledge.** The Art's task is to revive the buried Source of reason and make it actually experienced, by way of self-knowledge.
  "this it is the province of Hermetic Artifice to resuscitate and bring through self-knowledge into the experience of life" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Dig out the Salt, then exalt it by circulation.** The Salt, which is the Root of Reason, is first dug out from where it lies buried and then exalted by many "circulating media". The circulating media are not explained.
  "excavate and by so many circulating media to exalt" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *interpreted (basis: "excavate" matches "profoundly buried" in the same ¶); ATWOOD*

- **All the faculties restore their King.** During the "passage", every faculty takes part, under its rightful rule, in restoring its source, the "King". A faculty that resists causes suffering to the others.
  "if any one is refractory during the passage, it is the occasion of suffering to the better natures, which are all engaged voluntarily in the restitution of their King" (IV.4 ¶20). Also: "all are present together in obedience to their rightful rule and habitude" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **Search and prove one's own Identity.** The Wisdom can be reached only by investigating and testing one's own identity, and by no other route. (Whether "His" means man's identity or God's is open.)
  "It is by the searching and proving of His own Identity, not otherwise, that man can arrive at the assurance of this Wisdom" (IV.4 ¶27). Also: "every particular which the inquiring Spirit seeks within itself" (IV.4 ¶27).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **Give up selfhood and return to the Universal.** One goes back into the whole by abandoning separate selfhood.
  "returning again, however, into the Universal and abandoning the selfhood" (IV.4 ¶27).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Search for the Root of Reason, hold by it, act with it.** The seeker must rationally seek the source of Reason, hold to it and act together with it. The chapter defines "Root of Reason" in ¶20 (= Wisdom = saving Salt).
  "let him search for the Root of Reason rationally, and hold by it, and conspire with It" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *stated directive; the gloss on "conspire" is interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **The first entrance, and volatilizing then returning the fixed tincture of life.** The key operation: know the "first entrance", make the fixed tincture of life volatile, then bring it back. Whoever knows this is admitted to the whole, because one method runs through every stage. The operation is not explained, and whether it is material or inward is open.
  "He who knows the first entrance, and how to render the fixed tincture of life volatile, and to return it, being free" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "through all the interior recesses, the method is allied" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **Read the Adepts with the Key, not literally.** Study the Adepts' enigmas, fables, parables and maxims. They all point to one discovery, and even their contradictions teach whoever has the Key. Literal readings of fable only record ignorance.
  "Let him search into the enigmas, peruse the fables, and consider the parables and maxims of the wise Adepts" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "They all tend to one discovery, and declare the same, and even in their inconsistencies will be instructive to him who has the Key" (IV.4 ¶32); "Those low, literal, spiritless interpretations of poetic fable and philosophy" (IV.4 ¶29).
  *¶32 stated; the contrast with ¶29 is interpreted. ATWOOD*

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

- **Powers and temptations come into awareness.** In the vital experiment, powers, the regenerative origin and temptations are "revealed to conscience" (see Procedure).
  "powers were revealed to conscience" (IV.4 ¶9).
  *interpreted as a sign; ATWOOD*

- **Desire embodies itself in the "magic region".** In the magic region (not defined), every desire becomes fruitful and takes on body through "the ethereal conception". For this reason Atwood will not tempt readers with particular promises.
  "every desire is, in the magic region, made prolific, embodying itself, by the ethereal conception, as a principle to enact its voluntary accord" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Nature opens her resources.** At an advanced point, Nature opens to the worker "the vast resources of her essence", and with them comes the temptation of self-worship (see Impediments).
  "Nature opening up to him the vast resources of her essence and the mystery of the most wonderful creation" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *interpreted as a sign; ATWOOD*

- **Marks beyond the sea of sense.** Beyond the senses there is a real goal, and there are marks pointing to it. What the marks are is not said.
  "beyond the turbulent sea of sense, there is a haven and signal marks to direct where the Promised Land is to be found" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

## Impediments and dangers

- **The pull of outward things hides man's power.** In ordinary life, man's latent power is concealed by the attraction of external things.
  "concealed in this life by the external attraction and obstructive energies of cause" (IV.4 ¶3).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The Divine Light is imprisoned.** In this life the Divine Light is held behind barriers, and the ancients used means to remove them. An unattributed verse gives the same picture of a divine faculty chained from birth.
  "the use of means for removing the barriers by which the Divine Light is here held in thralldom" (IV.4 ¶17). Also: "the faculty divine Is chained and tortured, cabin’d, cribb’d, confin’d." (IV.4 ¶16, verse, voice UNCLEAR).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Rare conditions; unfit for "practical" minds.** The work faces obstacles and needs uncommon mental conditions, so it is seldom attempted and more seldom finished. Minds that want immediate worldly use are unfit for it.
  "From impediments likewise described, and rare intellectual conditions" (IV.4 ¶7). Also: "it is wholly unsuited to minds commonly so styled practical, who are impatient of every proposition that is not immediately applicable to the affairs of life" (IV.4 ¶8).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Temptations, including self-worship.** Temptations come up during the work, and Reason purified by the Art can fully resist them. When Nature opens her resources, the specific danger is self-worship. The answer is to forget oneself in admiration and love of the Wisdom.
  "those temptations which the Reason also, purified and singled out by Art for the encounter, is able entirely to withstand" (IV.4 ¶9). Also: "with every temptation of self-idolatry laid open to view, oblivious of all else in admiration and love of so much Wisdom" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *¶9 stated; ¶32 interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **Narrow imagination and small love.** Man is already limited by a poor imagination and a small love. Seeking private benefits would further shrink an infinite virtue.
  "the poverty of his imagination and the littleness of his love" (IV.4 ¶10). Also: "which might restrict to individual interests a virtue which is infinite" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **A guarded fortress.** The doctrine of inward Wisdom cannot be taken by curiosity or by demanding proof the senses can check.
  "there is a strongly enchanted fortress about it, whose forces yield not either to impertinent curiosity or the peremptory demands of sense" (IV.4 ¶14).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **External progress leads away from the Source.** Progress in outward things and the luxury it brings weaken moral character and lead further from the origin. Sense-bound modern science is "blind and baffled", treats sense as its guide and labours for selfish gain.
  "every advance we make in externals leads us further off from the First Source" (IV.4 ¶18). Also: "blind and baffled everywhere about the beguilements of sense" (IV.4 ¶28); "sense has been the acknowledged beacon light, and worldy utility the highest good" (IV.4 ¶29); "experimental philosophy labours for selfish aggrandizement" (IV.4 ¶30).
  *¶18 stated; ¶28–¶30 interpreted as impediments. ATWOOD*

- **Disbelief, a wise safeguard.** Incredulity blocks the work, but it is provided to stop people from going into practice before they are ready.
  "Incredulity is the strongest barrier of possibilities over the world, no doubt wisely provided to prevent a too rapid movement of mind into practice, before it is well prepared" (IV.4 ¶19).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Pride, avarice, ambition, ignorance.** Anyone ruled by these lower motives, who will not accept a subordinate place for them, should not take up the work, or he will be swallowed by his own delusion.
  "pride or avarice, or ambition or ignorance, accustomed to rule in this life, should disdain the subaltern station which the Divine Law assigns to them" (IV.4 ¶23). Also: "swallowed up in the gulph of their own overwhelming delusion" (IV.4 ¶23); "the viperous progeny of self-love" (IV.4 ¶23).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Believing the work impious.** Those who think the work impious should stop, or their belief will show itself as cowardice or as faithless probing of what they fear.
  "lest it should become manifested forth in some practical form of pusillanimity, or faithless attempt to interrogate the profundity it fears" (IV.4 ¶24).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Becoming "dividual".** Falling away into separate selfhood cuts a person off from the whole.
  "falling off from this, in becoming dividual, he ceases to be the Universe" (IV.4 ¶27).
  *interpreted (basis: the remedy is "abandoning the selfhood"); ATWOOD*

## The result

- **To find and to effect the Divine Nature.** The "greatest mystery" is that man can not only discover the divine nature but also bring it about.
  "that man should be able not only to find the Divine Nature, but to effect It" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Riches are by-products.** Gold, silver and treasures came as Wisdom's "dowry", benefits along the way and not the aim. Whether they are literal is open.
  "gained with her an ample dowry, gold, silver, and the glittering treasures of her creative light in abundance" (IV.4 ¶10). Also: "these intermediate benefits" (IV.4 ¶10).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The Catholic Effect of Life.** Starting from either mind or matter, the experiment reaches a universal effect of life in which inner and outer worlds are said to merge and confirm each other. In this way Hermetic Science would take in both physical and moral science.
  "it arrives at the Catholic Effect of Life" (IV.4 ¶11). Also: "the external and internal worlds are said to blend together in confluence harmony, establishing each other" (IV.4 ¶11).
  *stated (the blending as "said"); ATWOOD*

- **A new birth beyond nature's strife.** The life carried through dissolution is reborn in a state above nature that elemental conflict cannot destroy.
  "superior to nature and beyond the reach of elementary discord to destroy" (IV.4 ¶12).
  *stated (as a supposition); ATWOOD*

- **The Stone as a crown and body of light.** The regenerated soul puts on the Stone as a halo or crown of light, a new body. In it the soul rules and passes through the elemental world and overcomes evil, falsehood, ignorance and death.
  "The diadem of Wisdom is with this Stone, which, as a halo or crown of light, the regenerate soul puts on as a new body" (IV.4 ¶20). Also: "wherein it can rule over the elementary world and pass through it, overcoming evil and falsehood, and ignorance and death" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Reason made immortal; the faculties renewed under Light.** The philosophy makes Reason immortal. The faculties do not have to be sacrificed in the end: they are renewed together with their King and brought into harmony under the rule of Light.
  "this philosophy, which immortalizes it" (IV.4 ¶20). Also: "the brethren all are renewed together with him, and every dignified sentiment is set in accord, to testify and maintain the triumphant Monarchy of Light" (IV.4 ¶20).
  *immortalizing stated; renewal interpreted. ATWOOD*

- **Goodness known inside the veil.** Perfect goodness is known only inside the sanctuary, in the presence of Divinity, or when the understanding is wholly "conjoined". It cannot be known from outside.
  "in the presence of Divinity, true awe is felt, and man discovers and knows the perfect goodness, which profanely without the temple’s veil he cannot, or unless the understanding is absolutely conjoined" (IV.4 ¶21).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **Knowing the First Truth by experience; seeing in causes.** The highest privilege is direct, experiential knowledge of the first truth and a steady contemplation of the causal Light. This science shows all things in their causes. Atwood adds, as something "we are told", that nature's powers are then at our disposal, "if we can believe".
  "to know the First Truth of things really and experimentally, and to revolve the Causal Light in permanence of intellection" (IV.4 ¶22). Also: "which exhibits all things in their constructive Causes" (IV.4 ¶22); "the springs of Universal Nature, with her growth and fruits, are at our delegate disposal, if we can believe" (IV.4 ¶22); "Identically penetrating, enters into the Whole of Existence" (IV.4 ¶22).
  *stated; ATWOOD (the "disposal" claim reported and hedged)*

- **The Wisdom above all knowledge.** The goal is a Wisdom higher than all science, art and faith, which also contains them. It is what is meant by Heraclitus' Well and by the Nothing Socrates knew.
  "this Wisdom, which is above science, art, and every other kind of faith; which includes all knowledges, arts" (IV.4 ¶27). Also: "This is that Well of Heraclitus in which the Truth yet lies profoundly hidden" (IV.4 ¶27); "This is that Nothing which Socrates knew" (IV.4 ¶27).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Having the All; governing the world.** Whoever "knows Nothing" holds the whole, can perceive, make and bring about the whole, and by giving up selfhood rises and governs the world.
  "being composed of the whole, he is able to discern, to make, and to effect the whole" (IV.4 ¶27). Also: "he raises himself on high, and governs the world" (IV.4 ¶27).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Working together with the First Cause.** The highest outcome is that the upright worker co-operates with the First Cause.
  "he co-operates with the First Cause" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Admission to the temple of Divine Science.** Whoever knows the entrance and the volatilizing operation joins the company of those who have divine knowledge.
  "is already admitted to the temple of Divine Science, and joins in with the whole conclave" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "if they advance by it to the true end" (IV.4 ¶31).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

## Principles (general laws of the work)

- **Transmutation acts on the universal subject, not on species.** Transmutation works on the common subject underlying all kinds, not on particular species. The Stone is that subject's virtue, concentrated.
  "the principle of Transmutation, they have shown to be relating not to Species but to their Universal Subject, whose concentrated virtue the Stone likewise itself is" (IV.4 ¶2).
  *stated; ATWOOD (summarizing her witnesses)*

- **Secrecy: the practical technique passed on by mouth.** Because the multitude is unfit and individuals might abuse it, philosophers hid their knowledge and passed on the practical technique almost only orally.
  "communicating almost by word of mouth only the practical device" (IV.4 ¶7).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Motive matters more than machinery.** What the work now lacks is a worthy motive, not equipment. This is Atwood's reason for writing more openly.
  "experiment is in need rather of a motive to dignify it than of practical machinery" (IV.4 ¶8).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Experience is the test.** The Art stands or falls by experience: whether the transformation has been passed through consciously and proved in effects. Intellectual experience ranks above sense experience.
  "experience being the admitted test of philosophy" (IV.4 ¶12). Also: "passed through in the conscious experience, and proved demonstratively in facts, visible deeds, and effects" (IV.4 ¶12).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Knowing from within ("Identic co-operation").** How Nature works can be known only by taking part in it from within. Knowledge without the experience is merely empirical.
  "nor shall we ever learn, without Identic co-operation, how Nature works" (IV.4 ¶12). Also: "All our knowledge without the experience is empirical, the result only of observation of remote effects" (IV.4 ¶12).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **Alchemy is the mirror of the mind and proves itself.** Alchemy is the means by which the mind directly enters and verifies truth. Its evidence is the experience itself and cannot be had without it.
  "Alchemy has been declared to be the only true glass of the mind, which shows how to enter, and to touch, and to discover the Truth" (IV.4 ¶12). Also: "the evidence is self-sufficient, and without itself cannot, however truly imaginable, be known" (IV.4 ¶12); "all logical proof, which is barren without self-knowledge" (IV.4 ¶12).
  *stated; QUOTED: unnamed ("has been declared"), adopted by ATWOOD*

- **True science is discovery of causes.** Only knowledge of causes satisfies the idea of truth. If an unchanging Being prior to the sensible world can be proven in intellect and confirmed in phenomena, the ancient philosophy is the best. Knowing the Causal Nature is the worthiest knowledge there is.
  "a certain immutable Being of all, pre-existent to sensibles, which can be proven in intellect and confirmed in sensible phenomena" (IV.4 ¶13). Also: "neither can anything short of the Causal Discovery fulfill the Idea of Truth" (IV.4 ¶13); "the discovery of the Causal Nature is doubtless of all parts of knowledge the worthiest to be sought after" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *stated; the immutable Being is QUOTED: "the Alchemists and Greek physicians", which Atwood endorses conditionally; the rest is ATWOOD*

- **Universals in the mind as the starting ground.** That universals are naturally present in the mind gives a probable starting point for the inquiry.
  "the natural subsistence of Universals in the human mind may afford a ground of probability whence to proceed into their proof" (IV.4 ¶14).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Transcending reason, not contradicting it.** The ancient Wisdom goes beyond reason without contradicting it. What was their foundation is our upper limit.
  "that because the Wisdom of the ancients transcends, that it by any means contradicts human reason, but quite otherwise" (IV.4 ¶15).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Only a living power reaches inborn evil.** Outside measures modify character but cannot remake it. Only a living power can touch inborn evil, and only inward testing really finds it.
  "no power that is not vital can touch the evil that is inbred, or even discover it truly without introspective proof" (IV.4 ¶17). Also: "all our circumstantial labours do not rectify the mind, or turn the inbred inclination out of life" (IV.4 ¶17).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **What generation binds, dissolution unlooses.** The evil fixed by birth can be undone only by a matching dissolution. The chapter does not say here what that dissolution is (compare ¶12, Procedure).
  "That which generation binds dissolution only can unloose" (IV.4 ¶17).
  *interpreted; ATWOOD*

- **The Art is a remedy, salvation is by the Divine Will.** The Art heals imperfect souls and provides "means of assimilation". Salvation itself is done by the Divine Will alone. Atwood leaves open whether knowing the means is necessary, and reports that the Adepts say God reveals them to his elect. What the soul is assimilated to is not said.
  "the Art is said to be a remedy of imperfect souls, and offers the means of assimilation" (IV.4 ¶25). Also: "the Divine Will operates its fulfillment alone" (IV.4 ¶25); "Adepts declare that He has always revealed them to his elected children" (IV.4 ¶25).
  *stated; ATWOOD, with the Adepts quoted*

- **To know Nothing is to have the All.** To "know Nothing" means holding to no separate, fallen-away particular, and whoever does so has the whole.
  "For he who in such wise knows Nothing --- no one apostate particular --- has the All" (IV.4 ¶27). Also: "to know Nothing was the most happy life" (IV.4 ¶27, of Agrippa).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The language is the Art; the book only opens the way.** To understand the philosophers' language is to understand their Art. Atwood has only opened the way and promises success to whoever travels it, and to no one otherwise.
  "They, we repeat, who can understand the language of the philosophers, will understand their Art" (IV.4 ¶32). Also: "for this we have opened the way only, which if any one will consent to travel in, we assure him of success, but not otherwise" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *stated; ATWOOD*

- **One method throughout.** The same method runs through every inner stage, so whoever knows the entrance has access to the whole.
  "through all the interior recesses, the method is allied" (IV.4 ¶32).
  *interpreted ("allied" read as 'of one kind throughout'); ATWOOD*

## Open questions

- **Does Atwood herself claim man's power to "effect all things"?** "mind and manual efficacy, as it is narrated, by the Divine Will, to effect all things" (IV.4 ¶3). (a) It is her own claim; the paragraph is otherwise in her voice. (b) It is a traditional report she does not fully own, since "as it is narrated" marks a report.

- **Whose nature undergoes the voluntary annihilation?** "after operating voluntarily about her own annihilation" (IV.4 ¶6). (a) Universal Nature at her source: the subject is "mysterious Nature". (b) The operator's own nature followed inward: ¶12 puts dissolution in man ("bear the life within him through dissolution into a new birth"), and ¶6 speaks of "the metaphysical experience". The "stable monarchy upon her redeemed Light" (¶6) is close in wording to "the triumphant Monarchy of Light" (¶20), which belongs to the renewed faculties of the soul and so favours (b). The text does not link them.

- **The light kindling at the centre.** "there in the center the light will kindle and increase, and gain strength to radiate upwards through the whole circumference" (IV.4 ¶8). (a) An expression of faith that good will win out in humanity: the next sentence is "faith in humanity". (b) An image of the inner work, light kindled at the operator's centre spreading outward: compare ¶9, the Light "becoming dominant" in the circulation.

- **What does "genealogical method of ascent" mean?** (IV.4 ¶8). (a) Going back up the line of origin to the source: compare "inductions of the Understanding Spirit to its Source" (¶5) and "into her Initial Source" (¶6). (b) A stepwise ascent mirrored in the order of the book: "in an order indirectly indicative of" (¶8).

- **"her luminous radix" and "our irrational alliance"** (IV.4 ¶8). (a) Minerva's own root, which the Fire is spread to preserve. (b) A luminous root in man that the Fire keeps from being smothered by union with irrational nature; ¶9's "Reason also, purified" suggests a rational/irrational contrast. (c) A mythic flourish. The grammar leaves "her" and "it" open.

- **Literal or figurative riches?** "gold, silver, and the glittering treasures of her creative light" (IV.4 ¶10). (a) Literal metals gained as by-products: "intermediate benefits", "more attractive, even than gold or silver". (b) The riches of Wisdom: they are framed as Wisdom's "dowry" and "treasures of her creative light".

- **"Identic co-operation"** (IV.4 ¶12). (a) The knower takes part in, or becomes one with, Nature's working. (b) The same principle co-operates in us and in Nature: the Universal Efficient is in man's life-fire (¶4). Compare "Identically penetrating, enters into the Whole of Existence" (¶22). The term is not defined.

- **Are the "hypostatical", "supernal" and "integral" alliances one thing?** "hypostatical alliance" (¶17), "supernal alliance" (¶20), "integral alliance and power" (¶23). (a) A union of the Divine Light with the human mind (¶17 sets Light against inbred darkness). (b) The union of all the faculties under rightful rule (¶20), entered by obedience to the Divine Will (¶23). The text does not equate them.

- **Is "this Stone" the Salt itself or what the Salt becomes?** "The diadem of Wisdom is with this Stone" (IV.4 ¶20). (a) The same as the saving Salt / Root of Reason just named, since "this" points back to it. (b) A further product of exalting the Salt, since "exalt" may imply a transformation. No explicit identity is stated.

- **Who is the King?** "the restitution of their King" (IV.4 ¶20). (a) Reason, of which ¶20 says "this philosophy, which immortalizes it". (b) The Source or Root of Reason (Wisdom): "him who is their source", together with "the proper object of the rational faculty is in its Source".

- **The circulating media and the circulatory system.** The Salt is exalted "by so many circulating media" (¶20), and the ray of Light becomes concrete "in the circulatory system, becoming dominant" (¶9). These may be one operation described twice, or two unrelated uses of circulation. The text does not connect them.

- **"root of Light" and "Root of Reason".** Reason seeks "reminiscence in her root of Light" (¶15), and the "Root of Reason is Wisdom" (¶20). The wording is close and both are the root of Reason, but no identity is stated. If they are the same, the Root of Reason is also a root of Light, which would join the Salt line (¶20) to the Light line (¶9, ¶32).

- **The understanding "conjoined" with what?** (IV.4 ¶21). (a) With Divinity: "in the presence of Divinity". (b) With its own source or with the other faculties: the alliance in ¶20.

- **Is the Art of Alchemy the same as the "Art of Wisdom"?** ¶24 "this Art of Wisdom, long distinguished as holy" and ¶25 "The Art of Alchemy is of all Arts distinguished as holt" (apparently "holy"). (a) The same Art, since the two adjacent paragraphs call it holy in the same words. (b) Two descriptions with no identity asserted.

- **Subtle Nature and Universal Subject.** ¶12 "a Subtle Nature pervading the universe" that is "susceptible of artificial alteration through all", and ¶10 "a Universal Subject in nature" that is "susceptible of nourishment in Man". Both are universal and both can be worked on, but the text does not identify them.

- **Whose Identity is searched?** "the searching and proving of His own Identity" (IV.4 ¶27). (a) Man's own: the subject is "man", and the Wisdom is what "the inquiring Spirit seeks within itself". (b) God's: the capital "His" allows it.

- **What is the "catholic torch", and what do "this Reason" and "its Rule" refer to?** (IV.4 ¶32). The torch could be (a) Light, the Key: but the torch "kindles the Light", which suggests it is distinct. It could be (b) the universal Reason whose Rule man submits to, or the Wisdom. Or it could be (c) a separate agent. "this Reason" has no stated antecedent; the same paragraph then directs the seeker to "the Root of Reason".

- **What are the "wards"?** (IV.4 ¶32). (a) The philosophers' enigmatic writings, which can be read only with the Key: the next sentences are about "the language of the philosophers" and enigmas "instructive to him who has the Key". (b) The inner structure of the Mystery or of nature, which only Light gets through: they sit beside "enter" and "Life is the nucleus".

- **The fixed tincture of life: what kind of operation, and who is "free"?** "how to render the fixed tincture of life volatile, and to return it, being free" (IV.4 ¶32). On the kind of operation: (a) a material volatilizing and re-fixing, or (b) an inward operation on the life-principle that frees it and brings it back. The torch works "morally or physically" and Life is the nucleus (¶32), and the search is inward (¶27), which favours (b). On the grammar: "being free" may describe the tincture (freed, then returned), which attaches more naturally to "it", or the knower. The chapter does not settle either question.
