# Method material — II.3 The Mysteries Continued

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The Thing behind the trance phenomena.** The ancients did not stop at trance marvels. They went inward to investigate the cause behind them.
   - "desirous rather to investigate the Thing itself, the subject of so many marvels, they passed the first phenomena to look for Causes, experimenting within" (II.3 ¶4)
   - *interpreted* (reading the "Thing itself" as the subject of the work); ATWOOD.

2. **The operator's own whole vitality and essence: the philosophic man.** Theurgy works on the total essence of the person. Bacon's maxim speaks of dissolving the philosophers' egg and examining the parts of the philosophic man (our translation).
   - "to concentrate the whole vitality, to turn the spiritual eye, to purify and analyze the total essence" (II.3 ¶5)
   - Also: "Volo ovum philosophorum dissolvere et partes philosophici hominis investare, nam hoc est initium ad alia" (II.3 ¶5). Our translation: "I wish to dissolve the egg of the philosophers and investigate the parts of the philosophic man, for this is the beginning towards other things."
   - *stated* for the total essence as the thing worked on; the Bacon point rests only on our translation. ATWOOD; QUOTED: "the experimental Friar" (Roger bacon, n1), endorsed.

3. **What gets dissolved: the sensual compact, or conceptive vehicle.** The fixed bond by which the soul has tied herself to the body is the thing dissolved and then renewed.
   - "that which was fixed, viz., the sensual compact, is dissolved, and that which is dissolved is renovated" (II.3 ¶37)
   - Also: "she may dissolve her own conceptive vehicle, even the parental bond" (II.3 ¶37); "the compact earthy body of sense" (II.3 n2); "the dark dominion of the selfhood" (II.3 n2).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

4. **The first life and the lapsed soul (Proserpine).** In the descent, consciousness is drawn into the innermost recesses of its own original life. There the intellect reaches its kindred life, which is the fallen soul hidden in a dark ground.
   - "its own congenial life, which is Proserpine, and that lapsed soul of ours, seated in her dark hypostasis unknown" (II.3 ¶11)
   - Also: "the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life" (II.3 ¶7).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

5. **Black Saturn: the dark matter at the threshold.** The heavy, dark, grave-smelling region at the entrance to Hades is the adepts' black matter. The true matter of the work lies asleep within the senses, locked in by both body and spirit.
   - "It is the Black Saturn of the adepts, and that appearing corruption that precedes the mystical death and regeneration into new life" (II.3 ¶18)
   - Also: "lapis niger, vilis, foetens, et dicitur origo mundi et oritur sicut germinantia" (II.3 ¶18); "Sendivogius calls it Urinus Saturni, with which he waters his lunar and solar plants" (II.3 ¶18); "the true species was laid asleep in sense, and doubly locked up, as it were, within both corporeal and spiritual confines" (II.3 ¶18).
   - The identity is *stated*. Reading "true species" as the matter of the work is *interpreted*. ATWOOD; QUOTED: Sendivogius.

6. **The "earth" of the work is our chaotic sense-life.** The sage's "interior of the earth", where the Stone is hidden, is not soil. It is our own disordered life of sense.
   - "not the feculent dead soil, but our dark divulsed chaotic life from sense" (II.3 ¶29)
   - Also: "Visit the interior of the earth rectifying, says the sage, and thou shalt find the hidden Stone, the true medicine" (II.3 ¶29).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

7. **First Matter, or mineral nature, which never holds one shape.** The raw matter keeps changing form like wind-driven cloud. Lully's monster-shaped "first principles" are the same chaos. Atwood says they could only have been known by inspecting one's own inner life.
   - "speaking of the mineral nature or First Matter, he says, The eye of man never saw her twice under one and the same shape" (II.3 ¶30)
   - Also: "certain fugitive spirits condensed in the air, in shape of divers monsters, beasts, and men, which move like clouds hither and thither" (II.3 ¶30); "as from the self-inspection of them in life" (II.3 ¶30).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Vaughan, Raymond Lully (endorsed); ATWOOD for the self-inspection claim.

8. **The divine germ in its raw, deformed state.** In its unredeemed condition the subject is the divine germ of humanity: overgrown, viscous and hard to find. The text calls it the Demogogon not yet animated by its own light, a rock in the wilderness, and Glaucus encrusted with weeds.
   - "In such a deplorable condition is the divine germ of humanity said to be beheld under the thousand evils of its birth" (II.3 ¶30)
   - Also: "For it is the whole Demogogon, as yet not actually animated by contact of his own returning light" (II.3 ¶30); "This is the rock in the wilderness,, because in great obscurity and difficult to find the way of" (II.3 ¶30); "Our viscous soul, as Synesius calls it, circulating in the midst of all her Adamical defilements" (II.3 ¶30).
   - The divine germ is *stated*. What "it" and "This" refer to is unclear (see Open questions). ATWOOD.

9. **The hidden central mountain.** The Rosicrucian letter describes an invisible mountain at the world's centre that holds treasure and is guarded by beasts. Atwood applies it to the initiatory passage.
   - "There is a mountain situated in the midst of the earth or center of the world, which is both small and great" (II.3 ¶32)
   - Also: "but, by the providence of God, it is invisible" (II.3 ¶32); Atwood: "appears to have reference to this passage of initiatory progress in the Mysteries" (II.3 ¶31).
   - *interpreted*; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross, applied by ATWOOD.

10. **The noble body that suffers, then is exalted.** The adept describes a body moved "from one Lord to another" that suffers at first and rejoices at the end. Atwood sets it beside Wisdom's early torments ("So, likewise"). Neither the Lords nor the vinegar is explained.
    - "there is in Alchemy a certain noble body, which is moved from one Lord to another; in the beginning of which there is suffering with vinegar; but, in the end, joy with exaltation" (II.3 ¶37)
    - *interpreted*; QUOTED: the adept.

11. **Natural life, analysed down to its hidden root.** The Hermetic process takes "the natural life as it presents itself" and analyses it until it reaches a hidden original of life made of three essences, which are not named.
    - "he would arrive finally at the basement, wherein is hidden the true alkaline original of life in this threefold essence separately contained" (II.3 ¶41)
    - *stated* that natural life is the object of analysis; the three essences are not named. ATWOOD.

12. **The Salt of Saturn: the dark root of the Stone.** The deepest principle of the Stone is light-deprived. Its perpetual motion gives rise to the first material universe. It is called the mineral soul, Demogorgon, and Anaxagoras's central earth, "but its Being is Tartarus".
    - "The Salt of Saturn, that most abstruse principle of the Stone --- the most ancient Demogorgon --- aethero dempto --- deprived of light" (II.3 ¶41)
    - Also: "whose perpetual motion emanates the first material universe, and is the mineral soul" (II.3 ¶41); "A body immarcessible, than which ther is nothing more ancient, vigorous, and young." (II.3 ¶41).
    - *stated*; voice UNCLEAR (the adept's words or Atwood's).

13. **The "compound creature".** The adept says to open the compound creature and divide its elements. What it is goes unexplained (see Open questions).
    - "Open the compound creature; look up the elements; divide the elements, and you shall find the quintessential nature" (II.3 ¶52)
    - *stated* as an instruction, referent unexplained; QUOTED: the adept.

14. **The Power hidden in man.** The Stone, Salt and Light are finally named as a power within the human being. This is the Salt we are told to have "in ourselves".
    - "This therefore is that Power which is hidden in man, the true Light which lighteth every man that cometh into the world" (II.3 ¶55)
    - Also: "that Salt which the Savior orders that we should have it in ourselves" (II.3 ¶54); "as Abraham did, as soon as he had gotten the creature into his hands" (II.3 ¶55).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **Intellect: the golden bough.** The golden bough needed before the descent is read as a ray of living light, Lully's "Brancha Spiritualis". It works its way by reason alone through a murky ether to the lapsed soul.
   - "which we therefore understand ontologically, as a ray of living light, golden and flexible, the true Brancha Spiritualis of Raymond Lully" (II.3 ¶11)
   - Also: "insinuating by rational penetration alone through the murky circumference of the chloric ether into its own congenial life" (II.3 ¶11); "Intellectus naturam habens subtilem ad intellignedum res intellibiles" (II.3 ¶11). Our translation: "Intellect, having a subtle nature for understanding intelligible things."
   - That bough = ray of living light = Brancha Spiritualis is *stated*. That this ray is the intellect is *interpreted*, resting on the Lully line (our translation) and "rational penetration alone". ATWOOD.

2. **The re-entering Light of Reason, which is the adepts' Sulfur.** Reason's light flows back into the soul's dark moisture. It purifies that moisture, breaks up the old life, and in doing so produces the shifting scenes of images.
   - "the re-entering Light of Reason in the Mysteries, which is that Sulfur of adepts, causing all this manifold scenery in the disruption of life" (II.3 ¶30)
   - Also: "before it is purified by the inflowing light of reason" (II.3 ¶29).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

3. **The inquisitive light acting as a ferment (the fire).** An entering light meets its matching counterpart, kindles with it, and converts the surrounding medium into its own life. Fire wipes out the traces that generation left on the soul.
   - "the inquisitive light once entering as a ferment combats meeting its proper pole, and conjointly kindling with it, absorbs, transmutes, and occultates the surrounding medium into its own abyssal life" (II.3 n40)
   - Also: "Through fire the divine oracles more plainly teach, that those strains are all finally obliterated that accede to the soul from generation" (II.3 n40).
   - *interpreted* (the operational sense); voice UNCLEAR (a footnote attached to the mountain allegory's fire).

4. **Two fires of life, outer and inner, drawn to each other.** Past the Aquaster one sees the "conspiracy" between Prester and Sun. Fire descends from above against its nature, finds a body, and rises again with it in constant exchange. The Fire Spirit feeds from its root.
   - "the miraculous conspiracy that is between the Prester and the Sun, the external and internal fire of life, the thing desiring and the thing desired" (II.3 ¶41)
   - Also: "why all influx of fire descends against the nature of fire, and comes from above downwards, until having found a body, it reascends therewith in perpetual interchange" (II.3 ¶41).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: the adept.

5. **The Prester: the Fire Spirit of Life.** The ultimate agent is the life-fire that sustains everything, the supernatural centre of each living thing.
   - "the Prester of Zoroaster which in the Chaldean sense means the Fire Spirit of Life, and is that Identity in all which sustains all by the efflux of His power" (II.3 ¶54)
   - Also: "the supernatural center of every living thing, the infinitely powerful and all-efficient making power" (II.3 ¶54).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

6. **The true Efficient, found within.** The real operative cause is not outside. It lies in the intelligible substance of reason's own Law, and only a reason that sees more in causation than mere sequence reaches it.
   - "For there the true Efficient is to be found, which is not externally developed" (II.3 ¶46)
   - Also: "draw forth the true Efficient and to know it in co-identity" (II.3 ¶5).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

7. **Theurgic assistance.** The soul reaches her end with help that is called theurgic but not described.
   - "proceeding thus, by theurgic assistance, arrives at her desired end" (II.3 ¶46)
   - *stated* that such help exists; what it consists of is not given. ATWOOD.

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **Prior purification, ordeals and selection.** Only those who had completed the earlier rites and ordeals were found fit and admitted. By then their inclination had already been freed from sense.
   - "they only, amongst the ancients who had fulfilled the previous rites and undergone all the required ordeals, were entrusted with the passport" (II.3 ¶5)
   - Also: "after the ordeal rites had been undergone, and the few who were found fit, selected for further initiation" (II.3 ¶14); "the inclination being already freed from the dominion and all the superficial progeny of sense" (II.3 ¶9).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

2. **Concentrative energy and a penetrating intellect.** Freeing the spirit from the body does not by itself yield truth. The operator needs concentration and an intellect that penetrates other spheres rather than reasoning discursively within its own.
   - "for this a concentrative energy is needed, and an intellect penetrating into other spheres, rather than discursive in its own" (II.3 ¶5)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

3. **A will held by a purely rational motive.** The will must hold a motive grounded only in reason, firm enough to resist the temptations of the deeper sphere. It must be backed by zeal, piety and a reason that prevails.
   - "the will should conceive within itself a motive purely rational to withstand the temptations of its next including sphere" (II.3 ¶9)
   - Also: "it is only by exceeding zeal and piety of intention, such as is ascribed to Aeneas in search of his father, and a prevailing reason" (II.3 ¶12); Plato: "It is necessary that a man should have his right opinion as firm as adamant in him when he descends into Hades" (II.3 ¶12).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Plato (endorsed).

4. **Rational grasp of the Good.** Anyone who cannot reach the idea of the Good by dialectic, "according to essence", knows only opinion and will sleep in Hades.
   - "he who is not able by the exercise of his reason to define the idea of the Good, separating it from all other objects and piercing, as in a battle, through every kind of argument" (II.3 ¶12)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Plato, Republic VII (endorsed).

5. **Faith.** Only the intellect's own glance, made in faith, can hold the fleeting vapour of the lapsed soul.
   - "nothing but the glance of its proper intellect by faith can arrest it" (II.3 ¶11)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

6. **Love of truth, not of gold.** Those who hold the knowledge give it only to lovers of truth. Seekers of gold failed.
   - "they who have this knowledge know where and how likewise to bestow it, discerning betwixt the lovers of mammon and of truth" (II.3 ¶34)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

7. **Prayer, courage, sincere seeking of God, and one's own labour.** One goes on the darkest night, prepared by prayer and armed only with courage and the sincere seeking of God. The way is not asked of anyone, and one must not turn back.
   - "It is necessary that you arm yourself with a resolute heroic courage, lest you fear those things that will happen and fall back" (II.3 ¶32)
   - Also: "see that you prepare yourself by prayer" (II.3 ¶32); "You need no sword or other bodily weapon, only call upon you God, sincerely and heartily seeking Him" (II.3 ¶32); "only by the worthy man’s self-labor and investigation" (II.3 ¶32); "Be resolute, and take heed that you return not" (II.3 ¶33); "Nunc animis opus, Aenea, nunc pectore firmo" (II.3 n38).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross, applied by ATWOOD.

8. **Knowing how, and the right disposition; a fiery mind given to piety.** The soul can perform her own dissolution only if she knows how and is rightly disposed.
   - "if she know how, and have the right disposition awarded" (II.3 ¶37)
   - Also: "If thou extend the fiery mind to the work of piety," (II.3 ¶37).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the Oracle.

9. **Persevering labour and experience.** As in any science, success needs sustained work. Errors along the way do not refute the science.
   - "And as in the pursuit of other sciences and arts, though, and persevering labor and experience are required to ensure success" (II.3 ¶36)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

10. **Wise simplicity and a will subdued to the Law of Wisdom.** The hidden Power is known only to those who have submitted their own will.
    - "known only to the wisely simple who have subdued their will to the Law of Wisdom" (II.3 ¶55)
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

11. **Guidance.** At each threshold a guide leads: the Sybil, a guide who offers himself unasked, a conductor who opens the vestibule, the Hierophant, the priest who leads by the hand. The guide's identity is never stated.
    - "only follow your guide who will offer himself to you and will meet you in the way" (II.3 ¶32)
    - Also: "the Sybil leads her hero to the dark descent" (II.3 ¶16); "till the conductor laying open for him the vestibule, he enters" (II.3 n37); "the Chaldaic oracle promises that the mortal, approaching the fire, will have a light from divinity" (II.3 n37); "were passed on by the Hierophant of the inner temple" (II.3 ¶36); "taking me by the hand, brought me to the penetralia of the temple" (II.3 ¶45).
    - *stated* that a guide is present; who or what the guide is goes unexplained. ATWOOD; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross, Themistus, Apuleius.

12. **Secrecy.** The way was kept hidden for the safety of unprepared souls and for the sake of the wisdom to be revealed.
    - "every precaution has been instituted to keep the way a secret from the world, as well for its own sake as for the cause of justice and divine wisdom about to be revealed" (II.3 ¶7)
    - Also: "for it is entirely concealed from the world" (II.3 ¶5); "I would tell you, replies the Epidaurian, if it could be lawfully told" (II.3 ¶45).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter has no single numbered sequence. It tells the same passage several times: the Aeneid descent, the Hades of images, the Rosicrucian mountain, death and initiation, Hermetic analysis, the Introspection, and the alchemical ignition. The stages below follow the chapter's order. Lining the accounts up as one sequence is *interpreted*, except where the text states an order.

1. **Lesser Mysteries: the internal life opened.** The first, popular initiation reveals the inner life as an open field of contemplation, without rule. It changes little before purification. Mesmerism, "working in the same matter", gives the same kind of entrance.
   - "We have so far developed the nature of the internal life only, as it was at first revealed to the aspirant in the Lesser Mysteries; and this was the only popular initiation open to all" (II.3 ¶3)
   - Also: "Previous to the purificative rites little change, therefore, was effected" (II.3 ¶4).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

2. **Dissolving the sensible medium: the key of the vestibule.** Entrancing the senses dissolves ordinary sense-consciousness and opens it onto another life. Atwood offers images for this dissolution: alkali by acid; the selfhood by its own light through magnetic friction; animal life into vegetable; cerebral into ganglionic; the dense body of sense made to flow. The alchemical rule is to dissolve and recongeal, over and over.
   - "the key of the Hermetic vestibule may be said to be already in our hands, which are able to dissolve the sensible medium and convert it to the experience of another life" (II.3 ¶5)
   - Also: "the dark dominion of the selfhood by the magnetic friction of its proper light, the sensible or animal into the vegetable, the cerebral into the ganglionic life" (II.3 n2); "that the compact earthy body of sense may be rarified and flow as a passive watery spirit" (II.3 n2); Albertus: "all that we teach is nothing else but to dissolve and recongeal the spirit, to make the fixed volatile and the volatile fixed" (II.3 n2).
   - *stated*. n2 frames the images as a supposition ("shall we not suppose it so to be dissolved"). ATWOOD; QUOTED: Albertus Magnus, the Rosarium (endorsed).

3. **Translating consciousness beneath the medium, through darkening, toward the Central Source.** This is the second, concealed operation. Consciousness passes, clear and rational, out of the Aquaster into the Fire World as the dark descent begins.
   - "the next solution, or resolution rather, was operated which was to translate the consciousness underneath this medium by obscuration, towards the Central Source" (II.3 ¶5)
   - Also: "As the consciousness passing the middle region, clear and rational from out the Aquaster, enters the Fire World, and the Sybil leads her hero to the dark descent" (II.3 ¶16).
   - ¶5 is *stated*. Equating it with ¶16 is *interpreted* (sequence only; Aquaster and Fire World are not defined). ATWOOD.

4. **Descent into Hades: vital submersion and the black threshold.** Hades is the state in which consciousness is deliberately sunk into its first life. Its entrance is the Black Saturn, the apparent corruption before mystical death. The descent is easy; the way back is the real labour.
   - "the whole allusion is to a state of vital submersion in the Mysteries, when the consciousness is artificially drawn about the penetralia of its first life" (II.3 ¶7)
   - Also: "Facilis descensus Averni" … "Hoc opus, hic labor est" (II.3 ¶8); "It is the Black Saturn of the adepts" (II.3 ¶18).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

5. **Holding course through the image-world.** Once established in her essence, the mind must separate out the good from the evil joined to it at birth. It must not attend to formed lights or their voices. It must stay unmoved by the phantoms, keep its eyes upward, and go further in.
   - "her final duty to separate the good and reject the evil therein by birth allied" (II.3 ¶12)
   - Also: "he apply not his mind to it, nor esteem the voice proceeding thence to be true" (II.3 ¶20); "on entering the interior part of the Temple, unmoved and guarded by the sacred rites, they genuinely receive into their bosom divine illumination" (II.3 ¶22); "Let the immortal depth of thy soul be predominant, and all thy eyes extend upwards" (II.3 ¶26).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Psellus, Proclus, the oracle (all endorsed).

6. **Drying the moist vehicle.** Purification works by drying off the vapour that breeds images. The soul turns away from nature and presses straight to the centre without exploring the intermediate spheres, and so becomes "a dry splendor". In Hermes' figure, the watery, corrupted nature is drawn off the fire again and again until its redness is pure.
   - "when however the soul earnestly desires to depart from nature (i.e., strives to penetrate centrally without exploring the intermediate spheres), then she becomes a dry splendor from exhalation" (II.3 ¶27)
   - Also: "during the evaporative process of purification, and before the lucid vision of the light within" (II.3 ¶22); "take, my son, this watery and corrupted nature, which is as a coal holding the fire, which if thou salt withdraw so often until the redness is made pure" (II.3 ¶27).
   - The Porphyry line and Atwood's gloss on it are *stated*. Reading "evaporative" as drying, and the Hermes passage read as this same operation, are *interpreted* (¶22, ¶27). QUOTED: Porphyry, with ATWOOD's gloss; QUOTED: Hermes (endorsed).

7. **Rectifying the interior earth.** The chaotic sense-life is opened, rectified, dissolved and reunited, and so becomes a spiritual body. The inflowing light of reason purifies the moisture. Opposing forces clash until the attraction to the Hypostatic Reality is found and fixed in union.
   - "our dark divulsed chaotic life from sense, which opened and rectified, dissolved and reunited, is changed from an earthly to a spiritual body, by rapport divine" (II.3 ¶29)
   - Also: "until this attraction is found and finally established in union, the opposive powers display their mutual forces in discordant dissolute array" (II.3 ¶29).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

8. **Finding the Seed of Spirit.** When the lowest material level of this life is purified, the Seed of Spirit is found within it.
   - "within the material extreme of this life, when it is purified, the Seed of Spirit is at last found" (II.3 ¶30)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

9. **The mountain sequence: night, guide, wind, earthquake, fire, calm.** The letter gives its own fixed order:
   - go on the darkest night, with a guide, to the mountain at midnight;
   - a vehement wind shatters the rock, and beasts appear;
   - an earthquake overthrows what the wind left;
   - a fire consumes the earthly rubbish and uncovers the treasure, though it cannot yet be seen;
   - near daybreak comes calm and the day-star, and then the tincture.

   Only the fire is glossed: n40 says it wipes out the strains generation left on the soul.
   - "there shall follow a fire that will consume the earthly rubbish, and discover the treasure: but as yet you cannot see it" (II.3 ¶33)
   - Also: "a most vehement and very great wind that will shale the whole mountain and shatter the rock to pieces" (II.3 ¶33); "After this wind will come an earthquake which will overthrow those things which the wind had left" (II.3 ¶33); "near daybreak, there shall be a great calm, and you shall see the day-star arise" (II.3 ¶33).
   - The order is *stated* within the allegory. The meaning of the fire is *interpreted* via n40; wind and earthquake are not decoded. QUOTED: "these famous Christian philosophers", applied by ATWOOD.

10. **End of the Lesser Mysteries at the Stygian lake.** The soul stays "in Hades" as long as she regards her own image objectively. The Lesser Mysteries end with the soul at the edge of death, Tartarus in view, and the Hierophant passes the aspirants on.
    - "And here the Lesser Mysteries ended; the soul as it were, on the borders of the Stygian lake in view of Tartarus" (II.3 ¶36)
    - *stated*; ATWOOD. (See Open questions on how this fits with ¶14.)

11. **Initiation into the Grand Mysteries is lived as dying.** First come wandering and darkness, then horror at the verge, then sudden divine light, sacred knowledge and freedom.
    - "the first stage is nothing but errors and uncertainties; laborings, wandering, and darkness" (II.3 ¶37)
    - Also: "The mind is affected and agitated in death, just as it is in initiation into the Grand Mysteries" (II.3 ¶37); "But this scene once over, a miraculous and divine light displays itself" (II.3 ¶37).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Stoboeus, via Dr Warburton (endorsed).

12. **Philosophic death: the gate of blackness.** The soul has bound herself to the body, so she can unbind herself. She dissolves her conceptive vehicle and returns consciously to life under another law. The bodily elements stay in place, and the body is not destroyed but re-entered and strengthened. This is the one secret that unlocks the rest.
    - "she may dissolve her own conceptive vehicle, even the parental bond, and return consciously (the elementary principles remaining, nor yet suffered to depart) under the dominion of another law to life" (II.3 ¶37)
    - Also: "for to know this, indeed, is to know all, but to be ignorant of this, is to be ignorant of all" (II.3 ¶37); "Take away, therefore, the vapor from the water, and the blackness from the oily tincture, and death from the faeces; and by Dissolution thou shalt possess a triumphant reward" (II.3 ¶37); "And all without destruction to the mortal body" (II.3 ¶37).
    - *stated* (Atwood's gloss on Porphyry). The adept's three separate removals are not explained individually. ATWOOD; QUOTED: Porphyry and the adept (endorsed).

13. **Analysis down to the foundation.** Like taking a building apart stone by stone, the operator separates the parts of natural life "spiritually and wisely, one from another, graciously", discarding the rubbish, until reaching the basement where rebuilding can start. Beyond the Aquaster, in the Fire World, the hidden workings of fire are seen.
    - "should undertake to decompose it stone by stone, setting all aside, with the dirt and rubbish, as he proceeds, he would at last come to the earth which is at the foundation, and have space to build up anew" (II.3 ¶41)
    - Also: "Terra ab igni, subtile a spisso, suaviter cum multo ingenio" (II.3 ¶41). Our translation: "The earth from the fire, the subtle from the dense, gently, with much skill." Also: "he that has once passed the Aquaster, and entered the Fire World, sees what is both invisible and incredible to common men" (II.3 ¶41).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the adept.

14. **Conversion to the Generator.** The purified soul turns to and joins its source. Knowledge of true Being comes only by this conversion.
    - "it is necessary that the soul when purified should associate with its generator" (II.3 ¶43)
    - Also: "The soul will, after a manner, clasp God to herself" (II.3 ¶42).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Porphyry, Oracula Chaldaica (endorsed).

15. **Introspection: the formless Divine Light.** The initiate sees the Divine Light itself, without form or figure, and is told to listen to its voice.
    - "This is the Introspection which Psellus speaks of, as distinguished from the Superinspection which takes place in Hades" (II.3 ¶46)
    - Also: "When thou seest a Fire without Form, Shining flashingly through the depths of the World, Hear the voice of Fire" (II.3 ¶47).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Psellus and the Oracle (endorsed).

16. **Kindling: light meeting light.** Long converse with the thing itself, and a life conformed to it, suddenly kindle a self-nourishing light in the soul. Sight turned inward, purified and inquiring scientifically, finds the light that is its own substance.
    - "directed within, and being purified also, and scientifically inquiring, discovers at last that other light which is the substance of its own, until light meeting light apprehends itself alone" (II.3 ¶50)
    - Also: "from long converse with this thing itself, accompanied by a life in conformity to it, on a sudden a light, as it were a leaping fire, will be enkindled in the soul" (II.3 ¶50).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Plato, seventh Epistle (endorsed).

17. **The alchemical restatement: open, divide, build the new world, ignite.** Open the compound creature and divide its elements to find the quintessence. Open that, and the angelical spirit, the beam of Wisdom, appears. The first separation is seen with the senses, the second only with intellectual eyes. Making the furnace "with our glass" makes a new world, which Atwood glosses as a new hypostasis and a new stone. Vaughan's next step is to unite heaven and earth in triple proportion under generative heat. The Demogorgon, once visible but without its light, then ignites.
    - "there concurreth in the separation of the first, a sensible aspect, in the other we behold with intellectual eyes" (II.3 ¶52)
    - Also: "He that maketh a furnace with our glass to it maketh a new world (58); --- a new hypostasis, and a new stone" (II.3 ¶52); "unite the heaven in triple proportion to the earth, and then apply a generative heat to both, and they will attract from above the star-fore of nature" (II.3 ¶52); "which before was made visible without its subject light; but at length becoming ignited" (II.3 ¶52).
    - The instructions are *stated*. Furnace, glass, proportion and heat are not explained, and reading the ignition as a stage is *interpreted*. QUOTED: the adept, Hermes, Vaughan (endorsed), with ATWOOD's gloss.

18. **Perfection: multiply the Prester and place it in the supreme Ether.** The closing Latin may enjoin understanding, testing, investigating, noting, thinking and imagining, then make the Creator sit on his throne (our translation), but that reading rests on our translation only.
    - "the grand perfection of their Art was to multiply the Prester and place him in the most supreme Ether" (II.3 ¶56)
    - Also: "et statue rem integritate et fac sedere Creatorm in throno suo" (II.3 ¶56).
    - The perfection is *stated*; the Latin is *interpreted* (our translation only). ATWOOD, reporting "our doctors"; QUOTED: Liber de Creatione, Authore Abraham (n62).

Atwood says she has left out much on mystical death and regeneration, deferring it to "the manifestation of the Philosophic Subject" (II.3 ¶56).

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **Entering the deeper state: a sense of power, then darkness and corruption.**
   - "It discovers a fearful mystery in the opening sensation of power, in a life which, at its entrance, is described as dark, delusive, and dangerous, and more corrupted far than the foregoing" (II.3 ¶5)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

2. **The black threshold.** The air thickens and darkens, heavy and inelastic, with a grave-like smell. This is Black Saturn. Corruption that shows itself as the matter circulates toward renewal is a sign that the Art is working.
   - "the thickening darkness of the nether air verging to the chaos of matter and flowing out from the perpetual motion of the first life" (II.3 ¶18)
   - Also: "giving forth a murky odor, like that of graves" (II.3 ¶18); "the corruption and evil of mortality is made manifest in the ultimate circulation of the matter to be renewed, and on either side it is a signal of Art" (II.3 ¶37).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

3. **The tumult at the reception.** The ground bellows, woods move and dogs howl as the Goddess approaches. Claudian adds trembling shrines, lightning, a roar from the depths and triple Hecate. Plato likens the descent to an earthquake. None of this is decoded.
   - "Sub pedibs mugire solum, et juga coepta moveri Sylvarum, visaeque canes ululare per umbram, Adventante Dea" (II.3 ¶19)
   - Also: "Plato, amongst others, likens the descent of the soul into these oblivious realms of generation to an earthquake and other strong convulsions of nature" (II.3 ¶20).
   - *interpreted* (verses untranslated in the book); QUOTED: Virgil, Claudian, Plato.

4. **Formed lights versus formless light.** Lights in some form or figure ("superinspection") mark the Hades stage and are not to be trusted. Light without any form ("Introspection") marks the true stage and "will not deceive".
   - "the first called superinspection, when he who celebrates the divine rites sees a mere apparition, as, for instance, of light in some form or figure" (II.3 ¶20)
   - Also: "When the initiated person sees the Divine Light itself without any form or figure" (II.3 ¶46); "This will not deceive" (II.3 ¶46).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Psellus (endorsed).

5. **Crowded images by rapport.** In this state a person meets the whole image-world of his sphere, not just his own images.
   - "each by rapport in this state becomes conversant with the whole phantasmagoric universe of his sphere" (II.3 ¶22)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

6. **Endless change of shape.** The matter takes ever-changing, monstrous, cloud-like forms.
   - "as clouds driven by the wind are forced to this and that figure, but cannot possibly retain one constant form, so is she persecuted by the fire of nature" (II.3 ¶30)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Vaughan (endorsed).

7. **Perplexity at the start.** Consciousness is anxious and bewildered, unable to move forward, until the way is opened.
   - "continues for a while in a state of solicitude and painful amazement, unable of itself to discover, through so great a cloud of darkness, that Hypostatic Reality" (II.3 ¶29)
   - Also: "the initiated is seized at first with solicitude and perplexity, unable to move a step forward" (II.3 n37).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Themistus.

8. **The lapsed soul shows as a subtle, fleeting vapour.**
   - "whose vapor is so subtle and transient that nothing but the glance of its proper intellect by faith can arrest it" (II.3 ¶11)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

9. **What the found Seed looks like.**
   - "a decompounded ens, extremely heavy, shining through the darkness like a fiery star, being full of eyes like pearls or aglets" (II.3 ¶30)
   - *stated* as description, not explained further; QUOTED: the adept (unnamed).

10. **Dread and awe at the crossing.** Dread comes as the soul is about to give up her natal bond. Atwood reads Solomon's "fear of God" as the soul's awe as it begins to perceive itself in its Identic Source. Wisdom torments at first, then reveals her secret. Before the vision, astonishment and dread seize the initiate.
    - "as the spiritual regard in the mysteries, already involuted, and drawing towards its end, with awe, begins to perceive itself in that Identic Source" (II.3 ¶37)
    - Also: "dread fills the sould about to relinquish her natal bond in life" (II.3 ¶37); "at first she will walk with him by crooked ways, and bring fear and dread upon him" (II.3 ¶37); "those who are initiated are said to be seized with astonishment and dread" (II.3 ¶50).
    - Treating the dread as a sign is *interpreted* (the text calls it a feature of the transition). The Proclus line is *stated*. ATWOOD; QUOTED: son of Dirach, Proclus.

11. **Calm and the day-star.** After wind, earthquake and fire, near daybreak, the darkness clears.
    - "there shall be a great calm, and you shall see the day-star arise, and the darkness will disappear" (II.3 ¶33)
    - *interpreted*; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross.

12. **The Sun at midnight.** Apuleius reports crossing Proserpine's threshold and seeing the Sun at midnight. Later Atwood names "that midnight Sun of Apuleius" as the ignited Stone.
    - "At midnight I saw the Sun shining with a splendid light" (II.3 ¶45)
    - Also: "And this we take to be that midnight Sun of Apuleius" (II.3 ¶54).
    - Linking ¶45 to ¶54 is *interpreted* (same author, same image); ¶54 is *stated*. QUOTED: the Epidaurian; ATWOOD.

13. **A sudden flash in darkness, in a waking state.** The intellectual contact flashes like clear light in deep darkness. The sacred light is seen while awake, "rapt from sense", not in dream.
    - "this knowledge sometimes shines forth with a most rapid coruscation like a bright and clear light in the most profound darkness" (II.3 ¶50)
    - Also: "Nor was the vision like the dreams of sleep, But seen whilst vigilant you brave the deep" (II.3 ¶51); "Whence rapt from sense with energy divine" (II.3 ¶51).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Apuleius, Porphyry's Hymn to Plotinus (endorsed).

14. **The luciferous wheel.** Once ignited, the Demogorgon shines out of the dark abyss of being like a radiant wheel.
    - "reflects from out the dark abyss of being, as a luciferous wheel, with its radiant sections, all comprehending in their law" (II.3 ¶52)
    - *interpreted*; ATWOOD.

15. **Doves that lead the way.** Hinted to be a known alchemical image; left unexplained.
    - "And those doves that lead the way too, are they not known to our Alchemists and those chosen seats?" (II.3 ¶11)
    - *interpreted* (no gloss given); ATWOOD.

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Mistaking trance freedom and marvels for truth.** Insensibility, cures and exaltation in trance do not satisfy the work. A spirit's freedom from the body does not guarantee that what it reveals is true.
   - "it is not, as they say, that the Spirit is free from material bondage, or able to range the universe of her own sphere, that guarantees the truth of her revealments" (II.3 ¶5)
   - Also: "the insensibility, the cures, the mental exaltation" (II.3 ¶4).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

2. **Capture by deluding desires; the hard way back.** Unprepared souls are seized by deceptive desires and do lasting harm. Going down is easy; coming back is the labour.
   - "lest unprepared souls, presuming to enter, should be taken captive by deluding and fatal desires, and work irremediable evil there" (II.3 ¶7)
   - Also: "Sed revocare gradum superasque evadere ad auras, Hoc opus, hic labor est." (II.3 ¶8).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Virgil (the Sybil).

3. **Falling asleep in Hades.** Losing vigilance means being absorbed, without the body, in all the defilements of that state. It is the worst evil.
   - "To fall asleep in Hades was indeed to be absorbed, without the encumbrance of body, in all its defilements" (II.3 ¶12)
   - Also: "or, as Virgil has it, to be a king in hell" (II.3 ¶12).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

4. **Love of bodily beauty; inability to sustain contemplation.** These are the two ways souls get detained below (Theseus and Pirithous).
   - "the one because he was too much a lover of corporeal beauty, the other through his natural inability to sustain the arduous altitude of divine contemplation" (II.3 ¶13)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Proclus (endorsed).

5. **Apparitions born of one's own passions.** Lights and voices met in the rites come from the soul's own passions. They have no substance, and the imagination makes them.
   - "These apparitions are created by the passions of the soul, in performing divine rites, mere appearances, having no substance, and therefore not signifying anything true" (II.3 ¶20)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Psellus (endorsed).

6. **Pan's terrors.** The vaporous state of universal being shows itself in monstrous shapes to frighten off anyone who tries to grasp it.
   - "who exhibited himself in every variety of atrocious disguises of wild beasts, and monsters, and demoniacal appearances, that he might affright those who would captivate him" (II.3 ¶20)
   - Also: "Monstrum, horrendum, informe, ingens, cui lumen ademptu." (II.3 ¶30).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

7. **Images that look divine.** Some phantoms take on divine appearance and draw the insufficiently purified back toward matter.
   - "material images, assuming the appearance even of things divine, constantly attended on the Mysteries, drawing towards them souls not yet sufficiently purified, and separating them from truth" (II.3 ¶22)
   - Also: "the impulsive forms of certain terrestrial demons appear, which call the attention off from undefiled advantages to matter" (II.3 ¶22).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Proclus (endorsed).

8. **Clever, healing spirits.** The spirits of this region seem intelligent and skilled, even in healing, and use this to trap an unperfected spiritual faculty.
   - "the spiritual also, not et perfected, is liable to be caught in the traps of these exterior spirits" (II.3 ¶23)
   - Also: "giving testimony also of their virtue in the art of healing and other secrets, by which they deceive and detain the unwary from the search of better things" (II.3 ¶23).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Basil Valentine (endorsed).

9. **Forgetting the source.** The demons keep souls at work below, forgetting where they came from. Hades is a faithless depth that delights in images.
   - "Nor e’er remember in the dark abyss The splendid palace of their sire sublime." (II.3 ¶25)
   - Also: "incline not to the dark world whose depth is a faithless bottom and Hades dark all over, squalid, delighting in images" (II.3 ¶26); Synesius: "at natural war with the truth-seeking soul" (II.3 ¶24).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Proclus' Hymn to the Sun, the oracle, Synesius (endorsed).

10. **Clinging to nature.** A soul that keeps associating with nature takes on moisture and becomes heavy, shadowed and bound to images.
    - "But it attracts moisture when it continually endeavors to associate with nature, whose operations are effected in moisture" (II.3 ¶27)
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Porphyry (endorsed).

11. **Guardian horrors and alluring phantoms.** Experiences that are real yet illusory surround the inner chamber. In the Rosicrucian letter the mountain is ringed with beasts and ravenous birds through the envy of the Devil, and lions and dragons meet the seeker.
    - "the many real, though chimerical horrors and enticing phantoms that haunt around, guarding the secret chamber of their mineral soul" (II.3 ¶29)
    - Also: "there is an angel standing upon it, and that angel struggles to prevail" (II.3 ¶29); "compassed about with very cruel beasts and ravenous birds, which make the way thither both difficult and dangerous" (II.3 ¶32); "You will be encountered by lions, dragons, and other terrible wild beasts; but fear not nay of these things" (II.3 ¶33).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Enoch, Brethren of the Rosy Cross.

12. **Fear and turning back.**
    - "Be sure you fall not of." (II.3 ¶33)
    - Also: "lest you fear those things that will happen and fall back" (II.3 ¶32).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross.

13. **Delusive visions are obstacles, not refutations.** Errors met during the conscious transference oppose the science; they do not disprove it.
    - "those delusive visions and errors which occur during the conscious transference to a more excellent condition of being" (II.3 ¶36)
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

14. **Stooping toward the precipice.** The oracle warns against bending down toward the light-hating world below.
    - "Stoop not down, for a precipice lies below in the earth;" (II.3 ¶42)
    - Also: "Enlarge not thou thy destiny," (II.3 ¶42).
    - *interpreted*; QUOTED: Oracula Chaldaica.

15. **Taking any mental image for the goal.** Everything that appears in the mind other than the First Cause is the mind's own image. Divinity has no form and may not be imaged.
    - "Except the First Cause, whatever may appear or may not appear in the mind, know that to be the mind’s maya (or image or delusion) as of light or darkness" (II.3 ¶48)
    - Also: "it was forbidden to represent Him under any image whatever, even so much as a letter or point" (II.3 ¶48).
    - *stated*; QUOTED: Indian record (tr. Sir William Jones), Zohar (endorsed).

16. **Unreflective reason.** Reason that serves the senses, seeing in causation nothing beyond sequence, cannot understand.
    - "The rude, uneducated reason, however, which serves sensibles without reflection, will not understand" (II.3 ¶46)
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

## The result

1. **Knowing the true Efficient by identity.** The aim of Theurgy is to draw forth the active cause within and know it by becoming one with it.
   - "draw forth the true Efficient and to know it in co-identity" (II.3 ¶5)
   - *interpreted* ("co-identity" read as knowing by identity); ATWOOD.

2. **Joining the source: Wisdom, the Elysian light, the Generator.** The purified soul joins its Generator, and only this conversion gives knowledge of true Being. The chapter names the goal in several ways.
   - "the virtue of its after this conversion is said to consist in a scientific knowledge of true Being, which cannot be obtained either otherwise or without such a conversion" (II.3 ¶43)
   - Also: "It s necessary that the soul, when purified, should associate with its Generator" (II.3 ¶2); "the discovery of Wisdom in her light abode" (II.3 ¶9); "before inquiry can hope to meet its object in the Elysian light" (II.3 ¶5); "an inquiry of Intellect after its Paternal Source" (II.3 ¶13).
   - Stated for each passage. That they all name one goal is *interpreted*. QUOTED: Porphyry (endorsed); ATWOOD.

3. **A spiritual body.** The rectified sense-life is changed from an earthly to a spiritual body.
   - "is changed from an earthly to a spiritual body, by rapport divine" (II.3 ¶29)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

4. **A dry soul that shines with its own light.** Turned to herself, the soul unfolds her essence and lights her whole sphere.
   - "when she is converted to herself, she evolves her proper essence and irradiates the whole circumference with her own abundant oxygenating and dispersive light" (II.3 ¶27)
   - *stated*; voice UNCLEAR (follows Heraclitus; n30 cites Proclus).

5. **The tincture.** The chief treasure is a tincture able to turn the world to gold, if the world were worthy. Its meaning is not given.
   - "a certain exalted tincture with which the world, if it served God and were worthy of such gifts, might be tinged and turned into most pure gold" (II.3 ¶33)
   - *interpreted*; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross. (Atwood says the promise of gold drew futile searches, ¶34.)

6. **Regeneration into the Causal Source, with the body preserved.** The willing life passes out of its forgetful fall into conscious remembrance of its source. The body is re-entered and strengthened with "the elixir of an immortal life".
   - "the willing life was made to pass out of its present oblivious fall, through regeneration, into the reminiscent consciousness of her Causal Source" (II.3 ¶37)
   - Also: "Thou shalt preserve the flexible body likewise." (II.3 ¶38); "Even through death, re-entering into and fortifying it with the elixir of an immortal life." (II.3 ¶39).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the Oracle.

7. **Freedom among the Blessed.**
   - "And now become perfect and initiated, they are free, and no longer under restraint; but crowned and triumphant, they walk up and down in the regions of the Blessed" (II.3 ¶37)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Stoboeus, via Warburton (endorsed).

8. **The soul knows herself as a Whole.** United in consciousness, the soul knows her whole nature, not part of it. She shares in Deity, perceives the giver of life as present, and recognizes the revelation of her Law as her own self.
   - "the soul knows herself as a Whole which before knew but a part only of her human nature" (II.3 ¶46)
   - Also: "that the supplier of life is present" (II.3 ¶46); "believes its revelation which is her very self" (II.3 ¶46).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

9. **A new world: a new hypostasis and a new stone.** The new stone is spotless and crystalline, a magical earth clarified in the ether that carries wind and fire.
   - "even that Stone of the Apocalypse, the true crystalline rock without spot or darkness, that renowned Terra Maga in aethere clarificata, which carries in its belly wind and fire" (II.3 ¶52)
   - Also: "So hast thou the glory of the whole world, therefore let all obscurity flee before thee" (II.3 ¶52).
   - *stated*. "Terra Maga in aethere clarificata" is untranslated in the book; our rendering is "Magical Earth clarified in the ether". ATWOOD; QUOTED: Vaughan.

10. **The internal spiritual Sun: the Saturnian Salt made erect.** What is gained is an inner sun. Developed to intellect, it subdues all nature to its will.
    - "This is the true Astrum Solis gotten and conceived, the internal spiritual Sun which is the Perpetual Motion of the Wise" (II.3 ¶52)
    - Also: "that Saturnian Salt which, developed to intellect and made erect, subdues all nature to his will" (II.3 ¶52).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

11. **The Prester multiplied and set in the supreme Ether.** There the life-fire abides calm and vital, no longer consuming.
    - "he abides shining, not burning as below, or wrathful; but vital, calm, transmuting, recreating, and no longer a Consuming Fire" (II.3 ¶56)
    - *stated*; ATWOOD, reporting "our doctors".

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Mesmerism works on the same matter.** Mesmeric trance acts on the same material as the Hermetic philosophy and gives entrance to "another life". By implication it reaches only the first stage.
   - "Mesmerism, which of all modern arts is most pertinent to this philosophy as working in the same matter" (II.3 ¶4)
   - The shared matter is *stated*. Limiting it to the first stage is *interpreted*, from ¶5 ("key … already in our hands" versus the concealed next resolution). ATWOOD.

2. **Imagination makes the images.** The imaginative spirit, working through a humid, condensed vehicle, produces the apparitions and "demons" as moisture produces clouds.
   - "For it is the imaginative spirit which is the maker of these images, as in dreams, only more intense" (II.3 ¶22)
   - Also: "our pneumatic vehicle, becoming humid and condensed beneath her heaven, presents many formidable apparitions to the inner sense" (II.3 ¶22); "from an included vapor of the imagination" (II.3 ¶22).
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

3. **Desire gives the forms life.**
   - "desire, entering into those aerial forms, is said to vivify them" (II.3 ¶24)
   - Also: "where Desires are Images and Will their Act" (II.3 ¶12).
   - ¶24 is *stated* (reported from Psellus, Proclus, Iamblicus and Porphyry). ¶12 is *interpreted* (see Open questions).

4. **Even impure matter is useful.** The oracle allows that the products of evil matter have a use and that pure demons exist. The use is not stated.
   - "and that the germinations even of evil matter are of use" (II.3 ¶24)
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Chaldaic oracle.

5. **Life is a fire held in a moisture, and that moisture unpurified is Hades.**
   - "they describe the life of all things here below to be a thick fire imprisoned in a certain incombustible aerial moisture" (II.3 ¶29)
   - Also: "which moisture in its native state, before it is purified by the inflowing light of reason, is that Hades we are treating of" (II.3 ¶29).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: the Alchemists (endorsed); ATWOOD.

6. **The light produces the dark phase.** The Stone is generated with light, and then brings forth clouds and darkness. The scenery of images is caused by the re-entering light.
   - "Our Stone comes with light and with light it is generated, and then it brings forth the clouds, and darkness which is the mother of all things" (II.3 ¶30)
   - *interpreted* (placed directly after Atwood's statement that the Light of Reason causes "all this manifold scenery"); QUOTED: Hermes.

7. **The lowest matter, once purified, holds the seed.** The chaotic forms are called "first principles" not because they last or are rational, but because the Seed of Spirit lies in their purified extreme.
   - "not because they are permanent or their essence rational, in that unctuous dark condition, but because within the material extreme of this life, when it is purified, the Seed of Spirit is at last found" (II.3 ¶30)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

8. **Being "in Hades" means regarding one's image as an object.** The soul is in Hades as long as she regards her image objectively, before experimental knowledge.
   - "But the soul is said to be in Hades all the while that her hypostasis continues in darkness; that is, we would say, whilst she regards her image objectively" (II.3 ¶36)
   - *stated*; ATWOOD.

9. **What the soul binds, the soul can unbind.** Besides bodily death there is a philosophic death in which the soul frees herself from the body. It is possible because she herself made the bond.
   - "nature, indeed, binds the body to the soul, but the sol binds herself to the body" (II.3 ¶37)
   - Also: "it is the business of philosophers to study how to be dead" (II.3 ¶37).
   - *stated*; QUOTED: Porphyry, Plato (endorsed).

10. **Fire comes down, takes a body, and goes back up.** (See Agent 4.)
    - "comes from above downwards, until having found a body, it reascends therewith in perpetual interchange" (II.3 ¶41)
    - *stated*; QUOTED: the adept.

11. **Only the experience itself gives the knowledge.** No explanation or analogy reaches the goal. Reason grasps it only as an inference, yet it is reason's own true Hypostasis.
    - "a conception that transcends it, and which can only be attained through the identical experience" (II.3 ¶46)
    - Also: "Yet reason may, does perceive it, but abstractly only as an inference" (II.3 ¶46).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

12. **All in everything.**
    - "so that you may observe how all is in everything, and everything in all" (II.3 ¶52)
    - *stated*; voice UNCLEAR.

13. **The accounts record real inner experience.** The descents and visions are not theatre, astronomy or invention. Taken outwardly they are absurd, and the ancients reported what they had seen.
    - "Neither did they formerly invent, but what they knew and had seen, declared." (II.3 ¶55)
    - Also: "In an outward acceptation such an announcement of principles would be absurd" (II.3 ¶30); "whilst Astronomy has been the imputed spirit of the whole" (II.3 ¶49).
    - *stated*; ATWOOD.

## Identities asserted

## Open questions

1. **Is the work done on oneself, or by an operator on a patient?**
   - *Reading A, a solitary inner work with guidance.* Support: "only by the worthy man’s self-labor and investigation" (II.3 ¶32); the guide "will offer himself to you" (II.3 ¶32); the soul "may also liberate herself" (II.3 ¶37).
   - *Reading B, an external operator, as in mesmerism, at least for the first dissolution.* Support: n2 speaks of "the vital relationship of the patient in the mesmeric trance" and of a "superior attraction to life in the agent", and ¶4 says the Free Spirit philosophizes "according to the direction". Human conductors also appear: Hierophant (¶36), conductor (n37), the priest "taking me by the hand" (¶45).

2. **Pass through the intermediate spheres, or bypass them?**
   - ¶5 says the dark life is one "through which it is quite necessary to pass", and ¶9 speaks of "penetrating through darkness, and defilements, and dissolution even".
   - ¶27, glossed by Atwood, has the soul strive "to penetrate centrally without exploring the intermediate spheres".
   - *Reading A, the two agree:* one passes through without attending to or exploring the images. Support: "unmoved" (¶22), "all thy eyes extend upwards" (¶26).
   - *Reading B:* two different routes.

3. **Where do the Lesser Mysteries end?**
   - ¶14 puts "the concession of more arcane mysteries" right after the ordeals and before the descent (¶16).
   - ¶36, after the whole Hades discussion, says "here the Lesser Mysteries ended", with the soul at the Stygian lake.
   - *Reading A:* the Hades passage belongs to the end of the Lesser stage, and the Grand Mysteries begin with the death-initiation of ¶37.
   - *Reading B:* the descent already belongs to the arcane initiation, and ¶36 refers to a different boundary.

4. **Where are the Aquaster and the Fire World in the sequence?** Neither is defined.
   - ¶16 has consciousness pass "from out the Aquaster" into the Fire World at the start of the dark descent.
   - ¶41 has the adept enter the Fire World after analysing natural life to its basement, and see the root of fire there.
   - It is also unclear whether the Aquaster is the "middle region" itself (¶16 syntax).

5. **What does "Central Source" mean?**
   - Consciousness is carried "by obscuration, towards the Central Source" (¶5), and the centre is later the dark "earth … abiding durably in the center", whose "Being is Tartarus" (¶41).
   - *Reading A:* the Central Source is that dark root (Salt of Saturn).
   - *Reading B:* it is the same as the Generator, Paternal Source, Causal Source and Identic Source (¶2, ¶13, ¶37, ¶43). The text never states this.

6. **One Demogorgon throughout?**
   - The Demogogon "as yet not actually animated" (¶30), the light-deprived Demogorgon or Salt of Saturn (¶41), and the Demogorgon "now actually animated … becoming ignited" (¶52) read as one principle at three stages. That reading is *interpreted*, and the spelling varies.
   - In ¶30, "it" may be the Seed or the chaotic material extreme.
   - In ¶52, it may be the soul ("the dark abyss of being"; the Power "hidden in man", ¶55) or the worked matter of Vaughan's "little new world".

7. **What is the "compound creature" (¶52)?**
   - *Reading A, the human being.* Support: the Power is "hidden in man" (¶55), the Salt is to be had "in ourselves" (¶54), Abraham "had gotten the creature into his hands" (¶55), and the soul comes to know herself "as a Whole" (¶46).
   - *Reading B, a subject worked in a vessel.* Support: "divide the elements" and "a furnace with our glass" (¶52).

8. **How do Prester and Sun map, and which is the Prester?**
   - ¶41 sets "the Prester and the Sun" beside "the external and internal fire of life, the thing desiring and the thing desired". Read item by item, that makes the Prester the external fire.
   - ¶54 makes the Prester "the supernatural center of every living thing", which sits uneasily with "external".
   - ¶56 has the Prester "burning as below" until placed in the Ether.
   - The text may intend only a collective gloss.

9. **Is Tartarus the goal or the depth?**
   - ¶36 calls Tartarus "the next beyond Hades" and the "immortal abode", yet speaks of being "redeemed from thence".
   - ¶41 makes Tartarus the Being of the dark central earth.
   - ¶36 is garbled.

10. **"where Desires are Images and Will their Act" (¶12).**
    - *Reading A, a description of the inner state:* desires appear as images and the will enacts them at once. Support: ¶7 (deluding desires), ¶24 (desire vivifies forms).
    - *Reading B, a precept:* treat desires as mere images and let the will alone act.

11. **"the temptations of its next including sphere" (¶9).**
    - *Reading A:* the deeper dark sphere. Support: the inclination is "already freed" from sense.
    - *Reading B:* the sphere of sense around the will.

12. **"on either side it is a signal of Art" (¶37).**
    - *Reading A:* both the dissolving and the renewing are signs.
    - *Reading B:* both the philosophic name ("precious death") and the alchemical name ("gate of blackness") point to it.

13. **Who is speaking in ¶41?** There is no closing mark for the adept's speech. The Salt of Saturn chain of identities may be the adept's or Atwood's own.

14. **Unglossed operative images.** The chapter leaves these unexplained, though each marks a step, agent or sign:
    - "chloric ether" and the doves (¶11)
    - Urinus Saturni watering "lunar and solar plants", and the mists rising "Ex mari meo" (¶18)
    - "Ignis rubber super dorsum ignis candidi" (¶29; our translation "Red fire upon the back of white fire"), either a restatement of fire-in-moisture or a separate emblem
    - the noble body "moved from one Lord to another" and its "vinegar", and the three removals of vapour, blackness and death (¶37)
    - the "threefold essence" (¶41)
    - the seven-step ladder (¶42)
    - the "furnace" and "our glass", "triple proportion", "generative heat" and "star-fore of nature" (¶52)
    - "theurgic assistance" (¶46)
    - what "multiply the Prester" means operationally (¶56)
    - the use of "the germinations even of evil matter" (¶24)

    Atwood defers mystical death and regeneration to "the manifestation of the Philosophic Subject" (¶56).
