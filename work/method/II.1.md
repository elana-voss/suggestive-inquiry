# Method material — II.1 Of the True Subject of the Hermetic Art and its Concealed Root

Labels: *stated* / *interpreted*; voice. 'Endorsed' means Atwood adopts the cited view. Translations marked "ours" were made by the readers, not the book, and serve only as aids.

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The human being is the subject, the vessel and the operator all at once; the material of the work is his own life.** This is Atwood's conclusion for the chapter. She puts it as a question, then defends it. "Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art; his life the subject, the grand distillatory, the thing distilling and the thing distilled" (II.1 ¶30). Also: "that perfection which was to be found only by experimentally seeking at home within themselves" (II.1 ¶28, ATWOOD); "Man reformed by God into his image" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: the Moorish philosopher, endorsed); "will nowhere find a greater or better reserve to obtain his desire than in himself" (II.1 ¶9, same). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **The matter is drawn out of the person himself.** Morien says this to King Calid. Atwood cites it twice and calls it the plainest statement. "The thing, O king, is extracted from thee, in the which mineral thou dost even exist; with thee it is found; by thee it is received" (II.1 ¶16). Also: "This matter, O king, is extracted from thee" (II.1 ¶8). Ripley's verse calls man the mine from which a natural mercury must be drawn by art: "A naturall mercuryalyte which cost right nought, Out of hy myner by arte yt most be brought" (II.1 ¶13; that 'hy myner' means man is *interpreted* from "man, ys most noble creature" just before, ¶12–13). Further: "the work is both in us and about us" (II.1 ¶11, QUOTED: Trismegistus; *interpreted* as 'within'); "Lapis noster inter duos monticulos nascitur; in te et in me at in nostri similibus latet" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Kirchringius / M. Dunstani per n22; ours: our stone is born between two little hills; it lies hidden in you and in me and in those like us. Not translated in the book). — *stated (Morien, endorsed); supporting expressions interpreted*

3. **Choose the noblest subject, the one that contains all the rest, and that subject is man.** The Mercurial Spirit is in everything, but not every form holds enough of it for the work. So the adepts worked on the best and noblest subject. "Therefore, they say, the best and noblest ought to be chosen to operate with" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Boehme, Agrippa and Paracelsus' disciples, reported approvingly). Also: "why the most noble subject was chosen, and this only vessel for its elaboration" (II.1 ¶17, ATWOOD); "See out the noblest, as I said before" (II.1 ¶15, verse from Theat. Chem. Brit.; whether 'the noblest' means man is uncertain, see Open questions); "the highest mineral virtue nevertheless resides in man" (II.1 ¶12, QUOTED: Albertus Magnus). — *stated*

4. **More exactly, the subject is man's vital life: his fire, his vital spirit, his blood, his circulation.** "a pure, naked, and unmingled fire of infinite capability" (II.1 ¶17, QUOTED: the more modern class of adepts). The Art makes the human circulatory system capable of more than nature gives it: "in what our human circulatory system differs and occultly approximates, so that it can be made to comprehend all inferior existences, and supersede nature in her course" (II.1 ¶17, ATWOOD, *stated*; the chapter does not say how). Blood as matter: "For blood is the principle matter of each thing, Which hath any manner of increasing" and "Blood containeth the three things I have told, And in his tincture hath nature of gold" (II.1 ¶15, verse, *interpreted* as bearing on man). Further: "this is the vital spirit of the arterial blood, where the universal principles are in their natural generation unequally composed" (II.1 ¶21, voice UNCLEAR, referent disputed, see Open questions). Atwood labels the whole colour process "the process of working with the Vital Spirit" (II.1 ¶25). — *partly stated (¶17, ¶25), partly interpreted (¶15, ¶21)*

5. **All three principles lie together in one well-known thing, so nothing needs to be destroyed to get them.** Mercury, salt and sulfur are glossed as spirit, body and soul. They are found together in one place nearer than the metals. That place is called the root, the seed and the magnet. "there is a nearer place yet in which these three, viz., the mercury, slat and sulfur --- spirit, body and soul --- lie hid together in one thing, well known" (II.1 ¶11, QUOTED: Basil). Also: "this golden seed or magnet" and "this metallic seed, or root" (II.1 ¶11). — *stated (Basil); that the 'one thing' is man is interpreted from the chapter's context (¶11–¶17)*

6. **The matter costs nothing and is equally available to everyone.** "though it is a precious thing, more precious than everything, yet the poor as well as the rich may have it in the same equal plenty" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Aliphili). Also: "which cost right nought" (II.1 ¶13, Ripley); "The true blood to find without labour or cost" (II.1 ¶15, verse). — *stated*

7. **The 'antimony' of the tincture is not the antimony sold in shops. The true matter is extracted from 'the true mineral', and how to extract it is the chief secret.** "is not made of crude melted antimony, such as the apothecaries and merchants sell, but is extracted from the true mineral, as it is taken from the mountains" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Basil Valentine). Also: "how that extraction should be made, is a principal secret in which the whole art of Alchemy consists" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; what the 'true mineral' is stays open, see Open questions*

8. **Unlike any other matter, the true subject comes back stronger after every dissolution.** "this mystical nature revives, fortified from every successive dissolution, renewing its Whole resolutely from either extreme by union" (II.1 ¶25). Also: "every other matter labors for this perfection in vain; it can only attain to combustion, heat, and temporary light" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **The vessel is the one thing the authorities never reveal, and without it there is no work. The chapter places it inside the person.** "Philosophers have spoken sufficiently of all that is necessary concerning the work, with exception of the vessel; which is a divine secret, hidden from idolators, and without this knowledge no one can attain to the magistery" (II.1 ¶8, QUOTED: Maria, endorsed). Also: "that internal fire and vessel of the adepts" (II.1 ¶6, ATWOOD); "if the wise had not found a proper vessel in which to concoct it, the ethereal corner stone would never have been brought to light" (II.1 ¶8); "that living temple wherein alone the wise of all ages have been securely able to raise their rejected Corner Stone" (II.1 ¶8, ATWOOD); "vas philosophorum est aqua eorum" (II.1 ¶8, Hermes; ours: the vessel of the philosophers is their water); "the place is the principle also of the supernatural generation" (II.1 ¶8, Hali, Morien, Albert). — *stated that the vessel is concealed and essential; that it is within man is interpreted (from 'internal', 'living temple' and 'extracted from thee', ¶6, ¶8)*

10. **In the second half of the chapter the subject is restated as a latent divine principle in man: Divine Reason, the germ of Wisdom, the Celestial Prototype, the 'true man'.** What set man apart was never his body. It was a hidden causal principle in his life. "a Principle of Universal Science latent in human life, real and efficacious though cognizable only under certain conditions which they specify" (II.1 ¶39, ATWOOD; the conditions are not given here). Also: "a Divine Reason, on occult principle of Causal Efficience, said to be originally resident in his life" (II.1 ¶32, QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools of antique experience); "the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom" (II.1 ¶32, same); "on that form in man, which is the Celestial Prototype, rests the perfection of faith in all things" and "we must distinguish the true man from him who is here apparent" (II.1 ¶42, QUOTED: Rabbi Ben Jochai; that the 'true man' is the Prototype is *interpreted* from their adjacency); "God dwells, says the Jew Philo, in the rational part of man as in a palace" (II.1 ¶43, QUOTED: Philo, endorsed); "For this Reason is her temple wherein the Eternal Self-existent dwells" (II.1 ¶50, voice UNCLEAR, in the Plutarch passage). — *stated*

11. **The starting state is that ordinary reason is intellect only in potential.** "he denominates human reason, intellect in capacity" (II.1 ¶34, QUOTED: Aristotle, endorsed). It is subordinate to essential intellect and has to be awakened. — *stated*

12. **What is examined is a real, universal psychical experience, not trance, day-dream or visionary fantasy.** "For it is this, no common trance or day-dream, or any fanatical vision of celestials, that we propose to scrutinize, but the true psychical experience, catholic" (II.1 ¶46). — *stated; ATWOOD*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **Nothing really changes unless the vital force is present and active.** Solvents and ordinary fire achieve nothing vital. "nothing vitally alternative is achieved, unless the vital force be present and in action" (II.1 ¶5). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **The working fire is an inner fire. The gold-making fire burns most strongly in man.** "being ignorant of that internal fire and vessel of the adepts, so essential to the accomplishment of the Hermetic work" (II.1 ¶6, ATWOOD; the chapter does not explain the fire). Also: "fire, which is the true aurific principle in the life of all, burns more than all glorious in him erect" (II.1 ¶12, QUOTED: Albertus Magnus); "Our Mercury is philosophic, fiery, vital" and "it is prepared in the innermost chamber of life, and there it is coagulated" (II.1 ¶12, voice UNCLEAR, n10 Tract. Aureus). — *stated that it is essential and internal; what it is, interpreted*

3. **A single generating spirit (light, vapour, the Mercurial Spirit) is in everything and is present in man.** "the same light and mineral vapor, which produces gold within the bowels of the earth is also in man, and that the same is the generating spirit of all creatures" (II.1 ¶12, QUOTED: Crollius). Also: "the Mercurial Spirit is everywhere, and to be found in all things according to the nature of each" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Boehme, Agrippa and Paracelsus' disciples), but "it is neither apt to become universal of its own accord, or in every Form of virtue sufficient for the Hermetic work" (II.1 ¶9). Sendivogius: "there is in the air a secret food of life, whose invisible congealed spirit is better than the whole world" (II.1 ¶10, not explained further). — *stated*

4. **Man produces the 'true fiery water' from his own elements without knowing it, and it is the universal solvent.** "producest an essence, called, by us, the expert, the great and miraculous mystery of the world; that is the true fiery water" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Aliphili). Also: "through the breath and power of the water and earth in thyself, conjoinest the elements and makest them one" (II.1 ¶14). What it does to gold is under Procedure. — *stated*

5. **The Antimonial Spirit, rectified by Art, is the one thing that can stop life drifting toward dissolution. Its preparing agent is the Invisible Mercury, and it works by harmonizing the three principles.** "their Antimonial Spirit rectified by Art, being in bright lines of equal attraction and repulsion, as it were a perfect magnet in a star-like circle of irradiated circulation" (II.1 ¶20, QUOTED: the Alchemists / Boehme, n16). Also: "the agent in the preparation of this spirit, continues Bohme, is the Invisible Mercury" and "no process can finally fail where the invisible Universal Mercury, or spiritual air of Antimony, is present, condensed in its proper vehicle in any of the degrees of permanency" (II.1 ¶21); "the Principle of its operation consists in the power of harmonizing the three discordant principles of Attraction, Repulsion, and Circulation" (II.1 ¶21). — *stated; QUOTED: Boehme, presented approvingly*

6. **Nature contains a Spirit that drives every generation. It works through a passive inner principle, by a chain that links the invisible to the visible.** "there is in nature a certain Spirit which applies himself to the matter, and actuates in every generation" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Vaughan, endorsed). Also: "a passive intrinsical principle where he is more immediately resident than in the rest" and "a certain chain or subordinate propinquity of complexions between visibles and invisibles" (II.1 ¶26). Vaughan adds that he does not mean the divine spirit here. — *stated*

7. **The one who works the wonders is inside us, not an outside spirit.** "in us is the Operator of all wonderful effects" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Agrippa; Atwood says this passage alludes to Alchemy). Also: "in us, I say, is the Operator of miracles" (II.1 ¶26); "But the Spirit begetting all doth in us dwell" (II.1 ¶27, verse). — *stated*

8. **The soul (the 'king') sits in the vital spirit of the purest blood and governs mind and body. It acts most powerfully outside the body.** "the soul in man, the lesser world or microcosm, substituting the place of its center, is the king, and is placed in the vital spirit in the purest blood" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius, endorsed). Also: "That governs the mind, and the mind the body" and "hath a far greater operation out of the body, because out of the body it absolutely reigns" (II.1 ¶10). — *stated*

9. **Wisdom, which is Intellect in its highest sense, is a real power that forms and sustains all things, and an inner sight that can see God.** "an affirmative operative hypostasis, informing, invigorating, and sustaining all things" (II.1 ¶44, ATWOOD, giving the ancient tradition). Also: "she passeth through all things by reason of her pureness" and "being but one, she can do all things, and, remaining in herself, she maketh all things new" (II.1 ¶44, QUOTED: Solomon); "gave him a sight called Intellect, which is capable of beholding God" (II.1 ¶38, QUOTED: Crito, endorsed); "tat Motive Reason which is his life" (II.1 ¶42, ATWOOD). — *stated*

10. **The faculty needs an illumination flowing into it, as colours need sunlight.** "these require the splendor of the sun to discover their beauty, as do those the inspiring afflux of their frontal illumination" (II.1 ¶34, QUOTED: Aristotle, endorsed; 'frontal' may be a misprint for 'fontal', see Open questions). — *stated*

11. **Divine illumination, reached through piety and divine works, leads the soul. The soul cannot rise by its own guidance.** "the supreme faculty of the soul cannot by its own guidance aspire to the sublimest institution, and to the comprehension of Divinity: but the work of Piety leads it by the hand of God, by illumination from thence" (II.1 ¶51, QUOTED: Psellus, endorsed). Also: "without such assistance the Divine union is neither effected not rightly understood" (II.1 ¶52, QUOTED: Julian); "it is then that God becomes our leader" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: Plutarch). — *stated*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **The operator must be morally purified, just, deep in reasoning and generous toward others.** "to quit himself from the usurpations of vice, to be good and just and of a profound reason, ready at hand to help mankind" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Hermes, endorsed). Also: "true, sincere, intelligent, faithful" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Crollius on the true physician); "but with due solemnity, and sanctity of life" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: Plutarch); "in all ages entering into holy souls, she maketh them friends of God and prophets" and "therefore, can no defiled thing fall into her" (II.1 ¶44, QUOTED: Solomon; reading this as a requirement on the operator is *interpreted*). — *stated (¶14, ¶50)*

2. **He must know the natural principles within himself, because the root of the work is in him.** "he who in himself knows no natural principles, is very remote from this sacred science, because he has not the true root in him whereon to base his labor and intention" (II.1 ¶11, QUOTED: Geber, endorsed). — *stated*

3. **He must know himself and love God and must not be stubborn. The regenerate man is the one who can do the work.** "in his regenerate Wisdom possesseth all things" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: the Moorish philosopher). Also: "by knowing and loving Him" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Agrippa); "if thou knowest how to know thyself, and art not of a stiff neck, mayest easily comprehend" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius). — *interpreted: the texts describe what such a man can do and do not state these as conditions*

4. **The mind must be simple and pure.** "the human mind, especially that which is simple and pure, may be converted" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Agrippa). — *stated*

5. **He must be initiated and prepared by rites and discipline, and capable of 'deification'.** "not to be conceived of by such as are not duly initiated and capable of this deification" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Aristotle as reported by Atwood, endorsed). Also: "the end of which, as of all ceremonial rites and disciplines, was that the aspirant might be prepared and fitted for the attainment of the knowledge of the Supreme Mind" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: Plutarch). — *stated*

6. **He needs instruction: the art cannot be won by skill alone and has to be entrusted from one person to another.** "there is no power to obtain, no art or skill availeth, unless one intrust another with somewhat (as Hermes and Arnold bear witness)" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Boehme). Also: "For how hardly should they divine without instruction, or interpret the dark hieroglyphic seal?" (II.1 ¶6, ATWOOD); "inquire of the wise ancients to direct us about the true method and conditions of" Self-Knowledge (II.1 ¶46, ATWOOD). — *stated*

7. **His motive must be knowledge, not gold. Unwilling or greedy seekers are turned away.** "not so much out of covetousness for gold or silver, but for the knowledge’s sake" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius). Also: "we invite not the unwilling, nor will these studies be found to reward the sordid seeker after riches or gold only" (II.1 ¶30, ATWOOD). — *stated*

8. **He must work on things experimentally, not speculatively.** See Procedure 2 (Vaughan, ¶26). — *stated*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

The chapter gives two sequences. One is a timed laboratory-style sequence, cited from Hermes and Boehme (¶21–¶25). The other is an inward, contemplative sequence, cited from the philosophers (¶9, ¶26, ¶35–¶53). Atwood does not explicitly join them, but she calls the first "the process of working with the Vital Spirit" (¶25) and says the passages of Vaughan and Agrippa about the second are "in allusion to the art of Alchemy" (¶26). The general points come first, then each sequence.

**General**

1. **The real practice applies alchemy to human life, using a method and a medium of attraction: the self-knower draws things to himself as a magnet draws iron.** "applying the practice of Alchemy to human life; suggesting also, as did the latter, the method and medium of attraction" (II.1 ¶9, ATWOOD). Also: "even how he can draw and bring them to himself as a loadstone, iron" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Agrippa); "who is able to draw to himself the Central Salt of nature in abundance" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: the Moorish philosopher; the Central Salt is not explained). Alongside the vessel, a concealed practical art: "the concealed vessel or art of vital administration" (II.1 ¶8, not explained). — *interpreted (that this is the true practice is implied by the alarm it caused, ¶9)*

2. **The foundation is found only by practical, experimental penetration to the centre of things, not by speculation about outward effects.** "to change their abstractions into extractions" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Vaughan, endorsed). Also: "use their hands and not their fancies" and "pierce not experimentally into the center of things" (II.1 ¶26); without the "Protochemic Artifice", by which "this, and this only, the true foundation is discoverable" (II.1 ¶26). — *stated*

3. **The method is right investigation of oneself: look within, descend into oneself. The Egyptian maxim 'know thyself' was meant as an actual practice.** "we look within, or rather, that we may learn how to do so, inquire of the wise ancients to direct us about the true method and conditions of" Self-Knowledge (II.1 ¶46, ATWOOD). Also: "Let us descend into ourselves, and believe in ourselves if we be able, that that which we are is worthy of our investigation" (II.1 ¶53); "this advice was meant experimentally and ontologically" (II.1 ¶39); "the soul rightly entering into herself will behold all other things, and Deity itself" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Plato, endorsed); "If you investigate rightly, says Archytas, discovery will be easy for you" (II.1 ¶52). — *stated*

4. **The work reduces nature to uncover a hidden principle. It destroys only nature's present mode of life, not the thing that carries it, and casts off her surplus forms. This deliberate destruction 'according to nature' is what the doubters never understood.** "such a reducation of nature as shall discover this Latex without destroying her vehicle, but the modal life only" (II.1 ¶47, QUOTED: the Alchemists, reported by Atwood). Also: "where nature may be addressed in order to the rejection of her superfluous forms" (II.1 ¶47); "the artificial destruction according to the course of nature" (II.1 ¶47, QUOTED: Geber). — *interpreted ('Latex' and 'vehicle' are unexplained, see Open questions)*

5. **Ancient Wisdom came from a living experiment on nature: particular arts and means bring the central active cause into conscious existence and action. Since spontaneous revelation ended, sacred arts have been the means.** "Wisdom was the offspring of a vital experiment into nature, by certain arts and media producing the central efficient into conscious being and effect" (II.1 ¶52, ATWOOD, citing the Eleusinian accounts as confirmation). Also: "has given us observation through Sacred Arts, by which we have at hand sufficient assistance" (II.1 ¶53, QUOTED: the Platonic Successor); "there is no other means of strengthening the vehicle of the sold but by material Rites" and "To initiate or perfect the human soul by the power of materials here on earth" (II.1 ¶51, QUOTED: Psellus; what the rites and materials are is not stated); "calls the magic of Zoroaster the service of the gods" (II.1 ¶51, Plato via Psellus). — *stated*

6. **The key operational points were deliberately hidden: the immediate active cause, where the fire comes from and what it is aimed at, and the laboratory and vessels.** "what was their immediate efficient? Whence and whereon did they direct their fire? These things, with the laboratory, its vessels and various apparatus, they have disguised" (II.1 ¶47, ATWOOD). The chapter does not decode them. — *stated*

7. **The operation itself is simple. The understanding is the hard part.** "the Work is easy and simple, but the wisdom therein is great and the greatest mystery" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Boehme). — *stated*

**Sequence A: the cited process (Hermes, Boehme)**

8. **The central operation is to harmonize the three principles. The Art corrects life's inverted order by suppressing the dominant opposing force so that the proper ruler governs again.** "Which inverse order of relationship and vital ignorance it is the object of the Hermetic art to remedy, and, by occultation of the opposive principle, to restore the true rector to his original rule" (II.1 ¶21, voice UNCLEAR; that the 'opposive principle' is repulsion is *interpreted*, since repulsion is the disorder named just before). — *stated aim; interpreted particulars*

9. **The formula is dissolve, volatilize, fix.** "Si fixum solvas faciasque volare solutum Et volucrem figas, faciunt tutum Sove, Coagula, Fige" (II.1 ¶22; ours: if you dissolve the fixed and make the dissolved fly, and fix the winged, they make [you] safe. Dissolve, coagulate, fix). Not explained. — *interpreted (translation ours only)*

10. **The fiery water dissolves completely. It returns even fixed gold to a raw black earth, from which a water and a mild salt are obtained.** "it dissolves radically, incrudates even the mature, constant, and very fixed, fiery and abiding mass and matter of gold, and reduces it into a fit black earth, like to black spittle" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Aliphili). Also: "wherein we find a water and the true salt destitute of all odor, vehemency, and corrosive nature of the fire" (II.1 ¶14). — *stated*

11. **Hermes' order of operations: mortify, generate, vivify the spirit, cleanse, introduce light, let the contraries fight, rise from black to white.** "except thou understandeth how to mortify and induce generation, to vivify the spirit, to cleanse and introduce light, until they fight and contend with each other, and grow white and freed form their defilements, rising, as it were, from blackness and darkness, thou knowest nothing" (II.1 ¶23, QUOTED: Hermes, endorsed). — *stated*

12. **Stage 1, blackening (about three months of circulation by digestion).** The opposition of the forces stops, all three come to rest, and the matter sinks black and motionless. What was fixed, the 'sentient medium', is dissolved. "in three months circulation, says he, by digestion, the powder becomes black, the opposition of attraction and repulsion ceases (in the vital spirit)" (II.1 ¶23, QUOTED: our modern exponent, apparently Boehme). Also: "the attraction of the fixed which produced the repulsion of the volatile, is slain by the circulation which also dies itself, and all three enter into rest" and "sunk down black and motionless" (II.1 ¶23); "that which at first was fixed, viz., the sentient medium, is dissolved" (II.1 ¶23, *stated* gloss). Deeper working shows the original evil in the matter, and with it the principle of correction: "the original evil is made manifest in the matter to be renewed, and hence the principle of amendment and rectification also will appear" (II.1 ¶23). — *stated*

13. **Stage 2, whitening (three months more).** The principles revive, far stronger and without struggle. "The same Three Principles gradually assume a new life, continues Boehme, infinitely more powerful in virtue, but without any violent contest, and in three months farther, mild action of the principles in harmony have produced a brilliant whiteness in the matter" (II.1 ¶24, QUOTED: Boehme). — *stated*

14. **Stage 3, reddening (three months more): a yellow, red or purple tincture.** "which in three months more become a brilliant yellow, red, or purpling tincture" (II.1 ¶24, QUOTED: Boehme). — *stated*

15. **The process is repeated many times, and a stage that fails can be revived by adding more of the same Spirit.** "which also is many times passed through for the practical accomplishment" (II.1 ¶25, ATWOOD). Also: "This Spirit is so full of life, says the adept, that if the process fails in any stage, an addition of the same will renew it" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: the adept). — *stated*

16. **Multiplication: each further digestion with fresh material multiplies strength and quantity tenfold, in a tenth of the previous time.** "The white or red powder is increased tenfold in strength and quantity by each digestion of it with fresh i, wet with gas water, or oil of this antimony" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: the adept; 'fresh i' is corrupt). Also: "each digestion is made in tenfold shorter time than the preceding from a week to a few hours" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated*

17. **A further instruction, left in Latin: the salt of blood is to be transmuted by a urinous ferment.** "sanquinis salem per urinaceum fermentum sic transmutari debere, ut ultiman vitam amittat, mediamque servet, salsedinemque retineat" (II.1 ¶21, QUOTED: Arcanum Liquaris Alkahest; ours: the salt of blood must be so transmuted by a urinous ferment that it loses its last life, keeps its middle one, and retains its saltiness). Not explained. — *interpreted (translation ours only)*

**Sequence B: the inward sequence (philosophers)**

18. **Withdraw from the senses and free the mind from the body.** "the soul of man, being estranged from the corporeal senses, adheres to a divine nature, from which it receives those things which it cannot search into by it sown power" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed). Also: "when the mind is free, the reins of the body being loosed and going forth, as out of a close prison, it transcends the bonds of the members" (II.1 ¶9). — *stated*

19. **Bring the mind into a sleep-like state, cut off from its present life, by an unspecified 'recreation and appeasing'. It then passes into its divine nature.** "by a certain mysterious recreation and appeasing, the human mind, especially that which is simple and pure, may be converted and laid asleep from its present life so utterly as to be brought into its divine nature" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed; Atwood: "Both these passages are in allusion to the art of Alchemy"). — *stated (the means are not described)*

20. **Purify, then return into direct, experienced contact with the source. Particular exercises and preparations bring the understanding into contact with its cause.** "when this is purified and passed back into contacting experience with its source" (II.1 ¶35, ATWOOD; reading it as two steps is *interpreted*). Also: "by the help of certain mystical exercises and preparations, the human Understanding Medium is made to pass into contact with its Antecedent Cause" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Aristotle as reported, endorsed; the exercises are not described); "being returned into its principle" (II.1 ¶38, ATWOOD); "becoming purified from the body, will recollect all those things, the remembrance of which she loses in the present life" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: the philosopher). — *stated*

21. **Set aside the many powers, gather to one's own unity and the centre of life, and rise to the 'highest watchtower'.** "laying aside multitude and the variety of all manifold powers which she contains, she ascends to the highest watchtower of beings" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Plato, endorsed). Also: "as verging to her own union and to the center of all life" (II.1 ¶37). — *stated*

22. **Rise in thought in order to descend to the deepest level. A shallow reason cannot sound the well.** "he must ascend in thought who would, descending, hope to penetrate so far as to the superstantial experience of things" (II.1 ¶49, ATWOOD). Also: "The well out of which she is drawn is deep, and not therefore to be fathomed by the plummet of a shallow reason" (II.1 ¶49). — *stated*

23. **A new awakening, a 'divine recreation', brings reason to its full perfection.** "it is from a new awakening, a divine recreation, as it were, that it conceives the full perfection of a life" (II.1 ¶34, QUOTED: Aristotle, endorsed). — *stated*

24. **Wisdom shows itself in resolving all kinds into their one principle and putting them back together.** "Whoever therefore is able to analyze all the genera which are contained under one and the same principle and again to compose and connumerate them, he appears to be wise" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Archytas, endorsed). — *interpreted as part of the method; the text presents it as an intellectual capacity only*

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **Inner enlightenment: the cloud lifts, and one sees the 'point of our lodestone'.** "presently the cloud is taken away from before his eyes, and without any let, he can behold the point of our lodestone, answering to each center of the beams (viz. of the sun and moon philosophical)" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius, endorsed). Also: "The eyes of the wise look upon nature otherwise than the eyes of common men" (II.1 ¶10). — *stated sequence; the image is unexplained*

2. **Blackness and stillness: the forces stop opposing each other. This is named 'the head of Hermes' crow' at the start of the work.** "sunk down black and motionless. And thus the head of Hermes’ crow is said to be in the beginning of this work" (II.1 ¶23, QUOTED: our modern exponent). The appearance of the original evil, together with the principle of correction, is itself a sign: "on either side it is a signal of Art" (II.1 ¶23). — *stated (blackening); interpreted (that 'crow's head' names it)*

3. **The colour sequence black, white, then yellow/red/purple** (see Procedure 12–14). The red is greeted as the end of sin and the king's coronation: "the reign of sin is finished, and the king doth rule, and now he is invested wit the red garment" (II.1 ¶24, QUOTED: Boehme). — *stated colours; the meaning of the king is interpreted*

4. **The Stone appears as bright lines of balanced forces, like an armed magnet circling in a heaven.** "the Stone of Fire seen in bright lines, of equal attraction and repulsion, when made manifest, as it were, an armed magnet included and circulating in a perpetual heaven" (II.1 ¶25, voice UNCLEAR, n21 Nuysement). — *stated*

5. **Inquiry ends, because the wise mind holds its object within itself.** "that mind which is become wise needs not to investigate any further" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Diotima, endorsed with Atwood's gloss). — *stated*

6. **A sign of aptitude: reading this material awakens a sense of remembering.** "should find familiar scenes recur, thrilling into reminiscence, as of some long past life forgotten" (II.1 ¶53, ATWOOD). Also: "the road whereon we are journeying is towards his Native Land" (II.1 ¶53). — *stated*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Modern chemistry has nothing to do with alchemy except borrowed words. Its outward method drives out the living nature the work needs.** "modern art drives out, in fact, the very nature which the ancients prized" (II.1 ¶5). Also: "their notion of experience and method of experimenting are superficial and essentially atheistic" (II.1 ¶5). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **The false alchemists read the recipes literally and had no theory. Better ones tried to pull light or ether into outer vessels but lacked the inner fire and vessel.** "the means they employed were from literal readings of receipts" (II.1 ¶6). Also: "endeavored to draw light into focus of their vessels, to compress and entice the ether by mangetical disposition and attractions of various kinds" (II.1 ¶6). — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **Searching outward is a mistake, and the records are built to mislead anyone who looks abroad. The outer, sense-bound man is left to his calcining and weighing.** "the records of Alchemy are, above all, calculated to mislead those who have gone abroad thoughtlessly seeking for that perfection which was to be found only by experimentally seeking at home within themselves" (II.1 ¶28). Also: "with his senses and servile intellect all dark within" and "there to calcine, weight, and measure circumferences, from the first to the last round of material possibility" (II.1 ¶45); "seek not at random any longer in the outer world where so many before have foundered" (II.1 ¶46). — *stated; ATWOOD*

4. **The texts were deliberately concealed and falsified. Open hints were cancelled by false commentary or diversions. The vessel, the means and the apparatus are withheld, and the ground is sealed against misuse.** "his revealment was instantly annulled by false or weakening commentaries, or as quickly as possible withdrawn" (II.1 ¶8, ATWOOD; example: Sendivogius "inveigling the reader into a doubt artfully raised about some gold found sticking between a dead man’s teeth"). Also: "make little or no mention of the king at all" (II.1 ¶7); "the Seal of God lieth before it, to conceal the true ground, unless a man knew for certain that it would not be misused" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Boehme); "that the Initiated were so profoundly silent upon these means" (II.1 ¶53). — *stated*

5. **Without the method the claims cannot be tested, so neither belief nor dismissal is justified.** "being ignorant of the method, how should we presume to test the truth of this philosophy?" (II.1 ¶53). — *stated; ATWOOD*

6. **The body and senses hide nature's light. Attention pulled outward through the senses cuts understanding off from its source.** "nature, having her proper light, is by the shadowy body of sense, hidden from our eyes" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius). Also: "as man’s body is covered with a garment, so is man’s nature covered with the body" (II.1 ¶10); "the true light shut up as in a prison, the fountain of Universal Nature separated off from human understanding by the external attraction of it through the gates of sense" (II.1 ¶49, ATWOOD); "encompassed about with senses and passions" with only "obscure glimmerings, and as it were, in comparison, a confused dream" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: Plutarch). — *stated*

7. **Fallen imbalance: in human life repulsion outweighs inner attraction, so life flows outward toward dissolution and consciousness turns away from its First Cause.** "the wheel of human life turns forth, deviating from its axis, into a line which terminated finally in dissolution" (II.1 ¶20, QUOTED: the Alchemists / Boehme). Also: "nature has fallen off from her original balance" (II.1 ¶20); "the repulsive force so far predominating over the interior attraction, that the total circulatory life is expulsive, and drawn without to a debilitated consciousness away from its First Cause" (II.1 ¶21, voice UNCLEAR). — *stated*

8. **A vicious operator corrupts the Spirit through evil imagination.** "being destitute of light, defile by an evil imagination the very Spirit that ought to be refined" (II.1 ¶14, QUOTED: Hermes). — *interpreted: that the operator's mind acts on the matter is inferred*

9. **Reason on its own fails, as if under a spell. Mortal nature lacks Intellect because of 'generation', and the old experience is gone or its conditions are estranged.** "every attempt of the unassisted reason terminates negatively, as the Subject Identity slides evermore behind the retardant mind" (II.1 ¶32, ATWOOD). Also: "it is now either wholly departed or the conditions are estranged" (II.1 ¶32); "the enchantment which isolates the reflective faculty" (II.1 ¶33, ATWOOD); "a kindred privation of Intellect; this however is not deprived by God but by the essence of generation" (II.1 ¶38, QUOTED: Crito). — *stated*

10. **There are two opposite ways to fail. Some people with real inner experience ignore its rational ground and settle for illusory visions. Others reason only about outward things.** "remain accordingly satisfied in the dreams and deluding visions of an included imagination" (II.1 ¶39). Also: "in whom the spirit of inquiry is wholly drawn to externals" (II.1 ¶39). — *stated; ATWOOD*

## The result

1. **The product is the 'Corner Stone', a being of light, raised only in the inner 'living temple'.** "their rejected Corner Stone and Ens of Light" (II.1 ¶8, ATWOOD). Also: "the ethereal corner stone" (II.1 ¶8). — *interpreted (not explained further)*

2. **The aim is not just to obtain or prove the matter but to perfect it and make the Causal light manifest.** "the object was not simply to obtain the Matter or prove it only, but to improve, perfect, and bring the Causal light to manifestation" (II.1 ¶17). — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **Restoration: life's proper ruler is put back in command. In Kabbalist terms, the fall leads to reunion and a higher perfection.** "to restore the true rector to his original rule" (II.1 ¶21, voice UNCLEAR). Also: "the principle of reunion nevertheless abides in the generated life of individuals and will in process of time operate to a restitution and higher perfection" (II.1 ¶41, QUOTED: Hebrew Kabalists, endorsed); "the reign of sin is finished, and the king doth rule" (II.1 ¶24, QUOTED: Boehme). — *stated aim; the identity of the 'rector' is open*

4. **The tincture or Stone is a medicine for men and for metals, and it multiplies.** "this true tincture of Antimony, which is the medicine of men and metals" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Basil Valentine). Also: "Health, riches, and honor attend him who rightly attains it" (II.1 ¶25); "the terrestrial or Microcosmic Sun, the triumphal Chariot of Antimony" (II.1 ¶25); the adepts as "true physicians for the body and soul, dispensing the leaves of life for the healing of the nations" (II.1 ¶25, QUOTED: Boehme, predicting the future). — *stated*

5. **The individual spirit is united with the universal, and nature is raised up and multiplied.** "a certain Art by which a Particular Spirit may be united to the Universal; and nature by consequence be strangely exalted and multiplied" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Vaughan, endorsed). — *stated*

6. **The mind enters its divine nature, is enlightened, and gains power to produce wonderful effects.** "be brought into its divine nature, and become enlightened with the divine light, and withal receive the virtue of some wonderful effects" (II.1 ¶26, QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed). Also: "this Mind in men is God, and, therefore, are some men said to be divine, for there humanity is annexed to divinity" (II.1 ¶43, QUOTED: Hermes), "when it is moved into the catholic Intuition of its Source" (II.1 ¶43, voice uncertain). — *stated*

7. **The latent faculty of Wisdom is revealed. It knows nature intuitively and works magically as nature's source. The understanding becomes living energy with a discernment that was hidden before.** "the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom, which, when revealed and set alone, all the forms of things and hidden springs of nature become intuitively known and are implied essentially" (II.1 ¶32, QUOTED: cabalistic scriptures and schools). Also: "it works magically withal, discovering latent properties as a principle, governing and supplying all dependent existence" (II.1 ¶32); "it becomes to be a life in energy, and enjoys the most exalted and excellent faculty of discernment, which was before occult" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Aristotle as reported); "wherein reason becomes also into the substantive experience of her Law" (II.1 ¶39, ATWOOD); "man, by rationally conditionating, has succeeded in developing into action the Recreative Force" (II.1 ¶47, QUOTED: the Alchemists). — *stated*

8. **Knowing oneself means knowing all things and God, and being able to draw all things to oneself. The circle closes: end joins beginning in knowledge of God.** "Whosoever, therefore, shall know himself, shall know all things in himself; but especially he shall know God, according to whose image he was made" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed). Also: "he will have conjoined ends with beginnings, and will know that God who is the principle, middle, and end of all things" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Archytas); "the soul rightly entering into herself will behold all other things, and Deity itself" (II.1 ¶37, Plato). — *stated*

9. **Wisdom is a corrected intellect that serves as the measure of truth: a real good that can be attained, not an outward acquisition.** "namely, the standard of truth in a rectified intellect" (II.1 ¶44, ATWOOD). Also: "a very real, substantial, and attainable good" (II.1 ¶53); "whether there be still an entrance open, as there was once said to be, to the shut palaces of Mind" (II.1 ¶53, *interpreted* as the goal). — *stated*

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Work on the one subject that contains everything, and that one is enough.** "if one subject should be presented which contains all, and the comprehension of each subordinate form in a superior essence, then this one needs only to be investigated for the discovery of all" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Boehme, Agrippa and Paracelsus' disciples, approvingly). — *stated*

2. **Self-knowledge is the root of the alchemical tradition. Man's unrecognized inner power was the foundation of the whole magistery.** "Self-Knowledge to be at the root of all Alchemical tradition" (II.1 ¶30, ATWOOD). Also: "nor yet surmises the virtue that is in him, to prove and magnify the Universal Source. Yet that was the foundation of the whole Hermetic magistery" (II.1 ¶8); "Self-Knowledge would be a way to the knowledge of the Universal Nature" (II.1 ¶31, reported as formerly taught and proved). — *stated*

3. **Man is a microcosm: every level of creation has a counterpart in him, and he contains the whole cause of existence.** "Thou hast in thy body the anatomy of the whole world, and all thy members answer to some celestials" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius). Also: "he symbolizes with matter in a proper subject; with the elements in a fourfold body" (II.1 ¶9, QUOTED: Agrippa); "within himself the total Cause of existence" (II.1 ¶42, ATWOOD); "The Form of man, says the Rabbi Ben Jochai, contains all that is in the heaven and earth" (II.1 ¶42); Manilius, "quibus est et mundus in ipsis" (II.1 ¶29; ours: who have the world within themselves). Atwood cautions that the rank comes from Divine Reason, not from the body or the four elements (II.1 ¶32), and that on present evidence man cannot be proved a perfect microcosm (II.1 ¶31). — *stated*

4. **All motion comes from three principles: attraction, then repulsion as its reaction, then circulation arising from their conflict. Circulation mediates between them. When the two are equal it forms a circle and harmonizes them, provided the matter is fit. When either dominates, the path is an ellipse.** "the medium is always in the circulation produced from the action and reaction of centrifugal and centripetal forces" (II.1 ¶19, voice UNCLEAR: Atwood or Boehme). Also: "when they are in equilibrium, a circle is produced" and "will succeed if the matter be duly qualified for it" (II.1 ¶19); "Attraction is the first principle of motion in nature" (II.1 ¶18); "these three principles of attraction, repulsion, and circulation, the universal accord of life, are everywhere and in all" (II.1 ¶14). — *stated*

5. **Only one Matter can be harmonized in this way since nature lost her balance.** "there is but One Matter truly qualifiable or capable of qualifying matter to be harmonized in this way" (II.1 ¶20, QUOTED: the Alchemists / Boehme). — *stated*

6. **What multiplies is light. Nature spends herself, and Art completes her by adding supernatural light to natural light.** "adds to the natural effulgence a supernatural light; for what else but light should multiply?" (II.1 ¶25, voice UNCLEAR). Also: "nature expends all her forces, but leaves the victory to Art" and "this gold is endued with a magnetical virtue, which by the inspissate fulgor of its tincture, draws the divine increase after it" (II.1 ¶25). — *stated*

7. **The soul acts more powerfully outside the body than in it.** "hath a far greater operation out of the body, because out of the body it absolutely reigns" (II.1 ¶10, QUOTED: Sendivogius). — *stated*

8. **The principle of our being is near at hand within us, and so it can be known.** "If, therefore, we contain within us a proper principle of being, why should not this, thus proximate, be known?" (II.1 ¶26, ATWOOD). Also: "life in every particular the same inscrutable Identity through all" (II.1 ¶26). — *stated*

9. **Intuition underlies all proof. The laws of reason are an embryonic image of a hidden Archetypal Wisdom, which the ancients took to be the subject of nature's active force.** "intuition is the evidence and end of every rational proof" (II.1 ¶33, ATWOOD). Also: "there is a higher evidence of truth than the senses afford" (II.1 ¶34); "that Archetypal Wisdom which the ancients celebrated as the occult essence of that Law" (II.1 ¶35); "proclaims absolutely to be the catholic subject of the great efficient force of nature" (II.1 ¶35). — *stated*

10. **At this level one knows by contact: the intellect becomes one with what it knows.** "it becomes intelligible by contact and intellection; and that Intellect is the same with the intelligible" (II.1 ¶37, QUOTED: Aristotle, endorsed). — *stated*

11. **A man's true self is his highest faculty.** "the true man, according to Plato and Aristotelians, is this Intellect; for the essence of everything is the summit of its nature" (II.1 ¶35, ATWOOD). — *stated*

12. **Philosophy, the desire for truth, is reason reaching toward the light it came from, and the reaching is not in vain.** "an appetition of reason for its antecedent light" (II.1 ¶44, ATWOOD). Also: "Intellect does not extend herself towards the intelligible Cause in vain" (II.1 ¶44); "to desire and covet after the Truth is to aspire to be a partaker of the Divine Nature itself" (II.1 ¶50, QUOTED: Plutarch). — *stated*

13. **The soul is an impression of the Logos, the seal from which the world was made.** "the soul of man is an impression of this seal of which the prototype and original characteristic is the everlasting Logos" (II.1 ¶43, QUOTED: Philo). — *stated*

14. **Modern science points the same way: light is the common sustainer of life, in harmony through human circulation with the planetary spheres.** "identifying light, as the common vital sustenant, to be in motive accord throughout the human circulatory system with the planetary spheres" (II.1 ¶31, ATWOOD). — *stated*

## Open questions

1. **Where the vessel is.** Reading A: it is within man, the 'living temple'. Basis: "internal fire and vessel" (¶6), "living temple" (¶8), "extracted from thee" (¶8, ¶16), "this only vessel" for "the most noble subject" (¶17). Reading B: it is left unidentified. Basis: no passage says outright that the vessel is man. Related: "the place is the principle also of the supernatural generation" (¶8) may name the vessel, or may name a second hidden item, since "they do not openly reveal either". The "conceptive vehicle" (¶8) may be the vessel or the hypothetical matter.

2. **Who 'the king' or 'true rector' is.** ¶7 has the old adepts making "little or no mention of the king". This is either the hidden subject, which ¶10 identifies as "the soul in man", or only Tobit's image for a guarded secret. The "true rector" restored in ¶21 is either the interior attraction that repulsion now dominates or the First Cause that consciousness has turned from (both are named in ¶21). The king crowned at the red stage (¶24) is not explained.

3. **Laboratory process or inner process.** The Boehme sequence (¶21–¶25) uses laboratory language: "powder", "three months", "gas water, or oil of this antimony", "from a week to a few hours". Yet it is glossed in vital and psychic terms: "(in the vital spirit)" and "the sentient medium" (¶23), and Atwood calls it "the process of working with the Vital Spirit" (¶25). Basil's "true mineral, as it is taken from the mountains" (¶25) can be literal ore, as opposed to shop antimony, or something in man, given the Latin that follows ("in te et in me at in nostri similibus latet") and ¶28 and ¶30. The chapter does not settle this.

4. **Salt, mercury and sulfur against attraction, repulsion and circulation.** ¶14 says "these three principles" directly after "salt, mercury, and sulfur". That supports an identity, but there is no explicit copula, and ¶18–¶19 develop the triad on its own.

5. **What 'this' refers to in "this is the vital spirit of the arterial blood" (¶21).** It could be the Invisible Mercury, the harmonizing principle, or the ground on which the three principles act, i.e. the material of the Art. The description that follows, of the blood's disorder as what the Art remedies, favours the last, but the syntax allows all three.

6. **Who speaks in ¶18–¶21 and ¶23.** ¶21's "continues Bohme" implies the earlier paragraphs are Boehme's, while n16 sits at the end of ¶20 and ¶18–¶19 carry no attribution. "our modern exponent" (¶23) is apparently Boehme (¶24 "continues Boehme"). This decides whether the three-principle physics is Atwood's own claim.

7. **Whether the agents are one agent.** Inner fire (¶6), vital force (¶5), Mercurial Spirit (¶9), light and mineral vapor (¶12), fiery water (¶14), Invisible Mercury / Antimonial Spirit (¶20–¶21), Vaughan's Spirit (¶26), the "Operator" in us (¶26), Wisdom/Intellect (¶39, ¶44) and divine illumination (¶51). The chapter places several of these in man, but it never asserts that they are identical.

8. **The means of the inner operation.** The "mysterious recreation and appeasing" (¶26), the "certain mystical exercises and preparations" (¶37), the "material Rites" (¶51) and the "certain arts and media" (¶52) are all named and none is described. The rites may be literal ceremonies using physical materials (¶51 "the power of materials here on earth"), or the same thing as the "vital experiment into nature" (¶52). The "certain conditions which they specify" (¶39) are not given here.

9. **'Latex' and 'vehicle' (¶47, ¶51).** "this Latex" may refer back to "the true Light of Alchemy", or to a latent principle linked with the "Recreative Force", or it may be unexplained. "the vehicle of the sold" (¶51) is probably a misprint for 'soul'. Whether it is the same as the "vehicle" of nature preserved in ¶47 is not stated.

10. **'sol' or 'soul' (¶10).** "this same sol" may be a misprint continuing "the soul in man", or a deliberate 'sun' linked to "sun and moon philosophical" earlier in ¶10.

11. **"the point of our lodestone" (¶10).** Either a central point of attraction in nature (compare "as a loadstone, iron", ¶9) or the soul as man's centre ("substituting the place of its center", ¶10).

12. **"See out the noblest" (¶15).** Either man (¶12 "man, ys most noble creature"; ¶17 "the most noble subject") or the noblest blood or part within the body.

13. **"frontal illumination" (¶34).** As printed, illumination coming toward the mind. As a misprint for 'fontal', illumination from the source, which fits ¶33's "Fontal Nature".

14. **"which, according to these philosophers, is God" (¶39).** Either the end of inquiry is God, which Crito's Intellect as the sight that beholds God (¶38) favours, or Intellect/Wisdom itself is God.

15. **"in the circular progression of nature may be so practically manifested at last" (¶34).** Either the end of nature's cosmic cycle or a return of end to beginning achieved in one person (¶37 "conjoined ends with beginnings"; ¶41 restitution "in process of time").

16. **Voice of "when it is moved into the catholic Intuition of its Source" (¶43).** The footnote marker (41) comes before it, which suggests it is Atwood's own qualification of Hermes, but it continues the same sentence.

17. **Text corruptions that affect the procedure.** "fresh i" (¶25) is the material added at each multiplying digestion and cannot be recovered. "the mechanical part of the Three Principles passes into the hands of its proper manufacturers" (¶25) is not glossed.
