# Method material — IV.3 The Six Keys of Eudoxus, Opening Into the Most Secret Philosophy

*Voices: "Eudoxus" = the first-person adept of ¶2–16 (QUOTED; named only in the chapter heading; Atwood calls her supplementary source for ¶18ff "another and not less credible source", ¶17). From ¶17 on the frame is Atwood's, with long citations (Open Entrance / E. Philalethes, Sendivogius, Vaughan, Lucerna Salis, Eirenaeus, Khunrath, Julian, Helmont, the theosophist / Freher, Solomon, Scripture). Atwood qualifies the metallic claims: "literally taken they are not" true (¶26).*

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The Stone, a single hidden matter.** The work is done on "the Stone", said to lie hidden in the metals and glossed as glittering, containing a Spirit, and as the Sea of the Wise. Only one matter is used, but the text distinguishes "the Stone" from "Our Stone" without explaining the difference.
   - "In the caverns of the metals there is hidden the Stone, which is venerable, bright in colour, a mind sublime, and an open sea." (IV.3 ¶3)
   - Also: "it is the Sea of the Wise, in which they angle for their mysterious Fish" (¶3); "the Stone, which, according to Hermes, is the vine of the Wise" (¶5); "if you know the Stone which is the only matter of Our Stone" (¶16); "this plant (dry and withered in appearance)" (¶16, interpreted as the Stone).
   - stated (gloss in ¶3; single matter ¶16); QUOTED: Hermes via Eudoxus, Eudoxus.

2. **The Stone is a kind of fire.** The Stone is itself an "Astral Fire" akin to natural fire, born, fed and growing in elementary fire suitably proportioned to it.
   - "Our stone is an Astral Fire which sympathizes with the Natural Fire" (¶16)
   - Also: "as a true Salamander receives it nativity, is nourished and grows in the Elementary Fire, which is geometrically proportioned to it" (¶16).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **The Water hidden in the Stone is the foundation.** A liquid drawn out of the Stone contains everything the work needs. It has many names according to its degree of preparation or its effect; its proper name is Vinegar of the Wise.
   - "Our Water is a living Spring which comes out of the Stone by a natural miracle of our philosophy." (¶8)
   - Also: "The first of all is the water which issueth out of this Stone." (¶8); "search the source of the Liquor of the Sages, which contains all that is necessary for the work; it is hidden under the Stone" (¶10); "extract out of the Stone the precious juice which it encloses in its bosom" (¶16); "sometimes they call it wine, sometimes water of life, sometimes vinegar, sometimes oil, according to the different degrees of Preparation" (¶8); "it is properly called the Vinegar of the Wise" (¶8).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus (citing Hermes that the water is the foundation).

4. **The Water holds three principles not yet truly united.** Salt, Sulphur and Mercury (spirit, soul, body) are in the seemingly uniform Water. Distilling draws off the Water (soul and spirit) and leaves the body as a black earth, which must be kept.
   - "three natural principles of bodies --- Salt, Sulphur and Mercury --- which are the spirit, the soul, and the body" (¶7)
   - Also: "though they appear pure and perfectly united together, there still wants much of their being so" (¶7); "the Body remains in the bottom of the vessel, like a dead, black, and dredgy earth, which, nevertheless, is not to be despised" (¶7).
   - stated (the one-to-one mapping of the triads is open: see Open questions); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **Two compounds.** The compound of Nature (treated in the First Key) is made by Nature, the Artist only assisting with external means. The compound of Art is made by the Artist uniting fixed with volatile, and differs between the Second and Third Work.
   - "the philosophers have two sorts of compounds" (¶9)
   - Also: "who does nothing but lend a hand to Nature by the adhibition of external things" (¶9); "it is the Wise man who makes it by the secret union of the fixed with the volatile" (¶9); "Geber defines it, a mixture of Argent vive and Sulphur, that is to say, of the volatile and the fixed" (¶9).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus, Geber (endorsed).

6. **"our Mercury" is the first matter and water of the metals.** In Cosmopolite's allegory the "old man" is the Mercury, so named because he is the first matter of all metals and their water; he is also called steel and lodestone.
   - "Our Old Man is our Mercury" (¶10)
   - Also: "He is the first matter of all metals" (¶10); "He is their water" (¶10); "to which he gives also the name of steel and of the lodestone" (¶10); "the Spirit, which is our Mercury" (¶12).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus, Cosmopolite via Eudoxus.

7. **Two Mercuries carrying two tinctures.** Two Mercuries, White and Red, are made from the liquor: the White is the Lunaria (bath of the Moon), the Red the most sharp Vinegar (bath of the Sun). They contain the two tinctures.
   - "The Lunaria is the White Mercury, the most sharp Vinegar is the Red Mercury" (¶10)
   - Also: "of the liquor they make two Mercuries --- the one White and the other Red" (¶10); "the White Mercury is the bath of the Moon, and that the Red Mercury is the bath of the Sun" (¶10, reported from "Others", undisputed); "this is the secret of the two mercuries which contain the two tinctures" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

8. **The gold and silver of the work are "living", not common.** Common gold and silver are dead, gross bodies, not tinctures; the philosophers did not make their Stone from common gold.
   - "thou takest not common gold or silver, for these are dead; take Ours which are living" (¶21)
   - Also: "for they are not tinctures, but gross compacted bodies" (¶21, ATWOOD); "the philosophers did not use common gold to make their Stone (though so many deceptively write about it)" (¶22).
   - stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius, Vaughan; ATWOOD.

9. **Two natures, two fires, in every thing.** In the theosophist's account the matter of the work is a heavenly and an earthly matter to be united; a wrath fire and a light-and-love fire are present in everything, wrath predominating in outward nature since the fall.
   - "A heavenly and an earthly matter are to be mutually united and brought to a heavenly consistency." (¶38)
   - Also: "The serpent’s sting is the wrath fire, and the woman’s seed the light and love fire: these two are in every thing" (¶38).
   - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (n16: Freher), endorsed by Atwood.

10. **Man himself is also the subject, first of all.** Atwood's comment applies the work to man: the same process runs in the soul and in nature, man first; it acts above all in "the vital rectification", and its higher objects "transmute" the seeker.
    - "though this concerneth man, in the first place, yet secondly it concerneth the whole creation" (¶38)
    - Also: "The bruising of the serpent’s head is done spiritually and corporally, both in nature and in the soul" (¶38); "but above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification" (¶30); "transmute their worshipper into that Harmony and Beauty" (¶50).
    - stated (that man is the primary subject: theosophist ¶38; Atwood ¶30, ¶50); QUOTED: the theosophist, endorsed; ATWOOD.

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **The Secret Fire (Fire of the Wise).** One agent, never openly named by any writer, performs the sublimation and dissolves the Stone gently into Water. It is known more by divine gift than by reasoning.
   - "the Fire of the Wise, which is the only instrument which can work this sublimation" (¶5)
   - Also: "No philosopher has ever openly revealed this Secret Fire, and this powerful agent, which works all the wonders of the Art" (¶5); "this Secret Fire, which dissolves the Stone naturally and without violence" (¶5).
   - stated (function only; identity never given); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

2. **The secret fire draws the water out of the dry matter.** Without it, knowing the true matter is useless (the speaker lost fifteen years). "Our Fire" is called both secret and natural, and it perfects even the residue. Whether "Our Fire" and the "Red of Magic Fire" are the same as the secret fire is not stated.
   - "the secret fire of the wise, which makes to run out of this plant (dry and withered in appearance) a water which wets not the hands" (¶16)
   - Also: "the Understanding of Our Fire, which is both secret and natural" (¶16); "the whole compound, is made perfect by the action of our Fire" (¶7, Pontanus via Eudoxus); "strike upon it with the Red of Magic Fire, and a clear fountain will issue out" (¶10).
   - interpreted (plant = Stone; Our Fire = secret fire); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **"this Nature wholly Divine".** Only this nature dissolves the Elements of the Stone, and only after proportioned digestion and putrefaction. Whether it is the Secret Fire or the Water is open.
   - "The Elements of the Stone cannot be dissolved but by this Nature wholly Divine" (¶5)
   - Also: "by the distillation which is made by the rays of the Sun and Moon" (¶5, not explained).
   - stated (function); referent open; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

4. **The Spirit is the binding tie.** The Spirit joins soul to body in the Third Key, and joins paste to leaven in fermentation as water joins meal.
   - "of whom the Spirit is the only tie which works this conjunction" (¶7)
   - Also: "the Spirit ought to make the union of the paste with the leaven in the same manner as water moistens meal" (¶14).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **The Water is the active partner of the body.** The Water (spirit) overcomes and stirs the fixed nature in the body. Descending, it brings the soul back to its body and revives it.
   - "For Water is the strongest Nature which surmounts and excites the fixed Nature in the Body, that is, rejoices in it" (¶7)
   - Also: "the divine water is the thing descending from heaven, the reducer of the soul to its body, which it at length revives" (¶8).
   - stated; QUOTED: Hermes via Eudoxus; Eudoxus.

6. **"the blood of innocents" = the spirits of the bodies.** These spirits "feed" the two Mercuries and are the bath in which Sun and Moon (the King) are washed.
   - "the blood of innocents whose throats are cut; that is to say, the spirits of the bodies are the Bath where the Sun and Moon go to wash themselves" (¶10)
   - Also: "feed them with flesh of their own species" (¶10); "prepare the bath of the King with the blood of the Innocents" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

7. **The Alcahest.** The precious liquor, known by a thousand names, is the Alcahest: a permanent water that dissolves the metals completely, then stays united with them, increasing their weight and tincture.
   - "which is in sum the great Alcahest, which radically dissolves the metals -- a true permanent water" (¶10)
   - Also: "after having radically dissolved them, is inseparably united to them, increasing their weight and tincture" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

8. **The coagulating Sulphur within the Mercury.** The Mercury holds its own Sulphur at its centre, which is why simple boiling turns it to earth.
   - "the Mercury of the Wise carries in its centre its own Sulphur, which coagulates it" (¶12)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

9. **Other fires and waters in Sendivogius.** A second stage uses a different fire, "of putrefaction and generation", governed with discretion. The water used must be the "menstruum of the world", rectified until it can calcine gold. A clear water with salt-nitre from "Our Earth" also comes in. None of these is explained.
   - "the fire of putrefaction and generation, not on such a one as thou didst in the first operation" (¶21)
   - Also: "that water must be the menstruum of the world, out of the sphere of the Moon so often rectified until it can calcine gold" (¶21, voice unclear); "water and salt-nitre, which comes from Our Earth in which there is a river of living water" (¶21).
   - stated (as requirements; unexplained); QUOTED: Sendivogius; UNCLEAR for the menstruum sentence.

10. **The Mercury inside the matter does the work.** The transformation is done by the Mercury already present in the matter, "the workman", not by the Artist.
    - "The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself" (¶38)
    - Also: "leave the work to be done by the workman which is in it already" (¶38).
    - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed; extent of quotation unclear).

11. **Light is the ferment ("own metal", "Aurific Seed") of the Stone.** Atwood reads the Stone's "own metal" as Light: not common light, but the light that lies asleep in all nature and becomes active "in the first rotary wheel of life". Only Light multiplies.
    - "is not Light the true Aurific Seed" (¶30)
    - Also: "The Stone is here said accurately to be fermented with its own metal" (¶30); "not common Light, but that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient, which is potentially included everywhere in either kingdom of nature, though laid asleep" (¶30).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

12. **The vivific Ens / Universal Spirit / azoth.** The Stone's life-giving essence assimilates other things because it comes from the same origin as they do, the Universal Spirit. Perfected, it draws its own kind into itself; hence the name azoth.
    - "this powerful vivific Ens is of the same origin as that which it assimilates --- viz., the Universal Spirit" (¶29)
    - Also: "the Hermetic philosophers have given it the name of azoth, which adheres to bodies" (¶29); "Azoth of our Stone, continues he, reduces bodies to their First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form" (¶28, Khunrath).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Khunrath.

13. **Divine Light and the Spirit of Wisdom in the person.** In the inner work the agent is Divine Light shining on the purified soul, and Wisdom or the Spirit given from above; once loosened, it carries the work by its own Rule.
    - "the Divine Light will immediately shine on her" (¶30)
    - Also: "When once the Divine Light is loosened in life, everything is prospered as long as her Rule is observed" (¶39, ATWOOD); "except thou give Wisdom and send thy Holy Spirit from above?" (¶39, Solomon); "The Spirit of Wisdom runs lovingly by her own Rule to fulfill it" (¶56, ATWOOD).
    - stated; QUOTED: Julian (offered by Atwood as illustration), Solomon; ATWOOD.

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **Divine gift, sought through work, prayer, reading and meditation.** The Secret Fire and the whole magistery are known more by Heaven's inspiration than by reasoning. The operator who does not recognise the Fire should stop and pray; work, reading and meditation on Nature also overcome the difficulty. A faithful friend can guide.
   - "the knowledge of our Magistery comes rather by the Inspiration of Heaven than from the Lights which we can get by ourselves" (¶16)
   - Also: "the knowledge of this great Secret is rather a gift of Heaven, than a Light acquired by the natural force of reasoning" (¶5); "there is no difficulty which may not in the end be made clear by Work, Meditation, and Prayer" (¶5); "pray daily, read good books, and meditate night and day on the operations of Nature" (¶16); "without the inspiration of Heaven, or without the help of a faithful friend, one remains undoubtedly in this labyrinth" (¶7).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

2. **Knowledge of Nature, the matter, chemistry and heat.** The Third Key's long train of simple, natural operations requires an understanding of Nature's laws, the qualities of the matter, chemistry and the right degrees of heat. Reading the writers also needs discernment ("Lynx's eyes").
   - "a great understanding of the Laws of Nature, and of the qualities of Our Matter, as well as a perfect knowledge of chemistry and of the different degrees of heat" (¶16)
   - Also: "to the end that those who have not the Lynx's eyes may pursue wrong" (¶3).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **Study before practice (Atwood).** The operator must lay a foundation by studying the Hermetic records and the laws of the Art before interpreting or attempting anything.
   - "beware of hasty interpretation and trials of skill, without a proper foundation laid by study of the Hermetic records and scientific Laws of Art" (¶54)
   - stated; ATWOOD.

4. **A long, laborious, self-denying life.** Alchemy's true rewards come only after a lifetime of thought, patience, mastery of the passions and suffering. Abundance is earned by one's own labour, not by oppressing others.
   - "the rewards only of a long- suffering and laborious life" (¶26)
   - Also: "of unwearying thought, patience under afflictions, conquest over unruly passions, hunger, thirst, stripes merited and unmerited, persecutions, indigence, self-denial" (¶26); "not by the oppression of others or by unlawful means, but by industry, patience, and the labor of his own hand" (¶26, Rosary).
   - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Author of the Rosary.

5. **Self-knowledge.** Without understanding himself and the evil under which he lies "enchanted", the operator cannot understand fall and regeneration or redeem matter.
   - "if man does not enter in to understand himself, and the evil under which he lies enchanted in this life" (¶38)
   - Also: "nor does man any longer seek after the Truth that is in himself" (¶37).
   - stated; ATWOOD.

6. **Will and faith.** The materials and foundations are available now; what is lacking is will and faith.
   - "the will only is wanting to discover the way, and faith to resuscitate the Corner Stone, which ignorance has rejected from immemorial time" (¶37)
   - stated (Corner Stone unexplained); ATWOOD.

7. **The Artist prepares; he does not perform the work.** He prepares the way and the matter and leaves the transformation to the Mercury within. He still needs understanding and faith, and must be inwardly alive himself.
   - "the Artist is not to attempt it --- he cannot do it --- he is to prepare the way, the matter as is requisite" (¶38)
   - Also: "understanding and faith are required of him" (¶38); "which never can be done by one who is dead himself in his understanding and internal life" (¶38).
   - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed; extent of quotation unclear).

8. **Prepared by the Spirit; holy and true.** Only someone the Spirit has already prepared, prudent, holy and true, "that hath the Key of David", is competent to minister.
   - "he that the Spirit has already prepared, and endowed with prudence for the undertaking" (¶39)
   - Also: "he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the Key of David" (¶39).
   - stated (Key of David unexplained); ATWOOD.

9. **Charity, not gain; the healer chosen by God.** The model healer has a divine nature, acts from compassion and charity without hope of gain, and passes on what he has received. When ambition and gain come in, the gift is withdrawn.
   - "the hope of gain shall not be in his thoughts" (¶34)
   - Also: "compassion shall be his leader" (¶34); "But a physician chosen by God, continues Helmont, His own signs shall follow" (¶34); "he cured extraordinary maladies because he was endowed with a divine nature" (¶32, Petris on Hippocrates); "And that which a man has received, will he not be able to impart again" (¶32, ATWOOD).
   - stated; QUOTED: Helmont (praised by Atwood, ¶35), Petris; ATWOOD.

10. **The faithful few, labouring alone.** Wisdom's secret is held by the faithful. Only the right-minded and studious who persist will unravel the Mystery, each working for himself; the talent is given to few and needs fitting conditions.
    - "The right-minded and studious alone benevolently, conscientiously, and rationally persisting to the end, will unravel it: and in this case each must labor singularly for himself." (¶54)
    - Also: "The talent is granted only to a few, and for them conditions are needful to draw it forth to increase" (¶54); "but Her secret is with the Faithful" (¶36); "Sed tanto paucos dignantur munere divi" (¶55, Palingenius; ours: but the gods deem few worthy of so great a gift).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the prophet, Palingenius (endorsed).

11. **Humility; obedience to the Divine Will.** The adept must use the gift in obedience to the Divine Will for others' benefit, and must not credit his own hand.
    - "that it may be always applied aright in obedience to the Divine Will, for the benefaction of mankind and vindication of true virtue" (¶48)
    - Also: "lest thou say in thine heart my power the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth" (¶57, Deuteronomy, endorsed).
    - stated (¶48); interpreted (application of ¶57 by analogy); ATWOOD.

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

**Framework.**

1. **Three analogous works, one repeated cycle.** In each work the body is dissolved by the spirit, its blackness removed, it is whitened, and the white is quickened. The writers blur the works on purpose. Keys 1–3 belong to the First Work (the Second Key is "of the First Work"); the Fourth Key is "the entrance to the Second Work"; the Fifth is the Third Work. ¶9 complicates this mapping (see Open questions).
   - "in each work the Wise Artist ought to dissolve the body with the spirit; he must cut off the Raven's head, whiten the Black, and vivify the White" (¶3)
   - Also: "the operations of the three works have a great deal of analogy one to another" (¶3); "the operation of the Second Key of the First Work is ended" (¶5); "The Fourth Key of the Art is the entrance to the Second Work (and a reiteration in part and development of the foregoing)" (¶12).
   - interpreted (Raven's head = blackness, from ¶3's separation of the black fume); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

**Eudoxus' Keys.**

2. **First Key: free the Sulphur, separate the blackness, form the white Stone.** The first operation frees the enclosed Sulphur, extracts the seed from the body and joins spirit with body. Its decisive act is separating the black, foul fume, from which the white Stone forms. It ends at the first purification and whiteness.
   - "The First Key is that which opens the dark prisons in which the Sulphur is shut up" (¶3)
   - Also: "this is it which knows how to extract the seed out of the body" (¶3); "conjunction of the spirit with the body -- of sulphur with mercury" (¶3); "it is properly in the First operation that the Wise Artist cuts off the head of the Black Dragon and of the Raven" (¶3); "What is born of the Crow is the beginning of this Art." (¶3, Hermes); "it is by separation of the black, foul, and stinking fume of the Blackest Black that our astral, white, and resplendent Stone is formed" (¶3).
   - stated (sequence); interpreted (Dragon/Raven = the black fume); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **Second Key: sublimation by the Secret Fire; dissolve the Stone into Water.** The compound is dissolved and its Elements separated by raising the subtle above the gross. This makes possible the conversion of the elements and the extraction of the Principles, the only way to prepare "our Mercury". The Stone ("vine") becomes Wine, then Water of Life and Vinegar. The Key ends with proportioned digestion and putrefaction.
   - "this separation of the elements is not made but by raising up the subtle and pure parts above the thick and terrestrial parts" (¶5)
   - Also: "this conversion, which makes Water of Earth, Air of Water, and Fire of Air, is the only way whereby our Mercury can be prepared" (¶5); "makes it dissolve into Water in the great sea of the Wise, by the distillation which is made by the rays of the Sun and Moon" (¶5); "nor can a perfect dissolution be made of it, but after a proportioned digestion and putrefaction" (¶5).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

4. **Third Key: the longest and most concealed; separate, purify and reunite the principles of Mercury.** Its aim is to achieve "a perfect dissolution and glorification of the body" and reunite soul with body through the Spirit, producing a nobler new substance. The writers stay silent or feign about it.
   - "The Third Key comprehends of itself alone a longer train of operations than all the rest together." (¶7)
   - Also: "there is hardly one found who has not feigned, instead of showing the longest and the most important of the operations of our Practice" (¶7); "the separation and the purification of the Principles of our Mercury, which is made by a perfect dissolution and glorification of the body" (¶7).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **Third Key, step: wash the earth with its own spirit to extract the Fixed Salt.** After the Water is distilled off, the black earth that remains holds the Fire and Sulphur at its centre. It is washed with its own spirit to draw out the Fixed Salt, by suitable digestion and slow distillation.
   - "they must wash it exactly with its spirit, to extract out of it the Fixed Salt, which is the Blood of our Stone" (¶7)
   - Also: "the Fire and the Sulphur are hidden in the centre of the Earth" (¶7); "which is not accomplished till after a convenient digestion and a slow distillation" (¶7).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

6. **Third Key, step: reconcile fire and water in their earth by moistening it often.** The opposites are made friends in their own earth by re-wetting the earth again and again with its water, so the body is dissolved and penetrated and made fit for generation. Spirit (Eve) and body (Adam) are joined.
   - "moisten oftentimes the earth with its water, and you will obtain what you seek" (¶7)
   - Also: "the Wise Artist must make peace between the enemies, who radically love each other vehemently" (¶7); "All things must therefore being purged make Fire and Water to be Friends, which they will easily do in their earth, which had ascended with them." (¶7, Cosmopolite); "Must not the body be dissolved by the water, and the Earth be penetrated with its Humidity, to be made proper for generation?" (¶7).
   - interpreted (the moistening as the means of reconciliation, by sequence); QUOTED: Eudoxus, Cosmopolite.

7. **Third Key, step: dissolve the body wholly, then wash the residue until its blackness goes.** The whole body is dissolved to get all its humidity (ferment, sulphur, balm). The whole is reduced to water, volatile is united with fixed, and what rose is brought back down. The residue is washed with the water until the blackness is removed; the water is then "animated" with the Fiery Essence.
   - "you ought to wash them with this celestial water until you have removed away the blackness from them" (¶8)
   - Also: "It is therefore necessary to dissolve the body entirely to extract all its humidity" (¶8); "Reduce then the whole compound into water, and make a perfect union of the volatile with the fixed" (¶8); "the highest fume should be reduced to the lowest" (¶8, Senior); "then your Water shall be animated with this Fiery Essence, which works all the wonders of our Art" (¶8).
   - stated (washing, animation); interpreted (Senior's precept = returning volatile onto fixed); QUOTED: Eudoxus, Senior (endorsed).

8. **Third Key, step: whiten the faeces, extract the white sulphur / fixed salt, and pour the water back repeatedly.** The black residue is purified and whitened to separate a white sulphur; "all this mystery" is extracting the fixed salt. The distilled water carries up part of the fiery salt, and pouring it back on the body many times enriches the Mercury until it can be fixed.
   - "the affusion of the water on the body, reiterated many times, impregnates, fattens, and fertilizes our Mercury, and makes it fit to be fixed, which is the end of the second Work" (¶9)
   - Also: "purify the Serpent, born of the Slime of the earth" (¶9; Serpent not glossed); "whiten these foul and black faeces, to separate from thence the white sulphur" (¶9); "all this mystery is but the extraction of the fixed salt of our compound, in which the whole energy of our Mercury consists" (¶9); "The water which ascends by distillation carries up with it a part of this fiery salt" (¶9).
   - stated (affusion, extraction); interpreted (Serpent = the black residue); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

9. **Third Key, step: the two Mercuries and the bath of the King.** The two Mercuries are "fed" with the spirits of the bodies ("blood of innocents") and kept separate. The Stone is struck with "the Red of Magic Fire" so the hidden liquor flows out, and the King's bath is prepared with the Innocents' blood. This gives the animated Mercury, which keeps its virtue in a closed vessel. Cosmopolite's allegory of the old man (Mercury) swallowing gold and silver, dying and being burnt describes the purification of the Mercury and "the whole magistery". The speaker calls this the most essential point in the Practice.
   - "prepare the bath of the King with the blood of the Innocents, and you will have the animated Mercury of the wise, which never loses its virtue, if you keep it in a vessel well closed" (¶10)
   - Also: "strike upon it with the Red of Magic Fire, and a clear fountain will issue out" (¶10); "if you shall give our old man gold and silver to swallow, that he may consume them, and at length he also dying may be burnt" (¶10, Cosmopolite); "Let his ashes be strewed in the water; boil it until it is enough, and you have a medicine to cure the leprosy" (¶10); "if gold couples with it eleven times it sends forth its seed, and is debilitated almost unto death; but the Chalybes conceives and begets a son more glorious than the Father" (¶10); "one must make use of the citrine Mercury to make the Imbibition of the Red" (¶10, Flammel).
   - stated (sequence; old man = Mercury); interpreted (gold/Chalybes figure; the details of the allegory are not decoded); QUOTED: Eudoxus, Cosmopolite, Flammel.

10. **Third Key, recapitulated in ¶16: water from the plant joins the "dry water", giving the mercurial liquor.** The water drawn by the secret fire, united with "the dry water of the sea of the wise", dissolves into a viscous mercurial liquor, the beginning and Key of the Art. Converting, separating and purifying the elements gives the true Mercury, then the fixed Sulphur and the Universal Medicine. The royal bath must still be purified, healed and animated.
    - "by a magical union of the dry water of the sea of the wise, is dissolved into a viscous water -- into a mercurial liquor, which is the beginning, the foundation, and the Key of our Art" (¶16)
    - Also: "Convert, separate, and purify the elements, as I have taught you, and you will possess the true Mercury of the philosophers" (¶16); "you must know how to purify, to heal, and to animate the royal bath" (¶16).
    - stated ("dry water" unexplained); QUOTED: Eudoxus.

11. **Philosophic calcination.** The Stone is calcined by a natural process, not by violent heat. Common calcination destroys the body's radical humidity; philosophic calcination sublimes and distils the Stone and increases its humidity.
    - "not by the common calcination which is made by the violence of fire, but by a philosophic calcination which is purely natural" (¶16)
    - Also: "this philosophical calcination, which sublimes and distills the Stone in calcining it, much augments its humidity" (¶16).
    - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus ("with the most enlightened philosophers").

12. **Fourth Key (entrance to the Second Work): turn the Water into Earth by patient boiling.** The only operation is "terrification", making the Spirit (Mercury) bodily. The resulting earth is then repeatedly moistened with its own humidity and dried, which increases its virtue, weight and fertility.
    - "The terrification of the Spirit is the only operation of this work." (¶12)
    - Also: "it is this which reduces our Water into Earth" (¶12); "Boil them with patience" (¶12); "to corporify the Spirit, which is our Mercury" (¶12); "moisten it often with its own humidity, dry it as often" (¶12); "knowing how to make the dew of Heaven fall upon it" (¶12, unexplained).
    - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

13. **Fifth Key (Third Work): ferment the Stone with the Perfect Body.** The Perfect Body is the leaven, "Our Paste" is what is leavened, and the Spirit is the water that joins them. The whole becomes a leaven that can ferment new matter without limit; this "repeats the instruction of the whole work". Proportions follow bread-making.
    - "The Fifth Key includes the Fermentation of the Stone with the perfect body, to make thereof the medicine of the Third order." (¶14)
    - Also: "the Perfect Body is a necessary leaven of Our Paste" (¶14); "put a ferment to them and make the water earth, that the paste may be made a ferment" (¶14, Hermes); "Do not the bakers put more meal than leaven, and more water than the leaven and the meal?" (¶14).
    - stated (mapping meal = paste for proportions is interpreted); QUOTED: Eudoxus, Hermes via Eudoxus.

14. **Sixth Key: multiplication by reiteration.** The same operation is repeated (dissolving and coagulating, imbibing and drying) and the Stone's virtues increase without limit. Projection is omitted because success does not depend on it.
    - "which consists but in opening and shutting, dissolving and coagulating, imbibing and drying" (¶16)
    - Also: "whereby the virtues of the Stone are infinitely augmentable" (¶16); "will tell you nothing more of what relates to the Projection of the Medicine, because the success you expect depends not thereon" (¶16).
    - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

**Atwood's supplement on Multiplication and Projection (¶17–26; cited authorities, later qualified as not literally true, ¶26).**

15. **Multiplication (Open Entrance).** One part Perfect Matter with at most three or four parts Mercury of the First Work, in a sealed vessel over a gentle fire. All the operations run through again in seven days; repeated, in three days, one day, an hour; strength rises until it is incalculable after the fifth time. Lucerna Salis and Eirenaeus: re-dissolve and coagulate with its own mercury, each time raising its power.
    - "take one part of the Perfect Matter and to join it with three or four parts at most of the Mercury of the First Work" (¶18)
    - Also: "place both in a vessel well luted and sealed" (¶18); "all the operations before named pass through rapidly in seven days" (¶18); "be again dissolved and coagulated by the virtue of its mercury, white or red, of which it was prepared" (¶24); "It oft dissolve, and then congeal again" (¶25).
    - stated; QUOTED: Author of the Open Entrance (n1/n7 E. Philalethes), Author of Lucerna Salis, Eirenaeus.

16. **Two kinds of fermentation (Atwood).** Spiritual fermentation multiplies the tinctures and cannot use common gold or silver. Bodily fermentation is the "descent or incorporation" of the finished Stone into gold, used only to temper its strength for projection.
    - "there is a twofold fermentation --- a spiritual and a bodily" (¶21)
    - Also: "The spiritual fermentation is performed by multiplying the tinctures" (¶21); "This descent or incorporation, some wise authors have called a bodily fermentation" (¶22, Vaughan); "they used it only to qualify the utensive power of it when it was made, that they might the more easily find what quantity of base metal they should project upon" (¶22).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Vaughan.

17. **Sendivogius' process.** Living metals are put in "Our Fire" to make a "dry liquor". The earth resolves into a Water, the Mercury of philosophers, which consumes gold and silver down to their radical moisture. This is set on clear water with salt-nitre from "Our Earth", over the fire of putrefaction and generation, and digested through the colours until it tinges iron; water is then added and it is boiled again. The writer says his experience stopped there.
    - "the earth will be resolved into Water which is called the Mercury of philosophers" (¶21)
    - Also: "put them into Our Fire and let there be made of them a dry liquor" (¶21); "and this shall be the radical moisture of the metals" (¶21); "govern all things with a great deal of discretion" (¶21); "So far reached my experience: --- I can do no more, says the wary Artist, I found out no more." (¶21).
    - stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius.

18. **Projection, in steps.** One part Stone on four of molten silver (white) or gold (red) gives a powder. One part of the powder on ten of purified, heated mercury gives a weaker medicine, which is projected on purified molten metal. Project by degrees. Lucerna Salis: first ferment with a drachm of pure gold or silver, because the imperfect metals cannot bear the medicine's subtlety. Vaughan: one part on ten of molten gold gives a bloody powder, the "Arabian Elixir". Atwood names "the final Projection" "the metalline perfectibility of the matter".
    - "the final Projection, as the metalline perfectibility of the matter is sometimes called" (¶19, ATWOOD)
    - Also: "cause to be melted in a crucible four parts of one of the fixed metals" (¶20); "you will have the medicine, though of an inferior order" (¶20); "it is requisite that they should first be fermented with very good and most pure gold" (¶24); "this single grain brings all the gold to a bloody powder" (¶22); "it is always best to make projection by degrees until the Stone yields no more tincture" (¶20).
    - stated; ATWOOD (¶19); QUOTED: E. Philalethes, Author of Lucerna Salis, Vaughan, Eirenaeus.

**Atwood's comment (¶27–62).**

19. **Fermentation, the last operation = union with God.** In the last operation the Stone's parts are finally united and brought into relation with the external world, which completes its transmuting power. This is what the Kabalah calls the union with God of man "reduced to the simplicity of the Monad".
    - "that which in the Kabalah is denominated the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God, that in metaphysico-chemistry is called Fermentation" (¶27)
    - Also: "which union or consummation of its transmutative virtue is called Fermentation" (¶27); "In the last operation the union of the Philosophic Stone is said to be finally cemented, in its component parts agreeing and having relation to the external world" (¶27).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

20. **Fermenting with its "own metal" (Light).** The Stone is fermented with its own metal in its purest form, silver for the White and gold for the Red, in three days. Atwood reads this metal as Light.
    - "The Stone is fermented with its metal existing in the highest purity, with pure silver for the White, and with Red for fine Gold. And this is the work of three days" (¶29)
    - Also: "The Stone is here said accurately to be fermented with its own metal: for is not Light the true Aurific Seed" (¶30).
    - stated (the instruction; Atwood's gloss as Light); UNCLEAR voice for ¶29 sentence (n9 Khunrath), endorsed by ATWOOD ¶30.

21. **Repeated fermentation, rectification and recalcination of the Universal Spirit.** These cycles are repeated until its inner force is wholly drawn out and acts outwardly.
    - "so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined, until the central force, wholly drawn out, acts forcibly at the circumference" (¶29)
    - interpreted (centre/circumference = inner power acting outward); ATWOOD.

22. **Reduce to First Matter, then reanimate.** The Stone's azoth works in two steps: it takes bodies back to their First Matter, then gives them new life with the Universal Form.
    - "Azoth of our Stone, continues he, reduces bodies to their First Matter and reanimates them with the Universal Form" (¶28)
    - stated; QUOTED: Khunrath (reported with approval).

23. **Vital rectification: surrender, purification, Divine Light, connate spirit.** The soul gives herself to the divine, purification leading, and Divine Light shines on her. Thus "deified", she strengthens her connate spirit, which then keeps the whole body healthy. Purifying ceremonies make bodies worthy of divine help.
    - "when the soul gives herself to Divinity, and wholly delivers herself to the guidance of a better nature, divine institutions, through purification, taking the lead, the Divine Light will immediately shine on her" (¶30)
    - Also: "she transfuses a certain vigorous strength into her connate spirit, which when included, and as it were possessing dominion, becomes, through this spirit the cause of safety to the whole body" (¶30); "through purifying ceremonies bodies become worthy of receiving divine assistance and health" (¶30, the oracles).
    - stated (Julian's sequence); interpreted (that this is what Atwood means by "the vital rectification": introduced with "Thus Julian" and "for example"); QUOTED: Julian, the oracles; offered by ATWOOD.

24. **Raise the light fire through the wrath fire; reduce two natures to one.** The light fire is raised until it shines through and governs the wrath fire, which is kept as a servant, not destroyed. The dark Mercury is thus "tinctured" and turned from death to life. Earth is turned in and Heaven out.
    - "the latter must be raised up, and by its shining through the wrath, must subdue and keep it under" (¶38)
    - Also: "so that it may exercise its true natural office as a servant to the light" (¶38); "when the dark poisoning Mercury is thus tinctured, his anguishing death is turned into triumphing life" (¶38); "Earth must be turned in, and Heaven out." (¶38).
    - stated (raising and subduing); interpreted (Earth turned in, Heaven out); QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed).

25. **Gradual purification, step by step.** Once the Divine Light is released, purifying and perfecting proceed by small successive steps.
    - "Line upon line, Precept upon precept, (purifying and perfecting) here a little and there a little" (¶39)
    - stated (Atwood's parenthesis glosses the verse); ATWOOD.

26. **Turn inward, guided by Conscience.** The search is turned inward, steadily and patiently, until Conscience shows the higher objects to pursue. The work is kept complete in the mind as aim and model.
    - "with steady perseverance looking within, until Conscience reveal to us those higher objects of pursuit, and truer attractions" (¶50)
    - Also: "the revelation of all things is always at hand for him who knows how to investigate" (¶49); "Be this our aim and model, and our Hands Shall not wax faint until the work is done." (¶51, unattributed verse).
    - stated (¶50); interpreted (¶51 applied to the work); ATWOOD; UNCLEAR (verse).

27. **Discipline and ordeal, then liberation, then the "comparatively easy" final step.** The wise passed through prescribed disciplines, ordeals and humiliations to conform to the Divine Perfection. Once freed from sense, mixed desire and passion, the final step is comparatively easy: by faith the Identity passes from separate selfhood into universal accord.
    - "Once delivered from the exterior bondages of sense and heterogeneous desire, from the passions and false affections of this transitory life, the final step is declared comparatively easy" (¶60)
    - Also: "passing through every ordained discipline, ordeal rite, and humiliation, in order that they might become conformable to the Divine Perfection they desired to approach" (¶60); "as transcending by the energy of faith, from the separable selfhood, the Identity passes into universal accord" (¶60).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

28. **Go forth and return; meditate with eyes closed.** Meditate with eyes closed; if the heart fails, return again. The text ties this to the Kabalah's "To go forth and to return".
    - "Close thy eyes and meditate: and if thy heart fail thee, return again" (¶60)
    - Also: "To go forth and to return; therefore was the agreement cut off" (¶60); "Egredere et regredere, et propterea pactum praecisum est" (¶61; ours: Go forth and return, and therefore the covenant was cut).
    - stated (instruction), not explained further; UNCLEAR voice (Kabalah or Atwood; n28 Sephir Jezirah).

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **Black fume separated; shining whiteness.** The end of the First Key is marked by a first purification giving a shining white Stone, said to contain "the blood of the Pelican" (not explained).
   - "It is at this First Purification of the Stone, and at this shining whiteness, that the work of the First Key is ended." (¶3)
   - Also: "which contains in its veins the blood of the Pelican" (¶3).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

2. **The desired blackness.** A blackness in the vessel is the sign the philosophers desire; it appears only if the body has been dissolved entirely and all its humidity extracted.
   - "without which you ought not to hope ever to see in your vessel this blackness so desired by all the philosophers" (¶8)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **Order of distillation, as with vinegar.** Distilling the Water behaves like distilling common vinegar: the watery phlegm comes first and the effective oily part last.
   - "the water and the phlegm ascend first; the oily substance, in which the efficacy of the water consists, comes the last" (¶8)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

4. **Water thickening; viscous "ponticity".** A gradual thickening and hardening of the water shows success. Its viscosity marks its virtue, which grows with further rectification.
   - "When I saw that the water by degrees did become thicker and harder I did rejoice" (¶9)
   - Also: "Its exuberant ponticity is indeed the true character of its virtue" (¶9).
   - stated; QUOTED: Hermes via Eudoxus.

5. **Union of spirit and body ascending together.** At the end of the Third Key the two substances rise together leaving little residue, and appear inseparably united in a nobler form, like rectified alcohol compared with wine.
   - "ascend insensibly together, leaving but a little faeces in the bottom of their vessel" (¶7)
   - Also: "as different from its first liquid Form as the alcohol of Wine exactly rectified and actuated with its salt is different from the substance of the wine" (¶7).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

6. **Timely marks of coagulation, or proof of earlier failure.** In the Fourth Key marks of coagulation should appear soon. If they do not appear in their time they never will, which shows the body enclosing the Sulphur was not well dissolved.
   - "if they appear not in their time, they will never appear" (¶12)
   - Also: "it is an undoubted sign that you have failed in some essential thing in the former operations" (¶12).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

7. **Humidity increases under philosophic calcination.** Experience will show that natural calcination increases the Stone's humidity, unlike common calcination.
   - "Experience will give you knowledge in the Practice of this great truth" (¶16)
   - Also: "much augments its humidity" (¶16).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

8. **Sendivogius' colours and the iron test.** First peacock-tail colours, then one green throughout, then fiery-coloured ashes with the water almost red; then the iron-tinging test.
   - "until colors appear like a peacock’s tail" (¶21)
   - Also: "there appear throughout the whole one green color" (¶21); "when thou shalt see in the bottom ashes of a fiery color and the water almost red" (¶21); "dip in a pen, and smear some Iron with it; if it tinge" (¶21).
   - stated; QUOTED: Sendivogius.

9. **Colours run faster in each multiplication.** Each repetition passes through the whole regimen of colours in a shorter time.
   - "one natural day will complete the whole, and every regiment of the colors will in that brief space be passed through" (¶18)
   - stated; QUOTED: Author of the Open Entrance.

10. **Penetration in projection; the glassy, ruby-like mass.** The powder enters the metal at once, as rennet curdles milk. After the first projection the mass is brittle, glassy and ruby-like, with its virtue lessened.
    - "just like milk Is penetrated by the Rennet sour, And curdled in the minute of an hour" (¶25)
    - Also: "in the twinkling of an eye as it were, penetrate the mercury" (¶20); "Then brittle like to glasse that masse will be" (¶25).
    - stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus, E. Philalethes.

11. **The medicine is not used up by use.** Like fire, the Stone multiplies itself. Van Helmont cured some 10,000 people a year without his medicine diminishing.
    - "without any diminution of his medicine" (¶35)
    - Also: "That never shall thy stock with use be spent," (¶25, Eirenaeus); "it, like fire, is apt to multiply Itself in weight as eke in dignity" (¶25).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Eirenaeus.

12. **The perfected Spirit attracts its own kind.** Perfected, the Universal Spirit draws the same nature into itself "by a supernatural magnetism".
    - "is able to draw, by a supernatural magnetism, the Homogeneal Nature everywhere into itself, according to the Rational Form inbred" (¶29)
    - stated; ATWOOD.

13. **Signs in the person.** Once the Divine Light is loosened, the whole person acts "by rule and with power". Material gifts come as tokens of the Spirit's love. The capable judge by "the infallible touchstone" of Nature's Original Light. The Stone is finally "confirmed in operation, visibility, and luminous increase".
    - "is confirmed in operation, visibility, and luminous increase, when rising in rational supremacy over sense and finite reflection, the Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause" (¶62)
    - Also: "The whole individual is occupied; every look and action is by rule and with power" (¶39); "then he sends us these things as tokens and pledges of his love" (¶46, the disciple); "to discern the rational possibility by the infallible touchstone of her Original Light" (¶54).
    - stated (¶62, ¶46); interpreted (¶39 as a sign; ¶54 "her" = Nature); ATWOOD; QUOTED: the disciple (endorsed).

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Deliberately equivocal and feigned writings.** The writers blur the three works and feign about the Third Key; without discernment, inspiration or a guide one is lost in a labyrinth. Reading the philosophers alone will hardly succeed.
   - "when one imagines that they speak of one work, they often treat of another" (¶3)
   - Also: "be lost in this labyrinth, from whence it is very hard to get out" (¶3); "you will hardly arrive at your design by only reading the philosophers" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

2. **Not knowing the Secret Fire.** Even someone who knows the true matter can fail for years without it; and even with it, success does not come at the first attempt.
   - "after having known the true matter, you should spend fifteen years entirely in the work, in study and in meditation, without being able to extract out of the Stone the precious juice" (¶16)
   - Also: "even after you shall be arrived at the knowledge of the Secret Fire of the Wise, yet still you shall not attain your point at your first career" (¶16).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

3. **Discarding the heavy part.** It is an error to purify by keeping the subtle and throwing away the heavy; the residue must be kept and perfected.
   - "persuade themselves that they must only take the subtile and cast away the heavy" (¶7)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

4. **Incomplete dissolution of the body.** Without complete dissolution there is no blackness, and later no coagulation.
   - "for to corporify the Spirit, which is our Mercury, you must have well dissolved the body in which the Sulphur which coagulates the Mercury is enclosed" (¶12)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **Mixing the two Mercuries.** Mixing the White and Red Mercuries spoils the result.
   - "Keep them separately, and do not confound their species, for fear they should beget a monstrous Lineage." (¶10)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

6. **Violent fire.** Common calcination by violent fire destroys the body and its radical humidity.
   - "the first destroys the body and consumes the greatest part of its radical humidity" (¶16)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

7. **Impurities in what receives the medicine.** In projection, impure metals waste the Stone, and the unmediated medicine is too subtle for imperfect metals. A parenthesis warns that the "imperfect and impure metals" must not be misunderstood.
   - "the Stone sustains great loss on account of the impurities with which they abound" (¶20)
   - Also: "the imperfect metals would not be able to support its too great and supreme subtlety" (¶24); "The imperfect and impure metals (let not the allusion here be misapprehended) must also be purified" (¶24).
   - stated; QUOTED: E. Philalethes, Author of Lucerna Salis (parenthesis possibly Atwood's).

8. **Foreign admixture hinders Light.** Light multiplies only when freed from heterogeneous matter.
   - "when set free to attract its proper fuel indeficiently, without heterogeneous hindrance or alloy" (¶30)
   - stated; ATWOOD.

9. **Disordered motion of the spirit.** Most disease comes from erratic movement of the vital spirit.
   - "diseases, or at least the greater part, happen from the mutative and erroneous motion of the spirit" (¶30)
   - stated; QUOTED: Julian.

10. **The body's weight and a scattered mind.** The body and a mind busy with many things hinder knowledge of heavenly things.
    - "For the corruptible body presseth down the soul, and the earthly tabernacle weigheth down that mind that museth upon many things." (¶39)
    - stated; QUOTED: Solomon.

11. **Ambition, gain, arrogance, sloth.** When charity fails, the divine gift is withdrawn and the art dies; discursive theorizing replaces it. The renewed Light is again endangered by neglect or by uncharitable reaction to abuse.
    - "after that the study of ambition and gain were practiced, charity grew cold, mercy was extinguished, art perished, and the Giver of Lights withdrew his Gifts" (¶34)
    - Also: "arrogance and sloth, which long since extinguished Charity" (¶34); "suffer it again to fall into disuse through negligence, or by obloquy be tempted to an uncharitable ambuscade" (¶35, ATWOOD; syntax obscure).
    - stated; QUOTED: Helmont (endorsed, ¶35); ATWOOD (interpreted for ¶35).

12. **Unfit character; haste.** Ignorance, greed, selfishness, faithlessness and frivolity make success impossible, as do hasty interpretation and premature trials.
    - "In vain the ignorant, in vain the avaricious, the selfish, faithless, or frivolous, will in vain seek to percolate this Mystery." (¶54)
    - Also: "quem scire profanes Haud quaquam licet, et frustra plebs improba quaerit" (¶55; ours: which the profane may by no means know, and the wicked crowd seeks in vain).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Palingenius.

13. **Too little desire, or proud presumption; private ends.** Even a favoured operator can let the work fall into evil. Pursuing it for private ends leads to confusion.
    - "lest desiring too little or presuming too far in self-sufficient pride, it should fall, and Satan establish a monarchy upon the most holy of works" (¶56)
    - Also: "but leaves him to hopeless confusion who abandons the Divine purpose for the accomplishment of private ends" (¶56).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

14. **The operator's fault passes into the work.** Warnings: "Enlarge not thy destiny", "Neither break a superficies". The agent's sin may be multiplied in the patient and its offspring.
    - "lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring unto the third or fourth generation" (¶56)
    - Also: "Ne tu augeas fatum --- Enlarge not thy destiny" (¶56); "Neither break a superficies, as the Pythagoric precept runs" (¶56).
    - interpreted (agent = operator, patient = matter, offspring = product; see Open questions); ATWOOD.

15. **Pride in success.** Prosperity tempts the successful to forget God and credit their own power.
    - "then thy heart be lifted, and thou forget the Lord thy God" (¶57)
    - interpreted (applied to the work by analogy, "So the Israelites"); QUOTED: Deuteronomy (endorsed).

16. **Seeking gold; resting in metallic good.** Aiming at gold is a delusion: gold is supplied by nature, and the literal claims are false. Resting in metallic gain is Midas's error.
    - "rest in a mere metalline good, like Midas" (¶48)
    - Also: "Let not one then be allured by the ultimate promises of Alchemy" (¶26); "would it not, we ask, be a delirious speculation for any one to undertake to make the precious metal which is to be had in plentiful abundance" (¶26).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

17. **Lack of self-knowledge, will and faith.** See Operator 5–6. Ignorance has rejected the Corner Stone.
    - "so far do we live without the true knowledge of ourselves" (¶37)
    - stated; ATWOOD.

## The result

1. **A nobler new substance; the animated Mercury.** The Third Key ends in a new substance far nobler than the first, and in the animated Mercury, which keeps its virtue when sealed.
   - "the generation of a new substance infinitely nobler than the First" (¶7)
   - Also: "the animated Mercury of the wise, which never loses its virtue, if you keep it in a vessel well closed" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

2. **Purified bodies and spirits united permanently.** Once bodies are purified and joined with spirits, they never separate again, as the soul does not leave the glorified body.
   - "there is so much sympathy between the purified bodies and the spirits, that they never quit one another when they are united together" (¶10)
   - Also: "this union resembles that of the soul with the glorified body" (¶10); "the spirits desire to be in the cleansed bodies, and having them, they enliven and dwell in them" (¶10).
   - stated; QUOTED: Hermes via Eudoxus (endorsed).

3. **The fertile earth (Land of Promise).** Turned into earth, the mercurial water gains all its virtues and becomes extremely fertile.
   - "An earth admirable is it for fertility -- the Land of Promise of the Wise" (¶12)
   - Also: "our mercurial water shall obtain all the virtues which the philosophers attribute to it if it be turned into earth" (¶12).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus citing Hermes.

4. **A leaven that multiplies without limit.** After fermentation the whole preparation becomes a leaven able to ferment and multiply new matter endlessly.
   - "a leaven proper to ferment a new matter, and to multiply it to infinity" (¶14)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **The true Mercury, the fixed Sulphur, the Universal Medicine; the King renewed.** The true Mercury yields the fixed Sulphur and the Universal Medicine. In the royal bath the King is made young again.
   - "you will possess the true Mercury of the philosophers, which will give you the fixed Sulphur and the Universal Medicine" (¶16)
   - Also: "the mysterious fountain where the King bathes himself, is made young again, and retakes a new life exempt from all sorts of infirmities" (¶16).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

6. **The finished medicine.** It is a liquid, fiery, shining, subtle substance, too strong to proportion, which only its own glass can hold. Reduced to a dust, it is the portable "Arabian Elixir".
   - "a liquid fiery spiritual substance, shining like the sun" (¶22)
   - Also: "it is such a subtle moist fire that there is nothing bit (its proper) glass that will hold it" (¶22); "By this means they reduced their medicine to a dust, and this dust is the Arabian Elixir" (¶22).
   - stated; QUOTED: Vaughan.

7. **The fermented Stone adapts itself to all things.** Once fermented, the Stone can take on any nature and act on it, as a man united with God can do what he wills because he wills what God wills.
   - "transform itself into what it wills, and works for the diverse natures of all things, coequals itself to all" (¶27)
   - Also: "as man, united with God, becomes divinely empowered, and therefore can do what he wills" (¶27).
   - stated; ATWOOD.

8. **Universal medicine and other promised wonders.** The Stone is a balancing universal medicine for body and mind in every kingdom: it makes gold potable, burns perpetually, expels evil spirits, and grants memory and joy. Other writers add endless wealth, better-than-natural gems, and health for the world. Atwood says these claims are not literally true.
   - "It is the universal medium of restoration and preservation" (¶28)
   - Also: "by its own equilibriate virtue expels suffering and every disease whether of mind or body" (¶28); "it draws down sublime memory and prudence" (¶28); "would possess a universal medicine, moreover, capable of curing all diseases" (¶26, Eirenaeus); "even supposing the whole of the assertions to be true (and which literally taken they are not)" (¶26, ATWOOD).
   - stated; QUOTED: Khunrath, Author of the Rosary, Eirenaeus; qualified by ATWOOD.

9. **The real object: "the substance" of gold, then the Light.** The philosophers sought not metal but "the substance of these things", and once they had it they despised it beside the vision of the Light.
   - "which was not gold however, or silver, but the substance of these things" (¶26)
   - Also: "was despised and swallowed up in the vision of a yet more alluring prospect of the Light" (¶26).
   - stated (neither term defined here); ATWOOD.

10. **Vital rectification, and the power to heal others.** Light's chief benefit to man is inward, "the vital rectification", and whoever has received it can pass it on.
    - "Nor in the outward ministration of the medicament only, but above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification" (¶30)
    - Also: "bestow of this exuberant felicity for the benefaction of others" (¶32).
    - stated (term undefined; see Procedure 23); ATWOOD.

11. **Matter redeemed; a heavenly body from an earthly.** Matter is freed from the curse and raised from the dead, and an earthly body is made heavenly.
    - "to redeem matter from the curse, and to raise it from the dead" (¶38)
    - Also: "a heavenly body out of an earthly" (¶38).
    - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed).

12. **Divine Wisdom, with its physical tokens.** Perfecting the work brings Divine Wisdom, with wealth, power and health at command and a promise of immortality. The physical results are a "type" foreshowing human perfection.
    - "this inestimable gift of Divine Wisdom" (¶48)
    - Also: "having the means of abundant wealth, power, and health at their command" (¶48); "human perfectibility, and a prospect of a happy immortality set before them in plain physical type and experience" (¶48).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

13. **The seeker transmuted.** The higher objects purify and strengthen the seeker as they draw him and, once attained, transform him into the Harmony and Beauty he had dimly loved.
    - "purifying and corroborating as they draw, will, when at length they are worthily won, untie with and transmute their worshipper into that Harmony and Beauty" (¶50)
    - stated; ATWOOD.

14. **The circle closed: the true Christian Philosopher's Stone.** The method's end is the return of the line into its beginning, a union outside time. This is the true Philosopher's Stone, an epitome of the universe, in which the "Ethereal Hypostasis revolves in its First Cause". Palingenius' Ethereal Stone (our translation) may be the same thing (not stated; our translation).
    - "The line returns to form a circle into its’ beginning, and they join not in time, for their union is Eternity." (¶62)
    - Also: "This is the work --- this is the Hermetic method and its end" (¶62); "This, reader, is the true Christian Philosopher’s Stone" (¶62); "Fingendi Lepidem Aetherium" (¶55; ours: of fashioning the Ethereal Stone).
    - stated (identity of the Stone with method and end); interpreted (the line as the self returning to its source, from ¶58, ¶60); ATWOOD; QUOTED: Palingenius.

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **Nothing in the subject is to be thrown away.** Even the residue and impurities are turned into essence by the fire; the power is complete only when "turned into earth".
   - "he who pretends to separate anything from our subject knows nothing of philosophy" (¶7)
   - Also: "for in our subject there is nothing which is not good" (¶7); "But Hermes says that power of it is not integral until it be turned into earth" (¶7).
   - stated; QUOTED: John Pontanus (endorsed by Eudoxus), Hermes.

2. **Fixed and volatile must act on each other.** Each volatilizes and fixes the other until both reach perfect fixity, as earth and moisture each need the other to bear fruit.
   - "which, acting on one another, are volatilized and fixed reciprocally into a perfect Fixity" (¶9)
   - Also: "the earth will never produce fruit if it be not penetrated with its humidity, and that the humidity would always remain barren if it were not retained and fixed by the dryness of the earth" (¶9).
   - stated; QUOTED: Geber (endorsed), Eudoxus.

3. **Nature's laws are the rules.** The practice follows the laws of Nature; the operator meditates on what Nature can do when Art assists her.
   - "The laws of Nature are the rules you ought to follow in the practice of our magistery." (¶14)
   - Also: "and on what she may be able to do when she is assisted by the help of our Art" (¶16).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

4. **The two keys: the matter and the fire.** Knowing the one matter and understanding the secret yet natural fire together give the Keys of the Art.
   - "if you know the Stone which is the only matter of Our Stone, and if you have the Understanding of Our Fire, which is both secret and natural, you have the Keys of the Art" (¶16)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

5. **Natural fire becomes body in what is akin to it.** This is why philosophic calcination increases humidity.
   - "the igneous spirit of the natural fire is corporified in the substances which are analogous to it" (¶16)
   - stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

6. **Multiplication by repetition.** Each round of dissolving and coagulating raises the Stone's power; like fire, it increases itself.
   - "At first it is of virtue very small, Compared with the might it doth attain By oft reiteration" (¶25)
   - Also: "the tinging virtue will be augmented each time by ten degrees in perfection, which may be reiterated as often as one pleases" (¶24).
   - stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus, Author of Lucerna Salis.

7. **One universal law of Light; the magistery as a type of the Creed.** The Stone shows the universal law of Light at every stage, so the work corresponds to the Gospel. Metallic perfection belongs only to life's first level and is not the same as final union with God.
   - "their Stone exhibits, alike in its origin, development and artificial preparation throughout, the Universal Law of Light in Nature" (¶27)
   - Also: "the metalline perfection, which belongs to the primary evolution of life only" (¶27); "their magistery not only corresponds, but is in every deed a type, and promise, and foundation of our Christian Creed" (¶27).
   - stated; ATWOOD.

8. **Only Light multiplies.** Nothing but Light can transmute or multiply by assimilation.
   - "What else but Light can multiply, or does multiply, anyhow or anywhere" (¶30)
   - stated; ATWOOD.

9. **Health is equilibrium, given from an inner unity.** This matches the Stone's "equilibriate virtue".
   - "health is a certain symmetry or equilibrium of nature, which it is the pre-eminent power of the Telestic art to impart, ordering all things by the unity within" (¶30)
   - Also: "by its own equilibriate virtue" (¶28).
   - stated; QUOTED: Julian, Khunrath.

10. **One parallel process in soul and nature.** The work on matter and the regeneration of man follow one parallel process, differing in degree.
    - "The bruising of the serpent’s head is done spiritually and corporally, both in nature and in the soul, and though in different degrees, yet by a parallel process in each." (¶38)
    - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed; n16 "Of the Analogy in the Process of the Phil. Work with the Redemption of Man").

11. **The whole work: two natures reduced to one.** In summary, the whole work restores the original unity of two natures.
    - "That Two Natures be reduced to One, as they were at the beginning." (¶38)
    - Also: "Herein the Philosophers’ Stone or tincture lyeth." (¶38).
    - stated; QUOTED: the theosophist (endorsed; possibly Atwood's conclusion).

12. **Mesmerism is the first step only.** Mechanical Mesmerism reaches only the threshold of the temple of Divine Wisdom; the ancients entered it by a more scientific Handicraft.
    - "Mesmerism, as it is mechanically practiced in the present day, is a first step indeed, and this only before the entrance of that glorious temple of Divine Wisdom" (¶37)
    - Also: "a more scientific Handicraft enabled the ancients experimentally to enter" (¶37).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

13. **Success depends on discovering the true Intention.** The true Intention is latent in every mind but clear in few, which is why few succeed.
    - "The success of the experiment, as we have repeatedly shown, depends on the discovery of the true Intention." (¶54)
    - Also: "And this being more or less involved in every mind, is not clearly distinguishable in all" (¶54).
    - stated (Intention not defined here); ATWOOD.

14. **Wisdom works by her own Rule toward the Divine purpose.** The work fulfils itself when it serves the Divine purpose and fails when it serves private ends.
    - "The Spirit of Wisdom runs lovingly by her own Rule to fulfill it" (¶56)
    - stated; ATWOOD.

15. **Immortality only through union and surrender of self-will.** Immortality is reached only by giving up the divided self and its will to the Divine Will.
    - "For immortality is only to be consummated in union, and in the abandonment of the dual selfhood and its volition to the Divine Will." (¶58)
    - Also: "Wherefore I abhor myself, cries Jacob (when all else being deprived, he renounced himself), and repent in dust and ashes." (¶58).
    - stated; ATWOOD.

16. **Read veiled texts as veiled; the literal sense is not true.** Scripture is mystical throughout, and the alchemical promises are false taken literally.
    - "Clausum est Armarium --- the Scripture is obscure and mystical throughout" (¶46)
    - Also: "(and which literally taken they are not)" (¶26, ATWOOD).
    - stated; QUOTED: Agrippa; ATWOOD.

17. **Deliberate concealment.** Eudoxus withholds the mysteries from the profane; Atwood says she has reserved some things on purpose.
    - "others we have designedly reserved, lest falling into incapable hands, the highest trust of Nature should be betrayed" (¶54)
    - Also: "to hide the mysteries from the profane which are only intended for the wise" (¶16, Eudoxus).
    - stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Eudoxus.

## Open questions

1. **Mapping of Salt/Sulphur/Mercury to spirit/soul/body (¶7).** (a) In order: Salt = spirit, Sulphur = soul, Mercury = body. (b) Equated only as sets. (c) Sulphur = spirit, Mercury = body, following ¶3 "of the spirit with the body -- of sulphur with mercury". Against (a): ¶7 ties the Fixed Salt to the earth/body ("extract out of it the Fixed Salt").

2. **Is the "seed" of the First Key the Sulphur (¶3)?** It is the Sulphur released from its "dark prisons", or a distinct thing extracted in the same operation. Successive clauses; no identity asserted.

3. **What dissolves the Elements: "this Nature wholly Divine" (¶5)?** (a) The Secret Fire, which dissolves the Stone "naturally" (¶5). (b) The Water (Wine, Water of Life), just mentioned, which ¶8 calls "divine water" and "Divine Liquor".

4. **"the rays of the Sun and Moon" (¶5).** Literal sunlight and moonlight, or figurative names for agents not explained. ¶3 warns of "equivocal terms"; ¶10 makes Sun and Moon what is washed in the spirits' Bath.

5. **Is the Red of Magic Fire (¶10), the secret fire of the wise (¶16) and "Our Fire" (¶7, ¶16, ¶21) one agent?** For: both ¶10 and ¶16 have a fire making liquid flow from the Stone. Against: no identity is stated, and Sendivogius distinguishes the "fire of putrefaction and generation" from the first operation's fire (¶21).

6. **Which Key belongs to which Work?** ¶5 places the Second Key in "the First Work"; ¶12 makes the Fourth Key "the entrance to the Second Work"; ¶14 puts the Fifth in the Third. But ¶9, inside the Third Key, says the reiterated affusion "makes it fit to be fixed, which is the end of the second Work", and says "in the first work purify the Serpent". (a) The Third Key straddles the Works. (b) "Work" is used loosely. ¶3 warns that writers "speak of one work" while treating another.

7. **"power of it is not integral until it be turned into earth" (¶7; cf. ¶12).** "It" is (a) the Stone/compound, whose power needs its earth kept, or (b) the Water/spirit, complete only when fixed as earth. ¶12 supports (b) for the Fourth Key ("our mercurial water shall obtain all the virtues which the philosophers attribute to it if it be turned into earth").

8. **"the Chalybes" (¶10).** (a) The Mercury under the name "steel", which receives gold's seed. (b) A separate partner, not identified. The number "eleven" is not explained.

9. **"of the liquor they make two Mercuries" (¶10).** Whether "the liquor" is the Liquor of the Sages named later in ¶10 is not stated.

10. **"the dew of Heaven" (¶12).** (a) The earth's own humidity, re-applied ("moisten it often with its own humidity", next sentence). (b) An external agent.

11. **"the Stone which is the only matter of Our Stone" (¶16).** (a) "Stone" = raw matter, "Our Stone" = product. (b) One Stone named twice for emphasis.

12. **Who speaks in ¶2–16, and the parenthesis in ¶12.** The first-person speaker is presumably Eudoxus (heading; "fifteen years", ¶16), but there are no quotation marks. "(and a reiteration in part and development of the foregoing)" may be Atwood's interpolation.

13. **"menstruum of the world, out of the sphere of the Moon" (¶21).** Whether this is Sendivogius or Atwood's comment is unclear (it follows her "says the wary Artist" interjection). The term is not explained.

14. **Are the "metals" an allusion (¶24, ¶26)?** "(let not the allusion here be misapprehended)" may mean (a) "imperfect and impure metals" stands for something else, which fits ¶26 "literally taken they are not" and "not gold however, or silver", or (b) it only cautions about the purification step. Its voice (Atwood or Lucerna Salis) is unclear. Similarly "looking at home for that which their instinct and proper destiny would otherwise direct them to find abroad" (¶26): (a) the true object is inward or near at hand; (b) the surface sense, telling gold-seekers to keep to ordinary means.

15. **"except indeed out Mercury alone" (¶20).** Probably a misprint for "our Mercury", which would exempt the philosophers' Mercury from loss by impurity; as printed it is obscure.

16. **The vivific Ens and the Universal Spirit (¶29).** "viz." names either (a) the common origin, or (b) the Ens itself, which the following "so often fermented, rectified, and recalcined" favours.

17. **Voice of the ¶29 fermentation sentence ("And this is the work of three days").** Khunrath (n9) or Atwood's own summary. ¶30 "is here said accurately" suggests a citation she endorses. The three days are not explained.

18. **"how much greater benefits will it not bestow on man who nourished it?" (¶30).** "Man" is (a) the operator who nourished the Light in himself, (b) man in general, or (c) loose phrasing. This bears on whether the inner work is the operator's own; ¶32 "that which a man has received" suggests an individual.

19. **Extent of the theosophist's quotation (¶38).** Whether "The whole work therefore consists summarily herein", the Artist passage and "His design being no less than to redeem matter from the curse" are Freher's words or Atwood's conclusion is unclear; n16 stands at the end of ¶38. Also, "Earth must be turned in, and Heaven out" is either the earthly made inward and the heavenly outward (fitting wrath "predominated in outward nature"), or an unspecified physical inversion.

20. **"her Rule" (¶39).** The Divine Light (nearest noun) or Wisdom (the "she" of the preceding Solomon passage). ¶56 "The Spirit of Wisdom runs lovingly by her own Rule" favours Wisdom.

21. **"The Hands of the Wise are very heavy" / horns from the hand (¶39).** (a) The operator's hands literally carry power in practice, in the context of Mesmerism (¶37, ¶36). (b) A general figure for effective action.

22. **Aesculapius "of modern Mesmerism the only just pride" (¶36).** (a) Praise. (b) A limitation: Mesmerism has only a bodily healer, "a demigod only", below the gods who healed the mind. This bears on Atwood's ¶37 claim that Mesmerism is "a first step" only.

23. **"lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring" (¶56).** (a) Technical: the operator's fault passes into the matter and its product (addressed to one whose desire "should lead him to practice"). (b) Moral: inherited guilt ("third or fourth generation"; ¶57 Israelites). Also "Neither break a superficies": a literal caution in practice, or figurative, like "Enlarge not thy destiny".

24. **"To go forth and to return" (¶60–61).** (a) Repeatedly going out into meditation and coming back when the heart fails (next clause). (b) Going forth from and returning to the origin, as the line returning to its beginning (¶62). Also unclear: whether the meditation instruction is the Kabalah's words or Atwood's, and what "the agreement cut off" means.

25. **"These will vindicate the Light, if any, who observing its first dawn, shall suffer it again to fall into disuse" (¶35).** (a) The few defend the Light against those who let it lapse. (b) The few hold accountable those who abandon it.
