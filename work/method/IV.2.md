# Method material — IV.2 Of the Philosophic or Subtle Work

## The subject (what the work is done on)

- **The True Matter must first be found; it hides under countless names and must be known by its own idea and properties.** The chapter does not name it. — "It is a great point to find out the True Matter and proper Subject of this work" (IV.2 ¶6); also: "among a million of pseudonyms and abstruse appellations, whereby the Adepts have chosen to express it" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated; UNCLEAR (Atwood, or the author of The Triumph, whom the next sentence says "adds")*

- **What is transmuted is the component principles of human life: the human self passes out of animal existence toward Reason re-entering its First Cause.** — "Salvation is nothing else but a Transmutation (16) --- of the component principles of life in the circulation" (IV.2 ¶18); also: "a transmigrating of the human Identity out of this animal terrene existence through the ethereal elements of its original fundamentals of nature" (IV.2 ¶18); "to cleanse this leprous life of ours" (IV.2 ¶19). — *stated that salvation is this transmutation (Adept's sentence, completed by ATWOOD); interpreted that "life" here is the subject of the Work (basis: ¶18 completion, ¶19 cleansing "this leprous life of ours", ¶20 Nature reduced "to the Monadic simplicity of her Element")*

- **Nature is the material: she supplies the stuff and is disposed to give up her present life, then reduced to simple unity.** — "Nature, indeed, provides us with the foundations of Wisdom, and materials wherewith to construct her immortal Edifice of Light" (IV.2 ¶14); also: "to purify and dispose Nature to a sacrifice of her last life" (IV.2 ¶6); "neither is Nature reduced to the Monadic simplicity of her Element but by rejection of her Binary conception" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD (¶6 voice UNCLEAR)*

- **The subject already holds its own means of renewal: Water and Spirit are in everything, and each thing carries the seed of its own regeneration.** — "These two are in all things, and each has in himself, as Trismegistus says, the seed of his own regeneration" (IV.2 ¶18); also: "Learn to unite the Principles of our Chaos to a new Life" (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated; QUOTED: the Theosophist / the Adept (speaker boundary unmarked; see Open questions)*

- **The magistery is threefold, twofold and single at once, called the Animal, Vegetable and Mineral Stone.** The "Two" is not explained. — "Our magistery is Three, Two, and One --- The Animal, Vegetable, and Mineral Stone." (IV.2 ¶36). — *stated; QUOTED: Bloomfield*

- **The Smaragdine Tablet's One Thing, from which all things come by adaptation, born of Sun and Moon, carried by Wind, nursed by Earth.** Not decoded; Atwood presents the Tablet as "summarily instructing us" (¶43). — "The Father of it is the Sun, the Mother of it is the Moon, the Wind carried it in its belly; the nurse thereof is the Earth." (IV.2 ¶44); also: "so all things proceeded from this One Thing by adaptation" (IV.2 ¶44). — *interpreted as the subject (basis: ¶43 introduction; ¶44 "the miracles of the One Thing"); QUOTED: Smaragdine Tablet (Thrice Great Hermes), endorsed by ATWOOD*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

- **A single secret Fire is the only agent the Art uses: it purifies Nature and readies her to give up her present life.** What it is in itself is not said. — "the secret Fire of the Wise, which is the only agent granted by Art to purify and dispose Nature to a sacrifice of her last life" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated (function); UNCLEAR voice (as above)*

- **Three fires (natural, unnatural, supernatural) must be lit quickly so none smothers another; a fourth, airy-fiery agent comes over them and completes the work.** The fires are not identified. — "the natural, unnatural, and the supernatural Fires, they should be quickly lighted, says the Adept (2), lest one should put out the other, or that this should stifle that" (IV.2 ¶11); also: "over all which the Fourth, partaking of the aerial fiery element, supervenes for the accomplishment o the work" (IV.2 ¶11). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept (n2: Maieri Symbola Aureae Mensae)*

- **Fire and Azoth suffice.** Azoth is not explained. — "Ignis et Azoth tibi sufficient" (IV.2 ¶13; translation ours: "Fire and Azoth are sufficient for you"). — *stated; QUOTED: Hermes, via Norton*

- **The Water is the dissolving agent; its dissolving of the compound is the key to restoration.** — "For when by the power of the Water the composition is dissolved, it is the Key of the Restoration." (IV.2 ¶14); also: "O blessed watery Form! That dissolveth the Elements!" (IV.2 ¶14). — *stated; QUOTED: the Master (n7: Tractatus Aureus)*

- **The real worker is the Spirit of God brooding in nature; Water and Spirit do the whole work, the Spirit washing its own body with the water until it is immortal.** — "The work is performed by an invisible Artist; for there is a secret incubation of the Spirit of God upon nature" (IV.2 ¶18); also: "The two former principles perform all." (IV.2 ¶18); "The Spirit makes use of the water to purge and wash his body, and he will bring it at last to a celestial immortal constitution." (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept (n16: Lumen de Lumine)*

- **The Salt at the centre of "our loadstone" is the menstruum that can calcine gold; the Pole it turns to holds the heart of the mercury, which is a true Fire.** — "our loadstone contains in its inmost center an abundance of that marvelous Salt, which is that menstruum in the sphere of Saturn mentioned by Eirenaeus, which can calcine gold" (IV.2 ¶30); also: "In this Pole is the heart of the mercury, which is a true Fire, in which its Lord rests" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated (identities); QUOTED: the Adept (n19: Introitus Apertus); where the quotation begins is unmarked*

- **In the joining of three principles, one ("the Dragon") kills and subdues the other two; this is the death the three undergo together.** Which of the three is not said. — "The first one is the Dragon fell, That shall the other twaine both slay and quell" (IV.2 ¶36). — *interpreted (basis: follows "Till they together taste death and live", ¶36); QUOTED: Bloomfield*

- **Four celestial and elemental powers concur as the causes of the product: the highest orb is formal (the workman's mind), the eighth sphere instrumental (his hand), the planet special (a tool), the elements material (the thing made).** — "The First is like to a workman’s mind, The Second like to his hand ye shall finde; The Third is like a good instrument, The remnant like a thing wrought to your Intent" (IV.2 ¶34); also: "The virtue infused resulteth of them all" (IV.2 ¶34). — *stated; QUOTED: Norton (n23)*

- **Divine Light is the agent of the final stage: one ray of the True Light, working from within, cleanses corrupted life; the Lord himself is the initiator who illuminates.** — "one ray of which, intrinsically permeating, is able to cleanse this leprous life of ours, and convert it to the virtue and perfect potency of its Whole" (IV.2 ¶19, ATWOOD); also: "It is the Lord himself who is the Hierophanta. He sets his seal upon the Adepts, whom he illuminates with his beams" (IV.2 ¶16, QUOTED: the Bishop of Alexandria, n12 Clemens Alexandrinus); "For the Light is with Power, and is God." (IV.2 ¶41, QUOTED: Plotinus). — *stated (¶19, ¶41); interpreted that the Bishop's "Hierophanta" applies to the Work (basis: ¶17 "the Mysteries of Antiquity changed their form only to appear more resplendent")*

- **Scriptural images of a final refiner before whom lesser metals melt and are purged, set beside the Master's account without comment.** — "For he is like a Refiner’s Fire, and like fuller’s soap" (IV.2 ¶14, Malachi n9); also: "all these in the presence of the Elect One shall be like a honeycomb before the Fore" (IV.2 ¶14, Book of Enoch n8); "before whose feet all his antecedents fall away, and are consumed" (IV.2 ¶14). — *interpreted (basis: placement directly after the Master's account in ¶14); QUOTED: Malachi; Book of Enoch*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

- **The operator needs deep knowledge of Nature's principles and workings in all three kingdoms; the operation itself is easy, learning it from books is hard.** — "a profound knowledge of the principles and of the operations of Nature in the Three Kinds" (IV.2 ¶4); also: "a recreation and play of children" (IV.2 ¶4); "He must know hys first filosophie" (IV.2 ¶5). — *stated; QUOTED: Eudoxus; Norton (both endorsed by ATWOOD)*

- **The operator must possess the theory of the last stage and reach it by his own reasoning, steering by a guide each sees for himself, not by others' tracks.** The Star is not identified. — "to discover, by a theoretic conduct, the ultimate Art of Life" (IV.2 ¶3); also: "the right road, which the Star alone points out for all and each one by his proper sight beholding it" (IV.2 ¶4); "we ought to conduct ourselves rather by the polar star-light than by any footsteps" (IV.2 ¶4). — *stated (need for theory); interpreted (Star as a self-seen guide; basis ¶4 contrast with "footsteps"); ATWOOD*

- **The building is done by Mind alone, through its own reflective power; only a master can do it.** — "It is the part of Mind alone to represent herself in this way by her own reflective energy, to embody the ethereal Image, and chisel it out in Light." (IV.2 ¶14); also: "it is the work only of a Master mason, of Grand Architects, as the Brethren have it, to erect structures in the air" (IV.2 ¶14); "The task is too onerous for inferior craftsmen." (IV.2 ¶14). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Hands off: the operator only keeps the outward heat steady and does not handle the subject.** — "you must only see that the outward heat fails not, but with the subject itself you have no more to do than the mother with child that is in her womb" (IV.2 ¶18); also: "Proceed then patiently but not manually." (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept*

- **Self-denial: union is reached in theory only by removing the self; this matches Nature's rejection of her Binary in practice.** — "For as the absolute Identification in theory is not conceived but by self-ablation and avoidance" (IV.2 ¶20); also: "Non fit ad monadis simplicitatem reductio, nisi rejiciatur prius a teipso devitatio et tui abnegatio." (IV.2 ¶20; Atwood's own gloss is the sentence just quoted). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Humility and faith bring Christ forth anew in the regenerate person.** — "when Faith, by humiliation under the exemplar cross of Christ, brought Him forth anew in each regenerate life" (IV.2 ¶17). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Purity and likeness: the operator must make himself conformable in every respect to the God he would receive; only the pure can bear the God of Light.** — "thou must prepare thyself till thou art conformable to Him whom thou wouldest entertain, and that in every way of similitude" (IV.2 ¶39); also: "Fit thy roof to thy God in all thou canst" (IV.2 ¶39); "Apollo’s eyes endure None but the good, the perfect, and the pure." (IV.2 ¶38, Callimachus). — *stated (¶39); interpreted for Callimachus (basis: placement between ¶37 and ¶39); QUOTED: the philosopher (unnamed); Callimachus*

- **Preparation is not enough: the divine guest must be invited, by persistent entreaty, tears and sighs.** — "when thou hast set thy house in order, think not that thy guest will come without invitation" (IV.2 ¶39); also: "Perpetual knockings at his doore, Teares sullying his transparent rooms, Sighs upon sighs, weepmore and more, He comes." (IV.2 ¶40). — *stated; QUOTED: the philosopher (unnamed)*

- **For the final knowledge the operator must be still: thinking stops and the senses rest.** — "for the proper reception of divinity, quietude and a cessation of mental energy are becoming and necessary to the consummating knowledge of the First Cause" (IV.2 ¶33); also: "Be silent, repress your intellect, and do not speak." (IV.2 ¶33, Ulysses); "a most Divine Silence, and a rest of all the senses" (IV.2 ¶33, Trismegistus). — *stated; ATWOOD (drawing on Homer, endorsing Trismegistus)*

- **The operator must time the work by an astrological election (fiery ascendant and its Lord fortunate; Moon fortunate for the white Work; Fourth and sixth houses fortunate), and his own nativity can defeat it.** — "Whereof concord most kindly convenient Is a direct and Fiery ascendant" (IV.2 ¶34); also: "For the white Worke make fortunate the Moone" (IV.2 ¶34); "Unless that your nativity pretend infection, In contrariety of this Election" (IV.2 ¶34). — *stated; QUOTED: Norton (n23); Atwood does not comment*

- **The operator must work wisely.** — "Thus he who can work wisely, Shall attain unto our Maistery" (IV.2 ¶36). — *stated; QUOTED: Bloomfield*

- **Seek only the First Cause.** (See Impediments for the consequence.) — "whosoever in any other Light or Form of Light should look for the First Cause, or for any other Final Cause in this, except the First, would seek contrary to reason" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

1. **The Work has three successive operations, causally linked, which are degrees of the Spirit's passage from conception to manifestation (parallel to Nature's three kingdoms).** — "these three operations, which are in fact so many degrees through which the Spirit passes from conception to manifestation" (IV.2 ¶3); also: "a threefold order of legitimate operation and a relation of Causal sequences" (IV.2 ¶3). — *stated; ATWOOD*

2. **The "fixed principles" of the Art: transform the Elements, separate and recombine the Three Principles, make a white then a citrine Mercury, fix it and feed it its own blood until it becomes fixed Sulphur, the Stone.** The Three Principles and "its own blood" are not glossed. — "you must make the conversion of the Elements, the separation and the reunion of the Three Principles" (IV.2 ¶6); also: "a white Mercury and a citrine Mercury" (IV.2 ¶6); "you must fix this Mercury and nourish it with its own blood, to the end that it may be converted into the fixed Sulphur, which is the Stone of Philosophers" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated; QUOTED: author of The Triumph, endorsed by ATWOOD ("These are the fixed principles of the hermetic Art")*

3. **Death and revival of Nature: the secret Fire disposes Nature to give up her last life; a Divine Law then revives her by a cyclical course; the fluid Quintessence is made fixed and fireproof, and by turning its body over completely the hidden Light is woken.** — "convert and congeal the new-born Quintessence or mercurial water into an incombustible fixed unguent" (IV.2 ¶6); also: "the Divine Law that succeeds to animate her by a revolutionary course" (IV.2 ¶6); "by the entire revolution of its body, to awaken the occult Light to Life" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated (steps); interpreted ("revolutionary course" as cyclical turning, basis ¶6 "entire revolution of its body"); UNCLEAR voice (as above)*

4. **The Isis–Osiris fable (killing and scattering, preservation in a chest on the waters, resurrection stronger) conceals Intellect's art of bringing itself into effect by begetting a supernatural offspring.** Atwood does not map episodes to stages. — "Isis however, recollecting these, preserved them in a chest which floated on the Nilotic waters in safety until the period arrived for a restitution" (IV.2 ¶6); also: "came forth invulnerable from his ashes, and far more powerful than he was before" (IV.2 ¶6). — *stated (general meaning, ¶7); any stage mapping is interpreted (see Open questions); ATWOOD*

5. **Three fires lit quickly, then the Fourth supervenes** (see Agent). Three vessels of decreasing opacity; the third holds the one to be born, wrapped threefold like an embryo, pictured as Horus born after Typhon is beaten. — "the First indeed may be considered to be opaque, the Second less so, and the Third still less so. This last containing truly Him who is to be born" (IV.2 ¶11); also: "as the embryo in the mother is protected with a triple covering and sustained within until mature, even so is the metaphysical offspring said to be involved" (IV.2 ¶11). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept (fires); vessels voice UNCLEAR; Horus comparison ATWOOD*

6. **A water stage comes first and a fire/Light stage after: the forerunner Elias, the artist, baptizes with water before the Divine Light; "whom" is not to be sought until the white medicine is complete; White and Red medicines share one vessel and working until all living things are made dead, then vessel and method change.** — "this is he that was appointed a forerunner, baptizing with the water unto repentance3, who has foreshown all things in his apparition to the wise, whose birth is miraculous in the hypostatic transfiguration, and prior to the Divine Light" (IV.2 ¶12); also: "Whom to seeke it availeth right nought, Till the white medicine be fully wrought." (IV.2 ¶13); "Till all quick things be made dead; When vessels and forme of operation Shall chaunge in matter, figure, and graduation." (IV.2 ¶13); "I indeed baptize you with Water unto repentance" (IV.2 ¶15); "He shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with Fire" (IV.2 ¶15). — *stated (each element); the water-then-fire ordering is interpreted (basis: ¶12 "prior to the Divine Light"; ¶13 white medicine first; ¶15 Water baptism then Holy Ghost and Fire, echoing ¶12's "unto repentance"); QUOTED: the Adepts (n5 Introit. Apertus); Norton (n6); St Matthew (n11)*

7. **The Master's sequence: dissolve the compound with the Water (key of restoration); with this watery soul take up a sulphurous Form and mix it with "our Acetum"; pour fire on the Foliated Sulphur; the Final Cause enters over it and the mortal matter is drawn out; the Son-King is transformed in his Tincture.** Acetum and Foliated Sulphur are not explained. — "Now it behooves us with this watery soul to possess ourselves of a sulphurous Form, and to mingle the same with our Acetum." (IV.2 ¶14); also: "when thou shalt pour forth thy Fire upon the Foliated Sulphur, continues the Master, the boundary of hearts (i.e., the Final Cause) does enter in above it, and is washed in the same, and the mortal matter thereof is extracted" (IV.2 ¶14); "Then is he transformed in his Tincture." (IV.2 ¶14). — *stated; QUOTED: the Master (n7: Tractatus Aureus); gloss author UNCLEAR*

8. **Reduce the threefold to simple unity by means of the fourfold, rejecting the twofold, so that body, soul and spirit rest in Jesus; unite the principles of "our Chaos" to a new life, regenerated by Water and Spirit.** — "reduce the Ternary by the Quaternary, through the rejection of the Binary, to the simplicity of the Monad; that thy body, soul, and spirit be gathered to rest in the name of Jesus" (IV.2 ¶18); also: "Learn to unite the Principles of our Chaos to a new Life, and they will be regenerated by Water and the Spirit." (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated (instruction); interpreted (Ternary = body, soul, spirit; see Open questions); QUOTED: the Theosophist (n14: Khunrath)*

9. **The circulation: instead of letting the pair generate directly, the life is carried upward through a third principle that rejects/corrects it; it is set right, its own will overcome, it dies, and is raised by turning back; passing repeatedly between heaven and earth it gathers the strength of higher and lower.** — "by carrying the circulation upward through a Third principle for reprobation, it is rectified, overcome in its proper volition, and dying (if the divine rule be thenceforth followed), is raised again by reversion" (IV.2 ¶20); also: "through a diligent analysis passing, as it were, from heaven to earth and from earth to heaven, it receives the strength of superiors and of inferiors" (IV.2 ¶20); "Such is the recreant progress of mind, ascending and descending throughout life" (IV.2 ¶30); "It ascends from earth to heaven, and again descends to earth; and receives the strength of the Superiors and of the Inferiors." (IV.2 ¶44, Smaragdine Tablet). — *stated (sequence); interpreted ("reprobation" as rejection/correction, basis ¶20 "rejection of her Binary conception", "rectified"); ATWOOD*

10. **Will and Understanding, once separated and freed, attract each other like steel and magnet; philosophical Heaven and Earth (agent and patient) must be united in love.** — "as steel draws the loadstone and the loadstone in like manner turns toward steel, so is it with the separated principles of Will and Understanding in their freed state" (IV.2 ¶30); also: "the Heaven and Earth philosophical, even as agent and patient, must be united upon the throne of friendship and love" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated; simile voice UNCLEAR (Atwood or the Adept); Heaven/Earth QUOTED: the Adept*

11. **Fixing the volatile: with two gums (our translation) the fugitive is fixed below; the bond is made in three hours.** Unexplained. — "Gummis cum binis fugitivum figit in imis, Horis in trinis vinclat fortia finis." (IV.2 ¶31; translation ours). — *interpreted (rests on our translation alone); QUOTED: Maria (n20)*

12. **Rotary circulation raises the Spirit from a beginning in voluntary poverty through successive spheres; its hidden light grows outward until visible; the Stone, rejected in turn by each builder, becomes the cornerstone.** — "the Spirit goes on to increase and multiply its hidden light outwardly, until its substance, being replenished, it is brought forth to sight" (IV.2 ¶32); also: "Thus exalted by a rotary circulation, as it were, from the lowest to the most perfect form of vitality --- from its beginning in voluntary indigence, through each succeeding sphere of resolute conception" (IV.2 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD*

13. **The Stone is completed in three successive circulations; correspondingly, light grows threefold in the Understanding before a Fourth Form emerges, and the magistery is manifested by a triple turning-inward.** — "as the Stone of the Wise is completed in three successive circulations" (IV.2 ¶33); also: "by a Triplicate growth of Light in the Understanding, becoming consciously allied, It emanates a Fourth Form" (IV.2 ¶18); "made manifest by a triple introversion and multiplication of the hypostatic unit by the circulatory medium throughout life" (IV.2 ¶37). — *stated (each); interpreted that the three passages describe the same triple process (basis: all ATWOOD, all threefold, all culminating in Light/Stone); ATWOOD*

14. **Two sublimations and a double birth (visible and invisible) are needed before the Matter can be altered to the final end;** also sulphurs, three Argent vives and three Salts. Vaughan withholds the explanation. — "a first and second sublimation, of a double nativity, visible and invisible, without which the Matter is not alterable as to our final purpose" (IV.2 ¶35); also: "sulphurs simple and compounded, of three Argent vives and as many Salts" (IV.2 ¶35). — *stated; QUOTED: Vaughan (n24: Lumen de Lumine)*

15. **Bloomfield's sequence: join Fixed, Variable and Fugitive into one until they die and live together; the Dragon slays the other two; about sixty days of blackness; Phoebus appears with strange colours; then rises in full glory.** — "Look that thou join in one persons Three --- The Fixt, the Variable, and the Fugitive --- Till they together taste death and live." (IV.2 ¶36); also: "Three score days long, or neere thereabouts" (IV.2 ¶36); "Then Orient Phoebus in his hemisphere To us full gloriously shall appear" (IV.2 ¶36). — *stated; QUOTED: Bloomfield (n25)*

16. **Final stage: prepare and conform oneself, invite the divine guest, and receive a sudden illumination** (see Operator, Signs). — "This is the way that thoumust walk, in which, if thou dost, thou shalt perceive a sudden illumination" (IV.2 ¶41). — *stated; QUOTED: the philosopher (unnamed)*

17. **Atwood's summary of the method: a subtle analysis and pure synthesis of vital agencies and effects, by which the ancients sought and experimentally found the Word of Life.** — "by such a subtle analysis and pure synthesis of vital agencies and effects, the Word of Life would seem to have been sought after by our ancestors, and experimentally found" (IV.2 ¶43). — *stated; ATWOOD (hedged: "would seem")*

18. **Separate the subtle from the gross, gently and skilfully; the thing's power is complete when turned into Earth.** — "Thou shalt separate the Earth from the fire, the subtle from the gross, gently, with much sagacity." (IV.2 ¶44); also: "The power of it is integral, if it be turned into Earth." (IV.2 ¶44). — *stated; QUOTED: Smaragdine Tablet, endorsed by ATWOOD*

19. **A key practical step is deliberately withheld; six Keys follow for the intelligent inquirer.** — "our willful reservation of an important link in the application of these principles to practice" (IV.2 ¶45). — *stated; ATWOOD*

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

- **A forerunning manifestation appears to the wise before the Divine Light and foreshows everything.** — "who has foreshown all things in his apparition to the wise" (IV.2 ¶12). — *interpreted as a sign of the work (basis: ¶12 "prior to the Divine Light"); QUOTED: the Adepts*

- **After the mortal matter is drawn out: darkness and the waters withdraw, light breaks in, the dead Son lives; the King comes out of the Fire.** The Dragon is not decoded here. — "darkness and the waters flee away. The Dragon shuns the sunbeams which dart through the crevices, our dead Son lives" (IV.2 ¶14); also: "The King comes forth from the Fire, and rejoices with his spouse, and the occult treasury is laid open." (IV.2 ¶14). — *interpreted as signs (basis: sequence after "the mortal matter thereof is extracted", ¶14); QUOTED: the Master*

- **Blackness, about sixty days, then strange colours, then full brightness.** — "The Sun and Moon shall lose their light, And in mourning sable, they shall seem dight, Three score days long, or neere thereabouts" (IV.2 ¶36); also: "Then shall Phoebus appear first out, With strange colours in all the Firmament, Then our joy is coming and at Hand present" (IV.2 ¶36). — *stated (sequence); "mourning sable" as blackness interpreted; QUOTED: Bloomfield*

- **The magnet's centre turns to the Pole, where the virtue strengthens; a visible "Sign of Omnipotence" on the extravasated Central Pole, which fools disregard.** Unexplained. — "though they should see the Central Pole extravasated and bearing the notable Sign of Omnipotence" (IV.2 ¶30); also: "This center turns naturally towards the Pole where the virtue of the steel is gradually strengthened." (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated (image); meaning not given; QUOTED: the Adept*

- **The Spirit's hidden light becomes visible** (procedure 12). — "until its substance, being replenished, it is brought forth to sight" (IV.2 ¶32). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Divine presence shows as radiance and a sudden inner light, followed (per the Latin) by fire, wind, power, knowledge and a sound mind.** — "thou shalt perceive a sudden illumination" (IV.2 ¶41); also: "Eritque in te cum Lumine, Ignis; cum Igne, Ventus; cum Vento, Potestas; cum Potestas, Scientia; cum Scientia, sanae mentis integritas" (IV.2 ¶41; translation ours: "with the Light, Fire; with the Fire, Wind; with the Wind, Power; with Power, Knowledge; with Knowledge, the integrity of a sound mind"); "it is requisite to believe that we have seen Him, says Plotinus, when the Soul receives a sudden Light" (IV.2 ¶41); "appear to rise in radiance as if on fire. Certainly some one of the gods is present" (IV.2 ¶33, Odyssey). — *stated; the fivefold sequence rests on our translation; QUOTED: the philosopher (n27: Anima Magia Abscond.); Plotinus; Homer (Atwood draws the silence lesson from it)*

- **The hymn's signs that the God of Light is coming.** — "See! The glad sign the Delian Palm hath given, Sudden it bends; and hovering in the heaven, Soft sings the Swan with melody divine." (IV.2 ¶38). — *interpreted as bearing on the work (basis: placement after ¶36–37); QUOTED: Callimachus*

## Impediments and dangers

- **The Adepts deliberately confused the three operations to hide them from the profane; the tradition of practice is inconsistent; Atwood keeps the same reserve.** — "reserved strictly under the Master Key of their Dilemma, in order that the mysteries of this most venerable science might not be discovered to the profane" (IV.2 ¶3); also: "The willful confusion of the Hermetic doctrine has doubtless checked many aspirants" (IV.2 ¶4); "a track in the sands easily changeable" (IV.2 ¶4). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Even with the preliminary work, the true Matter and the means of purification, many failed for lack of the theory of the final stage.** — "many even with a perfect knowledge of the preliminary work, and having the true Matter in Hand also and means of purification, are said to have faltered in defect of the ultimate theory whereon to proceed" (IV.2 ¶4). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The fires, if not lit quickly, quench one another.** — "lest one should put out the other, or that this should stifle that" (IV.2 ¶11). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept*

- **Aiming at any end but the First Cause bears only the fruit of the Fall.** — "nothing else is worth seeking, or can terminate in good, but will be the fruit of the Fall only" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Letting the pair (Duad) generate directly gives an imperfect, unbalanced offspring; hence the Binary must be rejected.** — "if in the Duad the divine Idea were suffered immediately to bring forth, an imperfect offspring would result, and discordant by predominance of either generating extreme, as in this life is manifest" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Fools overlook the decisive sign.** — "fools and the ignorant will hold it for a small things, nor yet learn Wisdom" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept*

- **Contrary astrology: malefic aspects, great impediments, or an opposing nativity spoil the election.** — "Keeping th’ aspect of shrews them fro’" (IV.2 ¶34); also: "Save all them well from great impediments" (IV.2 ¶34); "Unless that your nativity pretend infection, In contrariety of this Election" (IV.2 ¶34). — *stated; QUOTED: Norton*

- **The impure and profane cannot bear the God's presence.** — "Far, ye profane, far off with beauteous feet" (IV.2 ¶38). — *interpreted (as above); QUOTED: Callimachus*

## Principles (general laws of the work)

- **The Work is threefold as Nature is.** — "As there are three reigns or grand distinctive distributions of the kingdoms of Nature, so we are informed that in the Philosophic Work, preceding her, there is a threefold order" (IV.2 ¶3). — *stated; ATWOOD (reporting "we are informed")*

- **Intellect makes itself effective in nature by begetting a supernatural offspring.** — "the methodical art of the same subtle Antecedent to bring itself, by begetting a supernatural offspring, into natural effect" (IV.2 ¶7). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Causal order of the Work: the First Cause (essence) becomes active in the Third (source of motion), passes through the Second (matter) into the Fourth (the end, the Good).** — "the speculative Motive of the first Cause is finally produced in reversionary order from the Third, in whom it becomes efficient, by the Second into the Fourth" (IV.2 ¶8). — *interpreted (numbering taken from ¶7's list of causes); ATWOOD*

- **Four degrees graded against the senses: one where sense rules, one matched to it, one exceeding and harming it, one destroying it.** — "Primus dicatur in quo sensus dominatur." (IV.2 ¶10); also: "Destructor sensus nescit procedere Quarto." (IV.2 ¶10). — *interpreted (translation ours); QUOTED: unattributed Latin verse, introduced by ATWOOD as "the universal principles" (¶9)*

- **The ancient Mysteries continue, brighter, in Christian regeneration.** — "the Mysteries of Antiquity changed their form only to appear more resplendent when Christianity came to be the prevailing religion" (IV.2 ¶17). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Salvation is transmutation.** — "Salvation is nothing else but a Transmutation" (IV.2 ¶18). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept, completed by ATWOOD*

- **Intermediates are needed for generation and discarded at completion.** — "though intermediates apply themselves naturally for the generation, they are rejected in the accomplishment" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **Theory and practice correspond: self-ablation in the knower = rejection of the Binary in Nature.** — "so, practically, neither is Nature reduced to the Monadic simplicity of her Element but by rejection of her Binary conception" (IV.2 ¶20). — *stated; ATWOOD*

- **The Hermetic Riddle and the Chaldaic oracle describe the same order: Monad, Duad, ruling Triad, and from the Matrix a "Prester", the Flower of glowing Fire.** — "The First all things in all gave the First Second all things in all, from the all in the First Second to the Third all; that he might discover, know, and possess all things universally." (IV.2 ¶20); also: "which the Chaldaic oracle no less orderly pursues" (IV.2 ¶21); "For in the whole world shineth the Triad over which the Monad rules." (IV.2 ¶22); "Whence is extracted a Prester the Flower of glowing Fire" (IV.2 ¶28). — *stated that the oracle pursues the same Art (ATWOOD, hedged "it would seem"); QUOTED: Hermetic Riddle; Chaldaic oracle (n18). The Riddle reads as an English rendering of the Khunrath epigraph (¶2): interpreted, by comparison of the two passages*

- **The mind's ascending and descending has an exact mechanism, simple yet hard for common sense: Mercury, Sulphur and an immortal Salt.** — "where there is an exact machinery to be observed, a method which, though simple, is difficult for common sense to conceive aright" (IV.2 ¶30); also: "wheel within wheel revolving vitally, Mercury, Sulphur, and an immortal Salt" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated (apposition); no faculty-by-faculty mapping given; ATWOOD*

- **The work is modelled on the creation in Genesis.** — "their work may be likened to the creation of the world" (IV.2 ¶30). — *stated; QUOTED: the Adept / "the Wise"*

- **The Stone's elements, being simplest, respond most to celestial influence.** — "Nothing in erth hath more simplicitie, Than th’ elements of our Stone woll be, Wherefore their, being in worke if generation, Have most obedience to constellation" (IV.2 ¶34). — *stated; QUOTED: Norton*

- **Three receivers in the person are answered by givers.** Not identified. — "Thou hast three that are to receive, and there be here accordingly that give." (IV.2 ¶39). — *stated; QUOTED: the philosopher*

- **Only what is itself part of God perceives God.** — "Nec Sentire Deum nisi qui pars ipse Dei sit." (IV.2 ¶42; translation ours). — *interpreted (rests on our translation); UNCLEAR voice*

- **As above, so below.** — "That which is above is as that which is below, and that which is below is as that which is above, for performing the miracles of the One Thing." (IV.2 ¶44). — *stated; QUOTED: Smaragdine Tablet*

## Open questions

- **Who states the ¶6 requirements (True Matter, secret Fire, Quintessence)?** (a) Atwood herself; (b) already the author of The Triumph (or Eudoxus), continued by "adds". Basis: "And to effect this, moreover, adds our author of The Triumph" (IV.2 ¶6) implies a prior statement by that author, but no quotation marks precede it; ¶4–5 quote Eudoxus and Norton.

- **Does the Osiris fable map onto stages?** (a) Scattering = dissolution, chest on the waters = preservation in the watery stage, restitution = revival (cf. the dead Son living, ¶14; Horus "when, Typhon being vanquished", ¶11); (b) Atwood intends only the general meaning she states in ¶7.

- **What does the ¶10 Latin verse grade?** (a) The four fires (natural, unnatural, supernatural, the Fourth), since ¶11 continues "the operation of the these, viz., of the natural, unnatural, and the supernatural Fires"; (b) the four causes / "universal principles" in general (¶9). Rests on our translation.

- **Which "Fourth", and are they one?** The chapter has: the Fourth cause, the Good (¶7–8); the aerial-fiery Fourth over the fires (¶11); "the form of the Fourth in that Burning Fiery Furnace", conceivable "only from what is written" (¶12); the "Fourth Form" emanated after a threefold growth of Light (¶18); "And the Fourth, as the artist, applies it to the work in the furtherance" and "the greatest Handicraft of all" (¶32). The text never asserts these are the same. Readings for ¶32: (a) the Fourth Form of ¶18; (b) the human operator. A recurring three-then-fourth pattern is evident; its identity is not stated.

- **Who is "Whom" in Norton's verse (¶13)?** (a) Elias the artist (¶12, immediately before); (b) the Fourth in the Furnace / the one to be born (¶11–12); (c) something in Norton's original context not quoted.

- **Who wrote "(i.e., the Final Cause)" (¶14)?** (a) Atwood's insertion, matching her four-cause scheme (¶7–8); (b) the Master's own text.

- **What are the Ternary and Quaternary of ¶18?** (a) Ternary = body, soul, spirit (purpose clause); (b) Ternary = the divine Ternarius and Quaternary = the four elements (incarnation passage later in ¶18). Also, the boundary between the Theosophist (n14, Khunrath) and the Adept (n16, Lumen de Lumine) is unmarked; ¶35 n24 calls Lumen de Lumine "Vaughan's".

- **What is the "Third principle" of ¶20?** (a) A judging/rejecting principle that prevents the Duad's direct offspring; (b) the third of the three circulations (¶33).

- **Is the steel–loadstone = Will–Understanding gloss Atwood's or the Adept's (¶30)?** No quotation marks; "adds the Adept" appears only later.

- **First, Second, Fourth in ¶32.** "fives" may be a misprint (e.g. "gives"); no Third is named. Readings: (a) successive principles or stages of the circulation; (b) the "builders" who each reject the Stone; (c) an echo of "The First", "the First Second", "the Third all" of the Hermetic Riddle (¶20).

- **Do Maria's "three hours" (¶31) correspond to the three circulations (¶33)?** Conjecture only; rests on our translation.

- **Which of the three is the Dragon (¶36)?** (a) the Fixt, listed first; (b) unspecified, first in operation.

- **Is Bloomfield's Phoebus (¶36) a sign in the matter or the divine Light?** (a) A visible stage after the blackness; (b) the God of Light whose coming ¶37–41 describe (¶37 defines the magistery as Light; ¶38 "Bright Phoebus comes"; "The God of Light").

- **Who is "the philosopher" who "continues" in ¶39?** (a) Vaughan (¶35); (b) the author of Anima Magia Abscond. (n27); (c) an earlier speaker.

- **What does "That" in ¶43 cover?** (a) The divine illumination of ¶39–42 alone; (b) the whole sequence of the chapter. Basis: it follows ¶41–42 directly, but ¶43 also speaks of "analysis and pure synthesis of vital agencies", recalling the operative stages. This decides whether the Work's sum is stated to be illumination.

- **Physical operation or inner process?** The chapter gives laboratory and astrological language (vessels, fires, heat, sixty days, election: ¶11, ¶13, ¶18, ¶34, ¶36) alongside explicitly mental and spiritual language (Mind's "reflective energy", ¶14; "progress of mind", "Will and Understanding", ¶30; "Light in the Understanding", ¶18; quietude, ¶33; illumination, ¶41). Atwood's own definitions (¶18, ¶20, ¶37, ¶43) are in the second register; she does not say how the first relates to it, and withholds "an important link in the application of these principles to practice" (¶45).

- **Is there a water stage before a fire/Light stage?** Suggested by ¶12 (forerunner "baptizing with the water", "prior to the Divine Light"), ¶13 (white medicine first; vessels change after "all quick things be made dead"), ¶14 (Water dissolves, then fire on the Sulphur) and ¶15 (Water baptism, then "the Holy Ghost and with Fire"). Not stated as a scheme.

- **Thesaurum absconditum (¶34):** (a) the Lord of the Fourth House; (b) the Fourth House itself.
