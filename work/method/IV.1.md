# Method material — IV.1 Of the Vital Purification, Commonly Called the Gross Work

Format of each point: plain meaning; verbatim expression(s) with refs; label (status; voice).

## The subject (what the work is done on)

1. **The Philosophic Subject, whose hidden nature handwork brings out.** The first practical step is preparing "the Philosophic Subject"; operating on it by hand brings its latent nature into view.
   — "the operation of the hands manifests something that is good, and draws the latent and hidden nature outwards, and brings it to light for good" (IV.1 ¶2); also "Preparation of the Philosophic Subject" (IV.1 ¶2).
   — *stated; QUOTED: the Monk Basil (endorsed)*

2. **The medium released by the trance is at first crude.** What the entrancing releases ("the medium", here also "the ether") is in its natural state unstable, unmanifest, full of fantasy and irrational, far below what it becomes after purification. Its "wild affections and impressures" must be removed.
   — "the medium in its natural state is volatile, immanifest, phantastic, irrational, and important, compared with what it subsequently is become" (IV.1 ¶4); also "depriving the ether of its wild affections and impressures" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *stated (that "the ether" here names the same medium is interpreted from the sentence sequence of ¶4); ATWOOD*

3. **"Our Brass" = the occult Nature, to be washed.**
   — "Take the occult Nature, which is our Brass, says Albertus, and wash it that it may be pure and clean" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Albertus (endorsed)*

4. **The subject is "sordid" and weighed down with foreign matter.** It carries many accretions and inborn defects that only full dissolution removes; the Argent vive is at first mixed with "extraneous Sulfurs".
   — "there is such a mass of heterogeneous superfluities adhering to our subject, that nothing short of dissolution can give it rest" (IV.1 ¶7); also "our Sordid Subject" (IV.1 ¶7); "to dissolve and ultimately obliterate its innate defects" (IV.1 ¶5); "this Argent vive was at first gross, unclean, fugitive, being mingled with extraneous Sulfurs" (IV.1 ¶10).
   — *stated; ATWOOD (¶5, ¶7); QUOTED: the Adept (¶10)*

5. **Lunar Vulcan = the first prepared Subject = Diana = the Adepts' secret natural interior Fire.** The Stone is prepared "with the assistance of the Lunar Vulcan". Atwood says this means the subject once first prepared. As "Lunar Caustic" it is roused into action by a stimulus from outside.
   — "the Lunar Vulcan, as we long ago suggested, is meant the first prepared Subject, which is also called Diana, and the secret natural interior Fire of Adepts" (IV.1 ¶29); also "this same Lunar Caustic is brought into act by an exterior excitation" (IV.1 ¶29).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

6. **Khunrath's starting matter: Adrop = Saturn = lead of the Wise; its loosening yields the "true matters" of the Stone.** Relaxing Adrop's coagulation makes Green Duenech and Vitriol of Venus appear, which are the Stone's true matters. A wet, impure "mud earth" then appears, which Khunrath calls the First Matter "of our very selves" and of the Stone.
   — "from Adrop (which in its proper tongue is called Saturn, i.e., the lead of the Wise), our heart of Saturn, the bonds of coagulation being dexterously relaxed" (IV.1 ¶36); "the Green Duenech and the Vitriol of Venus, which are the true matters of the Blessed Stone will appear" (IV.1 ¶36); "the mud earth, wet, humid, stagnant, impure, partaking of Adam, the First Matter of the creation of the Greater World of our very selves and of our potent Stone" (IV.1 ¶36).
   — *stated (the identities); QUOTED: Khunrath (Atwood presents it as instruction on the Gross Work). Whether "of our very selves" makes the human being the subject: see Open questions.*

7. **Vaughan's "Moon of the Firmament" = a middle Nature; Flamel's Solar and Lunar Mercury, one planted in the other.** These are the subject of the vital heat-operation. Neither is explained further.
   — "take the Moon of the Firmament, which is a middle Nature" (IV.1 ¶41); "the Solar and Lunar Mercury, and the plantation of the one in the other" (IV.1 ¶41).
   — *stated (middle Nature); interpreted (that the Mercuries are the subject); QUOTED: Vaughan; Flamel*

8. **The philosophic matter is drawn partly from air and the "defiled breath of vitality".**
   — "the philosophic matter, drawn in part from the heterogeneous air and defiled breath of vitality" (IV.1 ¶46).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **The Universal Element.** The Alchemists' object, unlike chemistry's common elements, is the Universal Element: an invisible vapor that they refine and compress into tangible form.
   — "to rectify the Universal Element, and compress its invisible vapor into a tangible Form" (IV.1 ¶47).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

10. **The riddle worked on is the operator's own darkened understanding.** Atwood glosses solving the Sphinx's riddle as rationally penetrating the darkened essence of one's own natural understanding. She also glosses the purification of "Antimony" as purging the operator's mind (Operator 11). Read together, these make the operator's own mind and life part of what is worked on.
   — "to solve the Enigma of the Sphinx; in other words, to penetrate rationally the darkened essence of his natural understanding" (IV.1 ¶48); also "him who is to enter into the radical dissolution of Life" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *stated (the gloss); interpreted (the operator as subject; basis ¶40, ¶47 "subversion of the selfhood", ¶48); ATWOOD*

## The agent (what does the work: fire, spirit, light, intellect…)

1. **The Hand is the instrument of the First Operation.** The operator's hand passes on the Spirit and, by long continued working (likened to grinding), dissolves the subject's inborn defects. Nature cannot dissolve herself because she has no hands. Atwood says the cited passages apply literally.
   — "the Hand was the instrument employed, not only to impart the Spirit as a natural gift, but by a continual mechanic trituration, as it were, to dissolve and ultimately obliterate its innate defects" (IV.1 ¶5); also "Nature cannot of herself enter into the dissolution, says the author of the Filum Ariadne, because she has no hands" (IV.1 ¶5); "The Hand, says Van Helmont, is the instrument of instruments, which the soul likewise useth, as a means by which it bears its image into operation" (IV.1 ¶5); "The Mercury of the philosophers, says Lully, comes not but by help of ingenuity, and the Manual operations of man" (IV.1 ¶5); "nature is not moved by theory alone, but by sagacious Handicraft and human assistance" (Vaughan, IV.1 ¶5); "the hand is found best able to express and impart what the mind has well premeditated" (IV.1 ¶34).
   — *stated; ATWOOD, with QUOTED: Lully, Vaughan, author of the Filum Ariadne, Van Helmont (endorsed). That the hand works as in mesmeric passes is interpreted (basis ¶4 "a few passes of the hand", "the hand or eye of another mesmerizing").*

2. **The Preparing Spirit dissolves its own body of Light and draws out a tinging Second Spirit.** Atwood remarks that this evidence "runs in advance" of the chapter.
   — "The Preparing Spirit dissolves the body of Light, and cleanses it from the corrupting causes, and extracts a Second Spirit subsisting and tinging in the body" (IV.1 ¶10); also "the Spirit preparing its body and extracting from it the tinging spirit" (IV.1 ¶10).
   — *stated (the description); the referents are unexplained; QUOTED: the Adept*

3. **An "internal agent" lies dormant in the subject and stirring wakes it.** Excessive stimulation wakes it too early (Impediment 7). The Lunar Caustic is likewise "brought into act by an exterior excitation".
   — "lest by too great excitation the internal agent awakening should cause a disseveration in the Chaos" (IV.1 ¶15); "this same Lunar Caustic is brought into act by an exterior excitation" (IV.1 ¶29).
   — *stated (each); linking the two is interpreted; ATWOOD*

4. **Will is the greatest purifying power.** The work aims at purification and the perfecting of life. Once the Divine Will is reconciled, Wisdom carries the aim through, and one's deeds and words "extend their Hands" to help.
   — "Will is the greatest power of purgation" (IV.1 ¶31); also "the Divine Will being conciliated, Wisdom runs lovingly by her own rule to fulfill it" (IV.1 ¶31); "our deeds and discourses extend their Hands, as it were, to assist us in our assent" (IV.1 ¶31).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

5. **Cheiron = Power and Motive in alliance.** It disciplines and directs the boundless "Heroic Fire" and corrects the "Armed Magnet". Guided by it, the Intellect crosses the realm of the senses until consciousness passes into Light. Heroic Fire and Armed Magnet are not explained.
   — "in his double capacity of Power and Motive in alliance, corrects and educates the Heroic Fire, tames and directs its illimitable virtue, and rectifies the Armed Magnet by an infallible rule" (IV.1 ¶33).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

6. **Vulcan = Motion; nothing is made without Motion.** The lame god stands for Motion. Without it the "Fabricate Intellect" is not born, as Pallas was brought forth from Jove with Vulcan's help.
   — "without the instrumentality of Motion, which the lame god personates, the Fabricate Intellect is not born" (IV.1 ¶34); also "thought carries itself by voluntary motion into effect" (IV.1 ¶34).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

7. **The Solution is done by a specific "virtue", not by corrosives.** The loosening comes from a virtue suited to its own object (as the Ostrich digests iron without corrosion), not from general corrosive force.
   — "there is a virtue of loosing the bars and bolts of Tartar" (IV.1 ¶39); also "They err, therefore, who ascribe this single combat only to Corrosives" (IV.1 ¶39); "It is convenient to meditate about this virtue" (IV.1 ¶39).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Van helmont (endorsed as cited for the Solution)*

8. **Fire (Vulcan) alone separates pure from impure; a second Vulcan works in the "Lesser World".** Only Fire separates good from evil, fixed from unfixed, medicine from venom. What the Vulcan of the Greater Orb leaves crude, "a certain other Vulcan" in the Lesser World ripens and purifies; the colors that come from the Fire show this.
   — "Fire only can effect that, and Vulcan is the sole and only master of all these" (IV.1 ¶40); also "Whatsoever the Vulcan in the Greater Orb leaves crude and perfects not, that in the Lesser World must be amended by a certain other Vulcan" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *stated (separation by Fire); which fire the Lesser World's Vulcan is: interpreted, see Open questions; QUOTED: the monk (Basil Valentine), placed by Atwood right after her gloss*

9. **A "fixed" agent reaches the root of a deep-seated disorder.** Purified ("fixed") Antimony goes to the center of fixed disease; unfixed purgers only clear the surface.
   — "Our Antimony, says Basil Valentine, which is fixed, searcheth out fixed diseases and eradicates them; which purgers, not fixed, cannot do" (IV.1 ¶40); also "they strike at the very inmost root of the disease" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *interpreted (applying it to the work rests on Atwood's gloss of Antimony, Operator 11); QUOTED: Basil Valentine*

10. **The fire of the work is a vital, luminous, constant heat, not common fuel.** Vaughan names one "vital" heat-operation, harder than all others. Flamel requires a fire like the sun, of one even degree, not of coals or wood. Nuysement's Latin says the innate heat of a "terrestrial sun" does the warming, not the external celestial sun.
   — "There is one operation of heat, says Vaughan, whose method is vital and far more mysterious than all other" (IV.1 ¶41); "it must not be a fire of coals, nor of any wood, but a bright shining fire like the Sun itself, whose heat must neither be excessive, but always of one and the same degree" (IV.1 ¶41); "Non igitur externus solis coelestis calor est qui profundum terrae calefacit sed potius solis terrestris innatus calor" (IV.1 ¶43).
   — *stated (Vaughan, Flamel); interpreted (Nuysement: rests on our translation only); QUOTED: Vaughan; Flamel; Nuysement*

11. **The "Vulcanic action" both destroys and sustains life.**
   — "Which Vulcanic action, to destroy life and to maintain it" (IV.1 ¶43).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

12. **Mind is the Key.** Once Mind reaches Self Knowledge by inquiry within, "the Efficient" goes outward and acts out its motive, so that what was only speculation is carried out in life.
   — "For Mind is the Key of this Hermetic Enigma, and no sooner does it attain to Self Knowledge, by proper inquiry within, than the Efficient proceeds out wards to image its motive in operation" (IV.1 ¶48).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

13. **The Motive is the true spiritual ferment.** See Principles 9.
   — "Everything depends upon the Motive, which is the true spiritual ferment" (IV.1 ¶44).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

## The operator (requirements: knowledge, moral state, will, guidance, secrecy…)

1. **Patience, toil, skill, attention, honesty about error.** The idle, the slothful, those unfit for handwork and the profane fail. The First Work needs steady effort and concentration, and the Eagles need "sagacity of mind".
   — "patience, toil, skill, unremitting attention, in the execution, and a free will to the discovery of error, without discordant slurring or disguise" (IV.1 ¶5); also "by reason of their indisposition and unaptness for Manual operation" (Crollius, IV.1 ¶6); "they want the spirit of Wisdom, and are not quick of understanding in these things" (IV.1 ¶6); "a concentrated attention is necessary" (Eirenaeus, IV.1 ¶6); "some sagacity of mind" (IV.1 ¶11).
   — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Crollius, Eirenaeus (endorsed)*

2. **Vigilance and no impatience for the end.** Stay watchful and long-suffering. Whoever daily wants to see the end is furthest behind. No one gets wisdom without toil and perseverance.
   — "And in the Gross Worke he is furthest behind, That dayly desireth the end thereof to find." (IV.1 ¶16); also "Men must wake when they desire to winke; For it is labor of watch and paines greate," (IV.1 ¶12); "For hasty men th’ end shall never see." (IV.1 ¶12); "Wisdom is the reward of voluntary and arduous research." (IV.1 ¶25).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal (endorsed); ATWOOD (¶25)*

3. **The work is costly.** The "food" is costly, poor men are warned off, and the operator must spare neither labor nor purse. Whether the cost is literal is not said.
   — "Also the foode is full costly meate. Therefore all poore men beware, says Arnolde," (IV.1 ¶12); also "Who would have trew worke may no labor spare, Neither yet his purse, though he make it full bare;" (IV.1 ¶16); "Neither is it wonderful that so many chemical students were in former times reduced to poverty, since they spared labor, but no expense" (Eirenaeus, IV.1 ¶6).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal / Arnolde; Eirenaeus*

4. **Instruction, assistants, companionship.** The first approach cannot be found from books without instruction. The incessant labor needs relays of helpers ("fresh horses"), chosen servants and capable, honest ministers. The beginner strengthens inclination and faith "in equal companionship and reciprocal benefaction". Eirenaeus promises a guide. Khunrath worked "now in companionship, and now not".
   — "Fresh horses there are verily needed to this Celestial Ploughshare and laborious assistance for a toil that is incessant" (IV.1 ¶9); also "the advice of Norton and his brother Adepts about the choice of servants, their capacities and qualifications" (IV.1 ¶9); "If your ministers be witty and trew, Such shall not need your workes to renew" (IV.1 ¶28); "having increased also his natural store of inclination and faith by practice in equal companionship and reciprocal benefaction" (IV.1 ¶44); "Eirenaeus promises a guide" (IV.1 ¶29); "neither instinct nor reason would probably suggest, without instruction" (IV.1 ¶3).
   — *stated (each); that these are working partners in the manual process is interpreted (basis ¶4 "the hand or eye of another", ¶11 "by hand to hand"); ATWOOD; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal; Khunrath*

5. **Learn by doing, error and comparison.** Khunrath: errors taught what was right; books read against each other explained one another; notes of conversations, experiments and ideas; constant meditation. Nobody is skilled at the start.
   — "What was wrong taught me what was right, from day to day one book throwing light upon another, I was enabled to interpret them." (IV.1 ¶29); also "carefully keeping note of conversations, experiments, and conceptions of my own as well as others" (IV.1 ¶29); "Nemo prima fronte reperitur discretus" (IV.1 ¶26); "Without experience ye may not do well." (IV.1 ¶28).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; Norton's Ordinal (endorsed)*

6. **Avoid things wild and vicious.** Atwood repeats the couplet at the chapter's close, with the requirement of purity "without and within".
   — "He that would seek tincture most specious, Must needly avoid all things wild and vicious;" (IV.1 ¶16; repeated IV.1 ¶49).
   — *stated (the rule); whether it is moral or material is interpreted (¶49 couples it with "pure without and within"); QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal (endorsed)*

7. **Grace and reason.** The Art comes by grace, not by reading, sitting still, fancy or will alone. Atwood: seek grace, and pursue truth within the Spirit's life by persistent reasoning.
   — "But, as I said, by grace it is obtained:" (IV.1 ¶19); also "Not all by reading, nor by long sitting still;" (IV.1 ¶19); "See k grace; and, by importunity of reason, seek for the clue of Truth within the Spirit’s life" (IV.1 ¶20).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Kelly; ATWOOD*

8. **Few are chosen.** Many inquire into the means; few go on to the work.
   — "of the many called to the consultation of means few are chosen to proceed with the Philosophic Work" (IV.1 ¶31).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **Freed from bonds, tested, virtuous; clean hands and a pure heart.** Only someone loosed from his bonds, experienced in the struggle, prepared and virtuous approaches the "mine of Sontes". Atwood adds the Psalm's requirement and calls it apt.
   — "he alone, who, being loosed from his bonds, has known the wars, being fitted forhis journey, a friend to the places and who has virtue" (IV.1 ¶39); also "He that hath clean hands and a pure heart, who hath not given up his soul unto vanity nor sworn deceitfully." (IV.1 ¶39); "We do not quote casually, or because the Scriptural phrase is popular; but because it is apt" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Van helmont; Scripture (endorsed by ATWOOD)*

10. **Propitiate God by prayer and works.** Man can labor but cannot force the Divine Blessing. God is to be propitiated by prayer and also by faithful, charitable works. Desire reaches its object directly by faith, or indirectly as just works breed hope, which kindles into faith and through ecstasy reaches its First Source.
    — "He therefore must be propitiated, not by prayer and supplication alone, but by faithful and charitable works preparing the way before Him" (IV.1 ¶40); also "Desire leads into its object by fait immediately; but mediately, by just works, that hope is engendered which, kindling into faith immediately; by ecstacy, penetrates to its First Source." (IV.1 ¶40).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

11. **Decoding key: "purifying the Antimony" = purging the operator's mind of self-will.** Basil's rule that all venom must be removed from Antimony before it is a medicine means, "in other words", that self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice and wavering must leave the mind of whoever enters "the radical dissolution of Life". Only the single will to rectify and perfect remains. The warfare is not carnal.
    — "all arrogant self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice, and variability of purpose, all but the one voluntary faith to rectify and perfect, be removed from the mind of him who is to enter into the radical dissolution of Life" (IV.1 ¶40); also "all venomous impurity is totally to be taken away from Antimony, before it can either be called a medicine truly or administered with safety" (IV.1 ¶40); "the weapons of this warfare are not carnal" (IV.1 ¶40).
    — *stated; ATWOOD (glossing QUOTED: Basil Valentine)*

12. **Friendship with Vulcan and the "Fiery bath of Love".** Only someone who has befriended the Fire and set up a "Fiery bath of Love" can truly show the separation of venom from medicine. The bath is not explained.
    — "unless he who hath firmly contracted friendship with Vulcan, and instituted the Fiery bath of Love" (IV.1 ¶40).
    — *stated (the requirement; the image is unexplained); QUOTED: the monk (Basil Valentine)*

13. **Rational inquiry into causes; the right intention held from the start.**
    — "Labor to know causes, advises the philosopher; he that seeks rationally finds the true end, not otherwise" (IV.1 ¶44); also "the Artist holding his right intention from the beginning" (IV.1 ¶30).
    — *stated; QUOTED: the philosopher (endorsed); ATWOOD*

## The procedure and its stages (in the order the chapter gives, if it gives one)

**Stage outline.** The chapter gives this sequence. (0) Prerequisites: "preliminary aids" and "a sufficient theory" (¶2), right intention (¶30). (1) Entrancing the senses, which is "a beginning only" (¶3–4). (2) The manual, linear purification by dissolution: the First Operation / First Work (Eirenaeus ¶6, ¶11), the "First Preparation" (Vaughan ¶36), Khunrath's "first act" (¶36). (3) The Second part of the Gross Work / Second Work: the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (¶36). There "hands and intellect are here alike incapable" (¶39); Nature works alone by external fire (Eirenaeus ¶11), "without manual cooperation" (Khunrath ¶36). (4) Only after this does "the Philosophic Work" truly begin (¶48–49).
— *Each element stated; aligning the different authors' divisions with one another is interpreted (see Open questions 1); ATWOOD with cited authorities*

### Stage 1 — Entry: entrancing the senses (a beginning only)

1. **Trance in the senses' own inner light.** The way in is to entrance the senses in their own "medial light". Atwood says recent practice does this by mesmerism (another person's hand or eye) or a passive fixed gaze. Books do not reveal this first approach.
   — "the tractive artifice now made publicly easy of entrancing the senses in their own medial light" (IV.1 ¶3); also "by the hand or eye of another mesmerizing, or by a passive fixed gaze" (IV.1 ¶4); "many ways of including the sensible medium and of unfolding the interior light temporarily" (IV.1 ¶5); "other revolutionary arts, too, by which the human spirit may be involuted and converted to its proper spheres" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *interpreted (that "this" in ¶4 is the entrancing of ¶3 is read from sequence); ATWOOD*

2. **The trance is only the start; purification must follow at once.**
   — "to effect this was, as we have repeatedly shown, a beginning only of the Hermetic art" (IV.1 ¶4); also "did not remain satisfied with a few passes of the hand, or any first phenomena whatever, but they proceeded at once scientifically to purify" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

### Stage 2 — The manual, linear purification (First Operation)

3. **Purify by dissolving the subject in its own vital fluid.** Strip the medium of its disordered impressions by dissolving "the circulating body in its own blood". Arnold gives the parallel formula: dissolve the Stone in its own mercury back to first Matter.
   — "depriving the ether of its wild affections and impressures by a dissolution of the circulating body in its own blood" (IV.1 ¶4); also "The work of philosophers, says Arnold, is to dissolve the Stone in its own mercury, that it may be reduced into its first Matter" (IV.1 ¶7).
   — *interpreted (terms unexplained; that the two formulas are one operation is not asserted); ATWOOD; QUOTED: Arnold (endorsed)*

4. **All operations are one alternation of dissolving and fixing, starting with complete solution.**
   — "the whole of which is nothing else than a successive dissolution and coagulation to make the fixed volatile, and volatile fixed. The beginning of the whole work is a perfect solution" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Albertus (endorsed)*

5. **One way only, Manual = Linear, in one matter and one vessel, throughout.** A single uniform course of dissolution, "decocted linearly", from beginning to end. One kind of vessel, conditions kept uniform, nothing mixed in. Many furnaces are useless without this Method.
   — "for the Purification we read but of one way, called by the Adepts Manual, and their Linear way, which supersedes all other from beginning to end of the Dissolution" (IV.1 ¶5); also "one linear decoction throughout and dissolution by line" (IV.1 ¶3); "there is indeed but one way of working, in one matter, one linear way throughout, one vessel uniform throughout, except removal" (Zachary, IV.1 ¶9); "Use one manner of vessel in matter and in shape, Beware of Commixtion that nothing miscape." (IV.1 ¶28); "To keep Uniformity of all things requisite;" (IV.1 ¶28).
   — *stated (that it is one uniform way); "linear" is not defined beyond this; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Zachary, Norton's Ordinal (endorsed)*

6. **Repeat without tiring; each repetition is a death renewed.** Grind, cook, repeat. The "Celestial Ploughshare" clears "the wasted field of human life", many times over.
   — "Tere, coque, et reitera, et non te taedeat --- Grind, coct, says the wise author of the Rosarium, and reiterate your labor and be not weary" (IV.1 ¶7); also "nor once nor twice; but many times the labor must be repeated, as each dying is renewed into a better life" (IV.1 ¶9); "to clear the wasted field of human life, and harrow it for a more congenial growth" (IV.1 ¶9).
   — *stated; QUOTED: the Rosarium (endorsed); ATWOOD*

7. **The Argent vive is made "pontically", by back-and-forth alternation, hand to hand.** "Pontically" is not explained.
   — "this Argent vive, which is decocted linearly, is generated pontically, as it were by a reciprocal alternation, distributing its advanced virtue by hand to hand" (IV.1 ¶11).
   — *interpreted; ATWOOD*

8. **The First Operation is literal hard labor on the matter as first found; it differs from the Second.**
   — "Neither would I have these things understood figuratively, continues he; I am not speaking here indeed of the commencement of the Supernatural Work, but of things as we first find them" (IV.1 ¶6); also "nothing can be achieved in the First Operation without sweating and much labor" (IV.1 ¶11); "the exact preparation of the philosophic Eagles may be considered the first degree of perfection in this Art" (IV.1 ¶11).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Eirenaeus (endorsed)*

9. **Feed the matter in balance and at leisure; simmer, never boil.** The matter's "eating" and "drinking" are kept even and unhurried, with a "sweate" between. It is kept at a simmer like butter or oil, not boiled like water. Food and drink are not identified.
   — "Eat as it drinketh and drink as it doth eate, And in the meane season, take it a perfect sweate." (IV.1 ¶12); also "Let not your matters eate over hastily. But wisely consume their foode leisurlie." (IV.1 ¶12); "Remember that water will bubble and boyle, But butter must simmer and also oyle;" (IV.1 ¶12).
   — *interpreted; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal (with Avicen, Rasis)*

10. **Trituration slow, gentle, continuous (Festina Lente).** Force does not do it; constant repetition does.
    — "the trituration of the Philosophic Subject, which ought to be slow, gentle, and continuous" (IV.1 ¶13); also "Festina Lente --- Make not too much haste" (IV.1 ¶13); "Gutta cavat lapidem non vi sed saepe cadendo." (IV.1 ¶14); "And so with long leisure it will waste And not with bubbling made in haste" (IV.1 ¶12).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

11. **Give the Matter wings but train it to fly slowly; bind it; release it after its due time.** The Matter is made able to rise. It is then accustomed to this gradually under Mercury and held back, lest it rise too soon and be lost. Only after its due time is it set free, which is the goal.
    — "permit it not to fly suddenly, rather deliver it to Mercury, to be instructed by him gradually to accustom itself to flying; yea, bind it with a cord" (IV.1 ¶15); also "after you have detained it for its due time, loose its bonds towards which all sons of Art aim to arrive as to their long-desired and sought for harbor" (IV.1 ¶15).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: Basil*

12. **Hold down the volatile while it is volatile; draw off its corrupt "redness" repeatedly.** The flying bird is drowned in flight so that it "lives" by not flying off. The watery, corrupt redness and the shadow over the "ray" are drawn off until the redness is pure. Then it is governed "according to Reason and according to the days specified".
    — "Take the flying bird, says Hermes, and drown it flying; and divide and separate it from its redness, which holds it in death" (IV.1 ¶15); also "not by flying away indeed to the region above, but truly by forbearing to fly" (IV.1 ¶15); "Take, my son, this watery corrupted redness, which is, as a live coal holding the fire, which if thou shalt withdraw so often until the redness is made pure" (IV.1 ¶15).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: Hermes*

### The working theory gained inside the practice (Atwood's inward counterpart)

13. **Reflexive analysis: the analyzer analyzes itself, alternately, down to the indivisible Unit of Truth.** The working theory develops only in practice, by rational inquiry into the Light within. The analyzing faculty turns its analysis back on itself and repeats this by alternation. It dissolves the "separable Selfhood" until it reaches an indivisible unity. Atwood calls this rational permeation into Nature's Causal Light.
    — "That which analyzes even must be analyzed; that, returning analytically, it may resolve the separable Selfhood and reiterate the same by alternation until it arrives at the inseparable Unit of Truth." (IV.1 ¶20); also "the way develops itself I the practice by rational inquisition of the Light within" (IV.1 ¶18); "And this is the way of rational permeation, by the Understanding of Nature, into her Causal Light." (IV.1 ¶20).
    — *stated (as Atwood's instruction); how it relates to the manual dissolution (its parallel "reiterate … by alternation") is interpreted; ATWOOD*

14. **Deliberate backward from the end to the first means in one's power; the work starts there.** Fix the right end. Investigate the means, and the means to those means, until one is reached that is directly in your power. That is where inquiry stops and work begins: what is last in analysis is first in execution.
    — "That Medium, therefore, which is last in the analysis is first in generation being proved able to the accomplishment" (IV.1 ¶31); also "till he discovers the first Medium which is immediately in his power, in the discovery of which inquiry terminates, and the work, beginning from thence, passes into accomplishment" (IV.1 ¶31); "neither is it said to be expedient to inquire about Ends so much as about things pertaining to ends" (IV.1 ¶30).
    — *stated; ATWOOD (after Aristotle)*

15. **Mind first, then hand.** In the work the hand carries out what the mind has first thought through. The Dactyli Idaei ("Fingers") laid open metallic veins by "incessant fiery artifice".
    — "in Hermetic works, the hand is found best able to express and impart what the mind has well premeditated" (IV.1 ¶34); also "to exhibit by their incessant fiery artifice the metallic veins therein imbedded" (IV.1 ¶34).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

### Stage 3 — The Second part of the Gross Work: Solution of the Philosophic Salt

16. **The Second Work = the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond).** It is among the Art's greatest subtleties. Agrippa knew the First Preparation but not this. Knowing the Salt and its solution is the secret of the Ancient Sages.
    — "By the Second Work we are to understand, therefore, the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond)" (IV.1 ¶36); also "The Second part of the Gross Work is described by Vaughan as one of the greatest subtleties of the Art" (IV.1 ¶36); "He who knows the Salt and its solution, knows the secret of the Ancient Sages." (IV.1 ¶37).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

17. **Khunrath's first act, done by hands and Fire.** (a) Adrop's coagulation is loosened. (b) The hidden Green Lion is lured out of its "Saturnine Hill", pierced, and its blood collected. (c) The mud earth / First Matter appears. (d) A black "Wine" appears. (e) The bond of soul to body loosens: spirit and soul separate step by step, the fixed becomes volatile, and the impure body dies, blackens and turns to precious Ashes.
    — "by diverse instruments and labors and the various artifice of the Hands and of Fire" (IV.1 ¶36); "The Green Lion, lurking and concealed, is drawn forth from the Cavern of his Saturnine Hill by attractions and allurements suitable to his nature." (IV.1 ¶36); "the bond by which the soul is tied to the body and united together with it into one substance is relaxed and dissolved" (IV.1 ¶36); "the impure body (of the Spirit) from day to day is consumed, is destroyed, dies, blackens, and goes to Ashes" (IV.1 ¶36).
    — *stated (sequence); Green Lion unexplained; QUOTED: Khunrath (presented by Atwood within her closing instruction on the Gross Work)*

18. **Khunrath's Second Operation, by nature alone.** Body, spirit and soul, already washed, purged and recombined with great labor, are kept in one vessel, one furnace and one fire. There they decompose and reunite "without manual cooperation". They then coagulate, take on colors, calcine and fix of themselves. Eirenaeus says the same of his Second: Nature alone, by well-regulated external fire.
    — "rot of themselves and without manual cooperation, by the sole labors of nature, are dissolved, conjoined, and reunited" (IV.1 ¶36); also "In the Second Operation, which takes place in one circular crystalline vessel justly proportioned to the quality of its contents, also in one theosophic cabalistically sealed furnace of Athanor, and by one fire" (IV.1 ¶36); "In the Second, however, Nature alone operates, and without any imposition of hands, by the sole assistance of a well-regulated external fire" (IV.1 ¶11).
    — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; Eirenaeus (endorsed)*

19. **Wrong ways to the Solution.** The common, tedious process (Lully's early practice) and odd methods such as Ripley's alternate heat and cold on his "Red Salt". Lully later did the Solution in nine days, with the secret "from God himself".
    — "in his first practices, he followed the common tedious process which after all is scarcely profitable" (IV.1 ¶36); also "his Art was to expose it to alternate fits of heat and cold, but in this he is singular" (IV.1 ¶36); "he performed the Solution in nine days, and this secret he had from God himself" (IV.1 ¶36).
    — *stated; UNCLEAR voice (Vaughan's report continuing, or Atwood)*

### Late testimonies on the vital heat, circulation and ferment (¶41–48)

20. **Hold the subject exactly balanced in two elements at once.** Every part of the "Moon of the Firmament" is to sit equally in two elements, neither nearer nor farther from either, under a "twofold geometry, natural and artificial". Which elements is not said.
    — "place her so that every part of her may be in two elements at one and the same time; these elements also must equally attend her body; not one further off, nor one nearer than the other" (IV.1 ¶41).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: Vaughan*

21. **Circulation to putrefaction.** Moisture is drawn up from the "earth" and returned to it, again and again. Drawing it off heats and dries; returning it cools and moistens. This goes on until the body decomposes.
    — "Our operation, concludes Morien, is nothing else but extracting water from the earth and returning it again, so long and so often until the earth is completely putrefied" (IV.1 ¶41); also "by elevation of the moisture the body is heated and dried, and by returning it again it is cooled and moistened" (IV.1 ¶41).
    — *stated; QUOTED: Morien*

22. **Two opposed works, each done apart, then reconciled.**
    — "For what one doth concoct t’other will drive away; But if thou canst each work perform apart, And knowest them afterwards to reconcile, Then thou art master of a princely Art" (IV.1 ¶42).
    — *interpreted (the two works are not named; plausibly the alternations of ¶41); QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

23. **Eirenaeus' "explanation of the whole difficulty".** Take 4 parts "Fiery Dragon" (carrying "Magic Steel") to 9 parts "Lodestone". Violent concussion turns them to a mineral water. Skim off the scum, discard the shell and keep the kernel, and purge three times with Salt and Fire. None of these terms is explained.
    — "Take four parts of our Fiery Dragon, which bears in his belly the Magic Steel, and conjoin to nine parts of our Lodestone, that by a violent concussion they may be reduced in to a mineral water" (IV.1 ¶43); also "reject the superfluous scum which swims upon it; leave the Shell and take the Kernel; and purge thrice with Salt and with Fire" (IV.1 ¶43).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: the same author (Eirenaeus)*

24. **Repeated firing of the "fixed Brass".** The fixed Brass is put back on the coals and Vulcan "stirred" into it, with additions, until "Paphia" shows wholly golden. Atwood presents this as a picture of the Vulcanic action.
    — "Drawing the fixed Brass out bodily, instructs this Abderite, thou shalt compose a certain oblong tongue, and placing it again upon the coals, stir Vulcan into it" (IV.1 ¶43).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: Democritus*

25. **Purify by successive exchange of ferments, as sap becomes wine.**
    — "is purified by successive interchanging of ferments, fretted, dissolved, and rectified into a consummate and immortal Form of Light" (IV.1 ¶46).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

26. **The operations are moral acts of will.** A "voluntary corrosive" removes defilement, gentleness softens hardness, beneficence sweetens harshness, and justice tempers intensity. The matter is then filled with hope, truth, beauty and understanding, replacing the rule of the senses, until the self is actually overthrown.
    — "By applying the proper voluntary corrosive they teach to obliterate its defilements; by gentleness to mollify its Durity; by beneficence to sweeten its acerbity; by justice to moderate its intensity" (IV.1 ¶47); also "to irradiate it with hope, truth, beauty, and universal intellection; supplanting the sensual dominion" (IV.1 ¶47).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

27. **Penetrate one's own darkened understanding by reason until it is lucid.**
    — "to penetrate rationally the darkened essence of his natural understanding, will, by conversion illuminating its obscurity, cause it to become lucid throughout, and to be no longer what it was before" (IV.1 ¶48).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

### Stage 4 — Threshold of the Philosophic Work proper

28. **The Philosophic Work truly begins only when the right Motive is found and all is purified, transparent, "without and within".** Everything before is preparatory.
    — "it is not until the right Motive is discovered, and until the mundification of the Spirit is completed in both kinds, and all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity, that the Philosophic Work has been said truly to being" (IV.1 ¶48); also "The philosopher’s worke doe not begin Till all things be pure without and within" (IV.1 ¶49).
    — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Norton (endorsed)*

29. **Duration.** Cleansing takes far longer than expected. "Three years" for the Gross Work would be lucky. Atwood says such periods refer "chiefly" to the Second Operation and are often a figure for the discovery of the philosophic Salt. Eirenaeus had the whole Arcanum in two and a half years.
    — "the periods are often to be understood metaphorically with respect to the discovery of the philosophic Salt" (IV.1 ¶17); also "If the Grosse worke with all his circumstance Were done in three years it were a blessed chance" (IV.1 ¶16); "The length of clensing matters infected Deceiveth much people for that is unsuspected." (IV.1 ¶12).
    — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal*

## Signs (what shows the work is proceeding)

1. **The "simperings" of the Stone.** The operator must know the Stone's simmering and hold it there, not at a boil. The sign itself is not further explained.
   — "To know the simperings of our Stone, Till it may no more simper do, nor cease," (IV.1 ¶12).
   — *interpreted; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal*

2. **Green Duenech and Vitriol of Venus appear** once Adrop's coagulation is relaxed.
   — "the Green Duenech and the Vitriol of Venus, which are the true matters of the Blessed Stone will appear" (IV.1 ¶36).
   — *stated (that they appear); QUOTED: Khunrath*

3. **Blackness: the black "Wine" = Blood of the Earth = Red of Lully; the body blackens to ashes.** A thick, deep-black substance appears. It is called "Red" for its tincture, the color of its virtue. Lully's Latin speaks of "that first blackness" (our translation).
   — "the Wine which the Wise have called the Blood of the Earth, which likewise is the Red of Lully, so name on account of its tincture which is the color of its virtue, thick, dense, and black, blacker than black" (IV.1 ¶36); also "de prima illa nigedine a paucis cognita" (IV.1 ¶36).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; Lully*

4. **Putrefaction: loss of Form, death "for a season".**
   — "it is brought to corrupt and to lose its Form, and for a season to remain dead" (IV.1 ¶41).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Morien*

5. **Colors.** In the Second Operation the principles become "diversifiedly colored". The colors that come from the Fire show that the Lesser World's Vulcan is at work.
   — "these three principles also are of themselves coagulated, diversifiedly colored, calcined, and fixed" (IV.1 ¶36); also "it very apparent in the corporeal aspect of colors which proceed from the Fire" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Khunrath; the monk (Basil Valentine)*

6. **Progressive beautification until wholly golden.**
   — "adorning now the shoulder and the breast of Paphia till she shall appear more manifestly beautiful, and, throwing the glaucous veil aside, shall appear entirely Golden" (IV.1 ¶43).
   — *interpreted; QUOTED: Democritus*

7. **A condition that makes the purging easy: Saturn seeing his beauty in Mars's glass.** Not explained.
   — "which will be easy to do, if Saturn have chanced to regard his beauty in the glass of Mars" (IV.1 ¶43).
   — *interpreted; QUOTED: Eirenaeus*

8. **The riddle solves itself stage by stage; meeting "the Light".** For one who has begun rightly, each stage opens new views. Some "have met the Light sooner, some later".
   — "it will solve itself at every stage, opening into new prospects within the veil of life" (IV.1 ¶44); also "Some have met the Light sooner, some later" (IV.1 ¶17).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **Fire tests the work.** Whether a work lasts is shown by Fire.
   — "because it shall be revealed by Fire; and the Fire shall try every man’s work of what sort it is" (IV.1 ¶46).
   — *interpreted (its application to the work); QUOTED: the apostle (endorsed)*

10. **Crystalline transparency marks readiness for the Work proper.**
    — "all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity" (IV.1 ¶48).
    — *stated (as a condition); ATWOOD*

## Impediments and dangers

1. **Drugs are no substitute for the mesmeric means.** Ether and chloroform work "by influxion of darkness": they cloud the mind and may kill. The other means works in light and purifies blood and intellect.
   — "by influxion of darkness, drowns the oxygenating spirit, prostrates and confounds the mental powers, and, further overwhelming, often produces syncope and death" (IV.1 ¶4); also "overcoming in light, oxygenates, purifies, and sublimes the arterial blood, and in proportion the intellectual powers" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *interpreted (which of "the one"/"the other" is which); ATWOOD*

2. **Stopping at the first phenomena.** See Procedure 2.
   — "did not remain satisfied with a few passes of the hand, or any first phenomena whatever" (IV.1 ¶4).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

3. **The Brazen Wall.** A barrier around "our Heaven" that must be climbed and passed before the inner balanced state of Being can be seen. Later: "brazen walls and adamantine" and a vast sea lie before the "royal coast", and moderate study, a few spontaneous revelations or instinct are not enough.
   — "this is that Brazen Wall celebrated by Antiquity, which surrounds our Heaven and must be scaled, and passed through before any one can hope to discern the equilibriate felicity of Being within" (IV.1 ¶4); also "brazen walls and adamantine are between, and all the breadth of that vast sea to be passed over before we can hope to set foot upon the royal coast" (IV.1 ¶23).
   — *interpreted (referent of "this": see Open questions); ATWOOD*

4. **Incomplete or interrupted dissolution lets the old state return.** If any part is left undissolved, or the work is dropped before the riddle is fully solved, "the Sphinx" regains control and wrecks the work. Fighting the "Powers of Darkness" takes great force.
   — "lest remaining in any part, of left alone, before the total solution of her enigma, the Sphinx should retrieve her dominion unawares and frustrate the work begun" (IV.1 ¶7); also "to fight against the Powers of Darkness requires great force" (IV.1 ¶7).
   — *interpreted; ATWOOD; QUOTED: the Spirit to Dr Dee*

5. **Without the Adepts' theory the dissolution is impossible.** Books give only general principles, and the Adepts disguise their meaning.
   — "it will be entirely impossible to accomplish without the Theory of their Arcanum" (IV.1 ¶7); also "whilst adepts are so very abstruse and envious in their disguises" (IV.1 ¶18).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Urbigeranus and other Adepts; ATWOOD*

6. **Haste and overshoot.** A very small excess can ruin all. Stirring "the centers" before "the circumferences" can receive them is warned against. Oedipus was lame so as not to hurry the riddle. Matter allowed to fly too soon is burned like Icarus.
   — "Excess of one half quarter of an hour May destroy all" (IV.1 ¶12); also "lest the centers should be stirred up before the circumferences are ready to conceive them" (IV.1 ¶13); "lest she should expound herself without a proper understanding unawares" (IV.1 ¶13); "like Icarus, having its unproved feathers burnt, fall headlong into the sea" (IV.1 ¶15).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal; ATWOOD; Basil*

7. **Over-excitation splits the Chaos.** Stimulating too strongly wakes the internal agent early. The Chaos divides and its two Principles turn against each other before the operator has control.
   — "lest by too great excitation the internal agent awakening should cause a disseveration in the Chaos, and the two Principles stand up one against the other, before the intended mastery is secure" (IV.1 ¶15).
   — *stated (the danger); the terms are unexplained; ATWOOD*

8. **Things that drag the work out.** A faulty start, unfit "patients", ignorant "agents" and the confusion of the records. To some it is never granted.
   — "the natural periods are protracted by faulty conditions from the commencement, by the indisposition of patients, as by the ignorance of agents" (IV.1 ¶17); also "the perplexity of the records have added to the natural difficulty, and to others it has never been vouchsafed" (IV.1 ¶17).
   — *stated; meaning of patients/agents: see Open questions; ATWOOD*

9. **The Gross Work is foul, perilous and full of faults; harder trials come after the first barriers.** Everyone makes mistakes. Beyond the first barriers come Herculean labors for whoever, nearing the "Nether confines", chooses Light.
   — "For the Gross Worke is foule in her kinde, And full of perils as ye shall finde," (IV.1 ¶25–26); also "Wherein a hundred ways and moe, Ye may commit a fault as ye therein goe" (IV.1 ¶28); "after the first floodgates and barriers are overpast, greater obstacles await him, and Herculean labors, who dares, approaching to the Nether confines, to make choice of Light" (IV.1 ¶25).
   — *stated; QUOTED: Norton's Ordinal; ATWOOD*

10. **An "ominous" association.** Pluto's Palace exposed together with Minerva's Artifice, or Vulcan beside her at the Altar, is a bad omen. Not explained.
    — "if Vulcan stands together with her at the Altar there, the Association is ominous" (IV.1 ¶34).
    — *interpreted; QUOTED: the wise Adept*

11. **At the Solution, hands and intellect are useless; corrosives are wrongly relied on.** The door is shut to force. The secret was never written down.
    — "since hands and intellect are here alike incapable, and the truth of this discovery was never yet put to paper" (IV.1 ¶39); also "Vestrae namque Manus nequeunt diffingere ferru." (IV.1 ¶38); "They err, therefore, who ascribe this single combat only to Corrosives" (IV.1 ¶39).
    — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Van helmont*

12. **Nature's intermediate fruits are lures.** At each stage the process offers tempting partial results. The true artist refuses them all and destroys each early product until perfection is reached.
    — "Nature halts many times before this final rest, at each stage offering the fruits of her conceptive imagination to allure" (IV.1 ¶46); also "with relentless hands, slaying the firstborn offspring until the Divine Perfection is attained" (IV.1 ¶46).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

13. **Ordinary chemistry drives off the divine source without recognizing it.**
    — "The common elements of Nature obscure their Divine Original, and Chemistry and all our experimental physics drive it forcibly without the means of Identification" (IV.1 ¶47).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

14. **Premature fixing.** Anything permanently fixed before purification is complete does not last.
    — "if any permanent confection is made or suffered to take place beforehand, the immature offspring does not abide" (IV.1 ¶48).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

15. **Self-will and vice in the operator.** See Operator 11.
    — "all arrogant self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice, and variability of purpose" (IV.1 ¶40).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

## Principles (general laws of the work)

1. **The practice is learned by seeing and doing, not from books; theory grows out of action.** Seeing the work once teaches more than many pages. The working theory comes only through practice. "Speculation proceeds from actions" (our translation).
   — "more is learned by once seeing the work done, than can be taught by the writing of many pages" (IV.1 ¶2); also "nor would it be possible, we think, for any one to discover the Practice from their books alone" (IV.1 ¶3); "The working theory, as we long ago suggested, can be obtained through the practice only" (IV.1 ¶18); "Ab actionibus procedit speculatio" (IV.1 ¶18).
   — *stated; QUOTED: the Monk Basil (endorsed); ATWOOD*

2. **Manual operation is the ballast of everything else; the Gross Work is "Art mechanical".**
   — "Manual operation is chiefly required, therefore; without which, indeed, every other operation, like a ship without ballast, floats and is uncertain" (IV.1 ¶2); also "For in this worke find ye nothing shall, But handie crafte, called Art mechanical," (IV.1 ¶28).
   — *stated; QUOTED: the Monk Basil; Norton's Ordinal (both endorsed)*

3. **The Art comes less from native intelligence than from "involved thought".** Each discovery opens a further inquiry.
   — "the Hermetic Art is not so much the offspring of natural intelligence as of involved thought" (IV.1 ¶18); also "each discovery opens into a new field of inquiry" (IV.1 ¶18).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

4. **Make haste slowly.** See Procedure 10 and Impediment 6.
   — "Festina Lente --- Make not too much haste" (IV.1 ¶13); also "Oedipus, he who of yore overcame the Sphinx, was lame and impotent in his feet" (IV.1 ¶13).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

5. **Deliberate about means, not ends; what is last in analysis is first in execution.** See Procedure 14.
   — "That Medium, therefore, which is last in the analysis is first in generation" (IV.1 ¶31).
   — *stated; ATWOOD (after Aristotle)*

6. **Success is by grace and Divine Will; it cannot be forced or passed on by human means.** Man may plough, plant and irrigate but cannot force the Blessing. Only God reveals the secret of the Solution, and no one can pass it on unless the Divine Will is with him.
   — "man indeed may experiment, plough, plant, and irrigate, but cannot of himself (or in alliance, unless he dare a deadly sin), compel the Divine Blessing without its free accord" (IV.1 ¶40); also "it is proper alone, as Lully says, to God to reveal it; since it is His alone prerogative, and no mortal can communicate it to another unless the Divine Will be with him" (IV.1 ¶39); "Wisdom was with thee, says the Hermetic Master; it was not gotten by thy care" (IV.1 ¶40).
   — *stated; ATWOOD; QUOTED: Lully, the Hermetic Master (endorsed)*

7. **The moist ethereal (lunar) principle feeds the solar light.** The Nemean Lion names this. Its exact referent is unclear.
   — "The ethereal humidity nourishes the Solar Light and educates it; and this is that Nemean Lion said to be born of her foam." (IV.1 ¶29); also "Sol est Fons totius caloris, Luna autem Domina Humiditatis." (IV.1 ¶29).
   — *interpreted; ATWOOD*

8. **Decoding key: the alternating operations = action and reaction of actives and passives, which prepares the matter to attract the Divine Seed.** This is Atwood's own reading of Vaughan, Flamel and Morien.
   — "This then is the true intention and manner of working to supply the right condition for attracting the Divine Seed, by action and reaction raising successively actives by passives, and, vice versa, passives by actives, until the spiritual ability is complete." (IV.1 ¶41).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

9. **Everything depends on the Motive (the spiritual ferment); the end depends on the beginning.**
   — "Everything depends upon the Motive, which is the true spiritual ferment; and according to the virtue of the fermenting principle is the result obtained." (IV.1 ¶44); also "Sie finis ab origine pendet." (IV.1 ¶45); "The end depends from the beginning" (IV.1 ¶46).
   — *stated; ATWOOD*

10. **The Salt and its solution is the "Gordian Knot", the core secret.** See Procedure 16.
    — "Here then liest the Gordian Knot of the Hermetic Mystery" (IV.1 ¶37).
    — *stated; ATWOOD*

11. **The one able to untie the knot has been "named, openly". It is a Light understood only through itself.**
    — "We have already named him, and openly; but this Light shining everywhere in Darkness, how hardly should it be comprehensible without Itself?" (IV.1 ¶37).
    — *interpreted (who is meant: see Open questions); ATWOOD*

## Open questions

1. **How the stages line up.** The chapter uses several divisions without equating them: Eirenaeus' First Operation (manual) vs Second (Nature alone, external fire) (¶6, ¶11); his "Supernatural Work" (¶6); Vaughan/Atwood's First Preparation vs Second part of the Gross Work = Solution of the Salt (¶36); Khunrath's first act (hands and Fire) vs Second Operation (no manual cooperation) (¶36); "the Philosophic Work" beginning only after full mundification (¶48).
   - Reading A: the Gross Work (= Vital Purification) has two parts, the manual purification and the Solution of the Salt (where hands fail, ¶39, and nature works alone, ¶11, ¶36). The Philosophic Work proper follows. Basis: ¶36 "Second part of the Gross Work"; ¶39; ¶48.
   - Reading B: Eirenaeus' and Khunrath's "Second Operation" is the later Philosophic or "Supernatural" Work, not part of the Gross Work. Basis: ¶6 distinguishes the First Operation from the "Supernatural Work"; ¶48 "truly to being".
   - Complication: ¶17 refers the Ordinal's years for "the Grosse worke" (¶16) "chiefly" to the Second Operation, which supports A, but the relation is not stated.

2. **What "the voluntary bond" is** (¶36).
   - A: the same as Khunrath's "bond by which the soul is tied to the body" (¶36), whose loosening is the Solution.
   - B: a bond of the will, whose solution matches removing self-will, leaving "the one voluntary faith to rectify and perfect" (¶40; cf. ¶31 "Will is the greatest power of purgation").
   - C: distinct from both; Atwood asserts no identity.

3. **Literal handwork vs inward or moral work.** Atwood insists the Hand is literally the instrument (¶5 "literal application"; ¶6 "Neither would I have these things understood figuratively"). The entry is mesmeric ("the hand or eye of another", ¶4). Yet the later operations are acts of will and virtue (¶47), self-analysis (¶20) and penetration of one's own understanding (¶48).
   - A: a literal mesmeric or manual process between persons (¶4, ¶9 assistants, ¶11 "hand to hand", ¶17 "patients"/"agents", ¶44 "equal companionship"), with an inner counterpart.
   - B: "hand" stands for the soul's instrument of putting thought into act (¶5 Van Helmont; ¶34). The chapter does not settle how the two relate.

4. **"Patients" and "agents"** (¶17).
   - Persons, i.e. the one entranced and the operators (by analogy with ¶4's mesmeric "another").
   - The matter and the operator.
   - A generic passive/active pairing.

5. **Referent of "this" = the Brazen Wall** (¶4).
   - The unpurified ether / circulating body itself (the barrier).
   - The purifying dissolution (the passage to be gone through).

6. **"Dissolution of the circulating body in its own blood" (¶4) and "dissolve the Stone in its own mercury" (¶7).** One operation stated twice, or distinct? No identity is asserted.

7. **"Pontically … by hand to hand"** (¶11).
   - Virtue passed back and forth between the hands of operators (¶4, ¶9).
   - An alternation within one manual process (cf. ¶20 "reiterate the same by alternation"; ¶41 actives/passives).
   - "Pontically" is undefined.

8. **Which spirit is "this Argent vive"** (¶10).
   - The extracted tinging Second Spirit.
   - The Preparing Spirit itself, which "reduces the bodies by dissolution into itself" and is "resolving the bodies mineral".

9. **The Chaos, the two Principles, centers and circumferences** (¶13, ¶15).
   - Is the "Chaos" endangered by over-excitation (¶15) the same as the Chamelion "which is our Chaos" (¶43)? Not asserted.
   - Centers/circumferences may be the inner agent vs the outer receptive part (cf. ¶15 "internal agent"), or undefined levels.
   - The ¶33 "center meeting center" may or may not belong to the same vocabulary.

10. **Who has been "named, openly"** (¶37).
    - The Light itself.
    - Cheiron / Power and Motive (¶33) or Vulcan / Motion (¶34).
    - God, who alone reveals (¶36, ¶39).
    - Someone named earlier in the book.

11. **"A certain other Vulcan" in the Lesser World** (¶40).
    - The fire or motion within the operator. Basis: Atwood's gloss of Antimony as the operator's mind, ¶40; Vulcan = Motion, ¶34.
    - The artificial fire of the art. Basis: "cocting the crude by heat", colors "which proceed from the Fire".

12. **Nemean Lion** (¶29).
    - The Solar Light as nourished by the ethereal humidity.
    - The humidity itself.
    - "Her foam" may refer to Luna, the humidity or Diana.

13. **The two elements and the two works** (¶41–42).
    - Vaughan's "two elements" may be heat/dryness and cold/moisture (Morien, ¶41) or actives and passives (Atwood, ¶41).
    - The two works of ¶42 may be these alternations; the verse does not name them.

14. **"Mundification … in both kinds"** (¶48).
    - Outward and inward (¶49 "pure without and within").
    - The paired principles (Solar/Lunar Mercury, actives/passives, the two works, ¶41–42).

15. **The human being as subject.**
    - Khunrath's First Matter "of our very selves" (¶36) and "ye are the Temple of God" (¶46) may make the person the vessel or ground of the work (cf. ¶47 "subversion of the selfhood", ¶48).
    - Or they may be a list of things sharing a First Matter, and a moral exhortation.

16. **Textual doubts affecting the entry stage.**
    - "important" in the list of the medium's defects (¶4): possibly a misprint for a word of deficiency.
    - "including the sensible medium" (¶5): possibly a misprint.
    - "ether" is used both for the anaesthetic and for the medium to be purified (¶4).

17. **The "ominous" association** (¶34).
    - A wrong union of Motion/Fire (Vulcan) with the Fabricate Intellect (Minerva).
    - Disclosing the hidden "to any one".
    - A sign of failure at some stage.
