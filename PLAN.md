# Extraction Plan — Atwood, *A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery*

## Goal
1. An mdBook (`src/`) condensing the book bottom-up: chunk → chapter → part → book.
2. A clear, modern statement of the **method** Atwood hints at (process, requirements, stages, signs, impediments), illustrated by her veiled expressions and vocabulary. This is the primary deliverable.
3. A dictionary (`src/22_Master_Lexicon_and_Concordance.md`) and a SillyTavern worldbook (`lorebook/Atwood_Hermetic_Mystery.json`).

## Ground rule: the book is the only source
- Every claim carries a reference (`[III.2 ¶14]`, footnote `[III.2 n7]`). No outside knowledge of the book, its authors, its cited sources, or later commentary.
- Enforcement: a script checks every quote verbatim against `input/`; an independent checker agent flags any claim it cannot trace to the text.
- Voices kept apart and tagged: **Atwood** (her own position) / **Quoted authority** (whom she cites) / **Appendix** (Table Talk, begun 1860, a later layer).
- Translations of Latin/French/Middle English are marked as ours unless the book gives its own.
- Footnote files are headed "References (Abridged)": record that, never fill gaps.
- Where meaning is unclear, record competing readings with their textual support; don't force one.

## Diagnosis of earlier runs
- Compression of ~20:1 per chapter lost vocabulary and citations.
- Lexicon and lorebook were derived from the summaries (two steps away from the source).
- Lorebook entries were keyword dumps (up to ~50 keys) → poor triggering.
- No traceability to source text.

## Pipeline
0. **Chunking (done).** `scripts/chunk.py` → `chunks/` (81 chunks, ~2–2.5k words; paragraph IDs; footnotes attached to the chunk citing them; Golden Treatise split at sections; intros kept with their quotes). `chunks/index.json` lists all chunks.
1. **Chunk agents** (fresh context each). Input: one chunk + table of contents + last paragraph of previous chunk. Output (`work/chunks/<id>.md` + JSON):
   - condensed notes with ¶ references
   - method hints: veiled wording (verbatim + ref) → plain meaning
   - term candidates with verbatim quote + ref
   - cited sources: who/what, quote, how Atwood uses it
   - voice tags; ambiguity register
   Part III–IV chunks and the Appendix: two independent readings + one reconciler.
2. **Verification.** Quote-check script; checker agent on every chunk output. Failures re-run.
3. **Chapter agents.** Read only their chapter's chunk notes (may consult source) → `src/` chapter files. Target compression ~8–10:1. Chunk notes are working material, not published.
4. **Part syntheses** from chapter files; **book synthesis** from part syntheses.
5. **Method track.** Two levels: a per-chapter method summary from that chapter's method entries (aspect by aspect, keeping quotes and refs), then the book-level method statement from the chapter method summaries. Built from the method entries (not from the chapter summaries). Each step labelled *stated* or *interpreted*, backed by Atwood's own expressions.
6. **Dictionary.** Merge term candidates; cluster synonyms (many names for one thing); one agent per term writes the entry from collected quotes only.
7. **Worldbook.** Generated by script from the dictionary: one concept per entry, ~5–10 tight keys, separate entries for persons and treatises. No LLM rewrite.

## Pilot findings and resulting changes
Pilot: `I.3_01` (one reader), `III.1_02` and `IV.3_01` (two readers + reconciler), all checked. Outputs in `work/final/`.
- All quotes verbatim and correctly referenced; voices kept apart; interpretations flagged.
- Two readers + reconciler produced clearly cleaner results than one reader → keep the double reading for Parts III–IV and Appendix.
- **Fixed — term variants:** the single reader merged different names as variants. `variants` now holds only spellings/forms of the same word; other names go in `equivalences` only when the text asserts the identity.
- **Fixed — too many method entries** (44–70 per chunk, many restatements; ~4,000 book-wide). Now one entry per distinct point, restatements merged into `also`, priority on passages where the text itself explains or identifies something; typically 15–30 per chunk. Added the per-chapter method summary level (step 5).
- **Fixed — uneven note length** (footnote-heavy `I.3_01`: 2,200 words of notes). Now at most 1:5 of substantive source text, hard cap 900 words.
- **Footnotes: kept.** In Part I Ch. III they carry much of the commentary (17 of 44 method entries in `I.3_01` came from footnotes); elsewhere they are mostly bare references, which cost little and feed the sources list. Substantive notes are read as text; bare references go to `sources` only.
