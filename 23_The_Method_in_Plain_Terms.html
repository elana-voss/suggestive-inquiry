Suggestive Inquiry: Summaries

The Method in Plain Terms

This page states, as plainly as the book allows, the method Mary Anne Atwood hints at in A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery: what the work is done on, by what agent, what it asks of the operator, its stages, signs, impediments and result. It is built from the method syntheses of Parts I–IV and of the Appendix, and every point rests on the book alone, with a reference to the chapter and paragraph or footnote in which the quoted words stand (III.2 ¶7 is paragraph 7 of Part III chapter 2; I.3 n7 is footnote 7 of Part I chapter 3; APP ¶35 is aphorism 35 of the Appendix). Labels: stated means the text itself says it; interpreted means it is inferred, and the basis is given; combining Parts never turns an interpretation into a statement. ATWOOD marks her own voice, QUOTED: name a cited authority (with a note when she endorses or rejects the view), UNCLEAR where the text does not show who speaks. Appendix marks points drawn from the Table Talk and Memorabilia, a later layer begun in 1860 and kept separate throughout. Quotations are verbatim, with the book’s spellings and apparent misprints left as they stand.

The method in brief

The book never gives the method as one list. The sequence below is what the five Parts together support; each step is referenced and labelled so the reader can see how firm it is.

  1. Learn the theory and find the intention before touching the practice. “theory ought to precede practice” (III.1 ¶9; stated; QUOTED: philosophers, as Atwood reports). “The success of the experiment, as we have repeatedly shown, depends on the discovery of the true Intention.” (IV.3 ¶54; stated; ATWOOD). The motive must be disinterested: “Wisdom must be sought for her own sake, neither for gold or silver or any intermediate benefit” (I.1 ¶17; stated; ATWOOD).

  2. Take as your matter your own life. “Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art” (II.1 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); the material is “the Thing itself, which is our Identity. This is the true Hermetic material” (III.2 ¶7; stated; ATWOOD). Part I keeps a laboratory register and only hints at this: “the work is both with you and about you” (I.3 ¶8; QUOTED: Hermes). Whether a material process runs beside the inner one is left open (see Open questions).

  3. Withdraw from the senses and set the spirit in motion. “the first step of philosophy is to set the mind a-going” (III.1 ¶8; stated; ATWOOD); “The first step in this Philosophy is to create motion in the spirit.” (APP ¶130; stated; Appendix). Entrancing the senses opens the door but is “a beginning only of the Hermetic art” (IV.1 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD).

  4. Dissolve. “The first rule of the work is a perfect solution” (II.2 ¶16; stated; ATWOOD): the vital spirit is separated from its sense-born forms and driven down through darkness, “to reduce the matter, with her false forms, to the verge of annihilation, but to entrap the bare spirit” (I.2 ¶24; stated; QUOTED: Democritus, endorsed). The sign is blackness, “the "head of Hermes’ crow", which is in the beginning of the work” (II.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD).

  5. Purify by repetition until white. “Separate, says Eiraneaus, the light from the darkness seven times, and the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete” (I.3 n39; QUOTED: Eiraneaus). The Philosophic Work proper begins only when “all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity” (IV.1 ¶48; stated; ATWOOD).

  6. Reunite under a divine act. The separated principles are rejoined “in harmonious proportion” (I.3 n23; stated; ATWOOD), and “the Divine Fiat comes mercifully to bless the union, and a new hypostasis is created out of the darkness to abide” (III.3 ¶10; stated; ATWOOD). The sign is the reddening of what was white: the light is “white before the multiplication of its internal form has rubified it” (I.3 n67; stated; ATWOOD).

  7. Fix, ferment, multiply. Fermentation, the last operation, is “the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God” (IV.3 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD); the tinging virtue is “augmented each time by ten degrees” (I.3 n70; stated).

  8. The result is a stable form of light in the regenerated life, “a consummate and immortal Form of Light” (IV.1 ¶46; stated; ATWOOD), bringing self-knowledge and union with the Source; elixir and gold are “vital products” (II.4 ¶58; stated; ATWOOD) whose literal sense the book asserts and denies by turns.

The order black → white → red is stated in each of Parts I–IV. The mapping of the Parts’ several stage-schemes onto one another is not.

Reading the veil: decoding keys

The book names a few things by many names. Each chain below gathers the names the text gives to one thing, with the strongest verbatim span for each link. Links are Atwood’s and stated unless marked; “interp.” marks a link reached by inference, with its basis.

1. The subject: the one thing, the vital spirit, the Universal Subject. Plain meaning: in Parts II–IV and the Appendix, the operator’s own life or vital spirit; in Part I, the one substance of nature, which Part III says is the same thing seen in man and in the world: “for what the natural intelligent Spirit is in man, that Ether is in the universe” (III.1 ¶5). Names: the One Thing, “so all things arose from this one thing by adaptation” (I.1 ¶9; QUOTED: Hermes); first matter, Universal Subject, radical moisture (I.2 ¶3, ¶4, ¶7); “the simple generated substance of life and light, immanifestly flowing throughout nature” (I.2 ¶34); “the salt of the world, Which contains in itself all Life” (I.2 ¶10; QUOTED: Lucerna Solis); Proteus, “the one universal entity of life, the omnia in omnibus before manifestation and individuality” (APP ¶138; Appendix); the Ether, “the Sphinx of the Universe” (III.1 ¶5); the Phantastic Spirit, “the natural vehicle of our life” (III.1 ¶5; as the thing worked on, interp. from III.1 ¶6); “his life the subject, the grand distillatory, the thing distilling and the thing distilled” (II.1 ¶30); the Mundane Spirit, “the photogenic medium, our New Imponderable, for it is the Common Soul; also the subject-matter of the Alchemists aforesaid” (II.2 ¶18); “the Thing itself, which is our Identity” (III.2 ¶7); “an unctuous mineral vapour”, “the Water of our Sea, the Water of Life” (III.2 ¶7; endorsed); “the Water that wets not the hands”, Magnesia, “true magic earth” (III.2 ¶8); “there is a Universal Subject in nature, and that Subject is susceptible of nourishment in Man” (IV.4 ¶10); “Take the occult Nature, which is our Brass” (IV.1 ¶4; QUOTED: Albertus, endorsed); in the Appendix, “The third life, the mineral or lowest life in man” (APP ¶59), “The body of brass is the impure natural-born vital spirit.” (APP ¶52), and “The One Thing; it is our psychical essence; our nervous system.” (APP ¶176). In its fallen state: “the vulgar Matter” (II.2 ¶16; = impure spirit, interp.), “the Black Saturn of the adepts” (II.3 ¶18), “our dark divulsed chaotic life from sense” (II.3 ¶29), the Augean Stable (APP ¶71; Appendix; in II.2 ¶19 its cleansing is the turning and purifying of the life). That Part I’s cosmic Ether and the human life of Parts II–IV are one subject rests on III.1 ¶5 and on Part I’s “within themselves the substance of that Universal Nature” (I.3 n8); Part I alone never says it in one sentence.

2. Mercury, water, argent vive: the subject dissolved. Plain meaning: the same thing in its fluid, dissolved state, impure at first and pure at last. “reduce the body into water, that is, into mercury, and this is called Solution” (I.2 ¶5; QUOTED: Friar Bacon); “argentums vivum nostrum est aqua alterius naturae” (I.2 ¶18; QUOTED: Lully); “a stone and no stone, in which the whole Art consists, spirit, soul and body” (I.2 ¶29; QUOTED: Arnold); “Our water is heavenly, not wetting the hands” (I.2 ¶33; QUOTED: Synesius); the Philosopher’s Mercury = “the impure vapor of life” (III.1 ¶24); “its solution, i.e., in its dissolved essence which is the mercury of the Philosophers, Proteus” (APP ¶144; Appendix); the Water of the Adepts = “product of the spiritual poles of mind” (III.3 ¶73); purified, “the Mercury of the Philosophers — pure, agile, intelligent, living” (II.2 ¶16; = purified spirit, interp.). Eudoxus’ register: “Our Old Man is our Mercury”, “the first matter of all metals” (IV.3 ¶10; QUOTED: Eudoxus); the Water as wine, water of life, vinegar or oil “according to the different degrees of Preparation” (IV.3 ¶8; QUOTED: Eudoxus); “the most sharp vinegar” = “that Blessed Nature” (III.1 ¶70; QUOTED: Synesius, endorsed). Part I adds that the Stone’s water is “not water in its true form, but fire” (I.3 ¶26; QUOTED: Hermes), and that Mercury = Ether is never stated in one sentence but chained through I.2 ¶16, ¶37, ¶43, ¶44, ¶49 (interp.).

3. The agent: Light, Sulfur, Fire, Gold, Sol, Reason. Plain meaning: the purified rational light in the operator, which the adepts call the seed and form of gold. “calling it Wisdom, Intellect, Gold, Sol, Sulfur, Tincture” (III.1 ¶21); “For it is Light indeed” (III.1 ¶21); “light or sulfur, as they call it, is the true form or seed of gold, and the concentering virtue of their philosophic stone” (I.3 n40); “the re-entering Light of Reason in the Mysteries, which is that Sulfur of adepts” (II.3 ¶30); “no other Fire but that of Intellect” (III.2 ¶62); “the Key thereof is Light” (IV.4 ¶32); “that ray of motive Light, pure, vital, and efficient, we have shown to be the true Form of Gold” (IV.4 ¶9); “The Sulphur, the pure light of life” (APP ¶125; Appendix); “the Divine aurific seed and true form of gold. That was the Golden Bough which Aeneas carried” (APP ¶152; Appendix). The Golden Bough is also “a ray of living light, golden and flexible, the true Brancha Spiritualis of Raymond Lully” (II.3 ¶11) and, with the Caduceus and “the ferrying Cup of Hercules”, the intellectual purpose, “that prolific Mustard-seed, and Light of divine Faith” (III.1 ¶36). Related fires: “a certain pure and hidden fire” (I.2 ¶12–17); “This Form is Light, the source of central heat” (I.2 ¶45; QUOTED: Eiranaeus); the Prester, “the Fire Spirit of Life” (II.3 ¶54); “the Lunar Vulcan, as we long ago suggested, is meant the first prepared Subject, which is also called Diana, and the secret natural interior Fire of Adepts” (IV.1 ¶29). That Light, Fire, Motive and Mind are one agent is interpreted; Part IV asserts each link but never the whole.

4. Salt: Will, the bond, Wisdom. Plain meaning: two readings the text does not reconcile — the bond of will or selfhood whose loosening is the pivot of the work, or Wisdom itself as the Stone’s oldest principle. “the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond)” (IV.1 ¶36); “He who knows the Salt and its solution, knows the secret of the Ancient Sages.” (IV.1 ¶37); “There is no true body but Will; it is the principle of the body — the Salt.” (APP ¶65; Appendix); “Saturn or the self-willed life” (APP ¶72; Appendix). On the other side: “this same Root of Reason is Wisdom, and that saving Salt which philosophers were wont anciently to excavate” (IV.4 ¶20); Salt = Wisdom, “that most ancient principle of the Stone” (III.3 ¶50; QUOTED: Khunrath); the Identic Salt = “the true grain” that “survives the wreck of the whole dissolute Being throughout” (III.2 ¶20); “The Salt of Saturn, that most abstruse principle of the Stone — the most ancient Demogorgon” (II.3 ¶41; UNCLEAR); “without Salt no man can attain to this Sacred Science” (III.1 ¶61). Which Salt is meant where is open (see Open questions).

5. The three principles. Plain meaning: not substances but three modes or relations of the one thing — body, soul, spirit; agent, patient, offspring; attraction, repulsion, circulation. “The three principles, the Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury, are merely different as modes of being of the same thing” (I.2 ¶39); “agent, patient, and offspring universal” (I.2 ¶36); “the paternal, maternal, and proceeding ens of life” = “a body, a spirit, and an occult soul” (I.3 n30; Sendivogius); “the mercury, slat and sulfur — spirit, body and soul — lie hid together in one thing, well known” (II.1 ¶11; QUOTED: Basil); “the soul, the spirit, and the body principle; the paternal, maternal, and proceeding substance of life; sulfur, mercury, salt” (III.1 ¶53); “the active and the passive, and the resulting experience of life” (III.3 ¶77); “Attraction, Circulation, Repulsion — the Alchemical Salt, Mercury, Sulphur” (APP ¶24; Appendix); “relates to principles rather than qualities: in the spirit they become qualities” (APP ¶81; Appendix). The juxtaposition in II.1 ¶14 of the three with attraction, repulsion and circulation has no copula; the Appendix states it. Only sulfur = soul holds across Parts (III.1 ¶53; “the Meridian Redness, that is the soul of gold”, I.3 ¶6, Hermes); the other two assignments vary (IV.3 ¶7, Eudoxus, order open).

6. The opposing, dissolving principle. Plain meaning: the self-willed spirit of the natural life, which must be let grow, met and mortified; poisonous yet necessary. “The dragon is the self-willed spirit, which is externally derived into nature, by the fall into generation” (I.3 n31); “for the root of this science is a deadly poison” (I.3 n31); the Wrathful Efficient is “the Green Lion, Typhon, Firedrake; or during the mortification, he is their venomous Black Toad” (III.1 ¶56); “Typhon, Satan, Aquafoetida, Ignis Gehanna, Mortis Immundities, etc.” (III.1 ¶61); “though impure in the beginning, and manifestly evil, it is nevertheless a necessary ingredient” (III.1 ¶61); the Sphinx: “if reason remains passive, this nature at length prevailing, will ravage and devastate and take possession of the whole mind” (II.2 ¶15); “to solve the Enigma of the Sphinx; in other words, to penetrate rationally the darkened essence of his natural understanding” (IV.1 ¶48); “Saturn or the self-willed life can only be overcome in the process by satiation” (APP ¶72; Appendix). Negative key: “vitriall, Which folys doe call the Grene Lyon” (I.1 ¶65; QUOTED: Ripley); “green” = “unripenes” (III.1 ¶65; QUOTED: Greene Lyon).

7. Colours and stage names. Plain meaning: the colours are inner changes of the matter, in a fixed order black, white, red; they mean “the qualities and changes which take place during the preparation, and multiplying the Mercury by its proper Light” (I.2 ¶39). Black: “The blackness of the night is the putrefaction of the same, and the clearness of the day signifies its resurrection to a state of comparative purity” (I.3 n12); “O happy gate of blackness, cries the sage, which art the passage to this so glorious a change” (I.3 n42); “Night, Saturn and the Antimony of the Wise will be present, Obscurity and the Head of the Crow” (III.3 ¶13); “this blackness so desired by all the philosophers” (IV.3 ¶8; QUOTED: Eudoxus). White: “the first manifested resplendence of the vital tincture” (I.3 n22); “a brilliant whiteness in the matter” (II.1 ¶24; QUOTED: Boehme); “Blackness passed he may grow white;” (III.1 ¶65); “at this shining whiteness, that the work of the First Key is ended” (IV.3 ¶3; QUOTED: Eudoxus). Red: “a most pure and rubicund tincture of the color of flesh and blood” (I.3 n42); “the king doth rule, and now he is invested wit the red garment” (II.1 ¶24; QUOTED: Boehme). All colours: “Iris, God’s messenger, and the tail of the peacock”, a “token of reconciliation” (III.3 ¶13). The Appendix adds green and purple: “The first vegetative life is green, it arises with hope” and “The purple color is its ripened Life” (APP ¶119; Appendix). Atwood’s own ladder of names runs “vile and cheap” → “serpent, dragon, or green lion” → “venomous toad” → “magnesia and lead of the wise” → “vitriolic solvent and most sharp acetum” → “milk, dew, quintessence” → “phoenix, salamander, their royal essence and Red Stone” (I.2 ¶40). The other ladders do not align with it (see Open questions).

8. Operations. Plain meaning: every named operation is one act, the dissolving and re-fixing of the life. “Solve, Coagula, Fige.” (I.2 ¶21; I.3 n43); “to dissolve and recongeal the spirit, to make the fixed volatile and the volatile fixed” (II.3 n2); “solve, that is comprehend, it on its own ground” (II.2 ¶15); all operations, “though differently designated, are in their source the same” (I.3 n14); “to dissolve, to calcine, to tinge, to whiten, to renew, to bathe, to wash, to coagulate, to imbibe, to decoct, to fix, to grind, to dry and to distill are all one” (III.3 ¶60; QUOTED: Synesius); “a successive dissolution and coagulation” (IV.1 ¶4; QUOTED: Albertus); “Solution refers to the separation of the sensuous medium itself.” (APP ¶176; Appendix). Instruments: Vulcan = “Motion, which the lame god personates” (IV.1 ¶34); “The Vulcan or Faber is the instrument of the motive agent — the mind.” (APP ¶130; Appendix); the Hand, “the instrument of instruments” (IV.1 ¶5; QUOTED: Van Helmont); “The hand outward is an image of the invisible hand, its clothing” (APP ¶177; Appendix). Stage names: the “gross work” is the preliminary preparation (I.3 n56), Vital Purification (IV.1 ¶1), “handie crafte, called Art mechanical” (IV.1 ¶28; QUOTED: Norton); the Second Work perfects “the newly informed embryo” (I.3 n7); the Philosophic is the Subtle Work (IV.2 ¶1).

9. Places and vessels. Plain meaning: the places of the work are states of the operator’s own life; the vessel is withheld and seems to be that life itself. “vas philosophorum est aqua eorum” (II.1 ¶8; QUOTED: Hermes; ours: the philosophers’ vessel is their water); “with exception of the vessel; which is a divine secret” (II.1 ¶8); “that living temple wherein alone the wise of all ages have been securely able to raise their rejected Corner Stone” (II.1 ¶8); “and that is glasse” (III.4 ¶31; QUOTED: Norton’s Master via Norton); “The Spirit finally constructs its own vessel and vitrifies it” (III.4 ¶32); “one matter, one linear way throughout, one vessel uniform throughout, except removal” (IV.1 ¶9; QUOTED: Zachary, endorsed); Athanor = philosophic furnace, lutum Sapientiae = Hermetic seal (I.3 n57); mountain = vessel in a furnace (I.3 n11; QUOTED: Scholiast). Hades is vital submersion, not corporeal (II.3 ¶7), the unpurified moisture, “that Hades we are treating of, the Purgatory of the wise” (II.3 ¶29); “Tartarus is the death of this life.” (APP ¶168; Appendix); Elysium = “meadow of Divine Ideas” = “Alchemists’ garden” (II.4 ¶9); the Augean palace = “supreme Ether” (II.3 ¶56); “The Hermetic sepulcher is the vehicle of the dead Divine Life within us” (APP ¶19; Appendix). The Appendix seats three lives in the body: “the eye of the Divine, animal rational head life; the heart of the celestial medial vegetable life; the lumbar region of the inferior mineral life” (APP ¶95; Appendix).

10. Persons, gods, beasts. Plain meaning: the figures personify faculties and states of the one life. The king is “the Rational Efficient” (I.3 n50), or the soul “placed in the vital spirit in the purest blood” (II.1 ¶10; QUOTED: Sendivogius, endorsed); “Venus personifies the central light of nature” (I.3 n49); Luna is “the passive luminary in the Philosophic Heaven” (I.3 n7), the Moon “the Passive Intelligence” (III.1 ¶40); Isis = Minerva = Wisdom (III.1 ¶5), “the purified spirit or intelligence, that is Isis” (III.1 ¶23); Proserpine is “its own congenial life, which is Proserpine, and that lapsed soul of ours” (II.3 ¶11); Osiris “the Divine Light” (III.1 ¶63); the Prester “the Fire Spirit of Life, and is that Identity in all” (II.3 ¶54); Cadmus, Jason, Orpheus, Aeneas are “the Rational Ferment” (III.1 ¶66); Cheiron “Power and Motive in alliance” (IV.1 ¶33); the vulture “the new born quintessential spirit or Proteus” (I.3 n11; QUOTED: Scholiast), the crow the same spirit “in a more obscured and passive condition” (I.3 n12); “By the king, the Rational Efficient is signified; by the brethren, the inferior degrees of illumination” (I.3 n50); “the masculine truly is the heaven of the feminine, and the feminine is the earth of the masculine” (I.3 ¶22; QUOTED: Hermes); “the Spirit is Eve, the Body is Adam” (IV.3 ¶7; QUOTED: philosophers via Eudoxus); the doves of Venus are “the maternal instinct” (APP ¶167; Appendix); “the Doves of Diana” (III.1 ¶14; QUOTED: Eirenaeus) are unexplained.

11. The product. Plain meaning: the purified life itself, made a fixed, luminous, self-multiplying form. “their stone is nothing more or less than the pure Ethereality of nature, separated by artificial means, purified and made concrete by constriction and scientific multiplication of its proper Light” (I.2 ¶44); “a fiery form of Light inspissate, and this is the Stone of Fire” (I.2 ¶44; QUOTED: the adept); “the most precious gold without spots” = “the Universal Ferment” (I.3 ¶60; QUOTED: Hermes); “the true Astrum Solis gotten and conceived, the internal spiritual Sun which is the Perpetual Motion of the Wise” (II.3 ¶52); “the Stone with the new name written in the Revelation” (II.3 ¶54); “the Corner Stone or first Material foundation of the Hermetic Art” (III.1 ¶66); “a pure petrification of the Spirit” (IV.1 ¶29); “a consummate and immortal Form of Light” (IV.1 ¶46); “a halo or crown of light, the regenerate soul puts on as a new body” (IV.4 ¶20); “a concrete Essence of Light, which Essence is the true Form or Idea of Gold” (APP ¶6; Appendix); “The soul in its metalline perfection is the philosopher’s stone.” (APP ¶181; Appendix). Also “Azoth, viz., the Universal Mercury, which alone with its internal and external fire is sufficient for thee” (I.1 ¶81; QUOTED: Khunrath); elixir, tincture and medicine (I.1 ¶73, ¶76) are co-references only; and the Appendix’s gold: “The Gold Alchemical is the first conceived Paternal Light — it is the seed metaphysical of mineral gold” (APP ¶20; Appendix).

12. The three lives (Appendix only). Plain meaning: the Appendix divides man into a lowest, mineral, generative life; a medial, celestial, heart life; and a head, rational life, and states the work as raising the lowest through the medial. “The third life, the mineral or lowest life in man” (APP ¶59); “the heart life, or the medial life, or the celestial life, or the vegetable life” (APP ¶97); “the Head life or the animal, the rational life” (APP ¶60); “There are three Lives to be represented. The Terrestrial, the Celestial, the Infernal.” (APP ¶32); “the Stone of the Wise is the perfected lowest life, its true consummation” (APP ¶46). The syntheses of Parts I–IV record no such scheme.

What the work is done on

  1. One thing only, present in every body; nothing foreign is added. “the whole matter I know to be one only thing” (I.3 ¶38; QUOTED: Hermes); “every body hath passive principles in itself for this Light to work upon, and therefore needs not to borrow any from gold or silver” (I.2 ¶8; QUOTED: the adept); “he who pretends to separate anything from our subject knows nothing of philosophy” (IV.3 ¶7; QUOTED: Pontanus via Eudoxus, endorsed). stated.

  2. In plain terms the matter is the operator’s own life, drawn out of himself. “Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art” (II.1 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); “The thing, O king, is extracted from thee, in the which mineral thou dost even exist” (II.1 ¶16; QUOTED: Morien, endorsed); “the Thing itself, which is our Identity. This is the true Hermetic material” (III.2 ¶7; stated; ATWOOD); “Man was the proper laboratory of the whole Art” (IV.4 ¶3; stated; ATWOOD); “The Hermetic Art is a perfectioning of the third or lowest or the mineral life in man” (APP ¶44; stated; Appendix). Part I says as much only through Hermes and the notes: “the work is both with you and about you” (I.3 ¶8; QUOTED: Hermes); the enquirers hold “within themselves the substance of that Universal Nature in which they, with all beings in common, as it were, unconsciously live” (I.3 n8; ATWOOD). Part I’s own voice chooses metals for experiment because “the metalline radix first presents itself in the experimental process, and is most easily apportioned” (I.2 ¶49; stated; ATWOOD).

  3. More exactly: the vital spirit in the blood, the Phantastic Spirit, the “impure vital atmosphere”. “the vitality of the greater world, which is included by respiration in the blood of all creatures” (II.2 ¶18; stated; ATWOOD); “the Phantastic Spirit, which is the natural vehicle of our life” (III.1 ¶5; stated; as the thing worked on, interp. from III.1 ¶6, “directed upon the same enigmatical source”); “The process takes place in and through the human body in the blood” (APP ¶6; Appendix); “The whole process of the Mystery was intended to purify the impure vital atmosphere which surrounds each individual life.” (APP ¶73; Appendix). Atwood herself hedges “the action of the vital heat stirred up artificially within the blood” (III.1 ¶58).

  4. Seen cosmically, it is the one spirit of nature: a middle, passive, convertible substance. “the simple generated substance of life and light, immanifestly flowing throughout nature” (I.2 ¶34; stated; ATWOOD, who adds that the definition does not teach how to work it); “a middle substance — passive, undetermined, susceptible of conversion and all extremes” (II.2 ¶26; stated; ATWOOD); “the elements of the Mercury, properties of the universal spirit” (I.2 ¶26; stated; ATWOOD); “a scrap of that ether shut in at our birth-conception” (APP ¶119; Appendix).

  5. It is no sensible substance; the common names deceive; it is found by going back in consciousness, not on earth. “it is no one of the many things with which sense brings us acquainted” (I.2 ¶34; stated; ATWOOD); “Not with our gross elements, our mercuries, sulfurs, and our lifeless salts; but in a far different nature, with stranger arts” (I.1 ¶33; stated; ATWOOD); “The pure matter of the philosophers is not to be found on earth” (APP ¶43; QUOTED: Raymond Lully, endorsed; Appendix); “go back in consciousness. It is immediately antecedent to the first consciousness.” (APP ¶137; Appendix). Against this Part I records “it is found flowing upon earth” (I.2 ¶29; QUOTED: Lully); see Open questions.

  6. As first found it is fallen, defiled and chaotic, yet everywhere and despised. “the ethereal spirit does not subsist of itself, separate or tangibly on earth; but, giving subsistence to other beings, is occultated even in their life, and defiled” (I.2 ¶20; stated; ATWOOD); “Our subject is presented to the eyes of the whole world, and it is not known!” (I.2 ¶33; QUOTED: Synesius / the New Light); “as a subject fallen from its sphere and defiled” (II.2 ¶16; stated; ATWOOD); “the medium in its natural state is volatile, immanifest, phantastic, irrational, and important, compared with what it subsequently is become” (IV.1 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD); “the chaos of te Vital Spirit (which always is in chaos, i.e., always coming to be, never is, and whilst in this life wants the rebirth)” (APP ¶165; Appendix).

  7. It carries its own agent and seed; beneath lies a latent divine Reason. “this water or mercury of the adepts has, within itself, its own good sulfur, or vital flame” (I.3 n5; stated; ATWOOD); “the Mercury of the Wise carries in its centre its own Sulphur, which coagulates it” (IV.3 ¶12; QUOTED: Eudoxus); “the total Reason is in this life of ours hidden, as the fire in fuel that is not kindled, or as gold in the dark ore unseen” (II.4 ¶39; stated; ATWOOD); “the rational ferment, the pure aurific seed of Light, is hidden in this life of ours” (APP ¶35; Appendix).

  8. All three principles lie in it together; what is dissolved is the bond to sense, not the body. “lie hid together in one thing, well known” (II.1 ¶11; QUOTED: Basil); “that which was fixed, viz., the sensual compact, is dissolved, and that which is dissolved is renovated” (II.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “And all without destruction to the mortal body” (II.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “Solution refers to the separation of the sensuous medium itself.” (APP ¶176; Appendix).

  9. A representative part suffices; it costs nothing; all have it, and having it qualifies no one. “Ramus, non arbor — The bough, not the whole tree, is to be taken” (III.1 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD); “the poor as well as the rich may have it in the same equal plenty” (II.1 ¶14; QUOTED: Aliphili); “all men have the material and live by it” (III.4 ¶2; stated; ATWOOD).

  10. The vessel is withheld; the Parts imply it is the life or body itself. “with exception of the vessel; which is a divine secret” (II.1 ¶8; stated); “in their gross body or composition” (III.3 ¶24; stated; ATWOOD); “The Spirit finally constructs its own vessel and vitrifies it” (III.4 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD). Part I presupposes a vessel and never discloses it (I.3 n22, n48). Its location is interpreted throughout.

  11. Having the matter is not knowing its preparation, which is never given. “I had now the prima material, the first principles, yet not their preparation, which is a thing most difficult above all things in the world” (I.1 ¶57; QUOTED: Flamel); Hermes never says “whence, when or how the Matter is to be taken” (I.3 n71; ATWOOD); “the Subject Matter and certain preliminaries, which are constantly concealed” (I.1 ¶64; stated; ATWOOD).

The agent

  1. The subject works on itself: freed, its own light transmutes it. “the thing transmuting also, when set free and segregated in its proper essentiality” (I.2 ¶8; stated; ATWOOD); “It is Mercury alone which perfects in our work” (I.2 ¶11; QUOTED: Aquinas); “purifying and to be purified itself by itself, in turn agent and patient” (II.2 ¶26; stated; ATWOOD); “The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself” (IV.3 ¶38; QUOTED: the theosophist, endorsed); “Alchemy is a process of fermenting the vital Spirit in and by its own Light.” (APP ¶27; Appendix).

  2. Light is the agent, and only Light multiplies. “It is the Light only that can be truly multiplied” (I.2 ¶8; QUOTED: the adept); “What else but Light can multiply, or does multiply, anyhow or anywhere” (IV.3 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD) — “not common Light, but that which in the first rotary wheel of life becomes efficient, which is potentially included everywhere in either kingdom of nature, though laid asleep” (IV.3 ¶30); “the Key thereof is Light” (IV.4 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD); “For the Light is with Power, and is God.” (IV.2 ¶41; QUOTED: Plotinus, endorsed).

  3. That light is purified Reason or Intellect; natural intellect cannot do it. “Mind to be the true Separator, the efficient as well as the regimen of the work” (III.1 ¶21; stated; ATWOOD); “the re-entering Light of Reason in the Mysteries, which is that Sulfur of adepts, causing all this manifold scenery in the disruption of life” (II.3 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); “But the natural Intellect cannot do this” (III.1 ¶20; stated; ATWOOD); natural reason is “as it were an iron key, intended to unlock the golden treasury of light within” (III.1 ¶13; stated; ATWOOD); “a small grain of the metaphysical ferment leavens the whole lump” (III.1 ¶24; ferment = Reason interp.); “The Rational Light is the strongest of all lights, and overcomes every other.” (APP ¶168; Appendix).

  4. Two fires, outer and inner; only the inner perfects; the philosophers’ fire is not common fire, and the prepared subject is itself that fire. “Include, therefore, and conserve in this sea, the fire, and the heavenly Flyer, to the latest moment of his exit” (I.3 ¶11; QUOTED: Hermes); “the internal alone is able, being purified, to perfect the work begun” (III.1 ¶43; stated; ATWOOD); “be not so careful of the fire of the Athanor as of your internal Fire” (III.1 ¶14; QUOTED: Eirenaeus, endorsed); “their own special fire as magical, creative, vital; whereas the common element is without sagacity or discrimination” (I.3 n41; stated; ATWOOD); “this same Lunar Caustic is brought into act by an exterior excitation” (IV.1 ¶29; stated; ATWOOD); “the Prester and the Sun, the external and internal fire of life, the thing desiring and the thing desired” (II.3 ¶41; QUOTED: the adept). Which fire is which in a given passage is never settled.

  5. Will, Motive, Mind, Motion. “Will is the greatest part of purgation” (II.2 ¶13; stated; ATWOOD); the Motive is “the true spiritual ferment” (IV.1 ¶44; stated; ATWOOD); “Mind is the Key of this Hermetic Enigma” (IV.1 ¶48; stated; ATWOOD); “Vulcan is not the fire, the ignis Philosophorum, but the stirrer up of the fire.” (APP ¶129; Appendix); “Will has substance, nay, is the only substance; it is the Universal Loadstone of Existence” (APP ¶8; Appendix); “that which Will has bound, Will only can unbind” (APP ¶125; Appendix); “The Universal Will being represented as agent dissolves the false forms in the self-will represented as patient.” (APP ¶94; Appendix).

  6. A kindred life is the ferment. “there is no ferment otherwise than from a kindred nature” (I.3 ¶58; QUOTED: Hermes); “Fire loves fire, say they, not to make gold, but to assimilate it” (III.1 ¶19; stated; ATWOOD); “The fire of the natural life entering into and fermenting the natural fire — the same life in another — opens the last, and develops and excites and sets free the celestial Life and Light — that is one main principle.” (APP ¶38; Appendix; “another” open).

  7. The dissolving principle is evil and poisonous, yet necessary; it is let grow and then bound. “though impure in the beginning, and manifestly evil, it is nevertheless a necessary ingredient” (III.1 ¶61; stated; ATWOOD); “this fire is suffered to grow profusely” (III.1 ¶56; stated; ATWOOD); “Sunk in ebriety; fast bind the god.” (III.1 ¶67).

  8. The decisive agent is divine: grace, the Fiat, media that work of themselves, a vertical ray from a divinized will. “by the inspiration of the living God alone” (I.3 ¶3; QUOTED: Hermes); “the Synthemata, i.e., the Theurgic aids and media themselves, perform by themselves their proper work” (II.4 ¶31; QUOTED: Iamblichus, endorsed); “This vertical separated Light then we take henceforth to be the true alternative principle in the Divine Art” (III.1 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD), from “the capable will of such only as are divinized” (III.1 ¶37); “the Divine Fiat comes mercifully to bless the union, and a new hypostasis is created out of the darkness to abide” (III.3 ¶10; stated; ATWOOD); “The work is performed by an invisible Artist; for there is a secret incubation of the Spirit of God upon nature” (IV.2 ¶18; QUOTED: the Adept, endorsed); “the Divine Will operates its fulfillment alone” (IV.4 ¶25; stated; ATWOOD); “it is love which attracts. Nothing else could move that principle from its self-conscious center into self-oblivion.” (APP ¶98; Appendix). None of the theurgic media is described.

  9. In the Gross Work the hand is the literal instrument. “the Hand was the instrument employed, not only to impart the Spirit as a natural gift, but by a continual mechanic trituration, as it were, to dissolve and ultimately obliterate its innate defects” (IV.1 ¶5; stated; ATWOOD with Van Helmont, Lully, Vaughan); “Nature cannot of herself enter into the dissolution, says the author of the Filum Ariadne, because she has no hands” (IV.1 ¶5; QUOTED). That the hand’s work is mesmeric passes is interpreted (IV.1 ¶4). Appendix: “The hand outward is an image of the invisible hand, its clothing” (APP ¶177).

  10. Inherent or supplied? Part I says both: “its proper Light” (I.2 ¶39) and “its own good sulfur” (I.3 n5) against “No, there must be an inward agent granted, Else would a thing unchanged still remain;” (I.2 ¶45; QUOTED: Eiranaeus) and “the external and internal agents” (I.3 n16; QUOTED: Scholiast). Unsettled.

The operator: what the work requires

  1. Theory, experience and the divine blessing. “three things said to be necessary for the attainment of the Hermetic science: viz., study, experience, and the divine benediction” (I.3 n1; stated; ATWOOD); “leave off experiments until the ground of wisdom is properly conceived” (I.1 ¶86; QUOTED: Bacon); “theory ought to precede practice” (III.1 ¶9; stated; QUOTED: philosophers, as Atwood reports). Against this, “Practice in the beginning is required, therefore, to interrogate and discern, from amongst so many shadowy representatives, the true light.” (I.2 ¶42; ATWOOD) and “The working theory, as we long ago suggested, can be obtained through the practice only” (IV.1 ¶18; ATWOOD). Both are hers; see Open questions.

  2. A pure, universal motive; never gain. “Wisdom must be sought for her own sake, neither for gold or silver or any intermediate benefit, lest these all should be denied together without the discovery of their source.” (I.1 ¶17; stated; ATWOOD); “nor will these studies be found to reward the sordid seeker after riches or gold only” (II.1 ¶30; stated); “if the motive be not universal, it must be returned” (III.1 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD); “In vain the ignorant, in vain the avaricious, the selfish, faithless, or frivolous, will in vain seek to percolate this Mystery.” (IV.3 ¶54; stated; ATWOOD); “Seek ye first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness” “is really the shortest as well as the truest road” (APP ¶134; Appendix).

  3. Moral purification: passions subdued, self-will removed, holiness of life. “to free himself from the vice of arrogance; and to be just and good, and of a profound reason, ready at hand to help mankind” (I.3 ¶25; QUOTED: Hermes); “a gradual subjugation of the passions” (III.1 ¶11; stated); “all arrogant self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice, and variability of purpose, all but the one voluntary faith to rectify and perfect, be removed from the mind of him who is to enter into the radical dissolution of Life” (IV.1 ¶40; stated; ATWOOD); “No impure leaven (need we repeat it?) can enter into Wisdom” (III.4 ¶7; stated; ATWOOD); “it is therefore necessary, before going into it consciously, that the will and purpose be perfectly pure and simple” (APP ¶27; Appendix).

  4. Desire, will and faith; faith as the ideal conception, with discriminating reason. “without desire on our part, art will labor for us in vain” (II.2 ¶13; stated; ATWOOD); “without faith, without the ideal conception, nothing is or can be proven” (III.3 ¶64; stated; ATWOOD); “the will only is wanting to discover the way, and faith to resuscitate the Corner Stone” (IV.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “Such skepticism, more properly perhaps called discrimination” (III.4 ¶15; stated; ATWOOD).

  5. Surrender of selfhood; prayer joined with work. “resign up themselves to become wholly one with that: else all their seeking is but a delusion” (III.1 ¶39; QUOTED: Boehme); “to abandon the life of selfhood” (III.4 ¶6; stated; ATWOOD); “every desire of the mind is in its kind a prayer” (III.4 ¶22; stated; ATWOOD); “Pray, says Sendivogius, pray; but work.” (III.4 ¶20); “not by prayer and supplication alone, but by faithful and charitable works preparing the way before Him” (IV.1 ¶40; stated; ATWOOD); “given up in passivity to the Divine Will” (APP ¶79; Appendix).

  6. Withdraw inward; meditate; investigate oneself as an actual practice. “Thou must enter with thy whole spirit into the center of nature, and there behold how all things are begun, continued, and perfected” (I.3 n19; QUOTED: Scholiast); “this advice was meant experimentally and ontologically” (II.1 ¶39; stated; ATWOOD); “profound meditation alone can give it to thee” (III.1 ¶37; QUOTED: Pontanus); “They only who have entered experimentally within to know themselves” (III.2 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD); “It is by the searching and proving of His own Identity, not otherwise, that man can arrive at the assurance of this Wisdom” (IV.4 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD).

  7. Patience, slowness, perseverance; no haste. “Gutta cavat lapidem non vi sed saepe cadendo.” (IV.1 ¶14; ours: the drop hollows the stone not by force but by falling often); “There is nothing stronger than Perseverance” (III.4 ¶44; QUOTED: Vaughan); “And in the Gross Worke he is furthest behind, That dayly desireth the end thereof to find.” (IV.1 ¶16; QUOTED: Norton); “Festina lente, Ne quid nimis, are sayings very applicable to the work” (APP ¶110; Appendix).

  8. Rare aptitude: a philosopher of stable wit, skilled hands, more than ordinary power. “None but a philosopher ever achieved the work, or for reasons that are imperative, ever will.” (II.2 ¶29; stated; ATWOOD); “But some Lords be of stable wit, Such only be apt to finish it” (III.4 ¶17; QUOTED: Norton); “cannot be brought to knowledge without sagacious handling and human help” (I.2 ¶18; stated; ATWOOD); “a great understanding of the Laws of Nature, and of the qualities of Our Matter, as well as a perfect knowledge of chemistry and of the different degrees of heat” (IV.3 ¶16; QUOTED: Eudoxus).

  9. Not alone — yet not from a master. “it can be revealed truly only by God, or by a master” (I.1 ¶36; QUOTED: Artephius); “no man, for reasons hereafter explicable, can operate the Hermetic artifice alone” (I.3 n17); “only follow your guide who will offer himself to you and will meet you in the way” (II.3 ¶32; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross); “without a Subject to work with and reciprocate the design it remains abortive” (III.4 ¶29; stated; ATWOOD); “without the inspiration of Heaven, or without the help of a faithful friend, one remains undoubtedly in this labyrinth” (IV.3 ¶7; QUOTED: Eudoxus). Against: “Nor can this secret be truly learned either from a master at all, but practice reveals it by the instinct of nature” (I.3 n36; QUOTED: Scholiast); “each must labor singularly for himself.” (IV.3 ¶54; ATWOOD); “You are always taken down there by spiritual guides; it is never human initiation, that” (APP ¶126; Appendix). Whether the companion is a person or a faculty is open.

  10. Hands off in the later work; cease intellectual effort at the end. “with the subject itself you have no more to do than the mother with child that is in her womb” (IV.2 ¶18; QUOTED: the Adept); “when we do not energize intellectually (all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)” (II.4 ¶31; QUOTED: Iamblichus, endorsed); “quietude and a cessation of mental energy are becoming and necessary to the consummating knowledge of the First Cause” (IV.2 ¶33; stated; ATWOOD).

  11. Secrecy, stewardship, humility after attainment. “That to yourself ye shall keep yt secretly; And only yt use, as may be God’s pleasure,” (I.1 ¶80; QUOTED: Ripley); “the Seal of God lieth before it, to conceal the true ground, unless a man knew for certain that it would not be misused” (II.1 ¶25; QUOTED: Boehme); “a good steward” (III.4 ¶20; ATWOOD); “watchful of every temptation, lest he should be drawn, even unwittingly, to abuse it” (I.3 n60; stated; ATWOOD).

  12. Further conditions. A woman can do the work: Perenelle “understands it as well as myself” (I.1 ¶57; QUOTED: Flamel). The Gross Work wants helpers “sober, wise, and diligent” (III.4 ¶37; QUOTED: Norton), a secret still place, and means: “Who would have trew worke may no labor spare, Neither yet his purse, though he make it full bare;” (IV.1 ¶16; QUOTED: Norton). Act from faith, not the head: “our business is to be efficient in the medial life — in faith” (APP ¶45; Appendix). Temperament matters: Mars and Venus know “by experience, not perception or intuition, which are the property of Mercury and Jupiter” (APP ¶113; Appendix).

The procedure, stage by stage

The frames the book gives. No Part gives one recipe, and the schemes are never mapped onto each other. Part I: a gross work of preparation (I.3 n56); a First Work of decoction “until, being at length on the verge of annihilation; i.e., freed from every exteriorly attracting form” (I.3 n7); then “the Second Work, which is only, in continuation of the First, to perfect the newly informed embryo and multiply its vivific light” (I.3 n7); the cycle “repeated seven times before the final resurrection of the Quintessence into a permanent form of life” (I.3 n40). Part II: Lesser Mysteries → Purificative Rites → final initiations (II.2 ¶20, hedged “as we take it”), equated with the Hermetic work, beside a laboratory colour-sequence at three-month intervals (II.1 ¶23–24). Part III: no single sequence, but an arc of preparation, inward turn, ferment and loss, dissolution in darkness, reunion under a divine act, repeated conjunctions, fixation. Part IV: on the reading IV.1 ¶17 favours, a Gross Work in two parts (manual, then the Solution of the Salt by Nature alone; whether this hands-off part is already Philosophic is open), a Philosophic or Subtle Work of three operations and a Fourth, and Fermentation as union with God; Eudoxus’ Six Keys stand beside this as a laboratory register of which Atwood says “literally taken they are not” (IV.3 ¶26). Appendix: two fermentations “by its similar” (APP ¶2–3) and two dissolutions (APP ¶182). The sequence below follows the common arc; placement of single operations is interpreted unless marked.

Stage 0. Preparation. Theory; the intention; a purified will; study of the records; the outward conditions of the Gross Work. “a sufficient theory” (IV.1 ¶2; ATWOOD); “The difficulty is to find this principium, the intention” (APP ¶35; Appendix). Flamel spent years “pondering the words of the philosophers, and then trying and proving the various operations which I thought they might mean by their words” (I.1 ¶57; QUOTED: Flamel); the preparation itself is never given. Deliberate backward “till he discovers the first Medium which is immediately in his power, in the discovery of which inquiry terminates, and the work, beginning from thence, passes into accomplishment” (IV.1 ¶31; stated; ATWOOD). Firm.

Stage 1. Entry: set the spirit in motion; withdraw from the senses. Plain terms: consciousness is turned inward and the vital spirit set moving; the entrancing of the senses, by another’s hand or eye or by a fixed gaze, opens the door but is only a first key. “the first step of philosophy is to set the mind a-going” (III.1 ¶8; stated; ATWOOD); “the soul of man, being estranged from the corporeal senses, adheres to a divine nature” (II.1 ¶9; QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed); entrancing the senses is “the key of the Hermetic vestibule” (II.3 ¶5; stated; ATWOOD); “by the hand or eye of another mesmerizing, or by a passive fixed gaze” (IV.1 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD), “a beginning only of the Hermetic art” (IV.1 ¶4); Mesmerism “is a first step indeed, and this only before the entrance of that glorious temple of Divine Wisdom” (IV.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “this life being fermented by this life, its similar, leads into the common trance” (APP ¶3; Appendix). Names: the vestibule; the Lesser Mysteries; “the common entranced life, i.e., the life of the sleep-walkers” (APP ¶2). Signs: trance; lucidity and prevision, which were not valued (II.2 ¶8, interp.). Firm as to rank; the mechanics belong to Part IV and the Appendix.

Stage 2. Solution: dissolve the life in its own blood; separate the subtle from the gross; descend into darkness. Plain terms: by repeated, gentle, continuous working the vital spirit is separated from its sense-born forms, the natural order of life is reversed, and consciousness is drawn down beneath its medium; the first “medial” light is cut off and the matter blackens. “the first work is to reduce the body into water, that is, into mercury, and this is called Solution, which is the foundation of the whole art” (I.2 ¶5; QUOTED: Friar Bacon); “The first rule of the work is a perfect solution” (II.2 ¶16; stated; ATWOOD); “the order of natural procedure ought to be introverted for a true and perfect manifestation” (I.2 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD); “to segregate the Vital Spirit away from those defilements and imaginative impressures which, by the birth into sense, had become implanted there” (II.2 ¶8; stated; ATWOOD); “to translate the consciousness underneath this medium by obscuration, towards the Central Source” (II.3 ¶5; stated; ATWOOD); “This Separation is indeed the primary object of the Art” (III.1 ¶21; stated; ATWOOD); “Nothing is done radically to meliorate the Vital Spirit previous to this dissolution of the first medial life” (III.1 ¶28; stated; ATWOOD); “depriving the ether of its wild affections and impressures by a dissolution of the circulating body in its own blood” (IV.1 ¶4; ATWOOD; terms unexplained); “the trituration of the Philosophic Subject, which ought to be slow, gentle, and continuous” (IV.1 ¶13; stated; ATWOOD); “Grind, coct, says the wise author of the Rosarium, and reiterate your labor and be not weary” (IV.1 ¶7); “reversing the circulatory order, so that, the sensible medium becoming occult, the inner source of its vitality is awakened, and the consciousness at the same time being drawn centrally” (APP ¶6; Appendix); “Dissolve the sensuous medium and you have the second or medial life.” (APP ¶182; Appendix). Names: putrefaction, night, Hades, the head of Hermes’ crow, the Black Saturn, the dragon, Typhon, the Augean labour. Signs: “sunk down black and motionless” (II.1 ¶23; QUOTED: our modern exponent); “a state of solicitude and painful amazement” (II.3 ¶29); “giving forth a murky odor, like that of graves” (II.3 ¶18); “the ethereal hypostasis is divided against itself” (III.1 ¶53); “a terrible sound is heard and a great fear is felt” (APP ¶50; Appendix). Firm that solution comes first and blackness marks it in I.3, II, III and IV; I.2 ¶36 puts a first whiteness before the annihilate state.

Stage 3. The verge of annihilation: catch and bind the bare spirit; the philosophic death; Nature alone. Plain terms: the dissolution is pressed until nothing of the old forms remains; the freed spirit is held, not let escape; the soul lets go even of its bond to the body’s life; in Part IV hands are taken off and Nature alone works. “to reduce the matter, with her false forms, to the verge of annihilation, but to entrap the bare spirit and help her on from thence to operate her own intrinsical freed will” (I.2 ¶24; stated; QUOTED: Democritus, endorsed); “it in some degree restores itself, if the force be continued” (I.2 ¶23; QUOTED: Bacon on Democritus); “torture and vex it in order to its annihilation” (III.3 ¶61; QUOTED: the adept, glossed “in the last exigence of life”); “the philosophers bind up their matter with a strong chain, that it may contend with the Fire” (I.3 ¶29; QUOTED: Hermes), “by means of the affinity which is between the body and its spirit” (I.3 n37; ATWOOD); “Take, my son, the flying bird and drown it flying” (III.1 ¶53; QUOTED: Hermes; also IV.1 ¶15); “let it be put into a furnace, which lute diligently, and observe that none of the spirit may escape” (I.3 ¶47; QUOTED: Hermes); “Shut thy vessel well that it may not breathe out” (III.3 ¶60; QUOTED: Synesius); the soul “may dissolve her own conceptive vehicle, even the parental bond, and return consciously (the elementary principles remaining, nor yet suffered to depart) under the dominion of another law to life” (II.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “By the Second Work we are to understand, therefore, the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond)” (IV.1 ¶36; stated; ATWOOD), where “hands and intellect are here alike incapable” (IV.1 ¶39) and “Nature alone operates, and without any imposition of hands, by the sole assistance of a well-regulated external fire” (IV.1 ¶11); “The human spirit has to die to everything that it now lives to, and lastly to its own identity, in order to become a simple receptacle and magnet for the Divine Seed.” (APP ¶87; Appendix); “Without the Golden Bough Aeneas could not proceed over or pass by Tartarus to see his Father. Tartarus is the death of this life.” (APP ¶168; Appendix). Names: the gate of blackness, Tartarus, the Stygian lake, the Solution of the Salt, “the Gordian Knot of the Hermetic Mystery” (IV.1 ¶37). Signs: “Now I have life, now I have none;” (III.1 ¶54); “dread fills the sould about to relinquish her natal bond in life” (II.3 ¶37); “pained with the void of the retreating infinitude” (III.2 ¶55); “there is a point of passivity, where there is no attraction but rather an indifference to both the life and the death; then the Artist must look to his Work, and seek to stir up the Motion” (APP ¶82; QUOTED: the Alchemists, endorsed; Appendix). Firm as a hinge in every Part; whether force or freeing is the rule, and whether the hands-off stage is still Gross or already Philosophic, are open.

Stage 4. Purification by repetition; whitening; the threshold of the Philosophic Work. Plain terms: light and darkness contend in the matter, the separation is repeated (seven times in Part I) until the spirit comes out white and clear; only then does the Philosophic Work begin. “The two contrary natures of light and darkness must contend together, as it were, in mortal strife, and the war must be waged unceasingly for the destruction of the foreign life until it succumbs” (I.3 n34; QUOTED: Scholiast); “Separate, says Eiraneaus, the light from the darkness seven times, and the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete” (I.3 n39); the matter does it to itself, “it dissolves and coagulates itself —whitens, rubifies, and makes itself to be both yellow and black” (I.2 ¶11; QUOTED: Aquinas); in Part II the soul holds course through the image-world, “strives to penetrate centrally without exploring the intermediate spheres), then she becomes a dry splendor from exhalation” (II.3 ¶27; QUOTED: Porphyry, endorsed), and “all those preparations, solutions, calcinations, etc. before it becomes to be the Mercury of the Philosophers” (II.2 ¶16; ATWOOD) are this purification (interp.); in Part IV the subject is “purified by successive interchanging of ferments, fretted, dissolved, and rectified into a consummate and immortal Form of Light” (IV.1 ¶46; stated; ATWOOD), by acts of will: “By applying the proper voluntary corrosive they teach to obliterate its defilements; by gentleness to mollify its Durity; by beneficence to sweeten its acerbity; by justice to moderate its intensity” (IV.1 ¶47; stated; ATWOOD). Whiteness: “Here thou mayest rest and wait, and enjoy the glory of thy white elixir” (I.3 n22; ATWOOD); “Till the white medicine be fully wrought;” (I.1 ¶60; IV.2 ¶13; QUOTED: Norton); “mild action of the principles in harmony have produced a brilliant whiteness in the matter” (II.1 ¶24; QUOTED: Boehme); “Then is your elixir perfect as to the white work” (III.2 ¶5). Threshold: “it is not until the right Motive is discovered, and until the mundification of the Spirit is completed in both kinds, and all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity, that the Philosophic Work has been said truly to being” (IV.1 ¶48; stated; ATWOOD); “The philosopher’s worke doe not begin Till all things be pure without and within” (IV.1 ¶49; QUOTED: Norton). Names: the white elixir, Luna, Elysium (whitening = Elysium interp., II T14), the First Key’s end. Firm that white follows black and precedes red; the number of repetitions is Part I’s; Part II’s Elysium and Part IV’s threshold are placed here by interpretation.

Stage 5. Reunion under the divine act; conjunction; the sudden light. Plain terms: the separated principles — spirit, soul, body — are rejoined in proportion; the purified passive spirit draws the soul back; God’s act makes the union; the mind’s eye is drawn to sudden contact with its source. “separate the matter into its essential relationships, and join them again together in harmonious proportion” (I.3 n23; stated; ATWOOD); “the spirit thereafter penetrates the body, and the body fixes the spirit — the soul being conjoined, tinges the whole of its proper color, whether white or red” (I.3 n21; UNCLEAR); “when we marry the crowned king to our red daughter, and in a gentle fire, not hurtful, she doth conceive a son” (I.3 ¶34; QUOTED: Hermes); “the soul when purified should associate with its generator” (II.3 ¶43; endorsed); “until light meeting light apprehends itself alone” (II.3 ¶50); “the eye of mind, all the while faithfully regarding, is drawn to contact suddenly, unable longer to sustain itself alone” (II.4 ¶19; stated; ATWOOD), “This is the method and arcane principle of Self- Knowledge, and the narrow way of regeneration into life” (II.4 ¶19); “the returning faith and desire of the Passive Spirit attracts the Soul again into herself” (III.3 ¶10; stated; ATWOOD), then “the Divine Fiat comes mercifully to bless the union” (III.3 ¶10); “In the first conjunction the Spirit predominates; in the second the Soul, i.e., its Light” (III.3 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); Freed Will and Understanding attract like steel and magnet, and agent and patient “must be united upon the throne of friendship and love” (IV.2 ¶30; QUOTED: the Adept); “Then thelight goes on seeking the Paternal Life, the fundamental life; and finding it through the maternal instinct (the doves of Venus) is conjoined” (APP ¶167; Appendix). Names: the marriage, the conjunction, Introspection, the Paternal Port, the Fiat. Signs: “come forth out of the black darkness together in union with the white virginal spirit, he will then know that he hath the great Arcanum of the world” (I.3 n53; QUOTED: “a more modern experimentalist”); “on a sudden a light, as it were a leaping fire, will be enkindled in the soul” (II.3 ¶50; QUOTED: Plato, endorsed); “Lux manifeste et visibilis ad oculum” (III.3 ¶10; ATWOOD’s gloss); “motum in te, experis internum et pro gaudio, lachrymabis!” (III.3 ¶9; ours: you will feel an inner motion in yourself and weep for joy); “underneath all this there is revealed that wonderful White Light” (APP ¶157; Appendix). Firm that a reunion follows the dissolution in every Part; that Part II’s contact, Part III’s Fiat and Part IV’s illumination (IV.2 ¶41) are one step is interpreted.

Stage 6. The Second Work: ferment, redden, fix. Plain terms: the new life is sown in its prepared matrix, fed by its own light, and passes from white to red; the volatile is fixed; Part IV names this fermentation and calls it union with God. “planted in its proper ethereal vehicle, well prepared and fallow to bring forth its virtue in manifold increase” (I.3 n40; stated; ATWOOD); “put in them the ferment confecting the earth with the water until the Ixir ferment even as dough ferments” (I.3 ¶58; QUOTED: Hermes); “the fermentive light is indeed white before the multiplication of its internal form has rubified it, and the silvery spirit is made manifest before the solar ray” (I.3 n67; stated; ATWOOD); the light “must be induced toenter, to suffer again, and die in order to transmute the foul material into itself” (I.3 n44; ATWOOD); “By a conjunction with its own permanent prepared spirit, the albified water is made red” (I.3 n54); “which in three months more become a brilliant yellow, red, or purpling tincture” (II.1 ¶24; QUOTED: Boehme); “To proceede with fire till all be Redd;” (III.1 ¶65; QUOTED: Greene Lyon); “To make by one fixt, one unfixed fixt” (III.3 ¶36), “without possibility of dissolution any more” (III.3 ¶35); “nourish it with its own blood, to the end that it may be converted into the fixed Sulphur, which is the Stone of Philosophers” (IV.2 ¶6; QUOTED: The Triumph, endorsed); fermentation is “the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God” (IV.3 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD), the Stone being fermented “with its own metal: for is not Light the true Aurific Seed” (IV.3 ¶30; ATWOOD); the Demogorgon “at length becoming ignited” (II.3 ¶52; ATWOOD; ignition as a stage interp.); “For production of effect there must be a return of the sulphur (this third, this obscure, this radical life) on its matrix.” (APP ¶183; Appendix). Names: the red garment, the Red Stone, the ignited Stone, Astrum Solis, the Fifth Essence, “the purple color is its ripened Life” (APP ¶119). Signs: “a most pure and rubicund tincture of the color of flesh and blood” (I.3 n42); “It is of a ruby color of the greatest weight; and this is a true sign of the tincture of the philosophers” (I.1 ¶76; QUOTED: Paracelsus). Firm that red follows white and that a fixing closes the work; the Appendix’s late sequence of “a wonderful birth of freedom to act every and realize every volition” then given up “under the Christ-life in its higher development and subjected to the Father’s Will” (APP ¶151) is placed here by interpretation.

Stage 7. Multiplication and projection. Plain terms: the finished light is dissolved and fixed again and gains in power each round; projected, it changes what it touches into its own kind; the Appendix adds that the matured life can ferment another life, and that the work is not finished in this world. “again dissolved and coagulated by the water of its mercury, white or red, of which it was prepared, then the tinging virtue will be augmented each time by ten degrees” (I.3 n70; stated); “if the process fails in any stage, an addition of the same will renew it” (II.1 ¶25; QUOTED: Basil Valentine); “the grand perfection of their Art was to multiply the Prester and place him in the most supreme Ether” (II.3 ¶56; ATWOOD); the five gates end in “multiplication and augmentation” and “projection” (III.3 ¶63; QUOTED); Part IV’s supplement has the matter “pass through rapidly in seven days” (IV.3 ¶18) and projection “by degrees until the Stone yields no more tincture” (IV.3 ¶20), with Atwood’s qualification, “even supposing the whole of the assertions to be true (and which literally taken they are not)” (IV.3 ¶26); Van Helmont’s proof, “the quicksilver, with a small voice, presently stood in its flux, and was congealed like to yellow wax” (I.1 ¶76; QUOTED: Van Helmont); “the Matter flows forth exteriorly from the new-born consciousness, it passes out into the hand and flows through the fingers, and is able to ferment another life and bring it into the same consciousness” (APP ¶164; Appendix); “it is not concluded in this world” (APP ¶153; Appendix). Firm that multiplication by repetition is stated in every Part; whether projection is literal is open.

On firmness overall. Stated orders: black → white → red (I.3 ¶9; II.1 ¶23–24; III.1 ¶65; IV.3 ¶3, ¶21); Lesser Mysteries → Purificative Rites → final initiations (II.2 ¶20); telete → muesis → epopteia (II.4 ¶23); First Work → Second Work (I.3 n7); Gross → Philosophic Work (IV.1 ¶48); manual then hands-off (IV.1 ¶11, ¶36); two fermentations and two dissolutions (APP ¶2–3, ¶182). Not stated: that these are one sequence; where Part II’s Elysium, Part III’s two conjunctions and three periods, Part IV’s three operations and Fourth, Eudoxus’ three works and the Appendix’s three lives fall against one another. Four ladders of names in Part I do not align: Atwood’s own (I.2 ¶40); Arnold’s “it is made citrine, then saltish, but without crystals” (I.2 ¶29); Aquinas’ unordered white, red, yellow, black (I.2 ¶11); the vulture’s “I am the White of the Black, and the Red of the White, and the Citrine of the Red” (I.3 ¶9). Numbers — seven distillations, fifteen days, thirty days, seven or ten months, three months, sixty days, “every day is a contracted year” (III.4 ¶44) — are only once glossed (“seven”, I.3 n47) and are stated to be “often to be understood metaphorically” (IV.1 ¶17).

Signs of progress

  1. Blackness at the start, with stillness, stench, dread and noise. “sunk down black and motionless” (II.1 ¶23; QUOTED); “giving forth a murky odor, like that of graves” (II.3 ¶18); “dread fills the sould about to relinquish her natal bond in life” (II.3 ¶37); “this also is accompanied with noise, as Haly says” (APP ¶50; Appendix). Absent blackness, nothing follows: “if they appear not in their time, they will never appear” (IV.3 ¶12; QUOTED: Eudoxus). stated.

  2. Corruption shows together with its cure. “the corruption is made manifest in the matter to be renewed, and hence the melioration will appear and each side is a signal of Art” (I.3 ¶44; QUOTED: Hermes); “the original evil is made manifest in the matter to be renewed” (II.1 ¶23). stated.

  3. Shifting shapes, demonic forms, Protean colours in the dark phase. “as clouds driven by the wind are forced to this and that figure, but cannot possibly retain one constant form” (II.3 ¶30; QUOTED: Vaughan); “in demoniacal forms is at first in vision made apparent” (III.2 ¶7); “from green to red and from red to black” (III.2 ¶20); “as a dark vapour, monstrum, horrendum, informe, ingens, cui lumen ademptum” (APP ¶71; Appendix). stated; visions or physical colours open.

  4. Test of a true light: without form; figured images are the mind’s own. “sees a mere apparition, as, for instance, of light in some form or figure” (II.3 ¶20; QUOTED: Psellus, endorsed); “know that to be the mind’s maya (or image or delusion) as of light or darkness” (II.3 ¶48; endorsed). stated.

  5. The colours must all be passed; whiteness, then the peacock’s tail, then red. “it is not perfected unless the colours be thoroughly passed and accomplished” (I.3 ¶5; QUOTED: Hermes); “a white fume, in substance like to pure silver, resolving bodies into their original whiteness” (I.2 ¶29; QUOTED: Atrephius); “until colors appear like a peacock’s tail” (IV.3 ¶21; QUOTED: Sendivogius); “liquid like resin, transparent like crystal, frangible like glass” (I.1 ¶76; QUOTED: Paracelsus). Appendix: green, “the first aspiration to something better”, then purple (APP ¶119). stated.

  6. The decisive sign: the masculine tincture rises from the black in union with the white. “come forth out of the black darkness together in union with the white virginal spirit, he will then know that he hath the great Arcanum of the world” (I.3 n53; QUOTED). stated.

  7. Sudden light; the day-star; the midnight Sun; the White Light; seen while awake. “you shall see the day-star arise, and the darkness will disappear” (II.3 ¶33; QUOTED: Brethren of the Rosy Cross); “At midnight I saw the Sun shining with a splendid light” (II.3 ¶45; QUOTED: Apuleius); “seen whilst vigilant you brave the deep” (II.3 ¶51); “in a moment, the solution will fall in; this is a revelation” (III.4 ¶22; QUOTED: Kirchringius); “we have seen Him, says Plotinus, when the Soul receives a sudden Light” (IV.2 ¶41); “a merging of the whole of the variety of Life into its first unity” (APP ¶157; Appendix). stated.

  8. The matter lives, moves and is felt; tears; the universe mirrored in it. “The Matter lives” (I.2 ¶45; QUOTED: Eiranaeus); “where a peculiar motion is present” (III.3 ¶24); “With thy hands thou shalt touch” (III.3 ¶9); “the pattern of the whole universe appears in those Subject Waters, as an image in a glass” (III.3 ¶39); “all natural secretion is superseded by super-essential hyperphysical exudation and tears” (APP ¶109; Appendix). stated; physical or inward open.

  9. Body asleep, mind awake; the lower eyes closed; agent and patient alternate in the inner sight. “the sleep of the body was the sober watchfulness of the mind” (III.3 ¶58); “there are two pairs of eyes; those which are beneath are closed, when those which are above are opened” (APP ¶163; QUOTED: Synesius, endorsed; Appendix); “The Matter is seen with the inner eye, either of the agent in the patient, or the patient in the agent, for in the working of the Material they alternate.” (APP ¶162; Appendix). stated.

  10. Certainty by contact; inquiry ends; once found, met everywhere. “that mind which is become wise needs not to investigate any further” (II.1 ¶37; QUOTED: Plato); “it will meet thee everywhere thereafter” (III.3 ¶78; QUOTED: Poemander); “When light meets light there is true self-perception, and man begins to know and to hate and to rectify himself and desire rectification.” (APP ¶126; Appendix). stated.

  11. Each stage opens new prospects; readiness shows as transparency; the finished work radiates outward. “it will solve itself at every stage, opening into new prospects within the veil of life” (IV.1 ¶44; stated; ATWOOD); “all things are reduced to a crystalline diaphaneity” (IV.1 ¶48); radiance “until it moved the dark circumference of sense itself” (II.4 ¶54; completion interp.); “The Golden Fleece is the outward appearance presented by the light of Life diffused over the body of the seeker in the third life” (APP ¶22; Appendix). stated unless marked.

  12. The proof addressed to the senses, and the false signs. “the transmutation of metallic bodies was a proof addressed to the senses” (I.1 ¶76; ATWOOD, reporting); “in less than an hour, the composition will pass from one color to another, till it comes to the perfect red or white” (I.3 n67; QUOTED: Maria). False: “Both red and white which were untrew.” (I.1 ¶65; QUOTED: Ripley); “the artist is rejoiced, and thinks perhaps his work is finished, and that he has the treasure of the world in hand; but it is not so” (I.3 n49); lucidity and prevision are not signs of the higher work (II.2 ¶8, interp.). stated unless marked.

Impediments and dangers

  1. Reading literally and working with furnaces, drugs or common fire. “surrounding many a literal soul with stills, coals, and furnaces, in hope by such lifeless instruments to sublime the Spirit of Nature” (I.1 ¶86; stated; ATWOOD); “men neither see nor do they believe there is any other fire but the common fire, nor any other sulfur or mercury — thus they are deceived by their own opinions” (I.3 n41; QUOTED: the adept); “modern art drives out, in fact, the very nature which the ancients prized” (II.1 ¶5; stated; ATWOOD); “Chemistry and all our experimental physics drive it forcibly without the means of Identification” (IV.1 ¶47; stated; ATWOOD); drugs “by influxion of darkness, drowns the oxygenating spirit, prostrates and confounds the mental powers” (IV.1 ¶4). Fire-digesters let the spirit escape, “which is the common catastrophe” (I.2 ¶24; stated; QUOTED: Democritus, endorsed).

  2. Haste, violence, too strong a fire, premature fixing. “Excess of one half quarter of an hour May destroy all” (IV.1 ¶12; QUOTED: Norton); “by force of too strong a fire” (III.1 ¶55); “burn not its flowers and its vivacity” (III.3 ¶60; QUOTED: Synesius); “if any permanent confection is made or suffered to take place beforehand, the immature offspring does not abide” (IV.1 ¶48; stated; ATWOOD); “it tempts you to break the seals upon the book of life artificially in order to hasten the work” (APP ¶102; Appendix); “Haste is the Devil’s part” (APP ¶103; QUOTED: Norton; Appendix). stated.

  3. Incomplete dissolution; discarding the heavy part; the Sphinx returns. “before the total solution of her enigma, the Sphinx should retrieve her dominion unawares and frustrate the work begun” (IV.1 ¶7; ATWOOD); “persuade themselves that they must only take the subtile and cast away the heavy” (IV.3 ¶7; QUOTED: Eudoxus); “if reason remains passive, this nature at length prevailing, will ravage and devastate and take possession of the whole mind” (II.2 ¶15; stated; ATWOOD). stated unless marked.

  4. Entering unpurified; imagination and desire make the images; evil imagination defiles the Spirit. “with this same misunderstanding and infected Spirit, we enter in for the discovery and contemplation of ourselves, it will be useless” (II.2 ¶14; stated; ATWOOD); “the phantasy, if suffered to prevail, will convert all to her own folly” (III.1 ¶21; stated; ATWOOD); “every imagination concerning it will be false” (III.2 ¶60); “it is self-desire that adulterates” (APP ¶105; Appendix). Yet Part III also credits “the elevating power which the imagination possesses” (III.1 ¶6); see Open questions.

  5. Dangers of the dark passage: capture, sleep, phantoms, healing spirits, the precipice, turning back. “lest unprepared souls, presuming to enter, should be taken captive by deluding and fatal desires, and work irremediable evil there” (II.3 ¶7; stated; ATWOOD); “To fall asleep in Hades was indeed to be absorbed, without the encumbrance of body, in all its defilements” (II.3 ¶12); “material images, assuming the appearance even of things divine” (II.3 ¶22; QUOTED: Psellus); “Stoop not down, for a precipice lies below in the earth;” (II.3 ¶42; III.2 ¶15; QUOTED: the oracle); “his natural inability to sustain the arduous altitude of divine contemplation” (II.3 ¶13); “No one”, says Vaughan, “enters into the Magian School but he wanders awhile in the region of Chimaeras” (APP ¶171; Appendix); “the revenging Demon” (III.3 ¶57). stated.

  6. Avarice, private ends, pride, power without conscience; the operator’s fault passes into the work. “this first and last defect of Avarice” (III.4 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD); “after that the study of ambition and gain were practiced, charity grew cold, mercy was extinguished, art perished, and the Giver of Lights withdrew his Gifts” (IV.3 ¶34; QUOTED: Helmont, endorsed); “lest the sin of the agent be multiplied in the patient, it should survive in the unhappy offspring” (IV.3 ¶56; ATWOOD); “Power thus gained is only legitimate, just and righteous when the will subserves the commandment and law of God; taken into the self-will and used accordingly it becomes diabolical” (APP ¶4; Appendix); “lest they should be made powerful in evil for the perpetuation of sinful things” (I.3 ¶11; QUOTED: Hermes). stated.

  7. The self-life appropriates the Light; stopping at intermediate gifts is idolatry. “Nature halts many times before this final rest, at each stage offering the fruits of her conceptive imagination to allure” (IV.1 ¶46; stated; ATWOOD); “rest in a mere metalline good, like Midas” (IV.3 ¶48; ATWOOD); “He first of all entraps the Light, and then he longs for more to feed that center which he has entrapped” (APP ¶72; Appendix); “you may stop in any of them; if you do, you commit idolatry” (APP ¶135; Appendix); “The creating itself into a being is the evil.” (APP ¶133; Appendix). stated.

  8. The false finish: the white light taken for the end; projection without fermentation. The light is “imperfect, alone, and transient, without the masculine tincture to fix it in manifestation” (I.3 n49; ATWOOD); unfermented, “there would ensue loss and damage in the projection” (I.3 n70). stated.

  9. Theory, enthusiasm, abstraction or unaided reason; or externals altogether. “But not reason, nor enthusiasm, nor ardent desire, nor an intellectual conception, nor abstraction, as we are taught, conjoins theurgist with the One” (II.4 ¶50; stated; ATWOOD); “stopped short nevertheless of objective realization” (III.2 ¶31); “every advance we make in externals leads us further off from the First Source” (IV.4 ¶18; stated; ATWOOD); the two opposite failures, “satisfied in the dreams and deluding visions of an included imagination” and “in whom the spirit of inquiry is wholly drawn to externals” (II.1 ¶39; stated; ATWOOD). “It requires Light to throw Light upon Light; darkness looking in and endeavoring only darkens.” (APP ¶118; Appendix).

  10. Lack of desire, faith or aptitude; instability; incredulity. “which being ignorant that it is ignorant has no desire to emerge” (II.2 ¶13; stated; ATWOOD); “They weene theire wits sufficient this arte to finde;” (III.4 ¶14; QUOTED: Norton); “Incredulity is the strongest barrier of possibilities over the world, no doubt wisely provided to prevent a too rapid movement of mind into practice, before it is well prepared” (IV.4 ¶19; stated; ATWOOD). stated.

  11. The books themselves: disordered, falsified, deliberately veiled; the withheld link. “introverted in their order, and dispersed with repetitions, to prevent the truth from becoming openly obvious” (I.1 ¶82; stated; ATWOOD); “when one imagines that they speak of one work, they often treat of another” (IV.3 ¶3; QUOTED: Eudoxus); Atwood’s own “willful reservation of an important link in the application of these principles to practice” (IV.2 ¶45; stated; ATWOOD); “we would deter all from hasty trial” (IV.2 ¶45).

  12. Danger to the operator from the work itself; death by the elixir; persecution. Bacon led “into forbidden realms of self-sufficiency and unlawful peace of mind” (I.1 ¶38; relayed report, ATWOOD “as it is suggested”); “a potent spiritual medicine, which would heighten the spiritual consequences of depravity and habitual excess” (I.1 ¶64; UNCLEAR); “thou wilt with my fire destroy thy five senses” (III.3 ¶64; QUOTED: Rosy Cross); “even the true adepts — for the public knew not to distinguish — suffered equal cruelty” (I.1 ¶66; ATWOOD). interp. as dangers of the work (I.1 ¶38, ¶64).

  13. Faulty outward conditions of the Gross Work. “Wind will hurt in everie case:” (III.4 ¶41; QUOTED: Norton); impressions that “pollute the ether” (III.4 ¶42); “the natural periods are protracted by faulty conditions from the commencement” (IV.1 ¶17; ATWOOD). stated.

The result

  1. The Stone: the purified life fixed as a concrete form of Light. “their stone is nothing more or less than the pure Ethereality of nature, separated by artificial means, purified and made concrete by constriction and scientific multiplication of its proper Light” (I.2 ¶44; stated; ATWOOD); “establishing her in a new and concentric Form of Light” (II.2 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD); “changed from a corporeal to a spiritual extreme” (III.1 ¶41); “a consummate and immortal Form of Light” (IV.1 ¶46; stated; ATWOOD); “a concrete Essence of Light, which Essence is the true Form or Idea of Gold” (APP ¶6; Appendix). Whether it is “Light essentialized in its proper substance” is put as a question, “an ultimate promise only” (I.3 n70).

  2. A ferment that transmutes and multiplies without limit, perfecting each thing in its kind. “which being transmuted, transmutes; and multiplying, multiplies its proper substance freely, in proportion to the virtue which it has acquired in the fermentation” (I.2 ¶48; stated; ATWOOD); “applied to any body, exalts and perfects it in its own kind” (I.2 ¶8; QUOTED: the adept); “one part thereof will tinge ten parts of an imperfect body, then one hundred, then a thousand, and so infinitely on” (I.3 n38); “it is a real ens of light” (APP ¶93; Appendix). stated.

  3. Self-knowledge: the Causal principle recalled; knower and known one; union in identity. “the Causal principle of nature is said to be developed into reminiscence and to arise in the experience of the recreated life” (I.3 n64; stated; ATWOOD); “Whosoever, therefore, shall know himself, shall know all things in himself; but especially he shall know God” (II.1 ¶9; QUOTED: Agrippa, endorsed); “nor as before, either in separation of subject and object; but absolutely, in Identity” (II.4 ¶42; stated; ATWOOD); “to meet with him, to be united to Him, and to see Him Himself — the Alone with the Alone” (II.4 ¶41; QUOTED: Plotinus, endorsed); “a conjunction with the Demiurgic Intellect” (III.4 ¶25; stated; ATWOOD); “The line returns to form a circle into its’ beginning, and they join not in time, for their union is Eternity.” (IV.3 ¶62; stated; ATWOOD); “True Science is the contact identical of the thing knowing with the thing known, subject with object.” (APP ¶171; Appendix). stated.

  4. Reason awakened and immortal; the faculties restored under their King; creative powers. “the germ of a higher faculty or Wisdom, which, when revealed and set alone, all the forms of things and hidden springs of nature become intuitively known” (II.1 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD); “until reason BECOMING A BRIGHT Light in the periphery of her fiery essence remains immortal, and is the Mistress of Life” (III.3 ¶80; ATWOOD); “with creative attributes and powers” (III.3 ¶52); the regenerate soul rules “over the elementary world and pass through it, overcoming evil and falsehood, and ignorance and death” (IV.4 ¶20; stated; ATWOOD); “as man, united with God, becomes divinely empowered, and therefore can do what he wills” (IV.3 ¶27; stated; ATWOOD); mind related to the Universal Power “and so can act Its will” (APP ¶3; Appendix). stated.

  5. Regeneration with the body preserved; moral transformation; the maker changed. “takes away from him the root of all sin and evil; changing his evil into good” (I.1 ¶57; QUOTED: Flamel); “the willing life was made to pass out of its present oblivious fall, through regeneration, into the reminiscent consciousness of her Causal Source” (II.3 ¶37; stated; ATWOOD); “this Essentiality it is which qualifies the true Adept; which sanctifies even as it qualifies” (III.3 ¶24; stated; ATWOOD); “above all, and most permanently, in the vital rectification” (IV.3 ¶30; ATWOOD); “the soul freed from the chains of corporeity, and the body is left as a mere husk” (APP ¶25; Appendix); self-will “becomes transmuted into a passive feminine principle” (APP ¶98; Appendix). stated.

  6. Medicine, gold and long life as by-products, in a sense the book asserts and withdraws. “a medicine the most potent and precious in the whole world” (I.2 ¶40; stated; ATWOOD); “cures all sorts of maladies whatever, and preserves any one in good health to the last term of his life” (I.3 n70; QUOTED: the adept); “much better indeed than common gold, more soft also, and more pliable.” (I.1 ¶57; QUOTED: Flamel); “the medicine of men and metals” (II.1 ¶25; QUOTED: Basil Valentine); gold and elixir “Both of which are vital products” (II.4 ¶58; stated; ATWOOD); “The fifth gate serves only for metals” (III.3 ¶63; QUOTED) beside “Reason alone can enter into it” (III.4 ¶11; ATWOOD); the object “was not gold however, or silver, but the substance of these things” (IV.3 ¶26; stated; ATWOOD); “The Gold Alchemical is the first conceived Paternal Light” (APP ¶20; Appendix). See Open questions.

  7. The higher aim: theosophy; proof of the unknown Cause; Christian regeneration; success in degrees and not concluded here. “universal theosophy, rather than to rest its capabilities of quickening and perfection in the mineral kingdom” (I.1 ¶42; stated; ATWOOD); “that very unknown Cause it was the alleged object of the Hermetic experiment to prove.” (I.2 ¶47); “with Christian regeneration for its most worthy end” (III.3 ¶61); “This, reader, is the true Christian Philosopher’s Stone” (IV.3 ¶62; stated; ATWOOD); “every step is progressive where the Rule of Reason is pursued” (III.4 ¶11; stated; ATWOOD); “it is not intended to be completed till the time comes” (APP ¶101; Appendix). stated.

Governing principles

  1. One matter, worked by and through itself; nothing foreign; like works on like. “fixed by the reciprocated action of its own proper Identity, as agent and patient, alternating to improve” (I.3 n21; QUOTED: Scholiast); “no foreign admixture is allowed to enter” (III.1 ¶21; stated; ATWOOD); “for the like is intelligible by the like” (III.3 ¶78; QUOTED: Poemander); “The whole work is an action of agent on patient, and the reaction of patient, advancing, on agent.” (APP ¶111; Appendix).

  2. Death before life; no discovery without decomposition; separation begins generation; what generation binds dissolution unlooses. “The destroyers are the renovators” (I.3 ¶44; QUOTED: Hermes); “separation was the principle and beginning of all generation.” (I.2 ¶25; QUOTED: Paracelsus); “nothing is discovered except the matte be decomposed” (III.1 ¶21; stated; ATWOOD); “Death is the way of Life” (III.2 ¶55); “That which generation binds dissolution only can unloose” (IV.4 ¶17; stated; ATWOOD); “that which Will has bound, Will only can unbind” (APP ¶125; Appendix).

  3. Reverse nature’s order; reduce to the origin; the end is in the beginning. “the reduction of bodies to their original matter, by introversion of the generated life, is requisite to a true manifestation and permanence in any form” (I.2 ¶49; stated; ATWOOD); “The aim in the Work is to reverse the whole action of these lives in their natural order.” (APP ¶35; Appendix); “Which Ferment must be of such a thing, As was the workes begyning;” (III.1 ¶65; QUOTED: Greene Lyon); “The end depends from the beginning” (IV.1 ¶46; stated; ATWOOD); “according to the virtue of the fermenting principle is the result obtained.” (IV.1 ¶44).

  4. Only Light multiplies; the ferment is kindred; each repetition improves the parent in the offspring. “as leaven makes leaven, and every ferment begets its own exaltation” (I.3 n55; stated; ATWOOD); “the parent is bettered in the offspring, even to the fourth generation, or fifth” (III.1 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); “there cannot come of any thing that virtue which it has not” (III.1 ¶43); “It is an ascending, not a descending, process.” (APP ¶132; Appendix).

  5. Transmutation is of the subject, not of species. “Species non transmutantur, sed subjecta specierum optime et propriisime” (I.2 ¶5; QUOTED: Rosarium; ours: species are not transmuted, but their subjects are, best and most properly); “relating not to Species but to their Universal Subject, whose concentrated virtue the Stone likewise itself is” (IV.4 ¶2; stated; ATWOOD).

  6. As above, so below; imitate nature and help her exceed herself; the work repeats creation. “that which is above is as that which is below, and that which is below is as that which is above, for performing the miracles of the One Thing” (I.1 ¶9; QUOTED: Hermes); “Thus nature, by the help of art, is said to transcend herself” (I.2 ¶44; stated; ATWOOD); “you must help her, that she may exceed her common course” (III.1 ¶13); “The laws of Nature are the rules you ought to follow in the practice of our magistery.” (IV.3 ¶14; QUOTED: Eudoxus); “Alchemy goes beyond nature; it takes her up and advances her beyond her created it and in this world.” (APP ¶155; Appendix).

  7. Microcosm and macrocosm; the three principles are modes of one thing; three in one, then a fourth. “Thou hast in thy body the anatomy of the whole world” (II.1 ¶10; QUOTED: Sendivogius); “consubstantial in their Cause” (III.2 ¶35; stated; ATWOOD); “the threefold law of life; the ingress, egress, and alternatig action of which, under dominance of either of its principles, constitutes the whole phenomena of the Hermetic process” (I.3 n35; stated; ATWOOD); “reduce the Ternary by the Quaternary, through the rejection of the Binary, to the simplicity of the Monad” (IV.2 ¶18; QUOTED: the Theosophist, endorsed).

  8. Self-Knowledge is the root; knowing is by contact; only the experience proves. “Self-Knowledge to be at the root of all Alchemical tradition” (II.1 ¶30; stated; ATWOOD); “intuition is the evidence and end of every rational proof” (II.1 ¶33); “the evidence is self-sufficient, and without itself cannot, however truly imaginable, be known” (IV.4 ¶12; stated; ATWOOD); “True Knowledge is experimental contact of mind with mystery” (APP ¶75; Appendix).

  9. Grace decides; man conditions, God gives; the final method is passive. “Light is not given to reason except God impart it” (III.1 ¶23; stated; QUOTED: the Magian); “he cannot compel, much less impart, the Divine blessing on his handiwork” (III.4 ¶20; ATWOOD); “the passive method of the Divine Intuition, and the three elevating causes of love, hope, and faith” (II.4 ¶40; QUOTED: Proclus, endorsed); “it is the duty of man’s will to follow God’s Will as He, according to His time and measure, opens the way” (APP ¶110; Appendix).

  10. Every desire is a prayer; intention returns in kind; hence secrecy. “No operation, however, in sacred concerns, can succeed without the intervention of prayer.” (III.4 ¶24; QUOTED: Iamblicus); “the intention is received back according to its kind most exactly” (III.4 ¶9; stated; ATWOOD).

  11. Theory open, practice hidden; read the spirit, not the letter; the symbols are real. “the roots of the Hermetic science are fairly stated; but the practice most carefully concealed” (I.1 ¶39; stated; ATWOOD); “All here is to be understood ethereally” (I.3 n49); “unhappy he, who mistakes the vestment for the Law itself” (III.3 ¶59); “They, we repeat, who can understand the language of the philosophers, will understand their Art” (IV.4 ¶32; stated; ATWOOD); “The Alchemical symbols, phrases, metaphors, which so disguise the art are more than what they seem; they point out a reality” (APP ¶61; Appendix).

  12. The Mysteries and the Art are one; the fables record real experience; the art is theurgy. the Vital Experiment is “the vital alteration that was deemed necessary and operated in the Mysteries” (III.1 ¶4; stated; ATWOOD); the Mysteries were “real inductions of the Understanding Spirit to its Source” (IV.4 ¶5; stated; ATWOOD); “represents the rites themselves as really efficacious to procure it” (II.2 ¶4); “the Hermetic is the only true key to the old myths, and to Homer’s Iliad and Odyssey” (APP ¶157; Appendix).

How the Parts differ

Part I (history, theory of the first matter, the Golden Treatise with Atwood’s notes) keeps the laboratory register: Athanor, sealed vessel, crucible, gold ferment, the colours as the adepts give them. Its own voice supplies the theory of the one subject as the Ether or Universal Spirit (I.2), the frame of two works (I.3 n7) and the inner register in the notes — “the work is both with you and about you” (I.3 ¶8), “the Hermetic method of Self-Knowledge” (I.3 n46) — while deferring the subject’s properties, the “conditions of success” (I.1 ¶39), the fire’s “artificial excitation” (I.3 n14) and the Rational Ferment (I.3 n32). It chooses metals because their root “first presents itself in the experimental process” (I.2 ¶49).

Part II moves the whole art into the human being — “Man then, shall we conclude at length, is the true laboratory of the Hermetic art” (II.1 ¶30) — identifies the subject with the Mundane Spirit and the mesmeric agent (II.2 ¶18), and equates the Hermetic work with the ancient Mysteries, supplying the order Lesser Mysteries → Purificative Rites → final initiations and the figures of Hades, Elysium and the Paternal Port. It adds the test of a true light (formless) and the passive final method.

Part III names the agent: Reason, Intellect, Light, “no other Fire but that of Intellect” (III.2 ¶62), “Mind to be the true Separator” (III.1 ¶21), and the “vertical separated Light” from a divinized will (III.1 ¶37). It states the mental requisites in full (III.4), gives the Gross Work its outward conditions through Norton, and holds that the physical side is “a secondary vestiment or witness” (III.3 n27) of an inner process, “both in an internal and in an external sense” (III.2 n38).

Part IV is the practice: a manual, repeated Gross Work entered through the entrancing of the senses “by the hand or eye of another mesmerizing, or by a passive fixed gaze” (IV.1 ¶4), with the literal Hand as instrument; a hands-off Solution of the Salt by Nature alone; a Subtle Work of three operations and a Fourth in which Mind alone builds; Eudoxus’ Six Keys reproduced and then declared not literal (IV.3 ¶26); fermentation as union with God; and the admitted “willful reservation of an important link in the application of these principles to practice” (IV.2 ¶45).

The Appendix (Table Talk, 1860 onward) is a later layer, almost wholly in Atwood’s own voice. It introduces the three lives (lowest, medial, head) and states the work as raising the lowest through the medial; makes Will the only substance and the Salt; names bodily seats (blood, heart, lumbar region, feet, hand) and a mesmeric cross of perpendicular and horizontal action; adds green and purple to the colours; and says the work “is not concluded in this world” (APP ¶153). The syntheses record no passage in which it cites, revises or contrasts the 1850 book, so no stated difference between the layers can be asserted; whether it develops or restates the method is open.

Open questions

  1. On the operator, or on a material in a vessel — or both? Every Part raises it. Part I: inner notes (I.3 n8, n19, n46, n64) beside laboratory notes (n57, n67, n70) and Van Helmont’s crucible (I.1 ¶76); Atwood followed “a certain experimental clue” she does not name (I.3 n71). Part II: the colour-process with “powder”, “three months”, “gas water, or oil of this antimony” (II.1 ¶23–25) glossed “(in the vital spirit)”; elixir and gold “vital products” (II.4 ¶58). Part III: “Apply nomanual labor” (III.3 ¶9) against “Manual Operation” (III.1 ¶37) and “With thy hands thou shalt touch” (III.3 ¶9); the physical “a secondary vestiment or witness” (III.3 n27). Part IV: the literal Hand (IV.1 ¶5) against “Proceed then patiently but not manually.” (IV.2 ¶18); physical results asserted “in plain physical type and experience” (IV.3 ¶48) and the literal reading denied (IV.3 ¶26). Readings: one inner process with material by-products; two parallel processes; “Hand” as the soul’s instrument throughout. Not resolved.

  2. One person or two? “joining self to self” (III.1 ¶8), “each must labor singularly for himself.” (IV.3 ¶54), “it is never human initiation” (APP ¶126) against “mesmerizing one another” (III.1 ¶8), “a Subject to work with” (III.4 ¶29), “by the hand or eye of another mesmerizing” (IV.1 ¶4), “the same life in another” (APP ¶38), “I throw its purpose into your mind” (APP ¶126, which begins “Supposing”). The “brother” of I.3 n17 and the companion of II.3 ¶32 are unidentified.

  3. Mesmerism’s place. “a first key” (III.1 ¶8; IV.4 ¶5), “the neglected Door-keeper” (III.4 ¶13), the public form of the entry (IV.1 ¶3–4), “a very external way — an imitation of the Divine Art” (APP ¶73). Method, or Atwood’s way in.

  4. Theory first or practice first. I.1 ¶86, I.3 n2 and III.1 ¶9 against I.2 ¶42 and IV.1 ¶18, all Atwood’s. Whether early practice is the “gross work” (I.3 n56) is unsaid.

  5. Master, guide or none. I.1 ¶36, I.1 ¶57, II.3 ¶32, IV.3 ¶7 against I.3 n36 and APP ¶126.

  6. Light inherent or supplied. I.2 ¶39 and I.3 n5 against I.2 ¶45, ¶49 and I.3 n16; the “superior ferment or attraction” of I.2 ¶4 is unnamed.

  7. One substance or two. One matter, nothing added (I.2 ¶11; I.3 ¶38; IV.3 ¶7) against “the union of masculine and feminine spirits” (I.2 ¶33), “the fire, and the heavenly Flyer” (I.3 ¶11), “mortify two Argent vives together” (I.3 ¶32; III.1 ¶43), “a living male and a living female” (III.1 ¶50), “That Two Natures be reduced to One” (IV.3 ¶38). The reconciling line is that the two are relations within one thing (I.2 ¶31; I.3 n30; III.3 ¶67); which of each pair is which is never mapped.

  8. Force the spirit or free it; spontaneous or willed. “if the force be continued” (I.2 ¶23; III.3 ¶61) and “a strong chain” (I.3 ¶29; III.2 ¶22) against “help her on from thence to operate her own intrinsical freed will” (I.2 ¶24) and “by means of the affinity” (I.3 n37); “spontaneously” (III.2 ¶8) and “works not of itself” (III.2 ¶16) against “Springing directly from ourselves” (III.4 ¶9); striving “diametrically opposed to the presence of the thing desired” (III.2 ¶57) against “the meritorious struggle of the powers” (III.3 ¶71).

  9. Active intellect or passive reception. II.2 ¶15, II.3 ¶5 and III.1 ¶20 demand rational energy; II.4 ¶31, ¶40 and IV.2 ¶33 demand its cessation. Probably stages, “(all preparative conditions having been fulfilled)” (II.4 ¶31); not stated.

  10. Imagination: agent or hindrance. “the elevating power which the imagination possesses” (III.1 ¶6) against “every imagination concerning it will be false” (III.2 ¶60) and the Appendix’s “it is self-desire that adulterates” (APP ¶105).

  11. What the Salt is. The voluntary bond (IV.1 ¶36), Will (APP ¶65), Khunrath’s soul–body bond (IV.1 ¶36), Wisdom and the Root of Reason (IV.4 ¶20; III.3 ¶50), the Identic grain (III.2 ¶20), the dark Demogorgon (II.3 ¶41), the impure Instrument (III.1 ¶61). No cross-chapter identity is asserted.

  12. Which thing is the Key, the Separator, the Fire. Mind (IV.1 ¶48; III.1 ¶21) or Light (IV.4 ¶32); Pontanus’ Fire, Basil’s Antimony, the vertical Light (III.1 ¶37); the Secret Fire (IV.2 ¶6; IV.3 ¶5), Lunar Vulcan (IV.1 ¶29), “a certain other Vulcan” (IV.1 ¶40), three fires and a Fourth (IV.2 ¶11); “moist fire” (I.3 ¶10). Never stated to be one. Who is “named, openly” (IV.1 ¶37) is unsaid.

  13. The vessel. “vas philosophorum est aqua eorum” (II.1 ¶8), the living temple (II.1 ¶8), the gross body (III.3 ¶24), glass (III.4 ¶31), the vessel the Spirit “vitrifies” (III.4 ¶32), one uniform vessel (IV.1 ¶9), the concealed vessel of I.3 n48; whether vessel and matter are one, compare “compact in one” (I.1 ¶33).

  14. Order of colours and stages. The four ladders of Part I; I.2 ¶36’s first whiteness against I.3’s first blackness; the two Elixirs of I.2 ¶46, ¶49; Part II’s whitening = Elysium and reddening = ignition without stated counterpart; Part III’s two conjunctions, three periods and five gates; Part IV’s two-part Gross Work against the “Supernatural Work” (IV.1 ¶6), Eudoxus’ Keys straddling his Works (IV.3 ¶9), the recurring three-then-Fourth; the Appendix’s two fermentations against its two dissolutions. None is aligned by the text.

  15. Literal gold, medicine and riches. Asserted (I.1 ¶57, ¶76; II.1 ¶25; III.3 ¶63; IV.4 ¶10 “dowry”) and withdrawn (III.4 ¶11; IV.3 ¶26; IV.3 ¶24 “(let not the allusion here be misapprehended)”). The book does not say what the “physical type” is.

  16. Timing. Seven, fifteen, thirty days, three months, sixty days, seven or ten months, three years, “until it be the Divine pleasure” (III.3 ¶80), “every day is a contracted year” (III.4 ¶44), “often to be understood metaphorically” (IV.1 ¶17). Literal or not: unsaid.

  17. Found on earth or not. “it is found flowing upon earth” (I.2 ¶29) against I.2 ¶20, ¶30, APP ¶43, ¶137.

  18. Appendix-only cruxes. “Third life” as start or goal (APP ¶35 against ¶3, ¶22); becoming creative as goal or forbidden (¶7–8 against ¶35, ¶151); the head life as sensuous or highest (¶32 against ¶35, ¶134); perpendicular and horizontal magnetism as passes or inner directions (¶13, ¶73, ¶173); the Golden Bough as the Light itself or an offering (¶152 against ¶168, ¶171); Vulcan and Minerva at one altar (¶82); “the Salt, the will, opens it secondfold” (¶182); the relation of the whole layer to the 1850 book.

  19. Deferred, undecoded and corrupt. Part I defers the subject’s properties (I.1 ¶24), the “conditions of success” (I.1 ¶39), the fire’s excitation (I.3 n14), the Rational Ferment (I.3 n32), why no one works alone (I.3 n17), Self-Knowledge (I.3 n46), “introversion” (I.2 ¶27). Part II never describes its means — “mystical exercises and preparations” (II.1 ¶37), “the true ferment” (II.2 ¶26), “Pontic Medium” (II.4 ¶49). Part III leaves “triply imprisoned” (III.1 ¶13), “Doves of Diana” (III.1 ¶14), “Bath of Diana” (III.1 ¶68), “the Door” (III.4 ¶44) and “Master Key” (III.4 ¶47) undecoded. Part IV withholds the link (IV.2 ¶45) and the device passed “almost by word of mouth only” (IV.4 ¶7). Readings hang on probable misprints: “unassisted nature” perhaps lacking a “not” (I.2 ¶49); “compounded” against “decompounded” (I.3 ¶5, n4); “fresh i” (II.1 ¶25); “important” for the natural medium (IV.1 ¶4); “the manifestation of the **** in man” (APP ¶168).

  20. Further unresolved points. Whether the sum of the Mystery is the illumination alone or the whole sequence (IV.2 ¶43 against IV.3 ¶62); whether the Nature annihilated is universal Nature or the operator’s own (IV.1 ¶36; IV.2 ¶6; IV.4 ¶6, ¶12); whether the Central Source (II.3 ¶5) is the dark Salt whose “Being is Tartarus” (II.3 ¶41) or the Generator (II.3 ¶37, ¶43); whether II.3 ¶11’s golden bough (intellect’s ray) and II.4 ¶7’s Golden Branch are one; who dies in the work (III.1 ¶26, ¶30, ¶49).
