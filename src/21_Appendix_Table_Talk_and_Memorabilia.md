# Table Talk and Memorabilia of Mary Anne Atwood

*Appendix: Table Talk and Memorabilia*

The Appendix is a separate, later layer of the book, headed "Begun August 1860": a long run of numbered sayings and short notes, not a continuous argument (APP ¶1). Across them Atwood restates the Hermetic Art in compressed form. Alchemy is "an universal art of vital chemistry" which ferments, purifies and finally dissolves the human spirit, opening "the elementary germ into new life and consciousness" (APP ¶6, ¶68). The work is done in man's own life, in the blood and body, and everything depends on a will that is pure and subject to God (APP ¶4, ¶27, ¶35).

## The three lives and the aim of the Work

Atwood names three Lives. The Terrestrial is the sensuous life, belongs to the Animal Kingdom and dominates the head; the Celestial is the emotional, receptive life of the Vegetable Kingdom, seated in the epigastric region with the solar plexus as its workshop; the Infernal is "the voluntary agent in life", the Mineral Kingdom, seated in the lumbar region (APP ¶32–33). Man, a microcosm, comprehends all three (APP ¶35). "Man is fallen; he is in the third or lowest life", and "The aim in the Work is to reverse the whole action of these lives in their natural order": the third life is raised and born through the medial or celestial life until it becomes receptive of the Divine. Man must not aspire to the head life above, for that would be "taking to himself the Divine Will" (APP ¶35). Our present state is "one of inversion"; we should be effective in the medial life, which is faith (APP ¶45). The Art is thus "a perfectioning of the third or lowest or the mineral life in man", and the Stone is that lowest life perfected, the analogue of Christ the Head Corner Stone (APP ¶44, ¶46). Atwood's sayings call this lowest life both what man is fallen into and the state reached by fermentation (APP ¶2–3, ¶22); the text leaves open whether these are the same life before and after its release.

## Fermentation, the central operation

"The word fermentation conveys an idea of the perfecting principle and of the possibility of transmutation beyond any other word" (APP ¶27). Present life, fermented by "its similar", passes into the common trance of the sleep-walkers; fermented again, into the third life, which "moves in and with the creative Essence" (APP ¶2–3). The text does not say whether "its similar" is another person's life or the same life acting on itself. Elsewhere Atwood states the "main principle": the fire of natural life, entering and fermenting "the same life in another", frees the celestial Life and Light; when one fermented life passes into another equally fermented, the patient becomes more perfect than the agent was (APP ¶38, ¶42). The philosophers' "take that stone" means "take life as it is and ferment it", that is, "by another life", both dying and reproduced in a third; the process is "ascending, not a descending" (APP ¶132). "The whole work is an action of agent on patient, and the reaction of patient, advancing, on agent" (APP ¶111).

The fermentation "has to be gone over and over again", which is why Hermetic books, describing different repetitions, seem to mix up their steps (APP ¶17, ¶84). Mesmeric passes, vertical and transverse, give an "external" imitation of Paracelsus's "Divine Art of the separation" (APP ¶13, ¶73). It begins with motion: "The first step in this Philosophy is to create motion in the spirit", the mind being the motive agent and Vulcan its instrument (APP ¶130); once freed, the spirit "works by itself, by its own law" (APP ¶131).

## Will, desire and the danger of self-will

"Will has substance, nay, is the only substance" (APP ¶8, ¶86); it is "the principle of the body --- the Salt" (APP ¶65). Will alone creates an image; passing into desire, it unites with the image into a being (APP ¶36). Hence the dangers. Power gained is righteous only "when the will subserves the commandment and law of God"; in self-will it becomes "diabolical", as with the Egyptians' enchantments against Moses (APP ¶2, ¶4–5). Forming the First Matter from one's own mind for private ends is "the true idolatry" and the origin of talismans (APP ¶37); a graven image is "a vocal utterance carved into the understanding spirit" and worshipped inwardly (APP ¶100); to stop in any gift short of the Universal is idolatry (APP ¶135). Haste is another form of self-will: Norton's "Haste is the Devil's part" and Ripley's "red poppies" warn against forcing the work (APP ¶102–103, ¶110). The cure is surrender: the spirit "has to die to everything that it now lives to, and lastly to its own identity" (APP ¶87), and the regenerate are born "of his own will, given up in passivity to the Divine Will" (APP ¶79). The will must aim singly at the highest good, since Lucifer's stirring of the central fire in his will would "burn up the ether which was Heaven" (APP ¶133); "Seek ye first the Kingdom of God" is the shortest road (APP ¶134).

## Body, blood and the signs of progress

"The process takes place in and through the human body in the blood", reversing the circulatory order so that consciousness is drawn inward to its First Cause (APP ¶6). Salt, Mercury and Sulphur are the blood's Attraction, Circulation and Repulsion; "life is attracted in, instead of out" (APP ¶24). Reiterated contritions change the circulation and carry the "ens of light" back to God (APP ¶78); the Mystery purifies "the impure vital atmosphere" around each life (APP ¶73).

Atwood records signs. The Golden Fleece is a purplish luster of life-light over the seeker's body in the third life, and at that point the seeker is the adept (APP ¶22–23). Released light shines "with a sudden coruscation" (APP ¶126). Natural secretions give way to "hyperphysical exudation and tears" (APP ¶109). In the "Philosophical dissolution" the body lies breathless, soul and spirit held to it "as by a thread" until refixation (APP ¶96). A principle of body survives in the "ashes or Caput Mortuum", and the whole life is restored from it (APP ¶26).

## Old images read as the Work

The sayings decode many images. The walls of Troy, built by Apollo's music, figure the enclosing of the lower life, and their fall is the "solution", accompanied with noise and fear (APP ¶49–50). "The body of brass is the impure natural-born vital spirit" (APP ¶52). The mineral soul first appears as an Augean Stable; Saturn, self-will, is filled with Rational Light until it bursts like a seed (APP ¶71–72). "Saturn (i.e., Will) has the Key to the prison where Sulphur lies bound" (APP ¶125). "The Vulcan of the Alchemists is Motion", lame because motion never returns into its principle (APP ¶129). Proteus is the one universal life before individuality, whose shifting forms explain the contradictory descriptions of the Matter; Aristaeus's lost bees are "the faculties of his soul" (APP ¶138, ¶141, ¶144). Aeneas's Golden Bough is "the Divine aurific seed", without which he cannot pass Tartarus, "the death of this life", to his Father (APP ¶152, ¶168). Atwood holds the Hermetic to be "the only true key" to Homer and Virgil (APP ¶157).

The closing notes give short definitions: "The One Thing; it is our psychical essence; our nervous system"; Solution is separation of the sensuous medium; Ether is the universal Matter and the philosophers' Metals its stages; "The soul in its metalline perfection is the philosopher's stone" (APP ¶176, ¶179–181). "Alchemy is philosophy; it is the philosophy, the seeking of The Sophia in the mind" (APP ¶183).

## Knowledge and the writers judged

"True Knowledge is experimental contact of mind with mystery" (APP ¶75); "True Science is the contact identical of the thing knowing with the thing known" (APP ¶171). The First Matter is "not to be found on earth": "go back in consciousness" (APP ¶137). Seekers who dive "by the one ray of intellect", like Kirchberger, get revelation without realization; passive seekers in Love go slower but surer (APP ¶114–115). Atwood ranks Behmen above the metaphysicians: Kant stayed on the "negative margin", Fichte and Schelling saw the Absolute ground without experiencing it, while Behmen was "in the Experience" (APP ¶96). Against Bacon's "servant and interpreter of Nature", man turned within and allied to God can be Nature's "revealer and master"; the a priori method is the true one (APP ¶120).

## The Work in this chapter

- **Matter**: "take life as it is and ferment it" (APP ¶132): the work is done on one's own life, its lowest, mineral part (stated; APP ¶44).
- **Agent**: "a Saturn (i.e., Will) has the Key" (APP ¶125): the will, which bound the light of life, alone frees it (stated); "it is love which attracts" upward (APP ¶98, stated).
- **Operation**: fermenting "by another life" (APP ¶132), repeated "over and over" (APP ¶17), in the blood (APP ¶6) (stated).
- **Stages**: common trance, then the third life (APP ¶3); dissolution of the sensuous, then the medial life, to reach the divine (APP ¶182) (stated).
- **Signs**: the purplish "Golden Fleece" (APP ¶22), "green" vegetative life as "hope" (APP ¶119), a "sudden coruscation" (APP ¶126) (stated).
- **Impediments**: self-will, idolatry and haste (APP ¶4, ¶37, ¶103) (stated).
- **Operator**: "all depends on the willing and the intention" (APP ¶35) (stated).
- **Result**: "The soul in its metalline perfection is the philosopher's stone" (APP ¶181), "a real ens of light" (APP ¶93) (stated).

## Terms introduced

- **Three Lives** — Terrestrial (head), Celestial or medial (heart region), Infernal (lumbar, voluntary) (APP ¶32–35).
- **third life** — the lowest, mineral life man is fallen into, to be raised (APP ¶2, ¶35, ¶44).
- **medial life** — the celestial life through which the third is born; faith (APP ¶35, ¶45).
- **fermentation** — the core operation, life acting on life to perfect it (APP ¶27, ¶132).
- **Philosopher's Stone** — the regenerated life's efflux, "a concrete Essence of Light" (APP ¶6, ¶93).
- **Salt, Mercury, Sulphur** — the blood's Attraction, Circulation, Repulsion (APP ¶24); Salt is the will (APP ¶65), Sulphur "the pure light of life" (APP ¶125).
- **self-will** — will acting for itself, not under God's law (APP ¶4, ¶124).
- **Caput Mortuum** — the "ashes" preserving the principle of body (APP ¶26).
- **Golden Fleece** — purplish light over the seeker's body (APP ¶22).
- **Proteus** — the one universal life before individuality (APP ¶138).
- **Golden Bough** — the aurific seed (APP ¶152).
- **Vulcan** — Motion (APP ¶129).
- **principium** — the beginning of the work, the intention (APP ¶35).

## Authorities cited

Behmen (also Boehme) is cited most and praised as the deepest ontologist, "in the Experience" (APP ¶27, ¶62–65, ¶96, ¶117, ¶122). Kirchberger and de St Martin, from the *Theosophical Correspondence*, are quoted and qualified (APP ¶78, ¶97–98, ¶107, ¶112–115). Paracelsus supplies the Archaeus, the evestrum (the soul's destiny-dream) and the Divine Art of separation (APP ¶32, ¶57, ¶73); Khunrath the Stone/Christ analogy and names for the Caput Mortuum (APP ¶26, ¶44, ¶46); Norton and Ripley warnings against haste (APP ¶29, ¶103); Vaughan, Basil Valentine, Lully, Flamel, Eirenaeus, Haly, Synesius and Sendivogius make single points (APP ¶12, ¶29, ¶43, ¶50, ¶55, ¶67, ¶89, ¶163). Virgil and Homer are read as Hermetic allegory (APP ¶141, ¶144, ¶157, ¶165–171); Plotinus and Proclus are praised for metaphysics only (APP ¶43, ¶137, ¶172). Kant, Fichte, Schelling, Hegel, Oken and Bacon are ranked or answered (APP ¶31, ¶96, ¶117, ¶120). Scripture (Moses, St Paul, Job, the Psalms, Commandments, our Lord) runs throughout (APP ¶4, ¶60, ¶100, ¶134, ¶143).
