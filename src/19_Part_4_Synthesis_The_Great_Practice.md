# Part IV Synthesis: The Hermetic Practice

Part IV turns from theory to practice. It follows the Work from its first manual labor on the "Philosophic Subject" through the threefold Subtle Work and the six Keys of Eudoxus to the Conclusion's summary of the whole Inquiry. Its central claim is that the practice begins as a literal, slow handwork but is carried out in man and on man. The decisive operations are the purification of the operator's will, the falling silent of the mind, and the reduction of a divided self to unity with its Source. In Atwood's words, “Life is the nucleus of the whole Hermetic Mystery, and the Key thereof is Light” (IV.4 ¶32).

## How the argument unfolds

**Of the Vital Purification, Commonly Called the Gross Work** opens the practice. The first approach is “entrancing the senses in their own medial light”, by mesmerizing or a fixed gaze, but Atwood insists this trance is “a beginning only” (IV.1 ¶3–4). The medium it releases is “volatile, immanifest, phantastic, irrational” and must be purified “by a dissolution of the circulating body in its own blood” (IV.1 ¶4). That purification has a single “Manual” and “Linear” way. The Hand imparts the Spirit and, by “continual mechanic trituration”, dissolves its innate defects (IV.1 ¶5). The labor must be slow: Norton's Ordinal warns that “Excess of one half quarter of an hour May destroy all” (IV.1 ¶12), and Atwood reads *Festina Lente* as a call for trituration that is “slow, gentle, and continuous” (IV.1 ¶13). The working theory “can be obtained through the practice only” (IV.1 ¶18). The chapter's second half moves inward. “Will is the greatest power of purgation” (IV.1 ¶31), and the Second part of the Gross Work is “the Solution of the Philosophic Salt (i.e., the voluntary bond)” (IV.1 ¶36). Of this knowledge Atwood says “Hands and intellect are here alike incapable” (IV.1 ¶39), and “Everything depends upon the Motive, which is the true spiritual ferment” (IV.1 ¶44). The Work proper begins only when “all things be pure without and within” (IV.1 ¶49).

**Of the Philosophic or Subtle Work** takes up that threshold. Atwood sets out three operations, “so many degrees through which the Spirit passes from conception to manifestation” (IV.2 ¶3), and she keeps the Adepts' reserve about their order. She notes that some who had the Matter and the means of purification still failed “in defect of the ultimate theory” (IV.2 ¶4). Through fables, Aristotle's causes and three Fires, the chapter arrives at the Theosophist's formula: “reduce the Ternary by the Quaternary, through the rejection of the Binary, to the simplicity of the Monad” (IV.2 ¶18). In *Lumen de Lumine* the work is done by “an invisible Artist”, the Spirit of God, and the operator only keeps “the outward heat” (IV.2 ¶18). Knowledge of the First Cause needs “quietude and a cessation of mental energy” (IV.2 ¶33). The chapter ends with an unnamed philosopher's “sudden illumination” (IV.2 ¶41), which Atwood calls the sum of the Mystery, though she withholds “an important link” of practice (IV.2 ¶45).

**The Six Keys of Eudoxus** sets out the practice in the most operational language of the Part, quoted without comment (IV.3 ¶1–16). Eudoxus warns that the three works are analogous and that the philosophers “designedly speak in equivocal terms” (IV.3 ¶3). The Keys run as follows: whitening the Stone; sublimation by “the Fire of the Wise”; the long Third Key preparing “our Mercury”; turning water into earth; fermentation; and multiplication. Eudoxus admits that he fully explained only the Third Key (IV.3 ¶16). Atwood then completes Multiplication and Projection from other authors and tells gold-seekers that “literally taken they are not” true (IV.3 ¶26). She reads Fermentation as “the union of man, reduced to the simplicity of the Monad, with God” (IV.3 ¶27). That ties the Keys back to the Monad of the previous chapter.

**The Conclusion** restates the whole. “Man was the proper laboratory of the whole Art” (IV.4 ¶3). The “ray of motive Light” is “the true Form of Gold” (IV.4 ¶9), and Mesmerism is only “a first key” to the vestibule of the Mysteries (IV.4 ¶5). Atwood answers objections. Outward improvement cannot reach inbred evil, for “That which generation binds dissolution only can unloose” (IV.4 ¶17–18). Success comes “by conditionating, in obedience to the Divine Will” (IV.4 ¶23). The Wisdom is “that Nothing which Socrates knew”, lost by becoming “dividual” and regained by abandoning selfhood (IV.4 ¶27).

## What this Part contributes to the Work

Part IV supplies the Inquiry's fullest account of procedure. It also supplies a set of decoding keys, several of which the text states outright.

- **Philosophic Salt → “the voluntary bond”** (IV.1 ¶36), stated. Its Solution is the Second part of the Gross Work. Whether the bond is the soul–body bond Khunrath describes or the operator's self-will is left open.
- **Antimony's venom → “arrogant self-will, sensuality, folly, avarice”** in the operator's mind (IV.1 ¶40), stated by Atwood.
- **Lunar Vulcan → “the first prepared Subject”**, also Diana and the Adepts' interior Fire (IV.1 ¶29), stated. Vulcan stands for Motion (IV.1 ¶34).
- **Motive → “the true spiritual ferment”** (IV.1 ¶44), stated.
- **fixed Sulphur → “the Stone of Philosophers”** (IV.2 ¶6), stated in *The Triumph*.
- **Salt, Sulphur and Mercury → “the spirit, the soul, and the body”** (IV.3 ¶7), stated by Eudoxus without saying which answers to which.
- **Water from the Stone → “the Vinegar of the Wise”** (IV.3 ¶8); **White and Red Mercury → the Lunaria and “the most sharp Vinegar”** (IV.3 ¶10), stated by Eudoxus.
- **blood of innocents → “the spirits of the bodies”** (IV.3 ¶10), stated.
- **Fermentation → union of man with God** (IV.3 ¶27), stated by Atwood after the Kabalah.
- **Aurific Seed / the Stone's metal → Light** (IV.3 ¶30), stated. The Conclusion makes the “ray of motive Light” “the true Form of Gold” (IV.4 ¶9).
- **Root of Reason → “Wisdom, and that saving Salt”** (IV.4 ¶20), stated.

On the elements of the Work, the Part establishes the following:

- **Subject.** It is one “Universal Subject”, nourishable in man (IV.4 ¶2, ¶10), and the vessel is man himself (IV.4 ¶3). In Eudoxus's terms nothing is cast away, since the black earth holds “the Fixed Salt, which is the Blood of our Stone” (IV.3 ¶7).
- **Agent.** In the Gross Work the hand is the instrument (IV.1 ¶5). Beyond it the agent is not the operator. It appears as “an invisible Artist” (IV.2 ¶18), as “the Fire of the Wise”, the knowledge of which is “rather a gift of Heaven, than a Light acquired by the natural force of reasoning” (IV.3 ¶5), as the “ray of motive Light” (IV.4 ¶9), and in the theosophist's words: “The Mercury which is therein doth all this itself” (IV.3 ¶38). Reading these as one agent seen under different names is an interpretation, not a statement of the text.
- **Operator.** The operator's part changes over the course of the Part. In the Gross Work it is the hand and the will (IV.1 ¶5, ¶31). In the Subtle Work it is keeping heat and keeping silent (IV.2 ¶18, ¶33). Throughout, it depends on the Motive and “the true Intention” (IV.1 ¶44; IV.3 ¶54).
- **Stages.** The Gross Work has a First Operation by hand and a Second in which Nature works alone “without any imposition of hands” (IV.1 ¶11). The Subtle Work completes the Stone “in three successive circulations” (IV.2 ¶33). The Keys give six operations within three analogous works (IV.3 ¶3–16). The text does not say how these schemes map onto one another.
- **Signs.** Whiteness ends the First Key (IV.3 ¶3). The water thickens (IV.3 ¶9). Coagulation must come in its time (IV.3 ¶12). In Bloomfield, blackness gives way to Phoebus (IV.2 ¶36), and the end is “sudden illumination” (IV.2 ¶41).
- **Result.** In the theosophist's words, “Two Natures” are “reduced to One” (IV.3 ¶38). This is “a new birth, superior to nature” (IV.4 ¶12), and the line returning to a circle is “the true Christian Philosopher's Stone” (IV.3 ¶62).

Taken together, the chapters can be read (interpreted) as one movement. Each starts with an outward or literal operation and resolves it into an inward one: handwork into purification of will, the metallic Keys into union, and Mesmerism into a first step only (IV.3 ¶37).

## Threads carried forward

- How the First and Second Operations relate (IV.1 ¶16–17).
- Whether the “voluntary bond” is the soul–body bond or self-will (IV.1 ¶36).
- The “important link” of practice that Atwood withholds (IV.2 ¶45). The practical device passed almost only by word of mouth (IV.4 ¶7).
- The identity of “the Fire of the Wise”, which the text never names (IV.3 ¶5).
- Whether “That” in IV.2 ¶43 covers only the illumination or the whole process.
- Undefined or unexplained terms: “Identic co-operation” (IV.4 ¶12), and how to “render the fixed tincture of life volatile, and to return it” (IV.4 ¶32).
- Whether the “King” renewed with the faculties is Reason or its Source (IV.4 ¶20).
