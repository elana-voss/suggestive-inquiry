# Introduction

This book is a structured digest of Mary Anne Atwood's *A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery* (1850). Its aim is to state, as plainly as the text allows, what the book says and the method it hints at.

## How to read it

- **Chapters** — one page per chapter of the book, in the book's order (Parts I–IV).
- **Part syntheses** — one page closing each Part, drawing its chapters together.
- **Overall Book Synthesis** — the argument of the whole book at a glance.
- **The Method in Plain Terms** — the method the book hints at: what the work is done on, by what agent, what it asks of the operator, its stages, signs, impediments and result.
- **Appendix: Table Talk and Memorabilia** — Atwood's later notes, headed "Begun August 1860". They are a later layer and are kept separate throughout.
- **Master Lexicon and Concordance** — every term, person, work and voice the book uses, with what the book says about each.

## Ground rules

- **The book is the only source.** Nothing is added from outside knowledge or later scholarship. Names keep the book's spellings.
- **Quotations are verbatim** and carry references: III.2 ¶7 is paragraph 7 of Part III, chapter 2; I.3 n7 is footnote 7 of Part I, chapter 3; APP ¶35 is item 35 of the Appendix.
- **Voices are kept apart.** Atwood's own position is distinguished from the authorities she quotes, and the text says whether she endorses, qualifies or rejects them.
- **Stated vs interpreted.** Where a meaning is inferred rather than said by the text, it is marked as such ("apparently", "interpreted") with its basis.
- **Translations** of Latin and other passages are the book's own where it gives one; otherwise they are marked as ours and are not used as evidence on their own.
