# Part III Synthesis: Concerning the Laws and Vital Conditions of the Hermetic Experiment

Part III turns from what the Hermetic art was to how it was done. Across four chapters Atwood argues that the alchemists' first matter is a real, substantial life that can be known only by experiment within one's own being (III.1 ¶3). She holds that this subject is finally “our Identity” (III.2 ¶7). It is worked by Intellect, not by outward fire, through a dissolution, a darkness and a reunion, until the human reason becomes an immortal Light (III.1 ¶13, ¶49–50; III.3 ¶80). Her central claim is that the work's agent springs from the operator himself. For that reason his intention “is received back according to its kind most exactly” (III.4 ¶9), and character becomes a condition of the experiment.

## How the argument unfolds

**Of the Experimental Method and Fermentations of the Philosophic Subject According to the Paracelsian Alchemists and Some Others** lays down the method. The subject is the Phantastic Spirit or Ether, figured as the Sphinx at the door of Isis (III.1 ¶5). Mesmerism is only “a first key” that shows “the labyrinth of life” but not how “to alter and amend the Thing” (III.1 ¶8). Natural reason is “an iron key”. It helps the spiritual intellect know itself, and that intellect then works “the whole change” (III.1 ¶13). Reading the Smagardine Tablet's “Thou shalt separate”, Atwood makes “Mind to be the true Separator” (III.1 ¶21). The sequence follows. A grain of “metaphysical ferment” leavens the whole (III.1 ¶24). The “celestial medial life” must be “cut off, even in its prime” (III.1 ¶26). The wrathful Efficient, called Typhon or the Green Lion, drives the matter through “the Gate of Putrefaction” (III.1 ¶56, ¶58). Agent and patient, Sun and Moon, are crucified together and reunited “under another law” as “the Elixir of Life” (III.1 ¶49).

**A Further Analysis of the Initial Principle, and its Eduction into Light** goes back to the ground this process works on. With the Kabalists, Atwood takes the Initial Principle as hidden, “nothing or no entity as respects us”, until it shows itself as Light (III.2 ¶3, ¶10). She identifies it with the alchemists' Matter. It is a universal vapour that “is the Stone”, and in the end “our Identity” (III.2 ¶7). The One Thing appears to be “that Identity which is made in the regeneration” (III.2 ¶16). Atwood brings in witnesses from Boehme, Basil Valentine, the German metaphysicians and the Platonists. In her account all of them reach one ground, which German metaphysics reaches by abstraction and the Rabbis by experiment (III.2 ¶29–31, ¶47–49). The chapter adds the inner experience of the passage. In the “Night of Matter” the intellect feels but no longer understands, until “Death is the way of Life” (III.2 ¶55). The Matter flies from whoever “intently” beholds it, so the operator must rest, like Endymion (III.2 ¶57).

**Of the Manifestations of the Philosophic Matter** sets out what appears once that ground is reached. In order, these are the first Water (the “Tears of Isis”), the tribulation, and the soul's light becoming visible “in the liquid crystal of the understanding ether” (III.3 ¶3, ¶10). Then come the “Head of the Crow”, the “tail of the peacock”, the marriage of Mind and Universe, and the Stone (III.3 ¶13, ¶15, ¶19). Atwood distinguishes two conjunctions: “In the first conjunction the Spirit predominates; in the second the Soul, i.e., its Light; which two are, by adepts, called Mercury and Gold” (III.3 ¶30). Exchanging active and passive among the principles is the “Quadrature of the Circle” (III.3 ¶30). The chapter closes with the return to the source. Life passes “back into the Initial germ” until reason is “BECOMING A BRIGHT Light” (III.3 ¶79–80).

**Of the Mental Requisites and Impediments Incidental to Individuals, Either as Masters or Students, in the Hermetic Art** draws the practical consequence. Since the agent is “Springing directly from ourselves” (III.4 ¶9), avarice, pride, disbelief and inconstancy are not side issues but impediments within the work itself (III.4 ¶3–7). The chapter lists the qualities required: faith, patience, discrimination, study before practice, prayer joined to labour, “suitable assistance”, a fit vessel and a secret place. It ends with Vaughan's lessons and “the Master Key” (III.4 ¶15–47).

## What this Part contributes to the Work

**Decoding keys** (stated in the text unless marked):
- **First matter / Matter**: the inner vital substance, the Phantastic Spirit or Ether (III.1 ¶5). It is finally “our Identity. This is the true Hermetic material” (III.2 ¶7). It is also named Diana (III.1 ¶70), Isis, “the Divine I Am” (III.3 ¶62), and the Abyss of the Kabalists and Water of Thales (III.2 ¶60).
- **Sphinx**: the Ether, whose subduing means “the Ethereal Spirit abandons her phantasy, and yields the clear light of understanding” (III.1 ¶20).
- **Gold, Sol, Sulfur, Tincture**: names for Reason or Intellect (III.1 ¶21). **Antimony** is the fire of life in man (III.1 ¶10).
- **Moon / Luna**: “the Passive Intelligence” that answers the seeking Reason (III.1 ¶40).
- **Mercury and Gold**: Spirit and the Soul's Light in the two conjunctions (III.3 ¶30). Mercury is also “the impure vapor of life” that putrefies (III.1 ¶24).
- **Typhon, Green Lion, Firedrake, Black Toad**: the wrathful Efficient of dissolution, the unclean but “necessary ingredient” (III.1 ¶56, ¶61).
- **Lunar Vulcan / connate spirit**: the inborn spirit whose “attractive grace” brings the self-willed agent to mortify itself; it plays Isis (III.1 ¶62–63).
- **Identic Salt**: the true seed that survives the dissolution (III.2 ¶20). **Corner Stone**: the virtue of the New Life, the Art's foundation (III.1 ¶66).
- **Sulphur, Mercury, Salt**: the Word of God in regeneration, the prepared selfhood, and their union (III.3 ¶77).
- **Vessel**: the gross body (III.3 ¶24). Elsewhere it is a glass vessel that “the Spirit finally constructs” and vitrifies (III.4 ¶31–32).

**Subject.** The work's subject is the operator's own vital life and essential self (III.1 ¶3; III.2 ¶7).

**Agent.** The agent is Intellect, “no other Fire but that of Intellect” (III.2 ¶62), whose divine principle is the “vertical separated Light” (III.1 ¶37). The new hypostasis is created by “the Divine Fiat” (III.3 ¶10). The agent comes from the operator and follows his will (III.4 ¶9; interpreted).

**Pairing.** “Both therefore have to be prepared --- the spiritual agent and the spiritual patient” (III.1 ¶41). Chapter 4 adds that without “a Subject to work with and reciprocate the design” the work is “abortive” (III.4 ¶29).

**Operator.** The operator must first be healed (III.1 ¶9), humbled “under the exemplary cross of Christ” (III.1 ¶60), receptive rather than straining (III.2 ¶57), and theurgically prepared (III.3 ¶59). He must also be upright in intention (III.4 ¶11).

**Stages.** The chapters give the stages in parallel terms: ferment, death of the medial life, putrefaction and reunion (III.1); separation, introspection and reunion, or “solution, sublimation, and fixation” (III.2 ¶20, ¶64); first Water, visible Light, darkness, colours and two conjunctions (III.3). Reading these as one sequence seen from different angles is an interpretation.

**Signs.** The signs are turmoil “like a tempestuous sea” (III.1 ¶24), “demoniacal forms” at first (III.2 ¶7), Proteus-like colours (III.2 ¶20), blackness, the peacock's colours, then ashy, white and yellow (III.3 ¶13, ¶19), and joy with an inner motion (III.3 ¶9).

**Impediments.** These are self-will, “his own blind purpose” (III.1 ¶38); fire that is too strong (III.1 ¶55; III.4 ¶44); resting in “the first fruits of his reason” (III.3 ¶22); striving (III.2 ¶57); and avarice, pride and disbelief (III.4 ¶3–7).

**Result.** The result is regeneration and “the living gold of the philosophers” (III.1 ¶49). The soul “arrives not at another thing but at itself” (III.2 ¶66), and reason becomes an immortal Light (III.3 ¶80).

## Threads carried forward

- Chapter 4 ends at the threshold of “the routine of Practice”. “The Master Key” is named but not explained, and manual practice must show the rest (III.4 ¶2, ¶47–48).
- How the “dissolute void of life” is “artificially induced” is not said (III.2 ¶16). The text also leaves open whose will is “the central magnet” (III.2 ¶24).
- The text leaves open whether the Moon, the Passive Intelligence, is an inner faculty or a second person (III.1 ¶40), and likewise whether the reciprocating “Subject” is a person or the material (III.4 ¶29). Chapter 4 also speaks of “the entranced Subject” (III.4 ¶42).
- Whether the ancients' “representative vessels” are physical or living is unsettled (III.1 ¶9). So is whether Pontanus' “Fire” and Basil Valentine's “Antimony” are the “vertical separated Light” (III.1 ¶37).
- Mesmerism is called “a first key” and “the neglected Door-keeper” (III.1 ¶8; III.4 ¶13). Its exact relation to the Hermetic practice is not settled here.
