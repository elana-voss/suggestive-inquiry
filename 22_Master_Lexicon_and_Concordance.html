Atwood: A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery

Hermetic Philosophy / Alchemy

  • Author’s Core Definition: An antique, universal divine science coeval with the universe itself, concerned with the means and principles of occult nature, the quintessential perfectibility of the three kingdoms in their homogeneal unity, and the regeneration of the human soul.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Linked directly to the Egyptian Chemia (black soil/pupil of the eye), the Sacred Art of Memphis, the Religion of Intellect, and the universal operation of the Sun.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Introduced as an antique tradition transmitted from Egypt through Persia, Greece, Arabia, and medieval Europe; characterized as an exact hyperphysical science veiled behind riddles and cabalistic metaphors to protect it from vulgar abuse.
    • Part II, Chapter I: Revealed as an internal, human-centered divine art—not a physical chemistry of base minerals, but a psychical chemistry operating upon Man as the complete microcosm.
    • Part IV & Appendix: Defined as divine chemistry and vital fermentation that purifies, dissolves, and regenerates the human vital spirit in the arterial blood, transforming the soul into a permanent concrete essence of Light.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “For it is most profounde philosophye / This subtill science of holy Alkimy.” (Part I, Chapter I [105])
    • “Alchemy is an universal art of vital chemistry, which by fermenting the human spirit, purifies, and, finally dissolving it, opens the elementary germ into new life and consciousness…” (Appendix [1853])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Must not be confused with vulgar pseudo-chemistry or greedy gold-making; true alchemy operates upon living universal nature rather than lifeless, gross minerals.

Smaragdine Table (Tabula Smaragdina / Emerald Table)

  • Author’s Core Definition: An ancient, unique, and authentic Egyptian artifact attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, containing in a few enigmatical lines the complete working principle and total subject of the Hermetic Art.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Establishes the fundamental Hermetic axiom of macrocosm-microcosm correspondence, the operation of the “One Thing,” and the genealogical mapping of Sun (Sulfur), Moon (Mercury), Wind (Air), and Earth (Salt).
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Translated and presented as the sole surviving Egyptian record of the Sacred Art, outlining the separation of subtle from gross, the ascent and descent of the spirit, and the integration of superior and inferior powers.
    • Part IV, Chapter III: Used as the primary operational blueprint for the Six Keys of Eudoxus and the rotation of the elements.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “True, without error, certain and most true; that which is above is as that which is below, and that which is below is as that which is above, for performing the miracles of the One Thing.” (Part I, Chapter I [15])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Functions as both a cosmological summary and a practical operational blueprint for the Great Work.

Hermes Trismegistus (Thrice Great Hermes)

  • Author’s Core Definition: Egypt’s legendary adept king and prince of wisdom, celebrated by antiquity for his spiritual intelligence of all things in their universal law and his discovery of quintessential perfectibility across the three kingdoms.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The archetypal founder of Hermetic Science, author of the Asclepian Dialogues, Divine Pymander, Tractatus Aureus, and Tabula Smaragdina.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Positioned as the foundational historical/mythic figure of the Hermetic story, living prior to the Pharaohs, who codified the theosophic ground of regeneration and physical alchemy.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Therefore I am called Thrice Great Hermes, having the Three Parts of the philosophy of the whole world.” (Part I, Chapter I [17])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Represents both a historical personage in ancient tradition and the personification of the Divine Intellect.

One Thing (Prima Materia / Universal Subject / Hyle)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The homogenous, universal living subject-matter of all existence, which contains all nature in itself purely and sincerely, capable of being transformed into fire, air, water, and earth.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The root of gold, silver, and all minerals; also referred to as Mercury, Argent Vive, divine water, and the secret salt of the world.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Introduced through the Emerald Table and early adept citations as the single root from which all adaptations and transmutations arise.
    • Part II, Chapter I: Identified as residing centrally within Man himself as the microcosmic seed.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “And as all things were from one, by the mediation of one, so all things arose from this one thing by adaptation.” (Part I, Chapter I [15])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Not to be mistaken for vulgar quicksilver or common earth; it is an unctuous, living, and spiritual humidity.

Religion of Intellect

  • Author’s Core Definition: The divine, rational, and interior spiritual worship practiced in ancient Egypt and classical mysteries, centered on the divine capabilities and regeneration of the human soul.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Opposed to vulgar idolatry, superstition, and sensory-driven religion; foretold in the Asclepian Prophecy to be proscribed and subjected to capital punishment during the old age of the world.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Highlighted in the prophecy of Asclepias as the sacred heart of Egyptian wisdom that was abandoned and vilified as dark ages supervened.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Believe me, likewise, that a capital punishment shall be appointed for him who applies himself to the Religion of Intellect.” (Part I, Chapter I [23-24])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Refers to direct intellectual contact with Divine Reality, not mere dry intellectualism or logical discourse.

Aelia Laelia Crispis (Bononian Enigma)

  • Author’s Core Definition: A famous ancient enigmatic inscription found at Bologna, claimed by adepts as an allegorical description of the contradictory properties of their universal subject.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Parallel to the paradoxical descriptions of the Philosopher’s Stone (neither male nor female, neither in heaven nor earth, corpse and sepulcher the same).
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Cited as evidence that Roman antiquities held hidden references to the occult material of the Hermetic Art.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Nec vir, nec mulier, nec androgyna… Sed omnia! … Both corpse and sepulcher the same!” (Part I, Chapter I [47-50])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: An allegorical riddle whose seeming impossibilities resolve when applied to the universal, unformed spirit.

Geber’s Gibberish / Hermetic Veil

  • Author’s Core Definition: The intentionally obscure, paradoxical, and seemingly foolish language used by Prince Geber and other true adepts to guard wisdom from the incapable and sordid world while guiding rational inquirers.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The origin of the word “gibberish”; a protective envelope around the universal magistery.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Introduced to explain why genuine alchemical texts read as unintelligible jargon to modern chemists and superficial readers.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Right skillfully has old Geber veiled a fair discovery, by his own art alone to be unmasked: his gibberish is not of the present day’s commonplace… but a part of wisdom’s envelope…” (Part I, Chapter I [66])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Not random nonsense, but a precise, coded language intelligible only to those who possess the key or theory.

Quintessence (Quinta Essentia)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The fifth element or celestial spirit of life extracted from bodies by Hermetic art; an immortal, spiritualized medicine capable of perfecting nature and prolonging life.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Associated with Artephius’s thousand-year life, Roger Bacon’s multiplicative light, and the universal solvent.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Referenced in connection with Artephius and Roger Bacon as the vital agent of renewal and multiplication.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “…by the grace of God alone, and the use of this admirable quintessence…” (Part I, Chapter I [72])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Distinct from elementary water or physical chemicals; a pure, ethereal life-force.

Projection / Powder of Projection

  • Author’s Core Definition: The final practical operation in Alchemy wherein a minute quantity of the perfected Stone or Powder is cast upon molten base metals to transmute them into pure gold or silver.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Demonstrates the miraculous multiplicative power of the fixed Light/Tincture.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter I: Illustrated by historical accounts of Flamel, Kelly, Dee, Van Helmont, and Sendivogius converting lead, mercury, or tin into pure gold.
    • Part IV, Chapter III & IV: Explained as the projection of the living Adept’s spiritualized consciousness onto other subjects to awaken and transform them.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “I made projection of a part, and have tinged many hundreds of ounces by it into the best silver…” (Part I, Chapter I [166])
  • Nuances & Disambiguation: Projection is the external proof of the Stone’s internal perfection and assimilative power.

Solution (Solutio / Reduction to Water)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The initial, indispensable operation in Alchemy wherein a solid, corruptible body is reduced into its radical moisture or argent vive, setting its subject-matter free from specific form.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The foundation of the Great Work; represented by the aphorism Solve et Coagula.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Defined as the reduction of metal or body into water, freeing the subject-matter to receive a new Form.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Species non transmutantur, sed subjecta specierum optime et propriisime: — therefore the first work is to reduce the body into water, that is, into mercury, and this is called Solution…” (Part I, Chapter II [227])

Radical Moisture (Humidum Radicale)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The fundamental, living, unctuous humidity composed of universal life-force that forms the underlying subject of all metals, plants, and animals.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Identical to Philosophic Mercury and the First Matter; the passive recipient of Formal Light.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Identified as the true transmutable subject within metals that is set free by a superior ferment.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “…it is the subject-matter of these metals, the radical moisture of which they are uniformly composed, that they say may be withdrawn by art…” (Part I, Chapter II [227])

Formal Light (Lumen / Sulphur Tingens)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The active, formal, and multiplying principle of creation; the divine agent that exalts and perfects every passive body it touches according to its kind.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The true sulphur tingens that ascends and descends from the first fountain of generation; the active counterpart to Radical Moisture.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Introduced as the only thing that can be truly multiplied, acting upon passive principles in all three kingdoms.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “It is the Light only that can be truly multiplied, for this ascends and descends from the first fountain of multiplication and generation.” (Part I, Chapter II [232])

Inversion of Nature (Solve, Coagula, Fige)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The real experimental reversing of ordinary natural procedure, wherein the fixed is made volatile and the volatile fixed, inverting the outward flow of energy toward the inner divine center.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Expressed in the formula Solve, Coagula, Fige; the essential operational mechanism of Hermetic regeneration.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Presented as the inversion of nature’s ordinary contrariety to unveil the central form.
    • Appendix: Defined as turning the linear outbirth back into a circle, uniting the end to the beginning in Eternity.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Si fixum solvas faciasque volare solutum / Et volucrem figas, facient te vivere tutum: / Solve, Coagula, Fige.” (Part I, Chapter II [254])

Free Ether / Lumen Vestimenti

  • Author’s Core Definition: The elder Quintessence of the Magi freed from the prison of gross matter; an uncompounded, compact, and tangible essence acting as the vehicle of divine Light.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The plastic fire (Ignis Artificiosus) of Zeno and Cicero; the all-pervading medium of theosophy.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Distinguished from common atmospheric air as an experimental, tangible essence (Lumen Vestimenti).
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “…in which pure estate, the Kabalists, also describing, call it, Lumen Vestimenti, the Vehicle of Light; and the Greeks, the Free Ether…” (Part I, Chapter II [249])

Proteus / Pan (Universal Mutable Subject)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The ancient mythic personification of the First Matter in its omniform, endlessly changing, and unfixed state before fixation.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Encompasses all nature; must be bound with “manacles and fetters” of divine fire to reveal its true form.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Introduced via Democritus and Orphic hymns as the all-containing, mutable subject of transformation.
  • Key Passages & Direct Quotes:
    • “Qui Proteum non novit, adeat Pana… Since all things nature first to thee consigned, / And in thy essence omniform combined.” (Part I, Chapter II [267, 284])

Salt, Sulfur, and Mercury (Tria Prima)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The three fundamental magnetic principles or modes of Being that emerge from the unity of the First Matter: Salt (body/patient/Attraction), Sulfur (spirit/agent/Repulsion), and Mercury (soul/medium/Circulation).
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The Paracelsian triad representing the three essential dimensions of every created entity; in man, body, soul, and spirit.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II: Introduced as the three internal modes into which the First Matter differentiates.
    • Part II, Chapter I & Appendix: Mapped onto the three principles of physical motion (Attraction, Repulsion, Circulation) and the Three Lives in Man.

Green Lion / Toad / Dragon (Intermediate Alchemical States)

  • Author’s Core Definition: Allegorical representations of the crude, unpurified, and poisonous stages of the vital spirit during initial sublimation and putrefaction.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Green Lion = raw, aggressive vitality; Toad = putrefying, venomous matter; Dragon = self-devouring, chaotic spirit.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter II & Part III, Chapter I: Described as the dangerous, intermediate forms assumed by the volatile spirit before calcination and whitening.

Crow / Raven (Corvus / Blackness / Nigredo)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The primary symbol of the first stage of the Great Work—the complete putrefaction, blackness (Nigredo), and mortification of the corruptible body and sensory ego.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The “head of the Raven” (Caput Corvi) must be severed to release the white astral Stone and blood of the Pelican.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Presented in the Tractatus Aureus as the chief principle of the Art flying without wings in the blackness of night.

Vulture (Quintessential Spirit / Sublimed Agent)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The quintessential spirit elevated upon the mountain of the vessel; the active, devouring agent during the initial stages of spiritual sublimation.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Stands upon the mountain crying out its polychromatic nature (“White of the Black, Red of the White”).
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Described in Section I of the Tractatus Aureus as the newborn spirit in the philosophic furnace.

Dragon Eating Its Own Wings (Self-Mortification / Auto-Dissolution)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The stage of digestion wherein the crude vital spirit, enclosed in the gentle moist heat of the Athanor, consumes its own volatility and lower desires.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Demonstrates the alchemical maxim that “the destroyers are the renovators”—corruption of the selfhood is the precondition for melioration.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Outlined in Section V of the Tractatus Aureus as the self-consuming phase of putrefaction.

Ixir / Ferment (Fermentum / Philosophic Leaven)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The highly perfected, solar ferment (Kindred Gold of the Wise) added to sublimed metallic or human bodies to leaven and multiply their virtue exponentially.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Analogous to yeast in dough or rennet in milk; the engine of true transmutation.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Emphasized in Section VII of the Tractatus Aureus as the indispensable agent without which no dough can ferment.
    • Appendix: Defined as the spiritual leaven that ferments the human vital spirit in its own Light.

Hen’s Egg (Ovum Philosophorum / Microcosmic Analogy)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The classical Hermetic analogy for the complete, self-contained vessel and microcosmic constitution containing all four elements in harmonious adaptation.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Symbolizes the closed energetic system of the Great Work.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Used in Section II of the Tractatus Aureus to illustrate the relationship between shell, white, yolk, and central fire.

Perscrutinator (Rational Fire / Secret Sulfur)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The active, searching, rational fire or secret sulfur that sits as the medium between fire and oil, probing and purifying the water.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The internal, spiritual agent of discrimination and purification.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Defined in Section II of the Tractatus Aureus as the temperate unguent that searches out the impurities of the matter.

Red Garment / Meridian Redness (Rubedo / Royal Tincture)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The final color stage of the Great Work (Rubedo), signaling the complete fixation, perfection, and royal majesty of the resurrected Stone.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Associated with the crowned king, the Meridian Redness, and the blood of the Pelican.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part I, Chapter III: Celebrated in Section III of the Tractatus Aureus as the investment of the reborn Son in the red garment and purple.

True Subject / Man as Microcosm

  • Author’s Core Definition: The fundamental discovery of esoteric Hermeticism: that Man himself—his vital spirit, divine intellect, and physical frame—is the proper subject, vessel, and laboratory of Alchemy.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Replaces external mineral gold-making; connects all macrocosmic principles to human physiology and psychology.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter I: Established as the central revelation of Part II, stripping away the chemical disguise.

Concealed Vessel (Vas Philosophorum)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The divine secret hidden from idolaters—not a glass crucible or furnace, but the closed human organism and its energetic circulatory system.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The matrix of supernatural generation; the place where the Prima Materia is cooked and refined.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter I: Highlighted as the well-guarded secret of adepts like Maria, Morien, and Albertus Magnus.

Antimonial Spirit / Spiritual Air of Antimony

  • Author’s Core Definition: The rectified, starlike magnetic spirit that harmonizes the three discordant natural principles (Attraction, Repulsion, Circulation) and restores human life to its axis.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The inner medical agent of Paracelsus and Boehme; the magnetic center of the arterial blood.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter I: Introduced as the rectifying agent that counteracts the fallen, expulsive circulation of human life.

Theurgy / Divine Works

  • Author’s Core Definition: The ancient, sacred art of divine operations practiced at Eleusis and in Egyptian temples; an efficacious spiritual technology that purifies the soul and unites it with Divine Being.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Superior to speculative philosophy; operates through Katharsis, Myesis, and Epopteia.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter II: Re-evaluated as the true historical reality behind the Greek Mysteries.

Katharsis, Myesis, and Epopteia (Initiatory Sequence)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The three sequential grades of ancient initiation: Katharsis (moral/physical purification), Myesis (closure of external senses / entry into trance), and Epopteia (beatific vision of Divine Light).
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Corresponds to the alchemical color stages (Nigredo, Albedo, Rubedo).
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter II & IV: Detailed through Neoplatonic and patristic citations as the authentic path to deification.

Golden Bough (Aureus Ramus / Brancha Spiritualis)

  • Author’s Core Definition: A living ray of pure, rational intellectual light that serves as an unwavering guide and propitiation for the soul during its descent into the subterranean abyss of Hades.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Carried by Aeneas; identifies the Brancha Spiritualis of Raymond Lully.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Part II, Chapter III: Presented as the essential intellectual weapon needed to navigate the terrors of Aornum.

Three Lives (Terrestrial, Celestial, Infernal)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The three distinct modes or centers of human life and consciousness:
    1. Terrestrial (Animal / Head): Sensuous, head-centered, ruling sensory cognition.
    2. Celestial (Vegetable / Heart & Solar Plexus): Emotional, instinctive, receptive (Archaeus).
    3. Infernal (Mineral / Lumbar Region & Will): The deepest, voluntary mineral life-force seated in the lumbar region, representing raw Will (Saturn).
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Microcosmic mapping of the three kingdoms of nature within man.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Appendix: Explicitly detailed in Notes 25–26 as the foundational anatomy of Hermetic auto-magnetism.

Vertical & Transverse Passes (The Metaphysical Cross)

  • Author’s Core Definition: The physical and metaphysical movements of vital magnetism: perpendicular/vertical passes withdraw consciousness from sense inward, while transverse passes restore outward sensory awareness. Their intersection forms the Cross ($+$).
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: The physical mechanism of mesmeric induction mapped to the universal symbol of redemption.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Appendix: Detailed in Note 9 as the operational secret behind mesmerism and the Hermetic seal.

Auto-Magnetism / Self-Fermentation

  • Author’s Core Definition: The advanced, self-contained Hermetic operation wherein the practitioner’s own purified Divine Will (Sulfur) acts directly upon their own vital spirit (Mercury), eliminating dependency on an external operator.
  • Symbolic & Systemic Relationships: Symbolized by the Pelican feeding its young with its own blood and the Ouroboros.
  • Contextual & Chronological Evolution:
    • Appendix: Identified as the ultimate, required stage of the Great Work that transforms the adept into the living Philosopher’s Stone.
